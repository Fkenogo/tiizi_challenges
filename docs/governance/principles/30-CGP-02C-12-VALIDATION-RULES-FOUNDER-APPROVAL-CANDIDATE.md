## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Founder Approval Candidate contains one bounded constitutional subject: Validation Rules.

It does not define document or repository validation procedures, engineering validation, testing, quality assurance, link checking, linting, version control, CI/CD, release gates, coding standards, implementation or operational workflow, reusable or cross-project governance, Founder Engineering Framework controls, validators, reviewer roles, a Validation Authority, technical thresholds, scoring, templates or checklists.

## 2. Constitutional Meaning and Purpose

**VLR-01.** Constitutional validation is the bounded examination of whether identified constitutional content, change or treatment is supported by evidence sufficient to assess its conformity with the applicable constitutional requirements within a declared validation scope.

**VLR-02.** The constitutional purpose of validation is to provide an objectively reviewable basis for determining whether the applicable constitutional requirements have been evidenced, while preserving every matter that remains incomplete, contradictory, unavailable, deferred or outside the validation scope.

**VLR-03.** Constitutional validation is Tiizi-specific, attributable and technology-neutral. It establishes no universal procedure, technical mechanism or reusable governance framework and does not extend beyond the subject, requirements, evidence and scope expressly examined.

## 3. Validation and Governance Review

**VLR-04.** Constitutional validation and governance review are distinct. Governance review examines constitutional content, reasoning, boundaries and consequences; validation examines whether the evidence supports a bounded conclusion concerning conformity with applicable constitutional requirements.

**VLR-05.** Governance-review evidence and findings may inform constitutional validation, and validation findings may identify a matter requiring governance review, but neither treatment replaces, completes or predetermines the other.

**VLR-06.** Validation does not determine review triggers, review depth, proportionality, Independent Review requirements, reviewer qualifications or review procedure. Those matters retain the constitutional treatment and deferrals already established for Governance Review.

## 4. Validation and Constitutional Acts

**VLR-07.** Constitutional validation is distinct from approval. A validation conclusion does not exercise Governance Authority, approve constitutional content or substitute for an explicit attributable approval decision.

**VLR-08.** Constitutional validation is distinct from adoption and constitutional effect. Validation does not incorporate content into the authoritative constitutional governance of Tiizi, make content authoritatively govern or establish an Approval Date, Adoption Date, Effective Date or Application Date.

**VLR-09.** A declaration that validation is complete, passed or satisfactory communicates only the bounded validation conclusion supported by its evidence. The declaration does not create constitutional status, cure a missing constitutional act or establish the validity of a matter outside the declared validation scope.

## 5. Constitutional Minimum and Consequence Boundary

**VLR-10.** Constitutional validation evidence must identify at minimum: the constitutional subject and validation scope; the applicable constitutional requirements examined; the evidence considered; the bounded conclusion; and every material limitation, exception, unresolved matter or exclusion necessary to understand that conclusion.

**VLR-11.** Validation must examine every constitutional requirement applicable within its declared scope according to that requirement’s attributable status and effect. Review proportionality, administrative simplicity, urgency, implementation readiness or apparent low consequence cannot waive an applicable constitutional requirement.

**VLR-12.** The breadth and assurance of validation must remain proportionate to constitutional consequence without creating a score, tier, threshold or mandatory technical method. Proportionality affects the evidentiary examination, not the existence of an applicable constitutional minimum.

**VLR-13.** A validation conclusion must remain bounded to the requirements and evidence actually examined. Validation of one proposition, section, instrument, dependency, status claim or constitutional treatment does not validate another by association, sequence, similarity or shared documentation.

## 6. Insufficient, Incomplete, Contradictory and Unavailable Evidence

**VLR-14.** Where evidence is insufficient to support conformity with an applicable constitutional requirement, validation must not represent that requirement as satisfied. Insufficiency does not by itself reject, invalidate, amend or change the constitutional status of the subject examined.

**VLR-15.** Incomplete validation evidence must remain explicit. Any conclusion supported by the available evidence must identify the incompleteness and remain bounded so that omitted evidence or unresolved requirements are not treated as satisfied.

**VLR-16.** Contradictory validation evidence must remain identifiable and must not be reconciled by convenience, chronology, prominence, repeated use or implementation behaviour. Where constitutional judgment is required, the matter remains subject to the existing Conflict Review and Escalation boundary.

**VLR-17.** Unavailable validation evidence cannot be presumed to establish either conformity or non-conformity. Its unavailability and the resulting limitation on the validation conclusion must remain explicit without supplying missing constitutional meaning, status or effect.

**VLR-18.** Later evidence may support a later attributable validation conclusion, but it does not silently rewrite the evidence, scope, limitations or conclusion of an earlier validation context or make an unsupported earlier claim constitutionally sufficient.

## 7. Attribution, Dependency, Traceability and Status Integrity

**VLR-19.** Validation evidence must preserve the attribution, scope, status and limitations of every constitutional source or decision on which the validation conclusion relies. Validation cannot supply Authority, attribution or approval absent from those sources.

**VLR-20.** Validation must preserve dependency integrity by identifying material dependencies within scope according to their attributable status and constitutional effect. A dependency must not be treated as approved, adopted, effective, superseded, retired, withdrawn, rejected or unaffected unless its attributable source establishes that treatment.

**VLR-21.** Validation must preserve an objectively intelligible relationship among the subject examined, the applicable requirements, the evidence considered, the conclusion reached and every recorded limitation. This relationship supports constitutional traceability without replacing Amendment Traceability or another existing constitutional record.

**VLR-22.** A validation conclusion and its documentary representation are distinct from constitutional status. Recording, indexing, publishing, presenting or omitting validation information does not create, correct, suspend or alter the constitutional status of the subject examined.

## 8. Technology-Neutral Integrity and Anti-Inference

**VLR-23.** Documents, reports, declarations, checklists, repositories, files, links, tests, tools, automation, hashes, signatures, interfaces and other mechanisms may support or communicate validation evidence. No particular mechanism is required by these Validation Rules merely by virtue of its form or use, and no mechanism independently establishes conformity, Authority, approval, adoption or constitutional effect. Any mechanism separately required by applicable constitutional governance retains that requirement and effect.

**VLR-24.** Performing, recording, maintaining, presenting or administering validation does not create a Validation Authority, confer Governance Authority or allocate accountability, a role or permission. This proposed treatment validates no named instrument, proposition, decision, implementation or historical claim.

## 9. Deferred Governance

The following remain outside this work package:

- document-validation procedures and operational validation methods;
- repository checks, link checking, formatting, linting, hashing and version-control practice;
- engineering validation, testing, quality assurance, coding standards, CI/CD and release gates;
- implementation, deployment and operational governance;
- reusable governance mechanics and cross-project governance standards;
- Founder Engineering Framework controls;
- assignment, qualification, independence or conduct of validators, reviewers, recorders or maintainers;
- validation roles, permissions, accountability and service levels;
- evidence formats, schemas, templates, checklists, technical identifiers and signatures;
- scores, tiers, thresholds, pass rates and technical assurance levels;
- scheduling, frequency, workflow, automation and remediation procedure;
- validation of a named instrument, proposition, decision, status representation or implementation;
- whole-instrument consolidation of CGP-02;
- Founder approval, adoption or constitutional effect of CGP-02; and
- authorization of Stage E0 completion, CGP-03 or another work package.
