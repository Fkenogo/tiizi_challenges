## 1. Purpose and Decision Boundary

This pack presents the seven constitutional questions reserved by the approved CGP-02C.3 planning package. Each proposed treatment remains a recommendation pending attributable Founder decision. This pack does not approve a proposition, create an approval procedure or make Approval Governance constitutionally effective.

The questions are bounded to Approval Governance. Adoption, constitutional effect, effective or application dates, lifecycle, amendment classification, review triggers, proportionality, supersession, retirement, conflict governance, validation doctrine, implementation, repository workflow and accountability allocation remain excluded.

## 3. AQ-01 — Constitutional Meaning of Approval

**Issue:** What constitutes constitutional approval, and what cannot substitute for it?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Platform Constitution requires attributable Founder decisions for constitutional statements. The Platform Authority Model states that Governance Authority approves standards and authority boundaries and that approval must be explicit and attributable. CGP-01 requires attributable, bounded governance and rejects silent authority.

**Proposed treatment:** Approval is an explicit, affirmative and attributable decision of Governance Authority over identified constitutional content and a bounded scope. Drafting, recommendation, review, validation, publication, implementation, silence and passage of time do not constitute approval.

**Option A — Approve the proposed treatment:** Preserve APV-01 through APV-04 and APV-24 as drafted.

**Option B — Require a narrower definition:** Treat explicit attribution as necessary but defer the content-and-scope boundary or the list of non-substitutes to later governance.

**Option C — Defer the question:** Leave the propositions unapproved and prevent Approval Governance from progressing to an approval candidate.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A consolidates existing doctrine and closes ambiguity about implied approval. Option B preserves less constitutional detail but leaves approval scope and anti-inference treatment incomplete. Option C preserves the current gap and blocks completion of this package.

**Affected propositions:** APV-01–APV-04 and APV-24.

## 4. AQ-02 — Canonical Constitutional-Capacity Wording

**Issue:** What wording identifies the Founder’s exercise of Governance Authority, and how should historical records be treated?

**Current approved doctrine:** CGP-02C.1 FQ-04 records `Founder, exercising Governance Authority` for future approval records, continues `Governance Authority` within constitutional standards and prohibits retrospective alteration of historical approval records.

**Proposed treatment:** Preserve that wording exactly, distinguish the approving actor from the Authority exercised and retain historical wording without retrospective normalization.

**Option A — Confirm the preserved wording and treatment:** Preserve APV-05, APV-06 and APV-21 as drafted.

**Option B — Preserve the wording but narrow this package:** Retain APV-05 only and defer the actor/Authority and historical-record treatments.

**Option C — Defer confirmation:** Keep the recorded CGP-02C.1 dependency but approve no capacity proposition in this package.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A gives future approval records consistent capacity language without rewriting history. Option B preserves the phrase but leaves its constitutional boundaries incomplete. Option C avoids duplication but prevents this standard from presenting a complete approval-capacity treatment.

**Affected propositions:** APV-05, APV-06 and APV-21.

## 5. AQ-03 — Minimum Approval Evidence

**Issue:** What minimum evidence is constitutionally necessary to support an approval decision?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Constitution requires attributable decisions and amendment minima. The Authority Model requires authority actions to be reviewable and to identify authority, action, purpose, basis and time. Discovery identifies inconsistent evidence practice and an incomplete approval-evidence standard.

**Proposed treatment:** Require a reviewable evidence set identifying the decision, content, scope, approving actor, Governance Authority capacity, date and governing basis, while distinguishing approved matters from rejected, amended, pending, deferred and excluded matters.

**Option A — Approve the complete evidence minimum:** Preserve APV-07 through APV-09 as drafted.

**Option B — Approve a reduced minimum:** Require decision, actor, capacity, date and content, while deferring scope, governing basis or decision-state distinctions.

**Option C — Defer the evidence standard:** Preserve existing higher-order requirements without consolidating a minimum in CGP-02C.3.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A makes approval objectively reviewable while remaining format-neutral. Option B lowers governance burden but leaves predictable attribution and scope gaps. Option C avoids new consolidation but retains the discovery gap.

**Affected propositions:** APV-07–APV-09.

## 6. AQ-04 — Minimum Approval-Record Content

**Issue:** What information must an approval record communicate without expanding the source decision?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Constitution and Authority Model require decision attribution, rationale or basis, affected governance and amendment history. CGP-01 requires historical intelligibility and no silent change. Existing records demonstrate inconsistent treatment but do not by repetition establish doctrine.

**Proposed treatment:** Require an approval reference, outcome, identified content, scope, actor and capacity, date and source evidence; retain Constitution-required amendment fields; and expressly record partial approval, conditions, rejection, deferral and non-effects where needed for accuracy.

**Option A — Approve the complete record minimum:** Preserve APV-10 through APV-12 and APV-22 through APV-24 as drafted.

**Option B — Approve only core attribution fields:** Require reference, outcome, content, actor, capacity and date, while deferring scope, source evidence and decision-state distinctions.

**Option C — Defer the record-content standard:** Approve no consolidated minimum until later documentation governance.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A provides enough information to communicate approval without converting the record into the decision. Option B is simpler but weakens reviewability and bounded-scope clarity. Option C preserves flexibility but leaves approval records constitutionally inconsistent.

**Affected propositions:** APV-10–APV-12 and APV-22–APV-24.

## 7. AQ-05 — Recorder and Approver Boundary

**Issue:** Who may create or maintain an approval record without gaining authority to approve or Governance Authority?

**Current approved doctrine:** EOG-01 and the Authority Model distinguish Authority from administration, operation, custody and presentation. Titles, possession, access and technical capability do not establish Authority. The planning package prohibits allocation.

**Proposed treatment:** Separate exercising approval from recording it. Supporting actors may accurately record an attributable decision but cannot acquire, expand or exercise Governance Authority through authorship, recording, custody, administration, operation or presentation. No supporting relationship is allocated.

**Option A — Approve strict functional separation without allocation:** Preserve APV-06 and APV-13 through APV-16 and APV-22 as drafted.

**Option B — Approve only the non-transfer principle:** State that recording confers no Authority, but defer the accuracy boundary and explicit non-allocation statement.

**Option C — Defer the boundary:** Leave recorder treatment to later relationship-allocation or documentation governance.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A protects Authority separation while allowing later allocations to remain open. Option B prevents direct Authority transfer but leaves record-integrity duties unclear. Option C risks continued conflation between the decision-maker and supporting documentation functions.

**Affected propositions:** APV-06, APV-13–APV-16 and APV-22.

## 8. AQ-06 — Approval-Reference Convention

**Issue:** What constitutional convention is required to identify an approval decision?

**Current approved doctrine:** Authority actions require attribution and historical intelligibility. Discovery identifies inconsistent approval references and no canonical convention. No technical identifier format is constitutionally approved.

**Proposed treatment:** Require one stable, unique approval reference for each constitutional approval decision, prohibit reuse for another decision and leave format, generation and storage outside constitutional scope.

**Option A — Approve a stable unique reference requirement:** Preserve APV-17 and APV-18, with related fields in APV-10 and historical restraint in APV-21.

**Option B — Require stability but not constitutional uniqueness:** Require a persistent reference while allowing later standards to determine uniqueness scope.

**Option C — Defer the convention:** Require attributable evidence but approve no approval-reference rule in this package.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A supports durable traceability without prescribing a format. Option B reduces immediate constraint but permits ambiguity across approval records. Option C leaves a known discovery gap unresolved.

**Affected propositions:** APV-10, APV-17, APV-18 and APV-21.

## 9. AQ-07 — Approval-Time Representation and Metadata

**Issue:** Which approval-time representation or metadata changes may be recorded without altering approved constitutional meaning?

**Current approved doctrine:** CGP-01 requires historical intelligibility. CGP-02C.1 preserves substance over label, and CGP-02C.2B distinguishes meaning-preserving maintenance from meaning-changing amendment. The planning package permits this question only where needed for approval-record integrity.

**Proposed treatment:** Permit only accurate, meaning-preserving approval-time representation or metadata; prohibit timing or labels from shielding a change in meaning; and preserve historically attributable wording and decisions.

**Option A — Approve the meaning-preservation boundary:** Preserve APV-15 and APV-19 through APV-21 as drafted.

**Option B — Approve only the no-meaning-change principle:** Retain APV-19 and APV-20 while deferring historical-record treatment.

**Option C — Defer this question:** Leave approval-time representation entirely to later amendment or documentation governance.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A protects approval-record integrity while avoiding implementation rules. Option B preserves the core boundary but leaves historical normalization risk. Option C avoids overlap but leaves the approved planning question unanswered.

**Affected propositions:** APV-15 and APV-19–APV-21.
