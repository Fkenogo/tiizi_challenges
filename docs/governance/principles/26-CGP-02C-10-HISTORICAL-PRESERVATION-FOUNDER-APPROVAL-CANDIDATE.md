## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Founder Approval Candidate contains one bounded constitutional subject: Historical Preservation.

It does not define storage, archival mechanisms, retention policy, repository implementation, document-versioning implementation, Governance Index, Validation Rules, a Platform Authority, accountability allocation, implementation workflow or the correction of any named historical instrument.

## 2. Constitutional Meaning and Purpose

**HPR-01.** Historical Preservation is the constitutional treatment by which governance, attributable constitutional decisions and their historical status context remain identifiable and intelligible within the scope and time to which they applied.

**HPR-02.** The purpose of Historical Preservation is to enable a later reader to understand what constitutional meaning applied, the attributable basis on which it applied and how its recorded constitutional position relates to later governance.

**HPR-03.** Historical Preservation protects constitutional meaning, attribution and context. It does not prescribe the technical mechanism by which a record or representation is stored, retained, archived, retrieved or presented.

## 3. Historically Applicable Governance

**HPR-04.** Historically applicable governance is governance whose constitutional meaning or status was established as applicable within an identifiable historical scope or period by attributable constitutional governance.

**HPR-05.** A later amendment, adoption, constitutional effect or status treatment does not retrospectively erase governance that was attributable within its historically applicable scope.

**HPR-06.** Historical applicability does not make prior governance current outside its historical scope and does not displace current governance established through separately attributable constitutional treatment.

## 4. Constitutional Intelligibility

**HPR-07.** Historical Preservation must retain enough constitutional context to distinguish the governance content or meaning represented, its applicable scope, its historical status and the attributable basis for that treatment.

**HPR-08.** Historical intelligibility must preserve the relationship among an instrument or bounded matter, the attributable decisions concerning it and the constitutional status context those decisions expressly established.

**HPR-09.** Pending, deferred, unresolved, partial and conditional matters must remain identifiable as such where that context is necessary to prevent a historical representation from overstating constitutional meaning or status.

## 5. Attributable Decisions and Historical Status Context

**HPR-10.** Preservation of an attributable constitutional decision must keep identifiable the decision, the Governance Authority exercised, the decision date where established, the declared scope and any express condition or limitation necessary to understand it.

**HPR-11.** Historical Preservation may communicate only attribution, rationale, date, scope, condition or status supported by an attributable source. It cannot supply, reconcile or retrospectively improve missing or ambiguous constitutional evidence.

**HPR-12.** Historical status context must distinguish, where attributable and relevant, whether governance was proposed, pending, approved, adopted, effective, superseded, retired, withdrawn, rejected, deferred, unresolved or unaffected.

**HPR-13.** The preservation of multiple decisions, statuses or historical periods within one record or representation does not merge them. Each constitutional treatment must remain independently identifiable by meaning, source, scope and time.

## 6. Relationship to Current Governance

**HPR-14.** Current governance is identified by the attributable constitutional treatment that establishes its current governing position, not by historical presence, prominence, recency or continued availability alone.

**HPR-15.** Historical Preservation does not freeze governance, prevent attributable constitutional change or grant prior governance precedence over current governance.

**HPR-16.** The preservation, omission, removal, publication or continued use of a historical representation does not by itself establish that governance is current, non-current or constitutionally inapplicable.

## 7. Separation from Amendment Traceability

**HPR-17.** Historical Preservation and amendment traceability are distinct. Amendment traceability preserves the bounded relationship among source governance, constitutional change and amendment treatment; Historical Preservation protects the intelligibility of governance, decisions and status context beyond amendment-specific change.

**HPR-18.** An amendment trace may supply evidence within Historical Preservation where relevant, but neither treatment replaces the other or creates a constitutional status absent from its attributable sources.

## 8. Separation from Status Treatments

**HPR-19.** Historical Preservation and supersession are distinct. Supersession establishes an attributable replacement relationship; Historical Preservation retains the intelligibility of governance and context before and after that relationship without establishing replacement.

**HPR-20.** Historical Preservation is distinct from retirement, withdrawal and rejection. Those treatments establish bounded constitutional status; Historical Preservation retains their attributable meaning and context without establishing, enlarging or reversing any treatment.

## 9. Technology-Neutral Integrity and Anti-Inference

**HPR-21.** Storage, archival, retention, repository, versioning, indexing and presentation mechanisms may represent or support preserved constitutional meaning, but they do not establish that meaning or its constitutional status.

**HPR-22.** A label, file, status field, index entry, cross-reference, repository action, publication, implementation, operational behaviour or repeated practice cannot create, enlarge, narrow or substitute historical constitutional meaning or status except to the extent already established by attributable constitutional governance.

**HPR-23.** Creating, maintaining, recording, presenting or administering historical material does not confer Governance Authority and creates no separate Historical Preservation Authority.

**HPR-24.** Historical Preservation does not retrospectively classify, correct, validate or invalidate the constitutional meaning or status of an existing or historical instrument, decision or matter where its attributable sources did not expressly establish that treatment.

## 10. Deferred Governance

The following remain outside this work package:

- storage media, locations, services and architecture;
- archival, backup, restoration, deletion, destruction, migration and retrieval mechanisms;
- retention periods, schedules, triggers, exceptions and policy;
- repository structure, administration, access, tooling and workflow;
- document formats, schemas, technical identifiers and hashes;
- document-versioning and historical-storage implementation;
- Governance Index and Status Integrity;
- Validation Rules and validation procedure;
- correction or reclassification of a named historical instrument, decision or status;
- allocation of who records, preserves, stores, maintains, publishes or administers historical material;
- accountability, stewardship, custody, administration, operation, roles and permissions;
- implementation workflow and technical controls; and
- authorization of any successor work package.

## 11. Candidate Status and Non-Effects

HPR-01 through HPR-24 were approved without amendment for inclusion in this Founder Approval Candidate through the attributable Founder Decision Record dated 2026-07-24.

This candidate:

- changes no historical or current constitutional meaning;
- changes no governance status;
- amends, approves, adopts, supersedes, retires, withdraws and rejects no governance;
- corrects or validates no named historical instrument or record;
- creates no Platform Authority;
- allocates no accountability, role or permission;
- defines no storage, archival, retention, repository or versioning implementation;
- defines no Governance Index or Validation Rules;
- changes no CGP-02 dependency or programme state; and
- authorizes no successor work package.
