## 3. HPQ-01 — Meaning, Purpose and Mechanism Boundary

### Issue

Whether Historical Preservation should protect the identifiability and intelligibility of governance, attributable decisions and historical status context without prescribing preservation mechanisms.

### Current approved doctrine

CGP-01 requires historical intelligibility while expressly separating it from versioning, retention, storage and archival mechanisms. The Blueprint requires Historical Preservation as deliverable 14 and mandates technology-neutral drafting.

### Proposed treatment

HPR-01 through HPR-03 define Historical Preservation, its constitutional purpose and its separation from technical mechanisms.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve HPR-01 through HPR-03.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies a different bounded meaning or purpose while preserving the mechanism exclusion.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed treatment; Blueprint deliverable 14 remains incomplete.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A gives the Blueprint subject a precise constitutional function already grounded in CGP-01 historical intelligibility. It protects meaning and attribution without converting constitutional governance into storage, archival or retention design.

### Consequences

- Option A establishes a coherent proposed preservation subject without prescribing implementation.
- Option B requires corresponding changes throughout the draft and traceability.
- Option C leaves the dedicated Historical Preservation subject unresolved.

### Affected propositions

HPR-01–HPR-03.

## 4. HPQ-02 — Historically Applicable and Current Governance

### Issue

Whether historically applicable governance should remain intelligible without becoming current, displacing current governance or being retrospectively erased by later treatment.

### Current approved doctrine

CGP-01 requires a later reader to understand which constitutional meaning applied. Existing amendment-classification and supersession propositions preserve historically applicable meaning while distinguishing it from the current governing position.

### Proposed treatment

HPR-04 through HPR-06 define historically applicable governance and its non-erasure boundary. HPR-14 through HPR-16 preserve the distinction between historical presence and an attributable current governing position.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve HPR-04 through HPR-06 and HPR-14 through HPR-16.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the historical/current relationship while preserving attribution and no implied status.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed treatment.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A allows governance history to remain intelligible without competing with current governance. It prevents both retrospective erasure and the opposite error of treating historical availability as current constitutional status.

### Consequences

- Option A preserves continuity and current-status discipline together.
- Option B requires cross-package consistency review with supersession and constitutional effect.
- Option C leaves the relationship between historical and current governance constitutionally ambiguous.

### Affected propositions

HPR-04–HPR-06, HPR-14–HPR-16.

## 5. HPQ-03 — Constitutional Intelligibility

### Issue

What minimum constitutional context Historical Preservation should require for governance to remain intelligible.

### Current approved doctrine

CGP-01 requires understanding of applicable meaning, decision validity and amendment effect. The Blueprint requires historical intelligibility, explicit uncertainty and preservation of pending and deferred matters.

### Proposed treatment

HPR-07 through HPR-09 require enough context to distinguish meaning, scope, status, attribution and unresolved or bounded matters.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve HPR-07 through HPR-09.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the proposed minimum context without introducing a technical evidence format.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed minimum.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A states the smallest constitutional context needed for later understanding while remaining technology-neutral. It also prevents uncertainty, partial scope or conditions from being lost through an overbroad historical representation.

### Consequences

- Option A supplies an objective but implementation-neutral intelligibility minimum.
- Option B requires corresponding traceability and status-context changes.
- Option C leaves Historical Preservation without a reviewable integrity threshold.

### Affected propositions

HPR-07–HPR-09.

## 6. HPQ-04 — Attributable Decision Preservation

### Issue

Whether preserved constitutional decisions should retain their attributable decision context without missing or ambiguous evidence being supplied by later representation.

### Current approved doctrine

The Platform Authority Model requires material Authority exercises to remain attributable by authority, basis, purpose and time. CGP-01 and completed CGP-02 packages prohibit retrospective improvement or inference of decision evidence.

### Proposed treatment

HPR-10 and HPR-11 require preservation of attributable decision context and prohibit Historical Preservation from supplying or reconciling unsupported evidence.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve HPR-10 and HPR-11.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the minimum attributable context while preserving the no-inference boundary.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed decision-preservation treatment.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A carries existing attribution discipline into historical context without inventing a technical record format. It keeps incomplete historical evidence explicitly incomplete rather than allowing later presentation to improve the constitutional record.

### Consequences

- Option A preserves attributable decisions without creating missing evidence.
- Option B requires amendments to intelligibility and anti-inference treatment.
- Option C weakens the link between historical meaning and the Authority that established it.

### Affected propositions

HPR-10–HPR-11.

## 7. HPQ-05 — Historical Status Context

### Issue

Whether Historical Preservation should distinguish attributable constitutional statuses and keep multiple treatments independently identifiable.

### Current approved doctrine

Approval, adoption, constitutional effect, supersession, retirement, withdrawal, rejection, pending and deferred treatment have distinct meanings in completed CGP-02 packages. Those treatments may coexist in related records without merging.

### Proposed treatment

HPR-12 identifies relevant historical status distinctions. HPR-13 requires separate meaning, source, scope and time where multiple decisions or treatments are preserved together.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve HPR-12 and HPR-13.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the status-context minimum while preserving independent attribution.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed status-context treatment.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A preserves the distinct constitutional meanings already developed across CGP-02. It prevents a combined historical record from collapsing separate status acts into one inferred treatment.

### Consequences

- Option A creates a coherent proposed historical-status boundary.
- Option B requires consistency review across the listed status terms.
- Option C permits ambiguity when multiple constitutional treatments concern the same matter.

### Affected propositions

HPR-12–HPR-13.

## 8. HPQ-06 — Amendment Traceability Boundary

### Issue

Whether Historical Preservation should remain broader than amendment-specific traceability while permitting amendment traces to provide relevant evidence.

### Current approved doctrine

ATR-01 through ATR-24 define amendment-specific traceability and expressly defer Historical Preservation mechanisms. The Blueprint lists Amendment Traceability Requirements and Historical Preservation as separate deliverables.

### Proposed treatment

HPR-17 distinguishes the two subjects. HPR-18 permits evidence relationships without substitution or creation of missing status.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve HPR-17 and HPR-18.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the relationship while keeping both Blueprint subjects constitutionally distinct.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed boundary.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A preserves the Blueprint architecture: an amendment trace explains a bounded change, while Historical Preservation protects the wider intelligibility of governance and status over time. Evidence may be reused without making either record perform the other’s constitutional function.

### Consequences

- Option A prevents duplication and subject collapse.
- Option B may require reconsideration of the amendment-trace boundary.
- Option C leaves the two Blueprint deliverables vulnerable to overlap.

### Affected propositions

HPR-17–HPR-18.

HPR-19–HPR-20.

## 10. HPQ-08 — Technology Neutrality and Anti-Inference

### Issue

Whether mechanisms and administrative activity should remain representations or supports only, incapable of establishing historical meaning, status or Authority.

### Current approved doctrine

CGP-01 separates constitutional governance from implementation. The Platform Authority Model and completed CGP-02 anti-inference propositions prevent presentation, recording, repository action and repeated practice from creating Authority or constitutional status.

### Proposed treatment

HPR-16 and HPR-21 through HPR-23 establish mechanism, representation and Authority boundaries without specifying implementation.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve HPR-16 and HPR-21 through HPR-23.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the mechanism boundary without allowing representation or administration to establish constitutional meaning.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed anti-inference treatment.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A keeps the constitutional subject durable across future technologies and record systems. It also preserves existing Authority discipline by ensuring that maintaining or presenting history cannot become a new source of governance power.

### Consequences

- Option A preserves technology neutrality and no-inferred-Authority discipline.
- Option B requires careful review against the Platform Authority Model.
- Option C risks implementation practice being mistaken for constitutional truth.

### Affected propositions

HPR-16, HPR-21–HPR-23.

## 11. HPQ-09 — Retrospective and Deferred-Governance Boundary

Whether this package should prohibit retrospective classification or correction and keep all mechanisms, index, validation and allocation matters deferred.

The Blueprint forbids reinterpretation of historical decisions and source-status correction through drafting. C.5 through C.9 each preserve comparable retrospective and deferred-governance boundaries.

### Proposed treatment

HPR-24 prohibits retrospective classification, correction, validation or invalidation unsupported by attributable sources. Draft section 10 preserves all excluded governance.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve HPR-24 and draft section 10.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the boundary without authorizing a named correction, mechanism or later Blueprint subject.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed boundary and leave the package incomplete.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A prevents a general preservation doctrine from becoming a vehicle for rewriting Tiizi’s existing governance history. It also keeps Governance Index, Validation Rules and implementation subjects within their separately governed boundaries.

### Consequences

- Option A preserves historical integrity and Blueprint sequencing.
- Option B requires a renewed scope and dependency review.
- Option C leaves the package exposed to retrospective and scope-expansion ambiguity.

### Affected propositions

HPR-24; draft section 10.
