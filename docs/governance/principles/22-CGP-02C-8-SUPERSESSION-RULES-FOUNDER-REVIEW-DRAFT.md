## 1. Constitutional Meaning and Purpose

**SSR-01.** Supersession is the constitutional treatment by which an explicit and attributable act of Governance Authority establishes that, within a declared scope, identified governance no longer occupies its former current governing position because identified governance governs in its place.

**SSR-02.** The purpose of supersession is to make the replacement relationship, its constitutional basis, its bounded scope and its historical consequences explicit and objectively reviewable.

**SSR-03.** Supersession must be expressly established. It cannot arise merely because governance is newer, differently titled, differently located, more frequently used, operationally preferred or represented as current.

**SSR-04.** Supersession changes the current governing relationship within its declared scope. It does not erase the superseded governance, make its historical operation unintelligible or retrospectively invalidate constitutional treatment that was attributable within its historically applicable scope.

**SSR-05.** Supersession is a bounded constitutional treatment. It does not by itself establish amendment, approval, adoption, constitutional effect, conflict disposition, retirement, withdrawal or rejection.

## 2. Distinction from Other Constitutional Treatments

**SSR-06.** Amendment and supersession are distinct. Amendment changes constitutional content or meaning within its approved scope; supersession establishes an attributable replacement relationship. Either may accompany the other only where the applicable attributable governance expressly establishes both treatments.

**SSR-07.** Approval and supersession are distinct. Approval establishes an attributable Governance Authority decision concerning identified content; it does not by itself establish that the approved content replaces existing governance.

**SSR-08.** Adoption and supersession are distinct. Adoption incorporates approved content into the authoritative constitutional governance of Tiizi; it does not by itself identify governance displaced from a current governing position.

**SSR-09.** Conflict disposition and supersession are distinct. A conflict disposition establishes the constitutional treatment decided within the conflict scope; it supersedes governance only where the attributable Governance Authority decision expressly establishes supersession.

**SSR-10.** Supersession is not equivalent to retirement, withdrawal or rejection. Those constitutional treatments remain separately deferred and must not be inferred from a supersession record.

## 3. Governance Authority and Attribution

**SSR-11.** Supersession may be established only through an explicit and attributable act within existing Governance Authority. No separate Supersession Authority is created.

**SSR-12.** Drafting, reviewing, recording, indexing, publishing, storing, presenting, administering or implementing governance does not confer Governance Authority and cannot establish supersession.

**SSR-13.** One attributable Governance Authority decision may record supersession together with another separately established constitutional treatment, but each constitutional treatment, its scope and the governance relationship it establishes must remain independently identifiable.

## 4. Bounded Supersession Scope

**SSR-14.** An attributable supersession must identify the governance being superseded, the governance that replaces it and the exact constitutional scope of the replacement relationship.

**SSR-15.** Where supersession applies to only part of an instrument, subject or constitutional treatment, governance outside the declared supersession scope retains its recorded position unless separately changed through attributable governance.

**SSR-16.** Supersession involving multiple instruments, provisions or replacement sources must identify each bounded replacement relationship. Chronology, document numbering or a general assertion that later governance prevails is insufficient by itself.

**SSR-17.** Supersession of one governance treatment does not by itself amend, supersede or reclassify dependent governance. Any additional constitutional treatment must remain separately attributable within its own approved scope.

## 5. Historical Integrity and Minimum Evidence

**SSR-18.** Superseded governance must remain historically identifiable and intelligible together with the period, scope and constitutional context in which it held its recorded governing position.

**SSR-19.** Supersession evidence must identify the attributable decision, the Governance Authority exercised, the decision date, the governance superseded, the replacement governance and the bounded supersession scope.

**SSR-20.** Supersession evidence must preserve the stated constitutional basis and rationale, any partial or conditional boundary declared by Governance Authority and any unresolved limitation necessary to prevent the evidence from overstating the decision.

**SSR-21.** Supersession evidence may reference approval, adoption, amendment-trace, dependent-impact or conflict-disposition records. It does not replace those records, create a missing constitutional status or enlarge their scope.

## 6. Integrity and Anti-Inference

**SSR-22.** A label, status field, index entry, cross-reference, repository action, publication, implementation, operational behavior or repeated practice cannot establish, enlarge or narrow supersession except to the extent already established by an explicit attributable act of Governance Authority.

**SSR-23.** The presence or absence of a reference to governance in a current document, index, register, interface or implementation does not by itself prove that the governance is current, superseded or historically inapplicable.

**SSR-24.** This proposed treatment does not retrospectively classify an existing instrument as superseded, validate a historical replacement claim or attribute supersession to a decision that did not expressly establish it.

## 7. Deferred Governance and Explicit Non-Effects

This Founder Review Draft does not:

- supersede an actual, named, current or historical governance instrument;
- determine whether any existing instrument is or is not superseded;
- resolve or dispose of an actual governance conflict;
- determine a precedence outcome for an actual conflict;
- redefine amendment, approval, adoption or constitutional effect;
- define retirement, withdrawal or rejection;
- define governance-index, status or cross-reference synchronization;
- designate an authoritative repository location or current-document index;
- define validation doctrine or procedure;
- define historical storage, retention, archival or versioning mechanisms;
- define temporary or emergency governance;
- allocate who drafts, reviews, approves, records, indexes, publishes, maintains or implements supersession;
- create or allocate a Supersession Authority, Platform Authority, accountability relationship, responsibility, role or permission;
- define implementation workflow or repository procedure;
- define technical schema, storage, identifier format, automation, publication or deployment;
- retrospectively correct or classify governance status; or
- authorize a successor work package.

Every proposition remains subject to Founder constitutional review. No proposition in this draft is approved, adopted or constitutionally effective.
