**Work package:** CGP-02B.1 — Blueprint Finalization & Drafting Principles

**Status:** Final drafting specification; governance freeze

## 1. Purpose

This blueprint governs the preparation of CGP-02C, the first constitutional draft of the Constitutional Amendment & Governance Review Standard. It translates the accepted [CGP-02 Discovery and Source Analysis](03-CGP-02-DISCOVERY-AND-SOURCE-ANALYSIS.md) into a bounded drafting specification.

This document:

- is not constitutional governance;
- is not an amendment procedure;
- creates no governance;
- approves no lifecycle, classification, review, approval, adoption, supersession or retirement rule;
- resolves no Founder decision; and
- does not alter the status or meaning of any existing governance instrument.

Its sole purpose is to define what CGP-02C must examine, how its drafting must remain constitutionally constrained and what evidence must be ready for Founder review.

## 2. Constitutional Drafting Principles

These principles govern how constitutional drafting is performed. They do not govern constitutional amendment, approval or review, and they create no constitutional doctrine.

### 2.1 Evidence Before Interpretation

Every constitutional proposition must derive from approved governance, an attributable Founder decision or an explicitly identified Founder question. Nothing may be inferred merely because it appears repeatedly.

### 2.2 Consolidation Before Creation

Existing approved doctrine must be consolidated before any new constitutional rule is proposed. Drafting must minimize new governance.

### 2.3 Explicit Uncertainty

Where approved governance is incomplete, uncertainty must remain explicit. Drafting must identify the constitutional question and must not silently resolve ambiguity.

### 2.4 Historical Continuity

Every drafting decision must preserve historical intelligibility, amendment traceability and constitutional continuity.

### 2.5 Authority Discipline

Drafting must never infer, extend or redistribute Authority without explicit constitutional approval.

### 2.6 Technology Neutrality

Drafting must avoid implementation mechanisms, repository tooling, technical workflows and software architecture unless already approved as constitutional governance.

### 2.7 Minimal Necessary Governance

CGP-02 must define only the constitutional governance required for stable operation. Operational procedures remain outside constitutional scope unless explicitly elevated through approved governance.

## 4. Drafting Scope

CGP-02C must prepare constitutional text within the following subject areas. Inclusion here requires drafting treatment; it does not pre-approve the content or outcome of that treatment.

### 4.1 Included Drafting Subjects

- constitutional amendment principles;
- constitutional governance-review principles;
- the boundary among amendment, interpretation, status maintenance, clerical housekeeping and programme change;
- governance-document lifecycle concepts;
- amendment classification concepts;
- review triggers, thresholds and proportionality;
- Governance Authority and approval governance;
- approval evidence and approval-record content;
- approval, effective, adoption and application concepts;
- amendment traceability and historical intelligibility;
- dependent-document and cross-reference impact review;
- conflict identification, escalation and disposition evidence;
- adoption and current-baseline identification;
- supersession, retirement, withdrawal and rejection concepts;
- temporary or emergency governance questions;
- validation requirements;
- governance-index and status synchronization questions; and
- re-review and periodic-review questions.

### 4.2 Excluded Drafting Subjects

CGP-02C must not define:

- technical repository procedures, commands, tooling or file formats as constitutional requirements;
- implementation architecture;
- product behaviour;
- entity lifecycle rules unrelated to governance documents;
- roles, permissions or technical access;
- an additional Platform Authority;
- an accountable recorder, steward, custodian, administrator or operator allocation;
- delegation actors or delegation procedures not already approved;
- the resolution of an instrument-specific pending Founder decision;
- the status correction of an existing governance instrument;
- Master Programme change control beyond identifying the constitutional/programme boundary; or
- a retrospective approval for any observed practice.

## 5. Constitutional Drafting Constraints

CGP-02C drafting must:

1. preserve the precedence of the Platform Constitution and other approved higher-order governance;
2. use only approved Platform Authorities and canonical terminology;
3. trace each proposed constitutional proposition to a normative source, a clearly identified Founder question or both;
4. distinguish approved doctrine from observed practice, programme control, historical artefact and proposed treatment;
5. preserve pending and deferred matters unless attributable approval changes them;
6. record apparent tension rather than resolve it through implied precedence; and
7. remain implementation-neutral and historically intelligible.

CGP-02C drafting must not:

- infer Founder approval;
- create governance from repeated practice;
- normalize conflicting metadata;
- merge programme governance with constitutional governance;
- reinterpret historical decisions;
- silently resolve conflicts;
- elevate implementation practice into constitutional doctrine;
- infer Authority from authorship, repository control, storage or administration;
- convert a drafting recommendation into an approved rule;
- treat approval, adoption, application, transition or completion as equivalent before their relationship is decided; or
- use this blueprint as evidence that any proposed rule has been approved.

## 9. Constitutional Questions

CGP-02C must prepare constitutional treatment or an explicit Founder-review question for each item below. This list supplies questions only.

### 9.1 Amendment and Classification

1. What constitutes a constitutional amendment?
2. How is an amendment distinguished from interpretation, refinement, correction, clerical housekeeping, status maintenance and programme change?
3. Which amendment classifications, if any, are constitutionally necessary?
4. What constitutional consequence determines the required depth of review?
5. Can any constitutional change be temporary or emergency-limited, and what Founder determination would that require?

### 9.2 Review and Conflict

6. What triggers governance review?
7. Is review periodic, event-triggered or both?
8. When is independent constitutional review required?
9. What must a conflict review record establish?
10. What evidence closes an escalated conflict without silently rewriting history?

### 9.3 Approval and Effect

11. What constitutes approval?
12. What constitutional capacity wording identifies the Founder’s exercise of Governance Authority?
13. What is the minimum approval evidence?
14. Who may record approval without that recording becoming approval Authority?
15. When is adoption distinct from approval?
16. When does approved governance become effective?
17. How do approval date, effective date, adoption date and application date differ, if at all?
18. What changes, if any, may occur during approval without constituting a substantive amendment?
19. What approval-reference convention, if any, is required?

### 9.4 Lifecycle and Current Status

20. Which governance-document lifecycle concepts require constitutional definition?
21. What is supersession, and how is its exact scope established?
22. What is retirement when no replacement instrument exists?
23. How are withdrawal and rejection distinguished from retirement or supersession?
24. What identifies the current authoritative instrument while preserving historical artefacts?
25. Which index or register is authoritative for each governance-document class?

### 9.5 Traceability and Validation

26. What information must every amendment trace preserve?
27. What dependent-document and cross-reference review is required before approval?
28. What is the minimum validation evidence for a constitutional change?
29. Which validation requirements are constitutional and which remain operational?
30. How must pending, deferred, rejected and unaffected matters be preserved?
31. What makes historical meaning and amendment effect objectively intelligible?

## 11. Drafting Completion Criteria

CGP-02C drafting is complete for Founder review only when:

- every included drafting subject has constitutional draft treatment or an explicit deferred Founder question;
- all 18 discovery gaps are mapped to proposed text, Founder decision material or an explicit justified deferral;
- all 8 discovery conflicts are dispositioned for Founder review without changing the affected source instruments;
- all 9 duplicate rule clusters are consolidated or explicitly retained with their source distinctions;
- every substantive proposed rule traces to a primary normative source, an explicit Founder question or both;
- observed practice is never presented as approved doctrine merely because it recurs;
- the constitutional/programme and constitutional/operational boundaries remain explicit;
- no new Platform Authority or accountability allocation is introduced;
- pending decisions remain pending unless the Founder expressly approves them;
- lifecycle, approval, adoption, effect, supersession, retirement, withdrawal and validation terminology is internally consistent within the draft;
- apparent tensions are surfaced rather than silently normalized;
- the Founder review package clearly distinguishes proposed text, recommendations, decisions requested and already approved doctrine;
- relative links, Markdown structure, whitespace and repository-scope validation pass; and
- the draft remains ready for independent review and attributable Founder approval without claiming either has occurred.

These criteria assess drafting completeness. They do not establish constitutional validity or approval.

## 12. Boundary Statement

This document creates no constitutional governance. It approves no amendment procedure, lifecycle, classification, review requirement, approval convention, adoption rule, supersession rule, retirement rule, validation rule or accountability allocation. No conflict or discovery gap is resolved here, and no existing governance status is changed.

CGP-02 remains incomplete until its constitutional standard has completed governed review and received attributable approval. Until then, the Platform Constitution, Platform Authority Model, CGP-01 and other approved instruments retain their existing authority and traceability without expansion by this blueprint.

With completion of CGP-02B.1, this blueprint enters governance freeze. Future changes require an approved governance change. The next authorized work is CGP-02C — Constitutional Amendment & Governance Review Standard.
