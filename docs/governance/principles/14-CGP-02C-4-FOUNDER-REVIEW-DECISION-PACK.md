## 3. ACFQ-01 — Meaning of Constitutional Adoption

**Issue:** What constitutes constitutional adoption?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Platform Constitution requires attributable Founder decisions and identifies effective-version treatment for amendment. The Authority Model requires Authority to be explicit, bounded and attributable. CGP-01 requires declared purpose and scope, reviewable change and historical intelligibility. No approved source currently supplies a canonical adoption definition.

**Proposed treatment:** Adoption is an explicit and attributable Governance Authority decision that incorporates identified, approved constitutional content into the authoritative constitutional governance of Tiizi within a declared scope. It creates no new Authority and cannot arise by implication.

**Option A — Approve the proposed treatment:** Preserve ACE-01 through ACE-04.

**Option B — Approve a narrower treatment:** Define adoption as explicit incorporation but defer Authority and anti-inference boundaries.

**Option C — Defer the question:** Approve no adoption propositions and prevent this package from advancing.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A closes the adoption-definition gap while preserving Authority and scope. Option B leaves predictable ambiguity about who establishes adoption and how implied adoption is prevented. Option C preserves the gap and blocks completion of this subject.

**Affected propositions:** ACE-01–ACE-04.

**Founder decision recorded:** Option A with amendment — Approved with amendment. Amend ACE-01 only as recorded in the Founder Decision Record.

## 4. ACFQ-02 — Approval and Adoption Distinction

**Issue:** When is adoption distinct from approval?

**Current approved doctrine:** Governance Authority approval must be explicit and attributable. The frozen Blueprint records inconsistent historical treatment of approval and adoption and requires their relationship to be decided rather than inferred.

**Proposed treatment:** Approval decides identified constitutional content. Adoption incorporates that approved content into the authoritative constitutional governance of Tiizi within a declared scope. Neither constitutional status implies or expands the other.

**Option A — Approve conceptual separation:** Preserve ACE-09 and ACE-10.

**Option B — Treat approval as adoption unless expressly excluded:** Collapse the two concepts by default.

**Option C — Defer the distinction:** Leave existing inconsistency unresolved.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A provides clear status boundaries and prevents approval from silently creating downstream status. Option B reduces documentary distinction but permits effect-bearing inference. Option C leaves future governance unable to communicate adoption consistently.

**Affected propositions:** ACE-09–ACE-10.

**Founder decision recorded:** Option A with amendment — Approved with amendment. Amend ACE-09 only as recorded in the Founder Decision Record.

## 5. ACFQ-03 — Meaning and Establishment of Constitutional Effect

**Issue:** When does adopted governance become constitutionally effective?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Platform Constitution and Authority Model require amendment records to identify effective-version treatment. Authority must be declared, bounded and attributable. No approved source defines constitutional effect generally.

**Proposed treatment:** Constitutional effect is the condition in which adopted content authoritatively governs within declared scope. Governance Authority must establish that effect expressly; use, implementation or representation cannot establish it.

**Option A — Approve the proposed treatment:** Preserve ACE-05 through ACE-08.

**Option B — Make effect automatic on adoption:** Preserve a definition of effect but require no separate express treatment.

**Option C — Defer effect doctrine:** Retain existing effective-version language without a general constitutional-effect definition.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A keeps effect attributable and bounded while allowing its date to coincide with adoption. Option B is simpler but risks silent effect where adoption records omit effect treatment. Option C preserves ambiguity around when adopted governance governs.

**Affected propositions:** ACE-05–ACE-08.

**Founder decision recorded:** Option A with amendment — Approved with amendment. Amend ACE-05 only as recorded in the Founder Decision Record.

## 6. ACFQ-04 — Constitutional Date Distinctions

**Issue:** How do approval date, adoption date, effective date and application date differ, if at all?

**Current approved doctrine:** Existing constitutional sources require attributable decision time and effective-version treatment, but the frozen Blueprint records inconsistent date terminology.

**Proposed treatment:** Define each date by the constitutional event it records. Dates may coincide but remain conceptually distinct. An application date is used only when expressly declared for a specified subject or scope and substitutes for no other status.

**Option A — Approve four distinct concepts:** Preserve ACE-12 through ACE-16.

**Option B — Approve three concepts and defer application date:** Preserve approval, adoption and effective dates; leave application-date meaning open.

**Option C — Collapse adoption and effective dates:** Treat adoption as necessarily effective on the adoption date while preserving approval and optional application dates.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A gives complete, attributable vocabulary without requiring different dates. Option B avoids premature application terminology but leaves one authorized question open. Option C simplifies records but prevents deliberate separation between adoption and effect.

**Affected propositions:** ACE-12–ACE-16.

**Founder decision recorded:** Option A with amendment — Approved with amendment. Clarify ACE-15 only; ACE-12 through ACE-14 and ACE-16 remain unchanged.

## 7. ACFQ-05 — Approval and Adoption in One Decision

**Issue:** May approval and adoption occur in one attributable decision while remaining conceptually distinct?

**Current approved doctrine:** Governance Authority decisions must be explicit and attributable. The Blueprint preserves inconsistent historical practice without treating it as doctrine.

**Proposed treatment:** One attributable Governance Authority decision may express both approval and adoption only when approval and adoption are separately identified and neither is implied.

**Option A — Permit one explicit combined decision:** Preserve ACE-11.

**Option B — Require separate decisions:** Approval and adoption must always be recorded as distinct governance decisions.

**Option C — Defer the question:** Permit no CGP-02 rule on combined treatment.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A preserves conceptual clarity without imposing unnecessary documentary separation. Option B maximizes formal separation but creates additional governance burden irrespective of consequence. Option C leaves future records without a stable boundary.

**Affected proposition:** ACE-11.

**Founder decision recorded:** Option A with amendment — Approved with amendment. Extend ACE-11 only as recorded in the Founder Decision Record.

## 8. ACFQ-06 — Minimum Anti-Inference Boundary

**Issue:** What minimum boundary prevents adoption, effect or dates from being supplied by implication?

**Current approved doctrine:** Authority cannot arise from title, access, possession or silent transfer. CGP-01 requires reviewable attributable change and historical intelligibility. The planning package expressly authorizes anti-inference treatment.

**Proposed treatment:** Constitutional status records communicate but do not create constitutional status. Administrative labels, repository treatment, publication, implementation, operational reliance, silence and passage of time cannot establish adoption or constitutional effect. Ambiguity remains unresolved rather than inferred, and historical records receive no retrospective constitutional status.

**Option A — Approve the complete anti-inference boundary:** Preserve ACE-04, ACE-08 and ACE-17 through ACE-21.

**Option B — Approve a narrower representation boundary:** Prohibit inference from labels and repository treatment but defer implementation, reliance and historical-record treatment.

**Option C — Defer anti-inference doctrine:** Depend only on general Authority principles.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A closes the major silent-status paths while remaining implementation-neutral. Option B reduces scope but leaves operational and historical inference risks. Option C leaves adoption and effect vulnerable to inconsistent administrative practice.

**Affected propositions:** Constitutional Anti-Inference Principle; ACE-04, ACE-08 and ACE-17–ACE-21.

**Founder decision recorded:** Option A with amendment — Approved with amendment. Add the Constitutional Anti-Inference Principle immediately before the existing detailed propositions and retain those propositions.

## 9. ACFQ-07 — Deferred Governance Boundary

It excludes lifecycle, classification, review, approval procedure, traceability, impact, conflict, supersession, retirement, validation, index synchronization, emergency governance, implementation, technical formats, new Authority and allocation.

**Option A — Confirm the complete boundary:** Preserve draft section 6.

**Option B — Narrow the boundary statement:** Retain only the principal adjacent-subject exclusions.

**Option C — Defer package completion:** Request a new authorization before changing scope.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Consequences:** Option A preserves the Founder-authorized boundary and makes non-effects reviewable. Option B risks ambiguity without authorizing added substance. Option C is required if the Founder wants scope expansion.

**Affected location:** Draft section 6.

**Founder decision recorded:** Option A with amendment — Approved with amendment. Retain the existing boundary and add only the Founder-directed concluding statement.
