## 1. Decision Boundary

This pack presents only Review Triggers and Proportionality questions. It does not ask the Founder to approve lifecycle governance, amendment classification, approval procedure, approval evidence, adoption effect, validation doctrine, traceability doctrine, implementation or repository workflow.

The questions are organized by constitutional function. Section A concerns the architecture of constitutional review triggers. Section B concerns proportionality and assurance boundaries. This organization changes no question, option, consequence, recommendation or affected proposition.

## 2. Section A — Review Trigger Architecture

### 2.1 RVQ-01 — Constitutional Bases for Governance Review

#### 2.1.1 Issue

Which constitutional circumstances should be recognized as bases for governance review without predetermining findings or creating a procedure.

#### 2.1.2 Current Approved Doctrine

Every constitutional rule must be objectively reviewable. Approved governance requires conflict discipline, attributable change and protection of constitutional integrity, but no canonical trigger set is approved.

#### 2.1.3 Proposed Treatment

RTP-01 through RTP-05 and RTP-14 identify potential constitutional change, conflict or ambiguity, absent reviewable basis and credible protection risks as bounded review bases.

#### 2.1.4 Options and Consequences

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | Approve the bounded consequence-based review bases proposed in RTP-03 through RTP-05. | Establishes why review may be warranted without creating thresholds, workflow or predetermined findings. |
| B | State only that constitutional governance must remain reviewable and omit named trigger bases. | Preserves the higher-order principle but leaves GAP-18 and cross-programme trigger ambiguity unresolved. |
| C | Leave review bases to each future constitutional instrument rather than establish a platform-wide set. | Preserves instrument-specific flexibility but leaves cross-programme trigger consistency unresolved. |

**Recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Propositions affected:** RTP-01 through RTP-05 and RTP-14.

**Decision required now:** Yes, for constitutional recognition of the proposed review bases.

### 2.2 RVQ-02 — Periodic Review Basis

#### 2.2.1 Issue

Whether review should arise from elapsed time, consequence-based triggers or a bounded combination of both.

#### 2.2.2 Current Approved Doctrine

Constitutional governance must remain reviewable and respond to real constitutional need. No universal periodic-review obligation, interval or re-review basis is approved.

#### 2.2.3 Proposed Treatment

RTP-06 rejects elapsed time as proof of deficiency while permitting an independently justified periodic basis where later approved governance identifies an enduring constitutional purpose.

#### 2.2.4 Options and Consequences

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | Use consequence-based triggers generally and permit periodic review only where separately justified by approved constitutional purpose. | Preserves progressive, minimum-sufficient governance without foreclosing justified periodic review. |
| B | Permit review only through identified consequence-based triggers. | Avoids unnecessary periodic burden but may not support instruments whose continuing purpose requires scheduled examination. |
| C | Require periodic review of every constitutional instrument. | Maximizes recurring examination but creates universal burden unrelated to constitutional consequence. |

**Recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Proposition affected:** RTP-06.

**Decision required now:** Yes for the conceptual basis; intervals and procedures remain deferred.

## 3. Section B — Review Proportionality and Assurance Boundaries

### 3.1 RVQ-03 — Consequence-Based Proportionality

#### 3.1.1 Issue

Which constitutional considerations should guide review proportionality without creating scores, tiers or thresholds.

#### 3.1.2 Current Approved Doctrine

CGP-01 establishes that formality, assurance and review burden follow constitutional consequence and identifies non-exhaustive consequence considerations. GVR-03 preserves that principle for CGP-02.

#### 3.1.3 Proposed Treatment

RTP-07 and RTP-08 carry the approved consequence considerations into the proposed review standard and expressly deny them scoring, tier or threshold effect.

#### 3.1.4 Options and Consequences

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | Preserve the CGP-01 consequence considerations as non-exhaustive constitutional judgement factors. | Provides consistent review guidance without creating a quantitative or procedural model. |
| B | State only the general consequence principle and omit named considerations. | Minimizes repetition but reduces consistency when judging review burden. |
| C | Incorporate the CGP-01 considerations only by normative cross-reference rather than restating them. | Avoids repetition but makes the review standard less self-contained while preserving the approved considerations. |

**Recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Propositions affected:** RTP-01, RTP-07 and RTP-08.

**Decision required now:** Yes for inclusion in the review standard; quantitative treatment remains excluded.

### 3.2 RVQ-04 — Objective Reviewability and Independent Review

#### 3.2.1 Issue

How Independent Review relates to the universal requirement that every constitutional rule be objectively reviewable.

#### 3.2.2 Current Approved Doctrine

Objective reviewability is required. FQ-03 deferred independent-review thresholds and review-depth design while preserving GVR-03 through GVR-05. No universal Independent Review requirement is approved.

#### 3.2.3 Proposed Treatment

RTP-10 through RTP-12 distinguish objective reviewability as a property of all constitutional rules from Independent Review as a potentially consequence-justified form of review whose exact thresholds and arrangements remain deferred.

#### 3.2.4 Options and Consequences

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | Preserve universal objective reviewability and recognize Independent Review as consequence-dependent rather than universal. | Maintains challenge capacity while avoiding an unsupported universal reviewer requirement. |
| B | Require Independent Review for every constitutional matter. | Maximizes separation but imposes uniform burden without regard to consequence. |
| C | Omit Independent Review from constitutional governance entirely. | Avoids new terminology but leaves the recorded FQ-03 dependency unresolved. |

**Recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Propositions affected:** RTP-10 through RTP-12.

**Decision required now:** Yes for the conceptual relationship; triggers, thresholds and reviewer arrangements remain deferred.

### 3.3 RVQ-05 — Constitutional Minima at Every Review Depth

#### 3.3.1 Issue

Which constitutional boundaries remain binding regardless of review burden or independence.

#### 3.3.2 Current Approved Doctrine

CGP-01 and GVR-04 prohibit proportionality from removing an approved constitutional minimum. GVR-05 preserves review/approval separation.

#### 3.3.3 Proposed Treatment

RTP-09 preserves applicable constitutional minima. RTP-13 and RTP-14 preserve review/approval separation and prevent triggers from predetermining constitutional outcomes.

#### 3.3.4 Options and Consequences

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | Preserve every applicable approved constitutional minimum at every review depth. | Allows proportionate burden without weakening Authority, attribution, precedence, meaning or binding protections. |
| B | State the non-reduction rule generally without naming the applicable minima in this package. | Preserves the constitutional boundary but provides less direct guidance within the review standard. |
| C | Distinguish universal minima from matter-specific protections while confirming that both remain binding whenever applicable. | Adds conceptual precision but introduces another distinction whose treatment is not yet necessary. |

**Recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Propositions affected:** RTP-09, RTP-13 and RTP-14.

**Decision required now:** Yes, as a non-reducible boundary on proportional review.
