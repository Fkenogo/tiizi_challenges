## 3. CRQ-01 — Meaning and Purpose

**Issue:** What is the constitutional meaning and purpose of governance-conflict review?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Platform Constitution requires conformity and objective review. The Authority Model preserves precedence, declared Authority and Governance Authority escalation. No approved source consolidates governance-conflict review as a bounded constitutional subject.

**Proposed treatment:** Define conflict through incompatible constitutional treatment within one scope, make the incompatibility objectively reviewable and preserve review as analytical until Governance Authority acts.

**Option A — Approve the complete treatment:** Preserve CRE-01 through CRE-05.

**Option B — Approve a narrower purpose:** Define review purpose without a conflict meaning or Authority boundary.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no conflict-review meaning in this package.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A consolidates existing conformity, reviewability, precedence and Authority boundaries without resolving a conflict. The alternatives leave either the subject or its constitutional non-effect ambiguous.

**Consequences:** Option A closes the bounded definition gap. Option B leaves inconsistent identification. Option C prevents completion of the Blueprint subject.

**Affected propositions:** CRE-01–CRE-05.

## 4. CRQ-02 — Conflict-Identification Basis

**Issue:** What constitutional conditions are sufficient to identify a governance conflict?

**Current approved doctrine:** Conflict cannot be resolved through preference or implied precedence, and uncertainty must remain explicit. No approved source defines the evidence required to identify a conflict rather than ambiguity, omission or difference.

**Proposed treatment:** Require identified governance, incompatible treatment, shared constitutional scope, governing basis and evidence; reject wording or preference alone; preserve uncertainty and non-effect.

**Option A — Approve the complete identification basis:** Preserve CRE-03, CRE-06 through CRE-09 and CRE-23.

**Option B — Use express contradiction only:** Exclude Authority, scope and constitutional-meaning incompatibility unless wording directly contradicts.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no conflict-identification basis.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A follows constitutional effect and governing relationships rather than documentary form while preserving evidence and uncertainty. The narrower option may miss genuine conflict, and deferral leaves inconsistent identification.

**Consequences:** Option A supports bounded objective review. Option B is simpler but constitutionally incomplete. Option C leaves GAP-14 untreated.

**Affected propositions:** CRE-03, CRE-06–CRE-09, CRE-23.

## 5. CRQ-03 — Identification, Review and Escalation Boundary

**Issue:** How must conflict identification remain distinct from conflict review and escalation?

**Current approved doctrine:** Review is analytical and unresolved Authority conflict escalates to Governance Authority. Identification, review and escalation are not yet consolidated as distinct conflict concepts.

**Proposed treatment:** Separate identification of the assertion, bounded examination of its basis and communication of an unresolved constitutional choice to Governance Authority.

**Option A — Approve complete separation:** Preserve CRE-04, CRE-09 through CRE-13.

**Option B — Merge identification and review:** Keep escalation distinct but treat identifying a conflict as completion of review.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no relationship among the three concepts.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A preserves different constitutional effects and prevents early analytical steps from becoming decisions. Merging or deferring would weaken reviewability and increase implied-resolution risk.

**Consequences:** Option A preserves a clear conceptual chain without workflow. Option B obscures examination quality. Option C leaves escalation evidence ambiguous.

**Affected propositions:** CRE-04, CRE-09–CRE-13.

## 6. CRQ-04 — Governance Authority Interaction

**Issue:** What relationship should exist between escalation and Governance Authority without creating another Authority?

**Current approved doctrine:** Governance Authority is the approved Platform Authority for constitutional governance, and unresolved Authority conflict must escalate to it. Review, recording and administration confer no Authority.

**Proposed treatment:** Reserve constitutional disposition to an explicit attributable Governance Authority act; make escalation communicative only; prohibit delegation, allocation and a Conflict Authority.

**Option A — Preserve Governance Authority exclusively:** Preserve CRE-05, CRE-12 through CRE-13 and CRE-17.

**Option B — Recognize a separate Conflict Authority:** Requires amendment of the Authority Model and exceeds this package.

**Option C — Defer:** Preserve general Authority rules without conflict-specific treatment.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A applies the existing Authority Model without extension or redistribution. Option B exceeds authorization, while Option C leaves escalation vulnerable to implied Authority.

**Consequences:** Option A preserves the approved Authority set. Option B cannot proceed within scope. Option C leaves a subject-specific Authority ambiguity.

**Affected propositions:** CRE-05, CRE-12–CRE-13, CRE-17.

## 7. CRQ-05 — Minimum Conflict-Review Evidence

**Issue:** What minimum evidence must a conflict-review record preserve?

**Current approved doctrine:** Constitutional rules and Authority actions must remain attributable and objectively reviewable. Existing sources require scope, basis, reasoning and uncertainty in related contexts but do not define a conflict-review minimum.

**Proposed treatment:** Preserve the asserted conflict, governance and status reviewed, scope, constitutional basis, evidence, reasoning, findings, uncertainty, limitations, date and attributable source.

**Option A — Approve the complete minimum:** Preserve CRE-06 through CRE-07, CRE-10, CRE-14 through CRE-16 and CRE-20.

**Option B — Approve a reduced minimum:** Require only the instruments, asserted conflict and finding.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no consolidated evidence minimum.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A consolidates existing attribution and reviewability requirements while remaining technology- and allocation-neutral. Reduced or deferred treatment would make the basis and limits of review difficult to examine.

**Consequences:** Option A supports objective review and historical intelligibility. Option B reduces evidence. Option C permits inconsistent conflict records.

**Affected propositions:** CRE-06–CRE-07, CRE-10, CRE-14–CRE-16, CRE-20.

## 8. CRQ-06 — Minimum Conflict-Disposition Evidence

**Issue:** What minimum evidence must be preserved where Governance Authority records a conflict disposition?

**Current approved doctrine:** Governance Authority action must be explicit, attributable and reviewable. Approval records, amendment traces, adoption records and constitutional-effect decisions retain distinct meanings.

**Proposed treatment:** Preserve the attributable decision, Governance Authority exercised, date, bounded conflict scope, treatment and rationale while keeping other constitutional records distinct.

**Option A — Approve bounded disposition evidence:** Preserve CRE-17 through CRE-18.

**Option B — Require only decision and date:** Defer scope, rationale and record separation.

**Option C — Defer:** Rely on later Governance Status and Decision Trace work.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A preserves attributable disposition without turning the record into approval, amendment, adoption or effect evidence. The alternatives weaken scope integrity or leave the disposition relationship undefined.

**Consequences:** Option A creates a bounded evidence minimum without procedure. Option B risks overbroad or ambiguous disposition. Option C leaves this subject incomplete.

**Affected propositions:** CRE-17–CRE-18.

## 9. CRQ-07 — Unresolved Conflict Treatment

**Issue:** How must an unresolved conflict remain represented without inferred resolution, precedence or constitutional change?

**Current approved doctrine:** Uncertainty remains explicit, genuine conflict cannot be resolved by preference and unresolved Authority conflict escalates to Governance Authority. No temporary constitutional-governance pathway exists.

**Proposed treatment:** Preserve unresolved status, competing treatments, reasons and known consequences; prohibit inferred precedence, compromise meaning, temporary rules or downstream status changes.

**Option A — Approve complete unresolved treatment:** Preserve CRE-08, CRE-12 and CRE-19 through CRE-21.

**Option B — Record unresolved status only:** Defer evidence and anti-inference consequences.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no unresolved-conflict treatment.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A applies explicit uncertainty and historical integrity without creating a temporary solution. The narrower and deferred alternatives leave material inference risks.

**Consequences:** Option A preserves the conflict honestly for attributable decision. Option B records less context. Option C permits inconsistent handling.

**Affected propositions:** CRE-08, CRE-12, CRE-19–CRE-21.

## 10. CRQ-08 — Anti-Inference Boundary

**Issue:** What anti-inference boundaries prevent conflict identification, review, escalation or recording from amending governance or creating Authority?

**Current approved doctrine:** Review does not establish governance, operational action cannot amend constitutional meaning and Authority cannot arise through implication.

**Proposed treatment:** Make every analytical and recording act non-constitutive; prevent status change, inferred consistency, implied Authority and retrospective conflict treatment.

**Option A — Approve the complete anti-inference boundary:** Preserve CRE-04 through CRE-05, CRE-09, CRE-13, CRE-18 through CRE-24.

**Option B — Approve no-amendment only:** Defer Authority, status, absence and historical protections.

**Option C — Defer:** Rely only on general CGP-02C.1 principles.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A applies existing Authority discipline to the conflict-specific paths that could otherwise imply change. Narrower or deferred treatment leaves known status and historical risks.

**Consequences:** Option A prevents silent governance change. Option B leaves adjacent inference paths. Option C leaves the subject-specific boundary uncertain.

**Affected propositions:** CRE-04–CRE-05, CRE-09, CRE-13, CRE-18–CRE-24.
