## 2. FQ-01 — Substance-over-label Treatment of Constitutional Change

### 2.1 Issue

Whether the constitutional effect of a change, rather than the label applied to it, determines whether constitutional amendment requirements apply.

### 2.2 Current Approved Doctrine

The Platform Constitution requires approved amendment for constitutional supersession. CGP-01 prohibits silent amendment, analogical scope expansion and operational practice creating constitutional meaning. Existing governance does not state one general “substance over label” formulation.

### 2.3 Proposed CGP-02C.1 Treatment

CHB-02 proposes that a change described as interpretation, refinement, correction, housekeeping, status maintenance, programme change or operational action cannot avoid amendment requirements when it changes constitutional meaning or effect.

### 2.4 Decision Options

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A — Founder-approved | Approve CHB-02’s substance-over-label principle. | Creates a general anti-evasion boundary while leaving detailed classifications for later drafting. Requires reviewers to assess constitutional effect rather than rely on document labels. |
| B | Limit CGP-02C.1 to the existing prohibition on silent amendment and remove the general effect test. | Minimizes new wording, but leaves ambiguity when a change is described as maintenance, interpretation or correction. |
| C | Defer the entire question and remove CHB-02 from CGP-02C.1. | Avoids deciding the boundary now, but prevents final approval of the current change-boundary package without a replacement treatment. |

**Founder decision:** APPROVED OPTION A.

**Decision date:** 2026-07-22.

**Recorded treatment:** CHB-02 is approved in principle for the Founder Approval Candidate. Detailed amendment classes, thresholds and procedures are not introduced here.

**Exact propositions affected:** CHB-02 directly; CHB-01 and CHB-03–CHB-04 interpretively.

**Explicitly deferred work package:** None for the principle-level decision; detailed classification remains under FQ-02.

## 3. FQ-02 — Amendment Classification

### 3.1 Issue

Whether and how later CGP-02 drafting should distinguish clarification, clerical housekeeping, status maintenance, interpretive change, material amendment and foundational change.

### 3.2 Current Approved Doctrine

Approved governance distinguishes constitutional amendment from operational practice and requires review proportionality. It does not approve a canonical amendment classification or housekeeping boundary.

### 3.3 Proposed CGP-02C.1 Treatment

CHB-04 distinguishes meaning-preserving clarification from meaning-changing action but explicitly defers classification and review treatment. CHB-02 supplies a proposed effect-based boundary without naming classes.

### 3.4 Decision Options

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A — Founder-approved | Defer classification design to the authorized Amendment Classification package while preserving CHB-02 and CHB-04. | Keeps CGP-02C.1 within scope and allows later classification to build on an approved change boundary. |
| B | Use one undifferentiated amendment treatment for every constitutional change. | Maximizes formal uniformity but may impose unnecessary burden and conflict with approved governance proportionality. |
| C | Authorize a later limited classification that separates meaning-preserving maintenance from meaning-changing amendment. | Narrows later design, but would require Founder review of the actual classes and their consequences before approval. |

**Founder decision:** APPROVED OPTION A.

**Decision date:** 2026-07-22.

**Recorded treatment:** Preserve CHB-02 and CHB-04. Do not introduce amendment classes, thresholds or procedures in CGP-02C.1.

**Exact propositions affected:** CHB-02 and CHB-04.

**Explicitly deferred work package:** Amendment Classification.

## 4. FQ-03 — Independent-review Thresholds

### 4.1 Issue

What constitutional consequence, if any, requires independent review or a particular depth of review.

### 4.2 Current Approved Doctrine

The Platform Constitution requires every constitutional rule to be objectively reviewable. CGP-01 requires governance burden to be proportionate to constitutional consequence and states that proportionality cannot remove an approved minimum. No universal independent-review threshold is approved.

### 4.3 Proposed CGP-02C.1 Treatment

GVR-03 and GVR-04 state consequence-based proportionality and preservation of constitutional minima. GVR-05 separates review from approval. The draft creates no threshold, tier or mandatory review form.

### 4.4 Decision Options

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A — Founder-approved | Defer threshold design to the later Review Triggers and Proportionality package. | Preserves the approved proportionality principle without prematurely fixing review levels or procedures. |
| B | Require independent review for every constitutional change. | Provides uniform assurance but may create disproportionate burden for low-consequence maintenance. |
| C | Leave independent review entirely discretionary. | Preserves flexibility but may produce inconsistent assurance for high-consequence amendments. |

**Founder decision:** APPROVED OPTION A.

**Decision date:** 2026-07-22.

**Recorded treatment:** Preserve GVR-03 through GVR-05 as principle-level propositions only. Do not introduce mandatory review levels, tiers or procedures in CGP-02C.1.

**Exact propositions affected:** GVR-03–GVR-05.

**Explicitly deferred work package:** Review Triggers and Proportionality.

## 5. FQ-04 — Canonical Founder/Governance Authority Capacity Wording

### 5.1 Issue

Whether approval records should use “Founder,” “Founder Governance Authority,” “Founder, exercising Governance Authority,” or Authority-category wording without naming the present actor.

### 5.2 Current Approved Doctrine

The Platform Authority Model establishes Governance Authority as the Authority category for constitutional amendments and states that its present source is Founder approval plus any later explicit governed delegation. Existing approval records use inconsistent capacity wording.

### 5.3 Proposed CGP-02C.1 Treatment

AMP-02 and GVR-05 name Governance Authority only. They do not select canonical Founder-capacity wording, allocate a future actor or define approval evidence.

### 5.4 Decision Options

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A — Founder-approved | Use “Founder, exercising Governance Authority” as the canonical present-capacity wording. | Clearly separates the current approving actor from the enduring Authority category and aligns with the approved CGP-01 metadata. |
| B | Use “Governance Authority” only in constitutional standards and decide actor wording separately for approval records. | Keeps constitutional text actor-neutral but leaves approval-record terminology unresolved. |
| C | Preserve multiple context-dependent formulations. | Avoids retrospective terminology changes but perpetuates ambiguity across approval evidence. |

**Founder decision:** APPROVED OPTION A.

**Decision date:** 2026-07-22.

**Recorded treatment:** Future approval records use “Founder, exercising Governance Authority.” Constitutional standards continue to use “Governance Authority.” Historical approval records remain unchanged, and no approval-record procedure is introduced in CGP-02C.1.

**Exact propositions affected:** AMP-02 and GVR-05.

**Explicitly deferred work package:** Approval Governance for any approval-record procedure.

## 6. FQ-05 — Temporary or Emergency Constitutional Governance

### 6.1 Issue

Whether Tiizi may establish temporary or emergency constitutional governance, distinct from a temporary operational response within already approved Authority.

### 6.2 Current Approved Doctrine

The Platform Authority Model permits operational urgency to support a temporary, attributable response within approved Authority but states that urgency does not create a permanent constitutional exception. No temporary constitutional-amendment model is approved.

### 6.3 Proposed CGP-02C.1 Treatment

AMP-05 rejects urgency as a source of constitutional amendment. GVR-07 and GVR-08 preserve precedence and conflict escalation. CHB-06 requires missing constitutional meaning to return to attributable governance. No emergency amendment rule is proposed.

### 6.4 Decision Options

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A — Founder-approved | Preserve only temporary operational response within existing approved Authority; do not recognize emergency constitutional governance. | Maintains the current boundary and avoids a second constitutional pathway, but may require ordinary amendment even under urgent constitutional need. |
| B | Authorize later drafting of a bounded temporary constitutional-governance concept. | Could address urgent constitutional gaps but creates significant questions about Authority, effect, expiry, review and historical integrity. |
| C | Prohibit any temporary response associated with constitutional uncertainty. | Maximizes formal restraint but conflicts with the Authority Model’s existing allowance for temporary operational response within approved Authority. |

**Founder decision:** APPROVED OPTION A.

**Decision date:** 2026-07-22.

**Recorded treatment:** Temporary operational responses may occur only within already-approved Authority. No temporary or emergency constitutional-governance pathway is created. Preserve AMP-05, GVR-07, GVR-08 and CHB-06.

**Exact propositions affected:** AMP-05, GVR-07–GVR-08 and CHB-06.

**Explicitly deferred work package:** Any future proposal requires a separately authorized CGP-02 package.
