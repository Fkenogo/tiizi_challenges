## 3. Apparent Tensions and Ambiguities

### CGP-XR-01 — Interpretation and Precedence

**Apparent tension:** A platform-wide principles document could be mistaken for authority to reinterpret or displace earlier instruments.

**Treatment:** CGP-01 explicitly limits interpretation to silence or ambiguity within approved boundaries. It cannot override EOG-01 through EOG-05 or any other approved instrument without attributable amendment.

**Adoption status:** The interpretive scope was confirmed through founder approval.

### CGP-XR-02 — Community Preference and Platform Obligation

**Apparent tension:** Community-first governance could be read as allowing local preference to prevail over Platform accountability, privacy, safety or security.

**Treatment:** CGP-01 expressly makes those approved obligations binding. Governance enables, protects and sustains participation, but participation is not an exception to constitutional protection.

**Adoption status:** No conflict requiring amendment is identified.

### CGP-XR-03 — Minimum Governance and High-impact Assurance

**Apparent tension:** Minimum sufficient governance could be mistaken for reduced auditability or relaxed Authority controls.

**Treatment:** “Sufficient” includes every approved minimum. Complexity requires justification, while high constitutional consequence may require stronger attribution, review and preservation.

**Adoption status:** Founder approval confirmed that proportionality cannot reduce an approved constitutional minimum.

### CGP-XR-04 — Stewardship Council and Group Governance Body

**Apparent tension:** EOG-02 uses Stewardship Council while the later C-GRP-08 amendment uses Group Governance Body as the canonical class and permits multiple bodies.

**Treatment:** Stewardship Council remains the approved alias and historical term. CGP-01 references the later canonical class without retrospectively rewriting EOG-02. Every body remains subordinate to the singular Accountable Steward relationship.

**Adoption status:** No amendment to EOG-02 is implied.

### CGP-XR-05 — Governance Maturity and Governance Health

**Apparent tension:** EOG-02 recognizes Governance Maturity and Group Governance Health as future conceptual areas. CGP-01 could accidentally turn them into assessment models.

**Treatment:** CGP-01 treats maturity as non-measured conceptual adaptability and creates no Group Health model, level, score, badge, threshold, compliance rule or incentive.

**Adoption status:** Later governance remains separately required for any new model.

### CGP-XR-06 — Progressive Governance and Existing Obligations

**Apparent tension:** Progressive evolution could be used to postpone privacy, safety, Authority, stewardship or other already approved requirements.

**Treatment:** CGP-01 permits later refinement only when constitutional need evolves, prohibits speculative governance and does not allow delayed compliance with an approved obligation.

**Adoption status:** No conflict requiring amendment is identified.

### CGP-XR-07 — Operational Governance Boundary

**Apparent tension:** Defining operational governance could be mistaken for allocating Operational Authority or prescribing procedures.

**Treatment:** CGP-01 defines constitutional governance as what and why, and operational governance as how. Operational governance derives authority from constitutional governance and cannot create constitutional meaning. Authority allocation and procedures remain deferred.

### CGP-XR-08 — Potential Domains and Implied Programme Approval

**Apparent tension:** Listing future governance areas could be read as approving, requiring or prioritising them.

**Treatment:** Approved CGP-01 labels them Potential Future Governance Domains. Each requires independent constitutional-necessity review and attributable approval; the list authorizes no structure, programme, committee or standard.

**Adoption status:** No future programme is approved by CGP-01.

**Adoption status:** Founder approval confirmed that the definition remains philosophical.
