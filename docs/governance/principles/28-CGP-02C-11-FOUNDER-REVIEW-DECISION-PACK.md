## 3. GIQ-01 — Governance-Index Purpose and Technology-Neutral Meaning

### Issue

Whether a governance index should be constitutionally treated as a technology-neutral documentary representation that makes governance and its attributable position identifiable without becoming the source of status.

### Current approved doctrine

The Blueprint requires Governance Index and Status Integrity and asks what identifies the current authoritative instrument. CGP-01 requires historical intelligibility and technology-neutral constitutional governance, while completed anti-inference doctrine denies index entries independent constitutional effect.

### Proposed treatment

GSI-01 through GSI-03 define a governance index by documentary function, state its constitutional purpose and preserve technology neutrality.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve GSI-01 through GSI-03.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies a different bounded purpose or meaning while preserving non-constitutive and technology-neutral treatment.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed treatment; Blueprint deliverable 15 remains incomplete.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A gives the Blueprint subject a precise constitutional purpose without constitutionalizing any current file or system. It supports identification of governance while preserving the existing rule that documentary representation cannot create status.

### Consequences

- Option A establishes a bounded, implementation-neutral foundation for the subject.
- Option B requires corresponding changes throughout the representation and integrity sections.
- Option C leaves the purpose of governance indexes constitutionally undefined.

### Affected propositions

GSI-01–GSI-03.

## 4. GIQ-02 — Status Representation and Constitutional-Status Boundary

### Issue

Whether status representations should communicate only attributable constitutional status and remain constitutionally distinct from the act that establishes status.

### Current approved doctrine

Approval Governance and Adoption and Constitutional Effect distinguish constitutional acts from their records and representations. The Platform Authority Model prevents recording, presentation and administration from acquiring Governance Authority.

### Proposed treatment

GSI-04 through GSI-09 define status representation, bound it to attributable sources and preserve the reciprocal rule that representation neither creates nor erases constitutional status.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve GSI-04 through GSI-09.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the proposed relationship while preserving attributable status and no inferred Authority.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed status/representation treatment.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A consolidates the recorder-versus-governance distinction already present across C.3 and C.4. It also prevents both accurate and defective documentary treatment from being mistaken for a constitutional act.

### Consequences

- Option A creates a coherent status-representation boundary.
- Option B requires cross-package consistency review against approval, adoption and effect.
- Option C leaves the central distinction of deliverable 15 unresolved.

### Affected propositions

GSI-04–GSI-09.

## 5. GIQ-03 — Current Constitutional Baseline Integrity

### Issue

What minimum constitutional information a representation claiming to identify the current baseline should communicate.

### Current approved doctrine

The Blueprint requires identification of the current authoritative instrument while preserving historical artefacts. Existing CGP-02 doctrine distinguishes attributable current status from pending, deferred, historical and other non-current treatments and requires uncertainty to remain explicit.

### Proposed treatment

GSI-10 through GSI-12 require subject, current status, scope and source identification; preserve relevant status distinctions; and prohibit inferred current status where evidence is incomplete or inconsistent.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve GSI-10 through GSI-12.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the proposed minimum while preserving attribution, status distinction and explicit uncertainty.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed current-baseline integrity treatment.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A states the smallest constitutional information needed for a current-baseline claim to remain reviewable without defining a format or index implementation. It prevents the representation from filling gaps with inferred status or precedence.

### Consequences

- Option A supplies an objective, technology-neutral integrity minimum.
- Option B requires amendments to the stale and inconsistent representation treatment.
- Option C leaves current-baseline identification dependent on undocumented practice.

### Affected propositions

GSI-10–GSI-12.

### Proposed treatment

GSI-13 through GSI-15 preserve historically accurate references, distinguish current representation from historical rewriting and retain source-context terminology.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve GSI-13 through GSI-15.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the current/historical relationship while preserving attribution and intelligibility.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed historical-reference treatment.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A allows current documentary status to remain accurate without making history appear to have used later decisions or vocabulary. It directly preserves the historical intelligibility established in C.10.

### Consequences

- Option B requires consistency review against Historical Preservation.
- Option C risks either stale current representation or retrospective rewriting.

### Affected propositions

GSI-13–GSI-15.

## 7. GIQ-05 — Stale, Incomplete and Inconsistent Representations

### Issue

How stale, incomplete or inconsistent status representations should be constitutionally identified without prescribing correction or synchronization procedures.

### Current approved doctrine

Approval, adoption, traceability, conflict and historical-preservation doctrine require missing or conflicting evidence to remain explicit and prohibit silent status inference. Conflict Review and Escalation already governs unresolved constitutional judgment without supplying a correction workflow.

### Proposed treatment

GSI-16 distinguishes the three documentary defects. GSI-17 makes the defect non-constitutive, and GSI-18 preserves unresolved constitutional judgment within the existing conflict boundary.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve GSI-08 and GSI-16 through GSI-18.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the proposed defect distinctions or conflict boundary without defining operational synchronization.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed treatment.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A makes documentary defects constitutionally visible without allowing them to alter governance or silently determine their own correction. It reuses the existing conflict boundary only where attributable sources cannot resolve the inconsistency.

### Consequences

- Option A creates a bounded integrity treatment without operational procedure.
- Option B may require consistency review with C.7 and the current-baseline minimum.
- Option C leaves GAP-17 and CON-08 materially unresolved.

### Affected propositions

GSI-08, GSI-16–GSI-18.

### Proposed treatment

GSI-19 through GSI-21 define the relationship among documentary functions, permit one or multiple representations and preserve independent attribution without inferred canonicality.

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve GSI-19 through GSI-21.

- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed relationship treatment.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

Option A addresses the observed variety of documentary mechanisms without constitutionalizing repository vocabulary. It permits different representational arrangements while requiring every status claim to trace back to constitutional sources.

### Consequences

- Option B may require a narrower definition of documentary function.
- Option C leaves indexes, registers and manifests constitutionally ambiguous.

### Affected propositions

GSI-19–GSI-21.

## 10. GIQ-08 — Technology Neutrality, Authority and Allocation Boundary

GSI-22–GSI-23.

## 11. GIQ-09 — Actual-Index, Correction and Deferred-Governance Boundary

The Blueprint prohibits drafting from normalizing conflicting metadata, reinterpreting historical decisions or elevating implementation practice. C.5 through C.10 preserve comparable retrospective and deferred-governance boundaries.

### Proposed treatment

GSI-24 prohibits actual designation, named correction, validation and retrospective status assignment. Draft section 10 preserves all excluded governance.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve GSI-24 and draft section 10.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the boundary without selecting an actual mechanism, resolving a named status or introducing Validation Rules.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed boundary and leave the package incomplete.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A keeps this package constitutional and prospective while leaving documentary correction and validation to separately governed treatment. It prevents an abstract integrity standard from silently changing the status of existing artefacts.

### Consequences

- Option A preserves the authorized boundary and later Blueprint sequence.
- Option B requires complete scope and traceability revalidation.
- Option C permits retrospective and operational leakage.

### Affected propositions

GSI-24; draft section 10.
