## 3. VLQ-01 — Constitutional Meaning and Purpose of Validation

### Issue

Whether constitutional validation should be defined as a bounded evidentiary examination of conformity with applicable constitutional requirements, with an objective and technology-neutral purpose.

### Current approved doctrine

Blueprint GAP-16 records no canonical constitutional validation minimum. CGP-01 requires objective, technology-neutral and minimum-sufficient governance, while C.1 requires constitutional rules to remain objectively reviewable.

### Proposed treatment

VLR-01 through VLR-03 define constitutional validation, its purpose and its Tiizi-specific, attributable and technology-neutral boundary.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve VLR-01 through VLR-03.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies a different bounded meaning or purpose while preserving technology neutrality and the constitutional/operational boundary.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed treatment; GAP-16 and Blueprint deliverable 16 remain incomplete.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A gives GAP-16 a precise constitutional subject without importing existing validation practice or a generic framework. It defines what constitutional validation examines while leaving procedure, tools and actors outside Tiizi constitutional doctrine.

### Founder decision

**Option A — Approved.**

**Decision date:** 2026-07-25

### Consequences

- Option A establishes a bounded foundation for the remaining propositions.
- Option B requires corresponding changes throughout the minimum-evidence and non-effect sections.
- Option C leaves constitutional validation undefined and prevents substantive Blueprint completion.

### Affected propositions

VLR-01–VLR-03.

## 4. VLQ-02 — Validation and Governance Review Boundary

### Issue

Whether validation should remain distinct from Governance Review while permitting evidence and findings from either treatment to inform the other.

### Current approved doctrine

C.1 and C.2C define Governance Review, review triggers, proportionality, objective reviewability and Independent Review. They do not define constitutional validation or permit review findings to substitute for approval.

### Proposed treatment

VLR-04 through VLR-06 distinguish review of constitutional content and consequence from validation of evidence supporting conformity, preserve reciprocal informational use and leave existing review doctrine unchanged.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve VLR-04 through VLR-06.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the relationship while preserving existing Review doctrine and no procedural allocation.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed distinction.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A prevents recurring review practice from silently absorbing Validation Rules while allowing coherent evidence flow between the subjects. It preserves all trigger, proportionality and Independent Review questions in their existing constitutional location.

### Founder decision

**Option A — Approved.**

**Decision date:** 2026-07-25

### Consequences

- Option A creates a clear reciprocal boundary without duplication.
- Option B requires consistency review against C.1 and C.2C.
- Option C risks review and validation being treated as interchangeable.

### Affected propositions

VLR-04–VLR-06.

### Proposed treatment

VLR-07 through VLR-09 apply those boundaries to validation and limit a validation declaration to the supported validation conclusion.

### Options

- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed constitutional-act boundary.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

Option A prevents a “passed” validation label from becoming a substitute constitutional act. It preserves the existing Governance Authority chain and ensures validation does not acquire approval, adoption or effect by implication.
