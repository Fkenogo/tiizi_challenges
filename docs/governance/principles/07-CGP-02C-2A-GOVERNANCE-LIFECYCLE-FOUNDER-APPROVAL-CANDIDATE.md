## 1. Constitutional Purpose of Governance Lifecycle

**GLC-01.** Governance Lifecycle provides a constitutional vocabulary for distinguishing the governed position of constitutional content over time. Its purpose is to support clarity, attribution and historical intelligibility without allowing a lifecycle label to create constitutional Authority or effect.

**GLC-02.** Governance Lifecycle is conceptual and technology-neutral. It does not prescribe workflow, repository process, storage, versioning, retention, automation or implementation.

## 2. Conceptual Distinctions Among Lifecycle Terms

The terms below are distinguished for Founder review. Their inclusion in this draft does not approve a procedure, mandatory sequence or constitutional effect beyond the numbered propositions.

### 2.1 Draft

**GLC-03.** Draft denotes proposed constitutional content that has not received attributable constitutional approval. Drafting, circulation, reliance, review or completion of a draft does not make its content approved governance.

### 2.2 Review

**GLC-04.** Review denotes constitutional examination of content, boundaries or evidence. Review does not itself approve, amend, adopt or make constitutional content effective.

### 2.3 Approval

**GLC-05.** Approval denotes an explicit and attributable decision of Governance Authority concerning identified constitutional content and scope. This conceptual distinction does not define an approval procedure, required evidence, effective date or downstream effect.

### 2.4 Adoption

**GLC-06.** Adoption is a lifecycle concept distinct from drafting, review and approval. Whether adoption is required for any instrument, how it relates to approval and what constitutional effect it has remain deferred to later approved governance.

### 2.5 Application

**GLC-07.** Application is a lifecycle concept distinct from the decision that approves constitutional content. Its exact meaning, necessity and effect remain deferred; the term must not be used to imply implementation, amendment or constitutional effect that has not been approved.

### 2.6 Transition

**GLC-08.** Transition denotes an attributable movement or recorded change between governed lifecycle positions. A transition record may communicate a change but does not independently establish the Authority, approval or constitutional effect on which that change depends.

### 2.7 Completion

**GLC-09.** Completion denotes that the declared scope of a governance work package or evidence package has been fulfilled. Completion does not by itself establish approval, adoption, application or constitutional effect.

## 3. Document Status and Constitutional Effect

**GLC-10.** Document status and constitutional effect are distinct. A title, filename, location, label, dashboard entry or programme status cannot establish constitutional effect that has not been explicitly and attributably governed.

**GLC-11.** Lifecycle terminology must not create competing constitutional truth. Where a status representation conflicts with the attributable governing source, the representation must not be treated as an equal Authority.

**GLC-12.** Governance Lifecycle must preserve historical intelligibility. A later lifecycle position must not silently conceal that an earlier draft, review position or governing position existed, while technical preservation mechanisms remain outside this standard.

**GLC-13.** A lifecycle concept that remains pending, undefined or deferred must not acquire constitutional meaning through repetition, convenience, local practice or analogy.

## 4. Explicitly Deferred Lifecycle Questions

This draft does not determine:

- a mandatory lifecycle sequence;
- whether every instrument requires separate approval, adoption, application, transition or completion events;
- approval procedure or approval evidence;
- adoption effect;
- effective dates or date relationships;
- application procedure or implementation consequence;
- transition workflow or record format;
- amendment classification;
- review triggers, thresholds or proportionality;
- supersession;
- retirement, withdrawal or rejection;
- validation doctrine;
- traceability doctrine;
- historical-preservation mechanisms;
- roles, permissions or accountability allocations; or
- repository or technical implementation.

These matters require their separately authorized CGP-02 packages or explicit Founder decisions. No answer is created by omission, observed practice or this deferral list.
