# CGP-02C.8 — Drafting Notes

**Subject:** Supersession Rules

**Status:** Founder Review drafting evidence; not constitutional governance

**Date:** 2026-07-24

## 1. Purpose

These notes record architectural observations, deferred constitutional questions and assumptions deliberately avoided while drafting the bounded Supersession Rules package.

## 2. Architectural Observations

### 2.1 Replacement Relationship

The draft treats supersession as a relationship among attributable Governance Authority, identified source governance, identified replacement governance and declared scope. It does not use chronology or documentary form as a substitute for that relationship.

### 2.2 Amendment Separation

SSR-06 distinguishes changing constitutional content or meaning from establishing replacement. This preserves the constitutional-change boundary without redefining amendment.

### 2.3 Adoption Separation

SSR-08 preserves adoption as incorporation into authoritative constitutional governance and supersession as a separately attributable replacement treatment. No adoption or constitutional-effect rule is added.

### 2.4 Conflict-Disposition Separation

SSR-09 applies the explicit deferral in CGP-02C.7. A conflict disposition may resolve incompatibility within its scope without silently establishing which governance is replaced.

### 2.5 Later Status Treatments

SSR-10 identifies only that retirement, withdrawal and rejection are not equivalent to supersession. Their meanings and rules remain wholly deferred to Blueprint deliverable 13.

### 2.6 Historical Integrity

The draft distinguishes loss of a current governing position from erasure or retrospective invalidation. Historical mechanisms, storage, versioning and archival treatment remain excluded.

### 2.7 Evidence without Implementation

SSR-19 through SSR-21 identify constitutional information categories required for attributable evidence. They prescribe no document schema, reference format, repository process or responsible actor.

No drafting recommendation is an approval.

## 4. Assumptions Deliberately Avoided

The draft does not assume that:

- a later document supersedes an earlier document;
- an amendment supersedes the amended source;
- approval or adoption establishes replacement;
- a conflict disposition necessarily supersedes one treatment;
- supersession is retirement, withdrawal or rejection;
- an index, status label or repository location establishes currentness;
- dependent governance changes automatically;
- an operational actor may establish supersession;
- a historical instrument is already superseded;
- a current-baseline index exists;
- validation practice is constitutional doctrine; or
- Blueprint order authorizes a successor package.

## 5. Drafting Boundary

The draft addresses one constitutional subject only. It:

- supersedes no governance;
- determines no existing instrument status;
- creates no Platform Authority;
- allocates no accountability, role or permission;
- defines no retirement, withdrawal, rejection, validation or index rule;
- creates no implementation or repository procedure;
- creates no adoption or constitutional effect; and
- authorizes no successor work package.
