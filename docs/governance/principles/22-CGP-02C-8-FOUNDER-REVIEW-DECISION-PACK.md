## 3. SSQ-01 — Constitutional Meaning and Purpose

**Issue:** Whether supersession should be treated as an explicit attributable replacement relationship within a declared constitutional scope.

**Current approved doctrine:** Approved governance requires attributable constitutional change, preserves precedence and historical intelligibility, and rejects implied Authority. No approved instrument supplies a complete general supersession treatment.

**Proposed treatment:** SSR-01 through SSR-05 define a bounded replacement relationship that changes the current governing position without erasing historical governance or silently creating another constitutional treatment.

**Options:**

- **Option A — Approve:** Accept SSR-01 through SSR-05 as drafted.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies a different bounded meaning or purpose without expanding the work package.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed treatment and preserve supersession as unresolved.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A consolidates attribution, scope, precedence and historical-integrity requirements already present in approved governance while leaving every adjacent constitutional treatment separate. It supplies the minimum stable meaning required by Blueprint deliverable 12 without superseding any instrument.

**Consequences:**

- Option A provides a general, bounded constitutional meaning for later governance.
- Option B requires revalidation against Authority, history and scope boundaries.
- Option C leaves GAP-11 unresolved and prevents completion of this Blueprint subject.

**Affected propositions:** SSR-01–SSR-05.

## 4. SSQ-02 — Supersession and Amendment

**Issue:** Whether amendment and supersession must remain distinct constitutional treatments.

**Current approved doctrine:** Constitutional change is governed by effect rather than label, and amendment must be explicit and attributable. Existing governance does not establish that amendment automatically replaces prior governance.

**Proposed treatment:** SSR-06 separates content or meaning change from the attributable replacement relationship; SSR-07, SSR-13 and SSR-21 preserve related approval and record boundaries.

**Options:**

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve the distinction and permit both treatments in one attributable decision only where each is expressly established.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies a different relationship between amendment and supersession.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Leave their relationship unresolved.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A prevents amendment labels or traces from silently establishing replacement while allowing Governance Authority to establish both treatments expressly where constitutionally intended. This follows the existing substance-over-label and attribution disciplines.

**Consequences:**

- Option A preserves independent constitutional meaning and traceability.
- Option B may require changes to established amendment boundaries.
- Option C leaves uncertainty about whether amendment displaces earlier governance.

**Affected propositions:** SSR-05–SSR-07, SSR-13, SSR-21.

## 5. SSQ-03 — Supersession and Adoption

**Issue:** Whether adoption should automatically identify and displace earlier governance.

**Current approved doctrine:** Adoption incorporates approved content into the authoritative constitutional governance of Tiizi. Existing approved treatment does not make adoption and replacement synonymous.

**Proposed treatment:** SSR-08 keeps adoption and supersession distinct; SSR-13 permits distinct treatments to be recorded together while SSR-21 preserves record separation.

**Options:**

- **Option A — Approve:** Adoption does not establish supersession unless attributable Governance Authority expressly establishes both.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies a different bounded relationship.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Leave the relationship unresolved.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A preserves the approved meaning of adoption and prevents incorporation of new governance from silently identifying displaced governance or replacement scope. It also permits an attributable decision to establish both acts without merging them.

**Consequences:**

- Option A preserves approval, adoption and supersession as independently attributable.
- Option B risks reopening the approved adoption boundary.
- Option C leaves current-baseline replacement ambiguous after adoption.

**Affected propositions:** SSR-05, SSR-08, SSR-13, SSR-21.

## 6. SSQ-04 — Supersession and Conflict Disposition

**Issue:** Whether an attributable conflict disposition should automatically supersede one of the conflicting treatments.

**Current approved doctrine:** Conflict identification, review and escalation are analytical, and disposition requires Governance Authority. CGP-02C.7 expressly defers supersession.

**Proposed treatment:** SSR-09 requires supersession to be expressly established even where Governance Authority records a conflict disposition.

**Options:**

- **Option A — Approve:** Keep conflict disposition and supersession distinct unless both are expressly established.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder identifies circumstances in which disposition inherently supersedes governance.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Preserve the relationship as unresolved.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A preserves CGP-02C.7’s approved boundary and prevents a decision resolving incompatibility from being enlarged into an unstated replacement relationship. It maintains attributable scope without limiting Governance Authority from expressly establishing supersession.

**Consequences:**

- Option A prevents inferred replacement from conflict evidence.
- Option B would require careful reconciliation with CRE-09, CRE-18 and CRE-21.
- Option C leaves a known integration gap between deliverables 11 and 12.

**Affected propositions:** SSR-05, SSR-09, SSR-13, SSR-21.

## 7. SSQ-05 — Retirement, Withdrawal and Rejection Boundary

**Issue:** Whether supersession should be treated as equivalent to retirement, withdrawal or rejection.

**Current approved doctrine:** The frozen Blueprint treats Supersession Rules and Retirement, Withdrawal and Rejection Rules as separate sequential deliverables.

**Proposed treatment:** SSR-10 states only the distinction and defers the later concepts without defining them.

**Options:**

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve the distinction and explicit deferral.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies a different boundary while retaining one constitutional subject.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the distinction and leave potential overlap unresolved.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A respects the frozen Blueprint architecture and avoids importing later lifecycle or status rules into Supersession Rules. It establishes only the boundary necessary to prevent silent conflation.

**Consequences:**

- Option A preserves deliverable 13 for separate planning and review.
- Option B risks scope overlap requiring planning revalidation.
- Option C permits ambiguity between replacement and later status treatments.

**Affected propositions:** SSR-05, SSR-10; draft section 7.

## 8. SSQ-06 — Governance Authority and Attribution

**Issue:** Which Authority establishes supersession and whether documentary or operational actors acquire Authority through their involvement.

**Current approved doctrine:** Governance Authority governs constitutional amendment and interpretation; authorship, recording, administration and implementation do not confer Authority.

**Proposed treatment:** SSR-11 reserves supersession to existing Governance Authority, SSR-12 rejects actor-based inference, SSR-13 preserves independently attributable treatments and SSR-19 requires Authority evidence.

**Options:**

- **Option A — Approve:** Use existing Governance Authority and create no Supersession Authority or actor allocation.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies a different treatment consistent with the approved Authority Model.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Leave the Authority basis unresolved and do not complete the subject.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A applies the existing Authority Model directly and prevents documentary custody, administration or implementation from becoming constitutional power. No new Authority or accountability allocation is needed.

**Consequences:**

- Option A preserves the eleven-authority model and attribution discipline.
- Option B may require an attributable amendment to higher-order Authority governance.
- Option C leaves supersession constitutionally inoperable and vulnerable to inference.

**Affected propositions:** SSR-01, SSR-03, SSR-11–SSR-13, SSR-19.

## 9. SSQ-07 — Bounded Supersession Scope

**Issue:** What must identify and constrain the constitutional scope of supersession.

**Current approved doctrine:** Constitutional changes and Authority must remain bounded and attributable. No general “latest document wins” rule is approved.

**Proposed treatment:** SSR-14 through SSR-17 require source, replacement and exact scope; protect unaffected provisions; reject chronology alone; and prevent automatic changes to dependent governance.

**Options:**

- **Option A — Approve:** Require explicit source, replacement and bounded-scope relationships.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies alternative minimum scope treatment.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Leave scope rules unresolved.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A applies constitutional proportionality and bounded Authority while avoiding inferred full-document replacement or automatic dependent-governance change. It supports partial and multi-instrument supersession without defining implementation.

**Consequences:**

- Option A provides objectively reviewable replacement boundaries.
- Option B must still prevent implied scope expansion.
- Option C creates substantial current-baseline and historical ambiguity.

**Affected propositions:** SSR-14–SSR-17, SSR-20.

## 10. SSQ-08 — Historical Preservation and Minimum Evidence

**Issue:** What historical implications and evidence must accompany attributable supersession.

**Current approved doctrine:** Constitutional history must remain intelligible, and attributable constitutional change requires a durable decision trace. No canonical supersession evidence minimum currently exists.

**Proposed treatment:** SSR-18 preserves historically applicable governance; SSR-19 through SSR-21 identify attribution, scope, rationale and record relationships; SSR-23 and SSR-24 prevent historical inference.

**Options:**

- **Option A — Approve:** Accept the technology-neutral historical and evidence minimum.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the minimum categories without adding implementation.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Leave evidence and historical treatment unresolved.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A supplies enough attributable evidence to make replacement objectively reviewable while preserving historically applicable governance. It does not prescribe storage, indexing, versioning or repository mechanics.

**Consequences:**

- Option A supports constitutional continuity and later auditability.
- Option B may strengthen or reduce evidence but requires traceability revalidation.
- Option C risks ambiguous replacement claims and loss of historical intelligibility.

**Affected propositions:** SSR-02, SSR-04, SSR-14, SSR-18–SSR-21, SSR-23–SSR-24.

## 11. SSQ-09 — Anti-Inference and Deferred Governance

**Issue:** Which acts must be prevented from silently establishing supersession and which later subjects must remain outside the package.

**Current approved doctrine:** Constitutional status arises only through attributable Governance Authority, and operational, documentary or repository behavior cannot create constitutional meaning.

**Proposed treatment:** SSR-22 through SSR-24 provide explicit anti-inference protections, while SSR-10, SSR-16–SSR-17 and draft section 7 preserve deferred subjects.

**Options:**

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve the anti-inference and deferred-governance boundary exactly as drafted.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the boundary without adding another constitutional subject.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed protections and preserve all treatment as unresolved.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A prevents labels, indexes, repository acts and implementation from acquiring constitutional force while protecting later Blueprint subjects from premature treatment. It is the minimum boundary needed to keep documentary and operational representation distinct from attributable constitutional status.

**Consequences:**

- Option A preserves Authority discipline, history and Blueprint sequencing.
- Option B requires a fresh boundary and dependency review.
- Option C leaves supersession vulnerable to documentary and operational inference.

**Affected propositions:** SSR-03, SSR-10, SSR-12, SSR-16–SSR-17, SSR-22–SSR-24; draft section 7.
