## 1. Constitutional Purpose and Meaning

**DGI-01.** Dependent-governance impact review is the bounded constitutional examination of whether and how a proposed constitutional amendment may affect governance that depends upon or references the constitutional meaning within the amendment scope.

**DGI-02.** The purpose of dependent-governance impact review is to make foreseeable constitutional consequences explicit before approval, preserve consistency across governance and prevent dependent governance from being silently contradicted, displaced or left constitutionally ambiguous.

**DGI-03.** Governance is dependent for the purpose of this review where an approved rule, definition, scope, Authority boundary, obligation or other constitutional treatment relies upon the constitutional meaning proposed to change.

**DGI-04.** A cross-reference may evidence a dependency, but a cross-reference, shared subject or documentary proximity does not by itself establish constitutional impact. The review must identify the governing relationship on which an impact finding relies.

**DGI-05.** Dependent-governance impact review is analytical. It identifies impact for attributable constitutional decision and does not itself amend, interpret, correct, approve, adopt, supersede or make any governance constitutionally effective.

## 2. Review Point and Boundary

**DGI-06.** Before Governance Authority approves a proposed constitutional amendment, dependent-governance impact must be examined sufficiently to identify known affected governance and any unresolved material uncertainty within the proposed amendment scope.

**DGI-07.** The impact-review boundary derives from the declared amendment scope and the constitutional dependencies connected to that scope. It must not expand merely because documents share terminology, location, authorship or subject matter.

**DGI-08.** An unresolved dependent-governance impact must remain explicit for the attributable Governance Authority decision. The impact review does not decide whether approval proceeds and does not resolve a constitutional conflict.

## 3. Impact Categories

**DGI-09.** A directly affected finding means that identified dependent governance would become inconsistent with, misstate or continue to rely upon constitutional meaning changed by the proposed amendment if no later governed treatment addresses that dependency.

**DGI-10.** A potentially affected finding means that a credible constitutional dependency exists but the available governance or evidence does not yet establish the amendment’s consequence for that dependency.

**DGI-11.** An unaffected finding means that the identified dependency relationship has been examined and no constitutional impact has been established within the declared amendment scope and available evidence. It is not a declaration that no future amendment or evidence could affect that governance.

**DGI-12.** An unresolved finding means that impact cannot be determined without resolving an ambiguity, missing constitutional basis or apparent conflict that this work package has no authority to decide.

**DGI-13.** An impact category is a review finding only. Categorizing dependent governance does not change its meaning, status, precedence, Authority, approval, adoption or constitutional effect.

## 4. Minimum Impact-Review Information

**DGI-14.** A dependent-governance impact-review record must identify the proposed amendment, its declared scope and any applicable amendment-trace reference already established for that amendment.

**DGI-15.** The record must identify each governance instrument or bounded provision reviewed and the constitutional dependency or cross-reference basis that placed it within the review boundary.

**DGI-16.** For each reviewed dependency, the record must identify the impact category, the rationale for that finding and the governance or evidence supporting it.

**DGI-17.** The record must identify known constitutional consequences, unresolved questions and any pending or deferred matter necessary to prevent the impact review from overstating certainty or completeness.

**DGI-18.** The record must preserve the review date, its attributable review source and any declared limitation relevant to later objective review. Recording attribution does not allocate a reviewer or confer Governance Authority.

## 5. Record Relationships and Cross-References

**DGI-19.** A dependent-governance impact-review record and an amendment trace are distinct. The impact-review record identifies dependent consequences; the amendment trace preserves the bounded relationship among source governance, constitutional change and attributable amendment treatment.

**DGI-20.** A dependent-governance impact-review record and an approval record are distinct. Impact-review evidence may inform a Governance Authority decision, but the review record cannot approve the amendment or substitute for the approval record.

**DGI-21.** Cross-reference impact must be identified where relevant, but identifying that impact does not update, redirect, replace or validate a cross-reference. Cross-reference synchronization remains separate governance.

## 6. Integrity and Anti-Inference

**DGI-22.** An impact finding must communicate no broader dependency, consequence or required treatment than its governing basis and evidence support. It cannot silently require an amendment to dependent governance.

**DGI-23.** Absence of an identified impact does not establish that no impact exists where relevant governance was not reviewed or the dependency basis remains incomplete. Missing or ambiguous impact must not be cured through inference.

**DGI-24.** This proposed treatment does not retrospectively classify historical governance, validate a prior impact review or assign dependent-governance consequences to an amendment whose attributable records did not establish them.

## 7. Deferred Governance and Explicit Non-Effects

This Founder Review Draft does not:

- perform an impact review for a specific amendment;
- amend, interpret, correct or reconcile dependent governance;
- reopen amendment traceability, approval, adoption, effect, lifecycle, classification or review proportionality;
- define conflict identification, escalation or disposition governance;
- define supersession;
- define retirement, withdrawal or rejection;
- define historical-preservation mechanisms;
- define governance-index or status synchronization;
- define validation doctrine or procedure;
- prescribe or perform cross-reference updates;
- define a complete decision-trace or change-log standard;
- define amendment, approval or review workflow;
- allocate who performs, records, maintains, validates or approves an impact review;
- create or allocate a Platform Authority, accountability relationship, role, permission or responsibility;
- define technical schema, storage, retention, identifier format or repository process;
- define implementation, automation, publication or deployment;
- retrospectively classify historical governance; or
- authorize a successor work package.

Every proposition remains subject to Founder constitutional review. No proposition in this draft is approved, adopted or constitutionally effective.
