## 3. RWQ-01 — Retirement Meaning and Purpose

### Issue

Whether retirement should mean an attributable ending of a current governing position without replacement being established by the retirement act.

### Current approved doctrine

The Blueprint requires retirement treatment, while SSR-05 and SSR-10 distinguish retirement from supersession and leave it separately deferred. Existing lifecycle and historical-integrity doctrine prevents a status from arising by implication.

### Proposed treatment

RWR-01 and RWR-02 define retirement as an explicit Governance Authority treatment ending an identified current governing position within scope, without itself identifying replacement governance.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve RWR-01 and RWR-02.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies a different bounded retirement meaning or purpose while preserving separation from supersession.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed treatment; Blueprint deliverable 13 remains incomplete.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A gives retirement a precise non-replacement function and preserves the already-approved distinction from supersession. It prevents documentary disappearance or non-use from silently ending a current governing position.

### Consequences

- Option A creates a reviewable proposed retirement boundary without applying it.
- Option B requires proposition and traceability amendment before candidate preparation.
- Option C leaves GAP-12 and the retirement portion of deliverable 13 unresolved.

### Affected propositions

RWR-01–RWR-02.

## 4. RWQ-02 — Withdrawal Meaning and Purpose

### Issue

Whether withdrawal should remove identified proposed or pending governance from further consideration without deciding its constitutional merits.

### Current approved doctrine

ATR-12 and ATR-15–ATR-17 require pending, deferred, rejected and withdrawn matters to remain distinct and forbid inferred status. The Blueprint requires withdrawal to be distinguished from retirement and supersession.

### Proposed treatment

RWR-03 and RWR-04 define withdrawal as an explicit Governance Authority treatment removing a proposed or pending matter from further consideration within scope without communicating a merits decision.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve RWR-03 and RWR-04.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies a different bounded treatment while preserving non-inference and the distinction from rejection.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed withdrawal treatment.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A preserves the difference between ending consideration and deciding against the content. It also prevents withdrawal from being inferred from silence, delay or repository treatment.

### Consequences

- Option A provides a distinct proposed treatment for pending matters.
- Option B requires corresponding boundary and evidence changes.
- Option C leaves withdrawal constitutionally undefined.

### Affected propositions

RWR-03–RWR-04.

## 5. RWQ-03 — Rejection Meaning and Purpose

### Issue

Whether rejection should be an attributable decision that identified proposed or pending governance is not approved within the declared scope.

### Current approved doctrine

APV-02 requires affirmative approval, APV-09 and APV-12 distinguish rejection from approved and deferred matters, and ATR-16 preserves rejected content as decision history without giving it effect.

### Proposed treatment

RWR-05 and RWR-06 define rejection as an explicit negative Governance Authority decision bounded to identified proposed or pending governance.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve RWR-05 and RWR-06.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder supplies a different bounded rejection meaning while preserving approval and effect boundaries.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed rejection treatment.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A ties rejection to an attributable negative decision rather than absence of approval. It preserves rejected content as historically intelligible without treating it as approved, effective or retrospectively invalid.

### Consequences

- Option A supplies a precise proposed negative-decision treatment.
- Option B requires amendments to mutual and evidence boundaries.
- Option C leaves rejection dependent on fragmented earlier references.

### Affected propositions

RWR-05–RWR-06.

## 6. RWQ-04 — Mutual Distinction

### Issue

Whether retirement, withdrawal and rejection should remain three distinct treatments based on the prior position and constitutional effect of the act.

### Current approved doctrine

The Blueprint names all three separately. Lifecycle explicit-uncertainty doctrine and SSR-10 prevent one undefined term from acquiring another term’s meaning through analogy.

### Proposed treatment

RWR-07 distinguishes ending a current governing position, removing a pending matter without a merits decision, and deciding not to approve a pending matter.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve the three-way distinction in RWR-07.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes one or more distinctions while retaining objective status boundaries.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the mutual distinction.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A makes each term do one constitutional job and prevents ambiguous status records. The distinction follows the Blueprint’s separate terminology and supports historical intelligibility.

### Consequences

- Option A preserves a coherent three-treatment architecture.
- Option B requires changes throughout the draft.
- Option C creates ambiguity across meaning, evidence and anti-inference propositions.

### Affected propositions

RWR-07.

## 7. RWQ-05 — Supersession and Amendment Boundaries

### Issue

Whether all three treatments should remain distinct from replacement and meaning-changing amendment.

### Current approved doctrine

SSR-01 defines supersession as an attributable replacement relationship; SSR-05, SSR-06 and SSR-10 separate it from amendment and the three treatments. AMP, CHB and AMC propositions require constitutional effect rather than labels to determine amendment treatment.

### Proposed treatment

RWR-08 prevents retirement, withdrawal or rejection from establishing replacement governance. RWR-09 prevents any of them from concealing a meaning-changing amendment.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve RWR-08 and RWR-09.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the external boundaries without permitting implied replacement or amendment bypass.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove one or both proposed boundaries.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A preserves the completed C.1, C.2B and C.8 architecture. It prevents a status label from being used either to install replacement governance or to avoid amendment requirements.

### Consequences

- Option A maintains dependency integrity.
- Option B requires cross-package consistency review.
- Option C risks overlap with supersession or amendment classification.

### Affected propositions

RWR-08–RWR-09.

## 8. RWQ-06 — Approval, Adoption and Constitutional Effect Boundaries

### Issue

Whether retirement, withdrawal and rejection should remain independently attributable from approval, adoption and constitutional effect.

### Current approved doctrine

CGP-02C.3 separates approval from recording and other status. CGP-02C.4 separates approval, adoption and effect and requires each constitutional act to remain independently attributable.

### Proposed treatment

RWR-10 and RWR-11 provide that none of the three treatments is established by approval alone and none incorporates content or makes content authoritatively govern.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve RWR-10 and RWR-11.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes the relationship while retaining attributable constitutional acts.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove one or both boundaries.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A applies the approved approval/adoption/effect architecture without redefining it. It ensures a single decision may record multiple acts only when each act and scope remain express.

### Consequences

- Option A maintains consistency with C.3 and C.4.
- Option B requires decision-linked changes to Authority and evidence propositions.
- Option C permits status ambiguity.

### Affected propositions

RWR-10–RWR-11.

## 9. RWQ-07 — Historical Preservation Boundary

### Current approved doctrine

AMP-07, GLC-12, ATR-22–ATR-23 and SSR-04 and SSR-18 require historical intelligibility while leaving technical preservation mechanisms outside their scopes. Blueprint deliverable 14 remains separate.

### Proposed treatment

RWR-12 distinguishes the constitutional treatment from preservation doctrine. RWR-23 requires historical identifiability and context without defining storage, retention, archival or other mechanisms.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve RWR-12 and RWR-23.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder narrows or changes the boundary while keeping deliverable 14 separate.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove the proposed preservation boundary.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A prevents a status-ending or negative treatment from erasing constitutional history while respecting the Blueprint sequence. It preserves the necessary outcome and defers the later doctrine and mechanisms.

### Consequences

- Option A maintains historical integrity without scope expansion.
- Option B requires revalidation against deliverable 14.
- Option C creates a risk of historical unintelligibility.

### Affected propositions

RWR-12 and RWR-23.

## 11. RWQ-09 — Anti-Inference and Deferred Governance

GLC-10–GLC-13, APV-23–APV-24, ATR-21 and ATR-24, and SSR-22–SSR-24 reject status creation through representation, practice or retrospective inference. The Blueprint preserves later Historical Preservation, index and validation subjects.

### Proposed treatment

RWR-21 through RWR-24 establish anti-inference and non-retrospectivity boundaries. Draft section 9 preserves all later governance and implementation exclusions.

### Options

- **Option A — Approve:** Preserve RWR-21 through RWR-24 and section 9.
- **Option B — Amend:** Founder changes a boundary without permitting implied status or importing later doctrine.
- **Option C — Defer or reject:** Remove some or all proposed protections.

### Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval

**Option A.**

### Reason for recommendation

Option A prevents administrative absence or practical abandonment from becoming constitutional truth. It also keeps later Blueprint subjects and operational mechanisms outside this bounded package.

### Consequences

- Option A preserves status and sequence integrity.
- Option B requires renewed scope and dependency validation.
- Option C permits ambiguity or premature scope expansion.

### Affected propositions

RWR-21–RWR-24 and draft section 9.
