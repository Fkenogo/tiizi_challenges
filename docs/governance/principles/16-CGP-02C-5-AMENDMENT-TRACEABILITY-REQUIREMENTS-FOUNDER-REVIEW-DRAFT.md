## 1. Constitutional Purpose and Meaning

**ATR-01.** A constitutional amendment trace is the attributable constitutional record that identifies a constitutional amendment, the source governance to which it relates, the scope of governed change and the decisions that establish its constitutional treatment.

**ATR-02.** The purpose of an amendment trace is to keep constitutional change attributable, bounded, objectively reviewable and historically intelligible. It must enable a later reader to distinguish the prior governing meaning, the changed meaning where established and the basis on which the change was constitutionally treated.

**ATR-03.** An amendment trace must preserve the relationship among the source governance, the proposed or established change, the declared amendment scope, the attributable amendment decision and any resulting constitutional status that has been separately established.

**ATR-04.** An amendment trace communicates constitutional change and its attributable treatment. Creating, maintaining, presenting or preserving the trace does not itself amend, approve, adopt or make governance constitutionally effective and creates no Authority to do so.

## 2. Minimum Amendment-Trace Information

**ATR-05.** Every constitutional amendment trace must have one stable and unique amendment-trace reference within Tiizi governance. The reference identifies the trace for attribution and later review; it does not establish the amendment or any constitutional status.

**ATR-06.** An amendment trace must identify the source governance and the provisions or constitutional meaning within the declared amendment scope. It must not imply that an entire instrument is affected where the attributable amendment is narrower.

**ATR-07.** An amendment trace must identify the prior governing meaning and the changed constitutional meaning where that changed meaning has been established. Proposed wording or treatment must remain distinguishable from established constitutional meaning.

**ATR-08.** An amendment trace must identify the declared constitutional purpose and rationale for the amendment without enlarging, substituting or retrospectively improving the rationale supplied by its attributable source.

**ATR-09.** An amendment trace must identify the attributable amendment decision, the approving actor, the Governance Authority exercised, the decision date and any applicable approval reference established by the source decision or approval record.

**ATR-10.** An amendment trace must identify the bounded amendment scope and the constitutional consequences expressly supported by the attributable decisions. It must communicate no consequence beyond that support.

**ATR-11.** Where approval, adoption, effective or application dates are relevant to the amendment, the trace may record only dates separately established through attributable Governance Authority. The trace does not create, supply or reconcile a missing or ambiguous date.

**ATR-12.** An amendment trace must distinguish affected, unaffected, pending, deferred, rejected and withdrawn matters to the extent necessary to prevent the trace from communicating a broader amendment or constitutional status than the source decisions establish.

## 3. Status and Scope Treatment

**ATR-13.** A matter identified as affected must remain bounded to the provisions, meaning and consequences supported by the attributable amendment decision. Association, proximity, shared documentation or common subject matter does not make another matter affected.

**ATR-14.** A matter identified as unaffected retains its existing constitutional treatment. The trace does not reaffirm, reopen or independently approve that matter merely by recording it as unaffected.

**ATR-15.** Pending and deferred matters remain pending or deferred unless a separate attributable Governance Authority decision changes their status. Their inclusion in an amendment trace supplies context only.

**ATR-16.** Rejected and withdrawn matters may be preserved as decision history but acquire no amendment, approval, adoption or constitutional effect from their inclusion in the trace.

**ATR-17.** Where the attributable sources do not establish whether a matter is affected, unaffected, pending, deferred, rejected or withdrawn, the amendment trace must preserve that uncertainty explicitly rather than assign a status by inference.

## 4. Record and Reference Boundaries

**ATR-18.** An amendment trace and an approval record are distinct. An approval record communicates an attributable approval decision; an amendment trace communicates the bounded relationship among source governance, constitutional change and the decisions and statuses relevant to that change.

**ATR-19.** An amendment trace is distinct from an adoption record, a constitutional-effect decision and a status representation. It may reference those records or decisions where they exist, but it cannot establish or replace them.

**ATR-20.** An amendment-trace reference and an approval reference identify different records and must remain distinguishable. Their relationship may be recorded without making either reference a source of Authority or constitutional status.

**ATR-21.** A title, label, filename, version designation, index entry, repository action, publication, implementation, administrative treatment or repeated use cannot create, enlarge or narrow an amendment through the amendment trace.

## 5. Historical Intelligibility and Anti-Inference

**ATR-22.** An amendment trace must preserve enough constitutional context for a later reader to understand the prior governing position, the bounded change, the attributable basis for that change and the constitutional treatment that was expressly established.

**ATR-23.** Historical intelligibility does not require this package to define versioning, storage, retention, archival, publication, technical identifiers or any other preservation mechanism.

**ATR-24.** This proposed treatment does not retrospectively assign amendment, approval, adoption, constitutional effect, date meaning or status to a historical record that did not expressly establish it.

## 6. Deferred Governance and Explicit Non-Effects

This Founder Review Draft does not:

- amend an existing governance instrument;
- approve, adopt or make any constitutional content effective;
- reopen governance lifecycle, amendment classification, review triggers and proportionality, Approval Governance, or Adoption and Constitutional Effect;
- conduct dependent-governance impact review or establish cross-reference review requirements;
- define conflict review, escalation or disposition;
- define supersession, retirement, withdrawal or rejection doctrine;
- define historical-preservation mechanisms;
- define governance-index or status synchronization;
- define validation doctrine or a complete governance-status or decision-trace doctrine;
- define an amendment procedure or workflow;
- allocate who authors, records, maintains, validates or approves an amendment trace;
- create or allocate a Platform Authority, accountability relationship, role, permission or responsibility;
- define technical versioning, schema, storage, retention, identifier format or repository process;
- define implementation, automation, publication or deployment; or
- retrospectively change any historical governance status.

Every proposition remains subject to Founder review. No proposition in this draft is approved, adopted or constitutionally effective.
