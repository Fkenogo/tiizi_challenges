# CGP-01 — Constitutional Governance Principles

**Version:** Founder Review Draft 1

**Status:** Draft for founder review; not approved constitutional governance

**Date:** 2026-07-21

## 1. Purpose, Status and Precedence

### 1.1 Purpose

CGP-01 states the platform-wide philosophy by which Tiizi should design, interpret and evolve constitutional governance. It explains what good governance is for, how much governance is appropriate, and how governance can support Community, truthful participation and durable accountability without becoming an end in itself.

This draft establishes no operative rule merely by existing. Its principles become approved governance only through attributable founder approval.

### 1.2 Precedence Boundary

If approved, CGP-01:

- provides platform-wide constitutional governance principles;
- guides future governance programmes and standards;
- may inform interpretation where approved governance is silent or ambiguous;
- does not silently amend, supersede, reopen or invalidate approved constitutional governance;
- cannot override EOG-01 through EOG-05 or another approved instrument without an attributable amendment; and
- must preserve the authority and traceability of previously approved governance.

Interpretation under CGP-01 must remain within the text, purpose and precedence of the approved instrument being interpreted. It cannot convert a pending decision into an approved rule, manufacture an allocation, or resolve a genuine conflict by preference.

An apparent tension with existing governance requires documented cross-reference analysis and, where necessary, attributable Governance Authority review. It must not be resolved through implied precedence.

### 1.3 Constitutional Position

Document 00 explains what exists in Tiizi. The Platform Constitution governs that conceptual world. Platform Principles state durable platform tests. CGP-01, if approved, would state the philosophy for creating and evolving governance within those boundaries.

CGP-01 is therefore interpretive and derivative. It is not a replacement constitution, a new Authority Model, an Entity Ownership Register or an operational governance manual.

## 2. Governance Philosophy

Tiizi governance exists to preserve the conditions under which people can participate together truthfully, safely and with confidence. Governance protects meaning, accountability, privacy, safety, historical intelligibility and the integrity of shared Community life.

Governance is not valuable because it creates more rules. It is valuable when it makes important boundaries understandable, responsibility attributable, truth dependable and change governable.

Tiizi should govern what materially requires shared constitutional certainty. It should leave human and Community discretion intact where no constitutional purpose requires uniformity. This balance prevents both under-governance, which allows ambiguity and unaccountable power, and over-governance, which replaces participation with unnecessary administration.

The constitutional mission remains the governing orientation:

> Tiizi is a community-first motivation platform that helps people build healthier lives through truthful collective participation.

Governance should make that mission more trustworthy and durable. It must not displace it.

## 3. Constitutional Governance Principles

### 3.1 Mission Fidelity

Governance should serve Tiizi's constitutional mission and ontology. A governance proposal should identify which constitutional purpose it protects and why that protection is necessary.

Governance that adds form without protecting Community, participation, truth, accountability, privacy, safety or durable evolution requires reconsideration.

### 3.2 Community Before Administrative Convenience

Governance should begin from the needs of healthy, voluntary Communities rather than the convenience of administration. Administrative simplicity is valuable, but it cannot justify weakening Community identity, participation meaning or constitutional accountability.

### 3.3 Truth and Attribution

Governed decisions, amendments and exercises of Authority must remain attributable. Governance must preserve the distinction between truth, interpretation, presentation, responsibility and operational action.

No custom, title, interface, possession of information or repeated practice may silently become constitutional authority.

### 3.4 Declared Purpose and Scope

Every constitutional rule should have a declared purpose and bounded scope. A rule adopted for one domain, risk or relationship must not expand by analogy into another without approved governance.

### 3.5 Separation of Constitutional Relationships

Authority, Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration, Operation, Contribution, Participation, Delegation and Presentation remain distinct. Governance should name the relationship it concerns and avoid using organisational titles as substitutes for constitutional meaning.

This principle preserves EOG-01; it makes no allocation.

### 3.6 Minimum Sufficient Governance

Tiizi should adopt the least burdensome governance capable of preserving the constitutional purpose at stake. “Minimum” does not mean incomplete, informal or optional where approved obligations require protection. “Sufficient” requires the clarity, attribution, reviewability and restraint appropriate to the consequence.

### 3.7 Reviewable and Attributable Change

Constitutional governance may evolve, but change must identify its source, rationale, affected instruments, approval status and consequences. Operational urgency, custom or local preference cannot silently amend governance.

### 3.8 Historical Intelligibility

Governance should allow a later reader to understand which constitutional meaning applied, why a decision was valid and how an amendment changed the governing position. Historical intelligibility does not itself prescribe versioning, retention, storage or archival mechanisms.

### 3.9 Privacy, Safety and Security Remain Binding

Community preference and governance proportionality do not override approved privacy, safety, security, least-privilege or minimum-necessary-use obligations. Simplicity must be achieved within those boundaries, not by removing them.

### 3.10 Conceptual Stability with Governed Evolution

Governance may refine how Tiizi's concepts are protected and expressed. It must not redefine a constitutional concept merely because a new process, product capability or technology would be easier to describe another way.

## 4. Progressive Governance

### 4.1 Meaning

Progressive governance is the principle that governance may develop as the constitutional needs, risks and relationships of a Community or programme become more complex. Governance need not begin with every structure that might later become useful.

Progressive governance supports timely constitutional sufficiency without requiring speculative bureaucracy.

### 4.2 Core Boundaries

Progressive governance means:

- governance may be refined through attributable approval as real constitutional needs emerge;
- optional structures need not exist before their purpose arises;
- later complexity does not make earlier constitutionally sufficient simplicity defective;
- absence of optional governance machinery is not, by itself, governance failure; and
- every later refinement remains subject to precedence, traceability and approved amendment requirements.

Progressive governance does not mean that approved obligations may be postponed, ignored or treated as optional. It does not authorize silent custom, informal Authority, unrecorded allocation or retroactive justification.

### 4.3 Community Application

Communities may evolve their governance without being required to imitate one institutional form. That evolution remains constrained by the singular Accountable Steward relationship and every other approved constitutional boundary applicable to the Community.

## 5. Governance Proportionality

### 5.1 Meaning

Governance proportionality requires the formality, assurance and review burden of governance to be commensurate with the constitutional consequence it addresses.

Relevant constitutional considerations may include:

- impact on people and Communities;
- authority and accountability consequences;
- privacy, safety and security sensitivity;
- effect on authoritative or historical truth;
- breadth and duration of the governing effect;
- reversibility of the decision; and
- risk of silent or competing governance.

These considerations guide judgement. They are not a scoring model, threshold system or implementation test.

### 5.2 Proportionality Boundary

Low complexity does not remove the need for declared authority where Authority is exercised. High consequence may justify stronger attribution, review and preservation even when the operational action appears simple.

Proportionality cannot reduce an approved constitutional minimum. It helps determine how future governance should satisfy that minimum without unnecessary burden.

## 6. Community-first Governance

Community-first governance supports belonging, voluntary participation, mutual respect, shared purpose and truthful accountability. It should help Communities govern what matters without requiring people to navigate constitutional complexity merely to participate.

Community-first governance therefore prefers:

- clarity before procedural density;
- purpose before institutional form;
- voluntary participation before administrative compulsion;
- support before unnecessary control;
- accountable simplicity before unbounded discretion; and
- truthful Community autonomy within Platform boundaries.

Community preference does not override constitutional accountability, Platform Authority, applicable Policy, privacy, safety, security, least privilege or another approved obligation. Community-first governance is not Community supremacy. It places Community purpose at the centre while preserving the shared constitutional protections that make Community participation trustworthy.

Governance should not become a prerequisite for participation unless later approved governance expressly establishes a necessary and proportionate requirement.

## 7. Governance-body Philosophy

### 7.1 Constitutional Foundation

CGP-01 references, but does not create, C-GRP-08 Group Governance Body as approved by the EOG-05 CG-08 amendment.

Group Governance Body is the canonical constitutional class. Stewardship Council remains an approved alias. A Group may determine the operational title of any governance body it establishes.

### 7.2 Voluntary and Progressive Establishment

A Group may establish zero, one or multiple governance bodies. The Group chooses whether and when such a body is useful, subject to later approved governance.

The absence of a governance body shall not, by itself, prevent a Group from being created, operating, accepting members or participating in Platform activities unless expressly provided by later approved governance.

### 7.3 Relationship Boundary

A governance body does not replace the singular Accountable Steward relationship. The existence, membership, title or activity of a governance body does not itself create:

- Authority;
- Accountable Stewardship;
- Custody;
- Administration;
- Operation;
- Delegation;
- Assignment of Responsibility;
- a role; or
- a permission.

Any later relationship must be separately declared, scoped and approved under EOG-01 and applicable governance.

### 7.4 Matters Not Defined

CGP-01 defines no governance-body composition, election, appointment, procedure, decision-making, tenure, role, permission, relationship allocation or lifecycle. It creates no requirement that a governance body exist and no limitation to a single body.

## 8. Governance Maturity Principles

### 8.1 Conceptual Meaning

Governance maturity is the conceptual capacity of governance to remain coherent, attributable, proportionate and resilient as circumstances change. It concerns the quality of constitutional adaptation, not the quantity of governance machinery.

A Community or programme does not become more mature merely by adding bodies, rules, titles or procedures. Simplicity may remain constitutionally mature where it continues to protect the relevant purpose and obligations.

### 8.2 Restraint

Governance maturity is conceptual only. CGP-01 creates no:

- maturity levels or stages;
- scores, badges or thresholds;
- compliance requirements;
- mandatory progression;
- Group Health or Group Governance Health metrics;
- governance incentives; or
- product behaviour.

No person, Group, Challenge, governance body or programme receives a maturity classification under this draft.

### 8.3 Evolution Without Compulsion

Governance may evolve as Communities and programmes encounter new constitutional needs. Such evolution must be justified by purpose, not by an assumption that more formal governance is always better.

## 9. Constitutional and Operational Governance

### 9.1 Constitutional Governance

Constitutional governance defines enduring concepts, principles, boundaries, authority types, accountability relationships, precedence and amendment requirements. It answers what must remain true across changing operations and technologies.

### 9.2 Operational Governance

Operational governance applies approved constitutional boundaries to the conduct, coordination and continuity of governed activity. It may later define procedures, responsibilities and operating controls within an approved scope.

### 9.3 Separation

Constitutional governance does not become an operational procedure merely because implementation requires action. Operational practice does not become constitutional governance through repetition, convenience or local acceptance.

Future operational standards must cite their constitutional basis. Where no basis exists, the operational need must return to attributable governance rather than create constitutional meaning by practice.

This distinction does not allocate Operational Authority or any other EOG-01 relationship.

## 10. Derivative Future Governance Standards

If approved, CGP-01 should guide later standards concerning:

- governance amendment and constitutional review;
- Group Governance Body governance;
- Group Charter governance;
- stewardship continuity and reassignment;
- Group and membership lifecycle;
- Challenge governance and lifecycle;
- moderation and Community conduct;
- roles and permissions;
- privacy and security;
- administrative and operational governance;
- auditability and historical preservation;
- the Entity Relationship Allocation Register;
- contributor and knowledge governance;
- domain admission and future domain governance; and
- governance documentation and traceability.

These programmes remain responsible for their own scope, decisions and approval. CGP-01 does not pre-approve their contents.

## 11. Explicit Non-effects and Deferred Areas

CGP-01 does not:

- create a constitutional entity;
- allocate an EOG-01 relationship;
- define entity ownership;
- create a Platform Authority;
- define a role or permission;
- define an election or appointment;
- define a governance procedure;
- define a lifecycle, state or transition;
- define a governance-body requirement;
- define a Group Health model;
- define a governance-maturity metric;
- define implementation or product behaviour;
- resolve a pending founder decision;
- authorize operational governance;
- amend EOG-01 through EOG-05; or
- override another approved constitutional instrument.

All derivative governance remains subject to its own evidence, scope, review, decision trace and approval.

## 12. Founder Review Questions

Founder review is requested to confirm whether:

1. governance exists to protect truthful, safe and accountable Community participation rather than maximize institutional form;
2. minimum sufficient governance and proportionality provide the correct constitutional restraint;
3. progressive governance permits attributable evolution without postponing approved obligations;
4. Community-first governance properly preserves accountability, privacy, safety and security;
5. the governance-body philosophy faithfully preserves C-GRP-08 and singular Accountable Stewardship;
6. governance maturity is correctly limited to a non-measured conceptual principle;
7. constitutional and operational governance remain sufficiently distinct;
8. the precedence boundary protects every approved instrument and pending decision; and
9. the identified future programmes are appropriate derivative governance areas.

No principle in this draft is approved until founder review is completed and an attributable approval record is issued.
