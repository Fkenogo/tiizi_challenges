## 1. Constitutional Purpose of Review Triggers and Proportionality

**RTP-01.** Review Triggers and Proportionality identify constitutionally relevant reasons for governance review and bound the formality, assurance and review burden by constitutional consequence. They do not prescribe review procedure or predetermine a review outcome.

**RTP-02.** A review trigger identifies a constitutional basis for examination. It does not exercise Governance Authority, establish that a constitutional defect exists, approve a change or create constitutional effect.

## 2. Constitutional Bases for Governance Review

**RTP-03.** A proposal that may create, amend, reinterpret or make an exception to constitutional meaning, scope, precedence, Authority, accountability or governing effect is a constitutional basis for governance review.

**RTP-04.** A credible apparent conflict, ambiguity, gap, competing source of constitutional truth or inability to identify an objectively reviewable governing basis is a constitutional basis for governance review.

**RTP-05.** A credible risk to constitutional integrity, authoritative truth, privacy, safety, security, least privilege, accountability or historical intelligibility is a constitutional basis for governance review.

**RTP-06.** Elapsed time alone does not establish constitutional deficiency. Periodic review may be constitutionally justified only where an approved instrument or later approved governance identifies an enduring constitutional purpose for that review; this proposition does not establish a period, schedule or procedure.

## 3. Proportionality by Constitutional Consequence

This package preserves GVR-03 through GVR-05 in the [CGP-02C.1 Founder Approval Candidate](05-CGP-02C-1-CONSTITUTIONAL-FRAMEWORK-AND-AMENDMENT-PRINCIPLES-DRAFT.md). It does not amend, replace or complete those propositions.

**RTP-07.** Review formality, assurance and burden must be proportionate to constitutional consequence. Organisation size, membership numbers, popularity, administrative workload and document length do not independently determine review depth.

**RTP-08.** Relevant constitutional consequences may include impact on people and Communities; Authority and accountability consequences; privacy, safety and security sensitivity; effect on authoritative or historical truth; breadth and duration of governing effect; reversibility; and risk of silent or competing governance. These considerations guide constitutional judgement and do not create a score, tier or threshold.

**RTP-09.** A lower-burden review remains subject to every applicable approved constitutional minimum. Proportionality cannot remove declared Authority, attribution, bounded purpose and scope, precedence, protection of constitutional meaning, historical intelligibility or another binding constitutional protection.

## 4. Objective Reviewability and Independent Review

**RTP-10.** Objective reviewability is a property required of every constitutional rule: its purpose, scope, basis, Authority boundaries and constitutional effect must be capable of examination against governed evidence.

**RTP-11.** Independent Review is conceptually distinct from objective reviewability. It denotes review with sufficient constitutional separation from the proposal or position under examination to challenge its assumptions and consequences, without allocating a reviewer, role or relationship.

**RTP-12.** Not every objectively reviewable matter necessarily requires Independent Review. Constitutional consequence may justify Independent Review, but the exact triggers, thresholds, reviewer qualifications and review arrangements remain deferred to later approved governance.

## 5. Review Boundary and Explicit Deferrals

**RTP-13.** Review depth does not alter the distinction between review and approval. A review, including Independent Review, remains analytical and does not by itself approve, amend, adopt or establish constitutional meaning or effect.

**RTP-14.** A review trigger does not predetermine the finding, require constitutional change or convert an observed practice, recommendation, pending decision or implementation condition into constitutional governance.

This draft does not determine:

- a review procedure, workflow or evidence package;
- mandatory review levels, tiers, scores or thresholds;
- which actor, role or relationship conducts review;
- reviewer eligibility, appointment or independence tests;
- a periodic-review interval, schedule or implementation;
- approval procedure or approval evidence;
- adoption or constitutional effect;
- lifecycle governance;
- amendment classification;
- validation doctrine;
- traceability doctrine;
- supersession, retirement, withdrawal or rejection; or
- repository or technical implementation.

These matters require attributable Founder decision or their separately authorized CGP-02 packages. No review label, recurring practice or lower review burden answers them through implication.
