## 1. Constitutional Amendment Principles

### 1.1 Governed Evolution

**AMP-01.** Tiizi constitutional governance may evolve only through an explicit and attributable constitutional amendment that remains within the approved precedence hierarchy. Evolution does not permit silent displacement of existing governance.

**AMP-02.** Governance Authority is the Platform Authority that may establish constitutional amendments. Recommendation, drafting, review, administration, operation, presentation, possession or technical capability does not establish amendment Authority.

### 1.2 Declared Purpose and Bounded Scope

**AMP-03.** Every constitutional amendment must serve a declared constitutional purpose and remain bounded to its approved subject and effect. An amendment approved for one domain, risk, relationship or instrument does not expand into another by analogy or convenience.

**AMP-04.** A lower-order instrument may refine higher-order governance within its approved scope but must not contradict it. Where a conflict reaches the Platform Constitution, the Constitution governs unless an approved amendment expressly supersedes the affected constitutional position.

### 1.3 No Silent Amendment

**AMP-05.** Operational urgency, custom, local preference, repeated practice, implementation behaviour, interface capability and administrative convenience cannot create or amend constitutional governance.

**AMP-06.** A constitutional amendment does not silently infer, extend, transfer or redistribute Authority. Any change to Authority must itself be explicit, attributable and constitutionally approved.

### 1.4 Historical and Conceptual Integrity

**AMP-07.** Constitutional amendment must preserve historical intelligibility. Later readers must remain able to understand the prior governing meaning, the amended governing meaning and that a governed change occurred, without the amendment silently concealing the earlier constitutional position.

**AMP-08.** Constitutional amendment may refine how Tiizi’s constitutional concepts are protected or expressed, but it must not redefine a constitutional concept merely because a process, capability or technology would be easier to describe differently.

### 1.5 Minimum Necessary Amendment

**AMP-09.** A proposed constitutional amendment must respond to a present, identifiable constitutional purpose. It must introduce no more constitutional governance than is necessary to preserve that purpose and must not create speculative governance merely because it may become useful later.

## 2. Governance Review Principles

### 2.1 Objective Reviewability

**GVR-01.** Every constitutional rule must be capable of objective review.

**GVR-02.** Constitutional governance review must assess a rule or proposed change against its declared purpose, bounded scope, applicable precedence, Authority boundaries and effect on constitutional and historical integrity.

### 2.2 Proportionate Review

**GVR-03.** The formality, assurance and review burden applied to constitutional governance must be proportionate to constitutional consequence. Organisation size, membership numbers, popularity and administrative workload do not independently determine review depth.

**GVR-04.** Review proportionality cannot remove an approved constitutional minimum. A lower-burden review remains subject to the same requirements for declared Authority, attribution, precedence and protection of constitutional meaning.

### 2.3 Review Does Not Establish Governance

**GVR-05.** Governance review examines constitutional governance; it does not by itself approve, amend, adopt or establish constitutional meaning. A review finding remains analytical until acted upon through the applicable approved Governance Authority.

**GVR-06.** Review must distinguish approved governance from drafts, recommendations, pending decisions, observed practice, operational action and implementation evidence. Repetition or apparent acceptance does not convert any of those sources into constitutional governance.

### 2.4 Conflict Discipline

**GVR-07.** An apparent constitutional tension must be identified and examined against approved precedence. It must not be resolved through preference, convenience or implied precedence.

**GVR-08.** A lower layer may refine but must not contradict a higher layer. Equal conflicting Authorities must not coexist silently, and an unresolved Authority conflict must be escalated to Governance Authority.

### 2.5 Binding Protections

**GVR-09.** Governance review must preserve applicable constitutional integrity, truth, privacy, safety, security, least-privilege, minimum-necessary-use and historical-integrity obligations. Proportionality or simplicity cannot remove an approved protection.

## 3. Constitutional Change Boundary

### 3.1 Constitutional Meaning

**CHB-01.** A change is constitutional where its governing effect changes what exists or why it exists, or changes an approved constitutional concept, principle, boundary, Authority type, accountability relationship, precedence position or amendment requirement.

**CHB-02.** The constitutional effect of a change is determined by what the change does, not by the label applied to it. A change described as interpretation, refinement, correction, housekeeping, status maintenance, programme change or operational action cannot avoid constitutional amendment requirements if it changes constitutional meaning or effect.

### 3.2 Interpretation Boundary

**CHB-03.** Interpretation must remain within the text, purpose, scope and precedence of the approved instrument being interpreted. Interpretation cannot expand a rule by analogy, convert a pending decision into an approved rule or resolve a genuine constitutional conflict by preference.

**CHB-04.** Clarification that preserves existing constitutional meaning is distinct from a change that establishes different constitutional meaning. The classification and review treatment of a proposed clarification remain subject to later CGP-02 drafting and Founder determination.

### 3.3 Operational and Programme Boundary

**CHB-05.** Operational governance defines how approved constitutional governance is implemented. It derives its legitimacy from constitutional governance and cannot independently create constitutional meaning.

**CHB-06.** A product capability, technical implementation, repository practice, operational procedure or programme-control decision does not amend constitutional governance. Where such work requires constitutional meaning that does not exist, the matter must return to attributable constitutional governance.

### 3.4 Draft and Practice Boundary

**CHB-07.** Discovery, analysis, drafting, review, recommendation and planning do not establish constitutional governance merely because they are complete, repeated, relied upon or recorded.

**CHB-08.** Custom, organisational title, information possession, interface access, administrative control and repeated practice do not create constitutional Authority and cannot serve as substitutes for approved amendment.

### 3.5 Scope Boundary for Later CGP-02 Drafting

This constitutional change boundary does not determine governance-document lifecycle, approval evidence, adoption, effective dates, amendment classification, supersession, retirement, validation, traceability or implementation procedure. Those matters remain outside CGP-02C.1. This paragraph is a drafting-scope statement, not a proposed constitutional proposition.
