# CGP-02C.2A — Governance Lifecycle Founder Review Decision Pack

**Draft under review:** [Governance Lifecycle Founder Review Draft](07-CGP-02C-2A-GOVERNANCE-LIFECYCLE-FOUNDER-REVIEW-DRAFT.md)

**Status:** Founder decision material; recommendations accepted in principle for the Founder Approval Candidate; constitutional approval pending

**Date:** 2026-07-22

## 1. Decision Boundary

This pack presents only Governance Lifecycle questions. It does not ask the Founder to approve a procedure, adoption effect, effective date, supersession, retirement, validation doctrine, traceability doctrine, amendment classification or review threshold.

The questions are organized by constitutional function. Section A contains decisions about governing principles. Section B contains decisions about the bounded vocabulary through which those principles are expressed. This organization changes no question, option or recommendation.

## 2. Section A — Constitutional Principle Decisions

### 2.1 LFQ-01 — Canonical Lifecycle Purpose and Vocabulary

#### 2.1.1 Issue

Whether CGP-02 should establish a minimum constitutional vocabulary for lifecycle distinctions rather than leave all terms to programme-specific usage.

#### 2.1.2 Current Approved Doctrine

Approved governance requires objective review, explicit attributable approval, historical intelligibility and separation of constitutional from operational governance. No canonical governance-document lifecycle is approved.

#### 2.1.3 Proposed Treatment

GLC-01, GLC-03 and GLC-04 establish lifecycle purpose and distinguish Draft and Review without creating constitutional effect.

#### 2.1.4 Options and Consequences

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | Approve a minimum constitutional lifecycle vocabulary with each term bounded from Authority and effect. | Improves consistency while leaving procedures and detailed effects to later packages. |
| B | Approve only a general lifecycle-purpose principle and leave all term definitions outside constitutional governance. | Minimizes constitutional vocabulary but preserves current cross-programme ambiguity. |
| C | Create no Governance Lifecycle section. | Avoids new doctrine but leaves GAP-01 unresolved. |

**Recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Propositions affected:** GLC-01, GLC-03 and GLC-04.

**Decision required now:** Yes, for approval of the proposed minimum vocabulary.

### 2.2 LFQ-05 — Status and Effect Separation

#### 2.2.1 Issue

Whether CGP-02 should state a general constitutional boundary that document or programme status cannot independently establish constitutional effect.

#### 2.2.2 Current Approved Doctrine

Authority cannot arise from title, interface, possession, repetition or convenience. Constitutional change must be explicit and attributable. Existing metadata conflicts demonstrate the need for clarity but do not themselves create doctrine.

#### 2.2.3 Proposed Treatment

GLC-10 separates status from effect. GLC-11 prevents competing truth, and GLC-13 prevents undefined terminology acquiring meaning through practice.

#### 2.2.4 Options and Consequences

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | Approve the general status/effect separation. | Provides a durable boundary across inconsistent metadata without correcting or normalizing existing documents. |
| B | Apply the boundary only to named lifecycle statuses approved later. | Narrows the rule but leaves current unclassified labels capable of ambiguity. |
| C | Leave status/effect interpretation instrument-specific. | Preserves flexibility but perpetuates competing status signals and inconsistent treatment. |

**Recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Propositions affected:** GLC-10 directly; GLC-11 and GLC-13 supporting.

**Decision required now:** Yes for approval of the general boundary.

## 3. Section B — Constitutional Vocabulary Decisions

### 3.1 LFQ-02 — Adoption as a Distinct Concept

#### 3.1.1 Issue

Whether Adoption should remain conceptually distinct from Draft, Review and Approval while its necessity and constitutional effect remain deferred.

#### 3.1.2 Current Approved Doctrine

CGP-01 has a separate adoption record, while EOG instruments often combine approval and transition. No general adoption rule is approved.

#### 3.1.3 Proposed Treatment

GLC-06 recognizes Adoption as a distinct lifecycle concept but expressly defers whether it is required, how it relates to Approval and what effect it has.

#### 3.1.4 Options and Consequences

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | Preserve Adoption as a distinct concept and defer necessity and effect. | Prevents premature synonymy while reserving the substantive decision for Adoption and Constitutional Effect governance. |
| B | Treat Adoption as synonymous with Approval. | Simplifies vocabulary but silently resolves an observed inconsistency without a developed effect model. |
| C | Omit Adoption from lifecycle vocabulary until its later package. | Maximizes restraint but leaves the current distinction unexpressed in the lifecycle model. |

**Recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Proposition affected:** GLC-06.

**Decision required now:** Yes for conceptual inclusion; necessity and effect remain deferred.

### 3.2 LFQ-03 — Application and Transition

#### 3.2.1 Issue

Whether Application and Transition should be recognized as distinct lifecycle concepts that cannot independently create constitutional effect.

#### 3.2.2 Current Approved Doctrine

Applied amendments and transition reports exist in approved programme records, but repeated use does not create a general constitutional rule. Authority and change must remain explicit and attributable.

#### 3.2.3 Proposed Treatment

GLC-07 and GLC-08 recognize the terms while deferring their exact meaning, necessity, effect, procedure and record form.

#### 3.2.4 Options and Consequences

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | Recognize both as distinct concepts with no independent Authority or effect. | Supplies a safe vocabulary boundary without converting observed practice into procedure. |
| B | Treat both as operational or programme terms only. | Avoids constitutional recognition but may preserve ambiguity between approved content and its later treatment. |
| C | Use one combined post-approval concept. | Reduces terms but prematurely consolidates concepts whose meanings and effects remain unresolved. |

**Recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Propositions affected:** GLC-07 and GLC-08.

**Decision required now:** Yes for conceptual inclusion; detailed meaning and effect remain deferred.

### 3.3 LFQ-04 — Completion Boundary

#### 3.3.1 Issue

Whether Completion should be constitutionally bounded so that finishing a governance work package cannot be mistaken for approval or effect.

#### 3.3.2 Current Approved Doctrine

Approved governance requires independent attributable approval. Programme records use Complete for finished work, but no general lifecycle rule defines its constitutional boundary.

#### 3.3.3 Proposed Treatment

GLC-09 defines Completion only as fulfillment of declared work scope and denies it independent approval, adoption, application or constitutional effect.

#### 3.3.4 Options and Consequences

| Option | Treatment | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | Approve the proposed Completion boundary. | Prevents programme completion from being mistaken for constitutional approval. |
| B | Leave Completion entirely as programme terminology. | Avoids constitutional treatment but leaves the recurring status/effect ambiguity unresolved. |
| C | Defer any constitutional treatment of Completion and remove GLC-09 from this package. | Avoids present treatment but leaves the recurring completion/effect ambiguity unresolved. |

**Recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Proposition affected:** GLC-09.

**Decision required now:** Yes for inclusion in the lifecycle vocabulary.
