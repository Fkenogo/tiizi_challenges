## 1. Purpose and Scope

Tiizi governance contains entities, governed subjects and relationships whose meaning depends on knowing where authority, accountability, stewardship, participation, custody and other governed relationships sit.

CGP-04 establishes the **Entity Relationship Allocation Register** through which those relationships are represented.

CGP-04 does not create a new Tiizi entity ontology.

It relies on entity identities and classifications established through existing Tiizi authority, including the Entity Ownership Register, and records the relationships applicable to those entities.

### CGP04-P01 — Necessary Allocation Principle

A relationship requires explicit allocation in CGP-04 where failure to identify its governed state would materially impair authority, accountability, stewardship, dependency control or safe downstream governance.

### CGP04-P02 — Existing Authority Principle

Where existing Tiizi authority already establishes a relationship, CGP-04 must represent that relationship faithfully and must not reopen it merely because the relationship is being entered into the register.

### CGP04-P03 — No Authority by Allocation Record

Recording an entity or relationship in CGP-04 does not itself create substantive authority beyond the attributable authority supporting that allocation.

### CGP04-P04 — Unresolved Allocation Principle

Where a material relationship is recognized but its substantive allocation has not been authoritatively determined, CGP-04 must preserve that state explicitly rather than infer an allocation.

---

## 2. Governing Distinctions

CGP-04 preserves distinctions already established within Tiizi governance.

### CGP04-P05 — Authority Is Distinct from Responsibility

Authority, accountability, stewardship, custody, administration, operation and participation are distinct governance concepts.

Possession of one does not automatically confer another.

### CGP04-P06 — Participation Does Not Confer Authority

Participation, membership, contribution or other product participation does not by itself confer Governance Authority, stewardship, custody or another governance relationship.

### CGP04-P07 — Governance Authority Cannot Be Silently Transferred

Governance Authority remains where existing constitutional authority places it unless an attributable governance decision changes that allocation.

Delegation, assignment, administration or operational execution must not be interpreted as silent transfer of Governance Authority.

### CGP04-P08 — Product Expression Does Not Erase Constitutional Distinction

Where a constitutional concept has a product expression, CGP-04 must preserve the distinction between the constitutional concept and its product expression.

In particular:

- Community and Group are related but not interchangeable;
- Undertaking and Challenge are related but not interchangeable;
- Commitment and Participation are related but not interchangeable;
- Motivation and Recognition are related but not interchangeable.

### CGP04-P09 — Evidence, Truth and Recognition Remain Distinct

Evidence eligibility, establishment of derived truth and recognition are distinct governed concerns.

Authority over one must not be inferred to confer authority over another.

---

## 3. Relationship Vocabulary

CGP-04 uses relationship concepts already established through Tiizi entity and ownership governance.

The register must not create a new relationship category merely because an existing allocation is incomplete.

Where the existing EOG relationship vocabulary applies, that vocabulary remains controlling.

### CGP04-P10 — Existing Relationship Vocabulary

The Entity Relationship Allocation Register must use the approved EOG relationship vocabulary wherever that vocabulary already covers the governed relationship.

### CGP04-P11 — Relationship Meaning Must Remain Stable

A relationship label must retain the governed meaning established by its source authority.

Similar administrative or product terminology must not silently redefine the governance relationship.

### CGP04-P12 — New Relationship Types Require Authority

A materially new governance relationship type may be added only where existing vocabulary is insufficient and attributable governance authority establishes the additional relationship.

---

## 4. Allocation Status Model

Every material register entry must make its current allocation state visible.

CGP-04 recognizes five allocation states.

### **ALLOCATED**

Existing attributable authority establishes the relationship and its allocation sufficiently for current governance.

### **PROVISIONAL**

The relationship or relevant entity is sufficiently established to be represented, but an identified unresolved dependency prevents the allocation from being treated as final.

### **DEFERRED**

The relationship is recognized, but its substantive determination belongs to later governance, another governed domain or an expressly deferred decision.

### **UNALLOCATED — FOUNDER DECISION REQUIRED**

CGP-04 requires the relationship to be allocated for its own governed purpose, but current authority does not establish the allocation sufficiently.

### **NOT APPLICABLE**

The relationship does not apply to the governed entity or subject.

### CGP04-P13 — Allocation State Must Be Explicit

Every material relationship represented in CGP-04 must have a determinable allocation status.

### CGP04-P14 — Provisional Is Not Final

A provisional allocation must not be represented downstream as a final allocation.

### CGP04-P15 — Deferred Is Not Unallocated Failure

A relationship deliberately assigned to later governance may remain Deferred without preventing CGP-04 completion unless existing authority expressly makes its resolution a CGP-04 completion dependency.

### CGP04-P16 — Unallocated Matters Must Not Be Filled by Inference

Where a required allocation remains undetermined, the register must state that condition rather than assign an entity because it appears operationally suitable.

---

## 5. Cross-Register Contract

The existing Entity Ownership Register and CGP-04 serve different functions.

The **Entity Ownership Register** establishes the inventory and classification state of governed entities.

The **Entity Relationship Allocation Register** establishes the governed relationships applicable to those entities.

### CGP04-P17 — Entity Identity Source

CGP-04 must use the governed entity identity and classification established by the applicable Entity Ownership Register or other controlling authority.

### CGP04-P18 — No Reclassification Through CGP-04

CGP-04 must not alter an entity's classification merely by allocating a relationship to it.

### CGP04-P19 — Provisional Entities May Be Represented

A provisionally classified entity may participate in the Entity Relationship Allocation Register where necessary, provided its provisional state and any material dependency remain visible.

### CGP04-P20 — Cross-Register Consistency

Where a change to authoritative entity identity or classification materially affects a relationship allocation, the affected register representation must be reconciled under CGP-03 without silently changing substantive authority.

## 7. Authority-Type Boundary

Existing EOG governance recognizes multiple Platform Authority types, including Governance, Identity, Participation, Participant, Knowledge, Policy, Acceptance, Calculation, Administrative, Operational and Presentation Authority.

CGP-04 does not collapse those authorities into a single operational role.

### CGP04-P21 — Authority Types Remain Distinct

A Platform Authority type must not be treated as interchangeable with another Authority type merely because the same implementation actor may later exercise more than one authorized function.

### CGP04-P22 — Runtime Catalogue Is Not Independent Authority

A runtime catalogue, implementation registry or equivalent technical representation does not become an additional Platform Authority type merely by representing governed authorities in software.

### CGP04-P23 — Implementation Actor Does Not Inherit Governance Authority

A technical service, administrator, operator, authentication provider, application role or software component does not acquire substantive governance authority merely because it executes a governed decision or control.

---

## 8. Accountability, Stewardship and Custody

Existing Tiizi governance establishes some accountability relationships while leaving others deliberately unallocated.

CGP-04 must preserve both conditions.

### CGP04-P24 — Accountability Requires Attribution

Where accountability is allocated, the accountable relationship must be traceable to the authority establishing it.

### CGP04-P25 — Stewardship Is Not Ownership of Governance Authority

An Accountable Steward may hold responsibility for a governed entity or relationship without thereby acquiring Governance Authority over the constitutional rules governing that subject.

### CGP04-P26 — Custody Is Not Authority

Custody of information, records, infrastructure or another governed resource does not by itself confer authority to determine its substantive governance meaning.

### CGP04-P27 — Administration and Operation Are Not Constitutional Allocation

Administrative or operational responsibility may execute governed requirements but does not itself change the constitutional allocation of authority or accountability.

### CGP04-P28 — Unnamed Holders Remain Unallocated

Where an accountable steward, custodian, administrator or operator has not been authoritatively designated, CGP-04 must not manufacture a named holder merely to complete the register.

---

## 9. Delegation and Derived Authority

Tiizi governance recognizes delegation as distinct from assignment and stewardship reassignment.

CGP-04 does not establish a universal delegation regime where one does not yet exist.

### CGP04-P29 — Delegation Must Be Attributable

Any delegation of governed authority must be traceable to authority capable of making that delegation.

### CGP04-P30 — Delegation Does Not Equal Transfer

Delegation of an authorized function does not transfer the underlying Governance Authority unless an attributable constitutional decision expressly provides otherwise.

### CGP04-P31 — Delegation, Assignment and Reassignment Remain Distinct

Delegation, assignment and stewardship reassignment must not be treated as interchangeable governance actions.

### CGP04-P32 — No General Delegation Model by Inference

CGP-04 must not infer a general delegation model beyond what existing authority establishes.

---

## 10. Deferred and Unallocated Matters

CGP-04 recognizes that a complete governance register can legitimately contain unresolved relationships.

Completion means that material relationships are truthfully represented—not that every future governance decision has already been made.

### CGP04-P33 — Deferred Matters Remain Governed

A Deferred relationship remains a governed relationship whose current state is that substantive determination belongs elsewhere or later.

### CGP04-P34 — Dependency Must Be Visible

Where a Provisional or Deferred allocation depends on another governed decision, the material dependency must remain identifiable.

### CGP04-P35 — Downstream Decisions Must Remain Downstream

CGP-04 must not resolve a product, Knowledge Governance, activity-integrity, motivation, identity or implementation question solely to make the allocation register appear complete.

### CGP04-P36 — Deferred D17 Questions Remain Deferred

CGP-04 does not by itself resolve DQ-01, DQ-02, DQ-04, DQ-05, DQ-09, DQ-10 or DQ-11.

Where those questions later affect a relationship represented in CGP-04, the register may be reconciled after the attributable decision is made.

---

## 11. Register Maintenance and Change

CGP-04 does not create a separate governance lifecycle for maintaining the register.

CGP-02 and CGP-03 already provide the necessary constitutional and traceability disciplines.

### CGP04-P37 — Substantive Change

A change that creates, removes, reallocates or materially changes a governed relationship must be attributable to authority capable of making that substantive decision and must comply with applicable CGP-02 governance.

### CGP04-P38 — Administrative Reconciliation

Where an attributable decision has already changed a governed relationship, CGP-04 may be mechanically reconciled under CGP-03 without creating another substantive decision.

### CGP04-P39 — No Register-by-Register Ceremony

A relationship allocation does not require a separate preparation, adoption, application or closure instrument merely because CGP-04 records it.

Any additional governance step must arise from applicable authority or material governance necessity.

### CGP04-P40 — Historical Intelligibility

Changes to the register must preserve sufficient traceability to determine previous and current material allocations where that history is necessary to understand governance authority or accountability.

---

## 12. Boundaries and Non-Effects

CGP-04 is a governance relationship instrument.

It is not an implementation authorization.

### CGP04-P41 — No Product-Role Definition

CGP-04 does not define application roles, screen permissions, UI personas or authentication roles merely because they may later implement governed relationships.

### CGP04-P42 — No Technical Permission Model

CGP-04 does not establish IAM, RBAC, Firebase Security Rules, Firestore permissions, API authorization, database ownership or equivalent technical controls.

### CGP04-P43 — No Knowledge Governance Pre-emption

CGP-04 does not determine canonical Knowledge Assets, Exercise or Wellness Activity governance, Knowledge Asset stewardship, canonical identity, taxonomy, modification rules or metrics/units governance reserved for Stage EK.

### CGP04-P44 — No Product Requirement Creation

A governance relationship represented in CGP-04 does not by itself prescribe a particular product workflow, screen, feature or technical implementation.

### CGP04-P45 — No Implementation Authority

Approval or completion of CGP-04 does not authorize software implementation.

Downstream product and technical work remains subject to the applicable Tiizi programme stages and authorities.

---

## 13. Completion Standard

CGP-04 does not require every future Tiizi relationship to be finally allocated.

Its purpose is to establish a truthful, controlled and usable relationship allocation state sufficient to complete the Stage E0 governance architecture.

### CGP04-P46 — Truthful Register Completion

CGP-04 may be complete where:

1. material relationships required for Stage E0 are represented;
2. existing allocations are faithfully recorded;
3. unresolved relationships are explicitly identified;
4. downstream matters remain appropriately deferred;
5. material dependencies remain visible;
6. no unresolved contradiction prevents safe downstream governance; and
7. the instrument has completed its required Founder review and approval.

### CGP04-P47 — Completeness Does Not Require Premature Allocation

CGP-04 must not make premature substantive allocations solely to eliminate Deferred, Provisional or Unallocated statuses.

### CGP04-P48 — Stage E0 Consequence

Upon CGP-04 satisfying its completion standard and receiving the required attributable Founder approval, the Master Programme may assess whether the Stage E0 completion gate—CGP-02, CGP-03 and CGP-04 Complete—has been satisfied.

CGP-04 itself does not commence Stage EK.
