## 3. ATQ-01 — Purpose and Meaning of an Amendment Trace

**Issue:** What is the constitutional purpose and meaning of an amendment trace?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Platform Constitution requires an amendment to identify its decision, rationale, affected documents and effective version. The Authority Model requires amendment history, attribution and reviewability. CGP-01 requires bounded, attributable and historically intelligible change. No approved source defines an amendment trace as a distinct constitutional record.

**Proposed treatment:** Define the amendment trace as the attributable record connecting source governance, bounded change and the decisions that establish its constitutional treatment. Its purpose is attribution, objective reviewability and historical intelligibility; it communicates but does not create constitutional change or status.

**Option A — Approve the complete proposed treatment:** Preserve ATR-01 through ATR-04.

**Option B — Approve a narrower definition:** Define only the record identity and defer purpose and anti-inference treatment.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no amendment-trace definition in this package.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:**

Option A consolidates the existing attribution, reviewability and historical-intelligibility requirements while preserving the distinction between a record and the constitutional acts it communicates. The narrower or deferred alternatives would leave the amendment trace’s purpose or effect ambiguous within the authorized subject.

**Consequences:** Option A closes the definition gap while preserving Authority and status boundaries. Option B leaves the record’s constitutional purpose and effect ambiguous. Option C prevents completion of the bounded subject.

**Affected propositions:** ATR-01–ATR-04.

## 4. ATQ-02 — Minimum Amendment-Trace Information

**Issue:** What minimum information must every constitutional amendment trace preserve?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Constitution, Authority Model and CGP-01 collectively require decision, rationale, affected material, Authority, approval, effective-version treatment, amendment history, source, consequences and historical intelligibility. They do not consolidate those requirements into one amendment-trace minimum.

**Proposed treatment:** Require a stable trace reference; source governance and affected meaning; prior and changed meaning where established; purpose and rationale; attributable decision, actor, Authority, date and approval reference; scope and supported consequences; relevant established dates; and matter-status distinctions needed to prevent overstatement.

**Option A — Approve the complete minimum:** Preserve ATR-05 through ATR-12.

**Option B — Approve the higher-order minimum only:** Require source, decision, rationale, scope and Authority while deferring reference, date and matter-status content.

**Option C — Defer:** Preserve distributed source requirements without a consolidated minimum.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:**

Option A brings the existing distributed amendment minima into one technology-neutral record boundary without extending their constitutional meaning. The alternatives would retain known gaps in attribution, status distinction or historical intelligibility that this bounded package is authorized to address.

**Consequences:** Option A provides a complete technology-neutral minimum. Option B reduces governance burden but leaves known attribution and status gaps. Option C leaves amendment traces inconsistent and the Blueprint gap unresolved.

**Affected propositions:** ATR-05–ATR-12.

## 5. ATQ-03 — Record and Constitutional-Status Separation

**Issue:** How must an amendment trace remain distinct from an approval record, adoption record or status representation?

**Current approved doctrine:** Governance Authority establishes approval and adoption; records communicate only what attributable decisions establish. Existing CGP-02 packages distinguish approval, adoption and constitutional effect, but no approved rule defines the amendment-trace boundary.

**Proposed treatment:** The amendment trace communicates the relationship among source governance, change and relevant decisions. It does not replace or establish approval, adoption, effect or status and remains distinct from the records that communicate those acts.

**Option A — Approve complete separation:** Preserve ATR-04, ATR-11 and ATR-18 through ATR-20.

**Option B — Approve approval-record separation only:** Distinguish the trace from approval while deferring adoption, effect and reference relationships.

**Option C — Defer:** Leave record relationships to later governance.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:**

Option A most clearly preserves the approved separation among Governance Authority decisions, approval records, adoption, constitutional effect and their representations. The narrower or deferred alternatives would leave routes by which an amendment trace could be mistaken for a status-creating constitutional act.

**Consequences:** Option A prevents a trace from becoming a substitute constitutional act. Option B leaves downstream status ambiguity. Option C permits inconsistent records to imply status.

**Affected propositions:** ATR-04, ATR-11, ATR-18–ATR-20.

## 6. ATQ-04 — Constitutional Relationship Preserved

**Issue:** What relationship among prior meaning, changed meaning, amendment scope and the attributable decision must remain intelligible?

**Current approved doctrine:** CGP-01 requires later readers to understand applicable meaning, validity basis and amendment effect. The Constitution and Authority Model require affected material, rationale, decision trace and amendment history.

**Proposed treatment:** Preserve the prior governing position, changed meaning where established, bounded scope, purpose, attributable decision and expressly supported consequences as one intelligible relationship without converting proposals into established meaning.

**Option A — Approve the complete relationship:** Preserve ATR-02, ATR-03, ATR-06 through ATR-10 and ATR-22.

**Option B — Preserve source and outcome only:** Defer explicit treatment of prior meaning and decision relationship.

**Option C — Defer:** Rely on general historical-intelligibility principles.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:**

Option A directly applies the existing requirement that constitutional change remain bounded, attributable and historically intelligible. The alternatives would preserve less of the relationship needed to understand what changed, why it changed and which decision supports that change.

**Consequences:** Option A makes constitutional change reviewable across time. Option B can obscure how and why meaning changed. Option C leaves the principal traceability purpose unconsolidated.

**Affected propositions:** ATR-02–ATR-03, ATR-06–ATR-10, ATR-22.

## 7. ATQ-05 — Treatment of Matter Statuses

**Issue:** How should affected, unaffected, pending, deferred, rejected and withdrawn matters be represented without changing their status?

**Current approved doctrine:** Authority and constitutional status cannot arise by implication. CGP-01 requires explicit uncertainty and historical intelligibility. No approved source supplies a consolidated amendment-trace treatment for all six categories.

**Proposed treatment:** Distinguish each category only where needed to prevent overstatement. Preserve its source status; provide context without reopening, approving or changing it; and leave uncertainty explicit where the source does not establish a category.

**Option A — Approve all six category boundaries:** Preserve ATR-12 through ATR-17.

**Option B — Approve affected, pending and deferred treatment only:** Defer unaffected, rejected and withdrawn treatment.

**Option C — Defer:** Require no category treatment in this package.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:**

Option A preserves each matter’s source status and makes uncertainty explicit, consistent with the existing prohibitions on inferred Authority and silent constitutional change. The alternatives would leave some statuses vulnerable to omission, overstatement or ambiguous historical treatment.

**Consequences:** Option A prevents silent status changes and scope expansion. Option B leaves gaps in historical and negative-decision treatment. Option C makes amendment scope harder to review objectively.

**Affected propositions:** ATR-12–ATR-17.

## 8. ATQ-06 — Amendment-Trace Reference

**Issue:** Is a stable unique amendment-trace reference constitutionally required, and how should it relate to an approval reference?

**Current approved doctrine:** Authority actions and amendments must remain attributable and reviewable. CGP-02C.3 proposes a stable approval-reference treatment but is not adopted or constitutionally effective. No approved source establishes an amendment-trace reference convention.

**Proposed treatment:** Require one stable unique amendment-trace reference, distinguish it from any approval reference, and allow their relationship to be recorded without defining technical format or giving either reference Authority or constitutional status.

**Option A — Require a stable unique trace reference:** Preserve ATR-05, ATR-09 and ATR-20.

**Option B — Require stable attribution without a distinct reference:** Defer the unique-reference requirement.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no reference treatment.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:**

Option A supports durable attribution and objective review while remaining neutral about technical format, generation and storage. The alternatives would leave amendment-trace identity less stable or preserve the existing reference-convention gap.

**Consequences:** Option A supports durable attribution without technical prescription. Option B preserves conceptual reviewability but may permit ambiguous record identity. Option C leaves the identified convention gap open.

**Affected propositions:** ATR-05, ATR-09, ATR-20.

## 9. ATQ-07 — Constitutional Dates

**Issue:** Which approval, adoption, effective or application dates, if any, belong in an amendment trace without being created by the trace?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Constitution requires effective-version treatment. CGP-02C.4 defines the four date concepts in a Founder Approved but not adopted instrument. No adopted rule requires every date in every amendment trace.

**Proposed treatment:** Record only dates relevant to the amendment and separately established by attributable Governance Authority. The trace cannot create, supply or reconcile a missing or ambiguous date.

**Option A — Approve relevance-based date treatment:** Preserve ATR-09, ATR-11 and ATR-19.

**Option B — Require decision date and effective date only:** Defer approval, adoption and application-date treatment.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no amendment-trace date rule beyond the existing Constitution.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:**

Option A records only constitutionally relevant dates already established by attributable Governance Authority and therefore preserves both date integrity and the anti-inference boundary. The alternatives would omit potentially relevant approved statuses or leave the known date relationship unresolved.

**Consequences:** Option A preserves date integrity without requiring irrelevant dates. Option B is narrower but may obscure separately established approval or adoption. Option C leaves known terminology inconsistency unresolved.

**Affected propositions:** ATR-09, ATR-11, ATR-19.

## 10. ATQ-08 — Anti-Inference Boundary

**Issue:** What minimum boundary prevents a trace, label, record or repository action from creating or enlarging an amendment by implication?

**Current approved doctrine:** Authority must be declared, bounded and attributable. Operational practice, repository treatment, custom and repeated use cannot silently amend constitutional governance.

**Proposed treatment:** Limit the trace to attributable source support; preserve uncertainty; distinguish all status records; and prohibit titles, labels, repositories, publication, implementation, administration or repeated use from creating or changing an amendment.

**Option A — Approve the complete anti-inference boundary:** Preserve ATR-04, ATR-10, ATR-13 through ATR-21 and ATR-24.

**Option B — Approve a narrower representation boundary:** Prohibit label and repository inference but defer status-category and historical treatment.

**Option C — Defer:** Rely only on general Authority rules.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:**

Option A applies the existing declared-Authority and historical-integrity principles to the specific representations used by an amendment trace. The narrower or deferred alternatives would leave identified paths through which scope or constitutional status could be inferred beyond the attributable source.

**Consequences:** Option A closes the principal paths to silent amendment. Option B leaves scope and historical inference risks. Option C leaves trace-specific ambiguity unresolved.

**Affected propositions:** ATR-04, ATR-10, ATR-13–ATR-21, ATR-24.

## 11. ATQ-09 — Deferred-Governance Boundary

**Issue:** Which matters must remain deferred to later CGP-02 subjects?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Founder Authorization Record approves every planning exclusion unchanged. The next Blueprint subjects remain separate and no successor package is authorized.

**Proposed treatment:** Preserve ATR-23, ATR-24 and draft section 6. Exclude dependent-governance impact review, cross-reference review, conflict governance, supersession, retirement, preservation mechanisms, validation, index synchronization, procedure, allocation, technical design and implementation.

**Option A — Confirm the complete deferred boundary:** Preserve ATR-23, ATR-24 and draft section 6.

**Option B — Amend the boundary:** Return any proposed scope change to programme planning and Founder authorization before drafting.

**Option C — Defer package completion:** Do not progress until the Founder supplies a different boundary.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:**

Option A preserves the Founder-authorized one-subject boundary and the frozen Blueprint’s separation of later governance subjects. The alternatives either require renewed authorization before changing scope or leave this bounded package unable to progress.

**Consequences:** Option A preserves one subject and the frozen Blueprint sequence. Option B requires reauthorization before any added subject can be drafted. Option C pauses the package without changing governance.

**Affected propositions and location:** ATR-23–ATR-24; draft section 6.
