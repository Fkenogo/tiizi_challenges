## 3. DIQ-01 — Purpose and Meaning

**Issue:** What is the constitutional purpose and meaning of dependent-governance impact review?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Platform Constitution requires conformity, affected-document identification and objective review. The Authority Model and CGP-01 require bounded, attributable and historically intelligible change. No approved source defines dependent-governance impact review as a distinct constitutional examination.

**Proposed treatment:** Define it as a bounded analytical examination of constitutional dependencies and cross-references, intended to expose foreseeable consequences without changing governance.

**Option A — Approve the complete treatment:** Preserve DGI-01 through DGI-05.

**Option B — Approve a narrower definition:** Define only dependency examination and defer purpose and non-change boundaries.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no impact-review definition in this package.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A consolidates existing conformity, reviewability and bounded-change requirements while preserving the distinction between review and constitutional action. The alternatives would leave the review’s purpose or constitutional effect ambiguous.

**Consequences:** Option A closes the definition gap without creating Authority. Option B leaves analytical and constitutional effects insufficiently separated. Option C prevents completion of the bounded subject.

**Affected propositions:** DGI-01–DGI-05.

## 4. DIQ-02 — Constitutional Review Point

**Issue:** At what constitutional point must dependent-governance impact be considered without defining workflow?

**Current approved doctrine:** Constitutional amendments must identify affected documents and remain objectively reviewable. Governance Authority approves amendments, but no approved rule states when impact evidence must be available.

**Proposed treatment:** Require sufficient impact examination before amendment approval so known affected governance and unresolved material uncertainty are visible to Governance Authority, without directing the approval outcome.

**Option A — Require consideration before approval:** Preserve DGI-02, DGI-06 and DGI-08.

**Option B — Require consideration before constitutional effect:** Allow approval before impact review is complete.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no review point.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A gives practical meaning to the existing affected-document and objective-review requirements at the point when Governance Authority decides the amendment. It does so without prescribing workflow or transferring the approval decision to the review.

**Consequences:** Option A makes foreseeable impact available for attributable approval. Option B permits approval with incomplete impact evidence. Option C leaves the timing gap unresolved.

**Affected propositions:** DGI-02, DGI-06, DGI-08.

## 5. DIQ-03 — Dependency and Review Boundary

**Issue:** Which forms of dependency or cross-reference place governance within the review boundary?

**Current approved doctrine:** Lower-order governance must conform to higher-order constitutional meaning, and change must remain bounded to declared scope. Documentary proximity and repeated practice do not establish Authority or constitutional meaning.

**Proposed treatment:** Include governance whose rule, definition, scope, Authority boundary, obligation or other constitutional treatment relies on the meaning proposed to change. Treat cross-references as evidence, not automatic proof, and derive scope from the amendment and actual constitutional dependencies.

**Option A — Approve the constitutional-reliance test:** Preserve DGI-03, DGI-04 and DGI-07.

**Option B — Use express cross-references only:** Exclude dependencies not expressed through a direct reference.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no dependency test.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A follows constitutional reliance rather than document form and therefore preserves both conformity and bounded scope. Option B may miss genuine dependencies, while Option C leaves the review boundary indeterminate.

**Consequences:** Option A captures actual constitutional dependencies without treating similarity as impact. Option B is easier to identify but constitutionally incomplete. Option C prevents consistent impact review.

**Affected propositions:** DGI-03–DGI-04, DGI-07.

## 6. DIQ-04 — Impact Categories

**Issue:** How should directly affected, potentially affected, unaffected and unresolved impacts remain distinguishable?

**Current approved doctrine:** Constitutional uncertainty must remain explicit and review findings do not independently change governance. No approved source supplies a canonical impact-category set.

**Proposed treatment:** Define four evidence-bounded review findings and state expressly that categorization changes no meaning, status, Authority or constitutional effect.

**Option A — Approve all four categories:** Preserve DGI-09 through DGI-13 and DGI-23.

**Option B — Use affected and unresolved only:** Defer potential and unaffected findings.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no category vocabulary.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A provides enough distinction to communicate certainty and uncertainty without turning categories into governance status. The alternatives would reduce clarity or leave impact findings inconsistent.

**Consequences:** Option A supports objective review and anti-inference. Option B obscures plausible and examined-no-impact cases. Option C leaves the gap unresolved.

**Affected propositions:** DGI-09–DGI-13, DGI-23.

## 7. DIQ-05 — Minimum Impact-Review Information

**Issue:** What minimum information must a dependent-governance impact-review record preserve?

**Current approved doctrine:** Amendments and Authority actions must preserve scope, affected governance, rationale, basis, attribution and relevant time. No approved source consolidates an impact-review record minimum.

**Proposed treatment:** Identify the amendment and scope, reviewed governance and dependency basis, category and rationale, supporting evidence, known consequences, unresolved or deferred matters, review date, attributable source and limitations.

**Option A — Approve the complete minimum:** Preserve DGI-14 through DGI-18.

**Option B — Approve a reduced minimum:** Require amendment, dependent instrument and impact category only.

**Option C — Defer:** Preserve distributed source requirements without a consolidated minimum.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A consolidates existing attribution and reviewability minima while remaining technology-neutral and allocation-neutral. The reduced or deferred alternatives would leave the basis and limitations of findings difficult to review.

**Consequences:** Option A supplies a complete bounded evidence minimum. Option B reduces burden but weakens rationale and uncertainty evidence. Option C permits inconsistent records.

**Affected propositions:** DGI-14–DGI-18.

## 8. DIQ-06 — No Automatic Governance Change

**Issue:** What boundary prevents an impact finding from amending, interpreting, correcting or superseding dependent governance?

**Current approved doctrine:** Review does not approve or amend governance, Authority cannot arise by implication and operational or documentary actions cannot create constitutional meaning.

**Proposed treatment:** Make every impact finding analytical, evidence-bounded and non-constitutive; prohibit it from changing governance or requiring an amendment by itself; and preserve missing or historical impact without inference.

**Option A — Approve the complete non-change boundary:** Preserve DGI-05, DGI-13, DGI-20 through DGI-24.

**Option B — Approve only the no-amendment rule:** Defer status, cross-reference and historical boundaries.

**Option C — Defer:** Rely only on general review principles.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A applies existing Authority discipline to each trace-specific route by which a finding could be mistaken for governance change. The narrower or deferred alternatives leave known status and historical inference risks.

**Consequences:** Option A prevents silent governance change. Option B leaves adjacent inference paths open. Option C leaves the subject-specific boundary uncertain.

**Affected propositions:** DGI-05, DGI-13, DGI-20–DGI-24.

## 9. DIQ-07 — Record Relationships

**Issue:** How should impact review relate to an amendment trace and approval record without merging their constitutional meanings?

**Current approved doctrine:** Amendment traces, approval records and Governance Authority decisions have distinct purposes in completed CGP-02 packages. None is adopted or constitutionally effective merely because it is referenced here.

**Proposed treatment:** Keep the impact-review record distinct: it identifies dependent consequences, while the amendment trace records amendment treatment and the approval record communicates approval.

**Option A — Approve complete record separation:** Preserve DGI-14, DGI-19 and DGI-20.

**Option B — Merge impact review into the amendment trace:** Require no distinct constitutional record meaning.

**Option C — Defer:** Establish no record relationship.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A preserves the existing conceptual boundaries while allowing records to reference one another. Merging or deferring would risk treating impact evidence as amendment or approval evidence without a clear distinction.

**Consequences:** Option A preserves attribution and prevents substitution. Option B reduces record separation but conflates purposes. Option C leaves future evidence architecture ambiguous.

**Affected propositions:** DGI-14, DGI-19–DGI-20.

## 10. DIQ-08 — Cross-Reference Impact Boundary

**Issue:** How should cross-reference impact be identified without prescribing or performing synchronization?

**Current approved doctrine:** Cross-reference reports are observed practice rather than universal doctrine. Constitutional and operational governance remain separate, and this package excludes index and synchronization rules.

**Proposed treatment:** Treat cross-references as possible dependency evidence, record their impact basis where relevant and prohibit the review record from updating, redirecting, replacing or validating them.

**Option A — Approve identification without synchronization:** Preserve DGI-04, DGI-15 and DGI-21.

**Option B — Exclude cross-references from this package:** Defer all cross-reference impact to Governance Index and Status Integrity.

**Option C — Expand to synchronization:** Requires new planning and Founder authorization before drafting.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A addresses the Blueprint’s cross-reference impact question while preserving the explicit synchronization exclusion. Option B leaves part of the authorized subject untreated, and Option C exceeds the package boundary.

**Consequences:** Option A identifies impact without operational prescription. Option B leaves an evidential gap. Option C cannot proceed under this authorization.

**Affected propositions:** DGI-04, DGI-15, DGI-21.

## 11. DIQ-09 — Deferred-Governance Boundary

**Issue:** Which matters must remain deferred to later CGP-02 subjects?

**Current approved doctrine:** The Founder authorization approves the planning exclusions unchanged. Conflict Review and Escalation follows this subject in the frozen Blueprint.

**Proposed treatment:** Preserve DGI-08, DGI-12, DGI-21, DGI-24 and draft section 7, excluding conflict resolution, supersession, preservation, index synchronization, validation, procedure, allocation and implementation.

**Option A — Confirm the complete deferred boundary:** Preserve the proposed treatment and section 7.

**Option B — Amend the boundary:** Return any expansion to planning and Founder authorization.

**Option C — Defer package completion:** Do not progress until a different boundary is supplied.

**Drafting recommendation — pending Founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason for recommendation:** Option A preserves one constitutional subject and the frozen Blueprint sequence. Any expansion would require separate authorization, while deferral would leave the authorized subject incomplete.

**Consequences:** Option A preserves scope and later dependencies. Option B requires reauthorization. Option C pauses the package without changing governance.

**Affected propositions and location:** DGI-08, DGI-12, DGI-21, DGI-24; draft section 7.
