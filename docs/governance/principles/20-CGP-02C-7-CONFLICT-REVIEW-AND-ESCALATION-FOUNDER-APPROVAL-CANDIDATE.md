## 1. Constitutional Meaning and Purpose

**CRE-01.** A governance conflict exists where two or more identified governance treatments cannot be applied within the same constitutional scope without contradiction, silent displacement or an unresolved incompatibility in constitutional meaning, precedence or Authority.

**CRE-02.** The purpose of conflict review is to make the asserted incompatibility, its constitutional basis, its scope and its unresolved consequences objectively reviewable before any attributable constitutional disposition.

**CRE-03.** A governance conflict must arise from identified governance and constitutional treatment. Difference in wording, emphasis, document location, authorship, practice or preference does not by itself establish a governance conflict.

**CRE-04.** Conflict identification and conflict review are analytical. They do not themselves interpret, amend, approve, adopt, supersede, retire, reject or make governance constitutionally effective.

**CRE-05.** Conflict disposition requires an explicit and attributable act within existing Governance Authority. Identifying, reviewing, escalating, recording or presenting a conflict does not confer Governance Authority or create a separate Conflict Authority.

## 2. Conflict Identification

**CRE-06.** Conflict identification must identify the governance instruments or bounded provisions concerned, their recorded constitutional status, the treatment asserted to be incompatible and the constitutional scope in which the incompatibility is asserted.

**CRE-07.** The identification must state the governing basis and evidence for the asserted conflict, including any relevant precedence, Authority, purpose, scope or constitutional-meaning relationship.

**CRE-08.** Where the available governance or evidence does not establish whether an incompatibility is a conflict, ambiguity, omission or unresolved constitutional question, that uncertainty must remain explicit. It must not be cured through inference.

**CRE-09.** Identifying a governance conflict does not suspend, displace, invalidate, narrow or expand any governance instrument or provision.

## 3. Conflict Review and Escalation

**CRE-10.** Conflict review must examine the identified governance, recorded status, applicable precedence, Authority boundaries, declared purpose, constitutional scope and asserted incompatible meaning without presuming a disposition.

**CRE-11.** Where approved governance already supplies one unambiguous conforming treatment within the reviewer’s interpretive boundary, the review may record that treatment and its basis. It must not present interpretation as a new constitutional disposition.

**CRE-12.** Where the conflict cannot be addressed without choosing among genuinely incompatible constitutional treatments, changing constitutional meaning or resolving an Authority conflict, the unresolved matter must be escalated for attributable Governance Authority decision.

**CRE-13.** Escalation communicates an unresolved constitutional matter to Governance Authority. It does not direct the decision, approve an outcome, delegate Governance Authority or allocate who must initiate, receive, record or maintain the escalation.

## 4. Conflict-Review and Disposition Evidence

**CRE-14.** A conflict-review record must identify the asserted conflict, the governance and bounded provisions reviewed, their recorded status, the review scope and the applicable constitutional basis.

**CRE-15.** The record must preserve the evidence considered, the reasoning applied, the findings reached, unresolved uncertainty and any limitation necessary to prevent the review from overstating its scope or conclusion.

**CRE-16.** The record must preserve its review date and attributable review source. Attribution makes the review objectively examinable and does not allocate a reviewer, recorder or Governance Authority.

**CRE-17.** Where Governance Authority establishes a conflict disposition, the disposition evidence must identify the attributable decision, Governance Authority exercised, decision date, bounded conflict scope, constitutional treatment established and stated rationale.

**CRE-18.** Conflict-disposition evidence may reference an approval record, amendment trace, adoption record or other separately established constitutional record. It does not replace those records, create a missing status or enlarge the disposition beyond the attributable decision.

## 5. Unresolved Conflict Treatment

**CRE-19.** An unresolved governance conflict must remain expressly identified as unresolved. Its existence does not authorize an inferred precedence outcome, compromise meaning or temporary constitutional rule.

**CRE-20.** Unresolved-conflict evidence must preserve the competing treatments, the reason resolution remains unavailable and any known constitutional consequence or uncertainty within the review scope.

**CRE-21.** Carrying an unresolved conflict forward does not amend affected governance, decide whether another constitutional action proceeds or establish supersession, retirement, withdrawal, rejection, adoption or constitutional effect.

## 6. Integrity and Anti-Inference

**CRE-22.** Conflict identification, review, escalation, disposition recording, labels, status representations, administrative action, repository action, implementation or repeated practice cannot change constitutional governance except to the extent already established by an explicit attributable act of Governance Authority.

**CRE-23.** Absence of an identified conflict does not establish constitutional consistency where relevant governance was not reviewed, the review scope was incomplete or the governing basis remained uncertain.

**CRE-24.** This proposed treatment does not retrospectively classify a historical tension as a governance conflict, validate a prior conflict outcome or assign constitutional disposition to a record that did not expressly establish it.

## 7. Deferred Governance and Explicit Non-Effects

This Founder Approval Candidate does not:

- identify, review or resolve an actual, named or historical governance conflict;
- determine a precedence outcome;
- amend the approved precedence hierarchy;
- define supersession;
- define retirement, withdrawal or rejection;
- reopen amendment classification, review proportionality, Approval Governance, Adoption and Constitutional Effect, Amendment Traceability Requirements or Dependent-Governance Impact Review;
- approve an amendment or establish adoption, constitutional effect, an effective date or an application date;
- define validation doctrine or procedure;
- define governance-index, status or cross-reference synchronization;
- define implementation workflow or repository process;
- allocate who identifies, reviews, escalates, receives, decides, records, maintains or validates a conflict;
- create or allocate a Conflict Authority, Platform Authority, accountability relationship, responsibility, role or permission;
- define technical schema, storage, retention, identifier format, automation, publication or deployment;
- retrospectively classify or dispose of historical governance; or
- authorize a successor work package.

CRE-01 through CRE-24 were approved without amendment for inclusion in this Founder Approval Candidate through the attributable Founder Decision Record dated 2026-07-24. This candidate does not adopt, apply or make itself, CGP-02 or any Conflict Review and Escalation requirement constitutionally effective.
