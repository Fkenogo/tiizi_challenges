# FIT-PSA-001 --- Box Jump

**Working package:** Tiizi V2 Activity Content & Catalogue Definition\
**Batch:** CLU-01 --- Core Launch Utility\
**Document type:** Canonical Activity exemplar\
**Status:** Founder/Product working baseline\
**Activity Code:** `FIT-PSA-001`\
**Domain:** Fitness\
**Category:** Power, Speed & Agility\
**Classification:** Plyometric\
**Baseline-supported measurement:** Repetitions → reps

## 1. Canonical Meaning

### Canonical title

**Box Jump**

### Description

A plyometric exercise in which the participant jumps from a starting
surface onto a stable raised platform and establishes a controlled
landing on that platform.

The raised landing platform is part of the canonical undertaking. The
Activity does not prescribe one universal platform height, target
repetition count, pace or scoring method.

## 2. Equipment Is Part of Meaning Without Becoming Identity

Box Jump requires a suitable raised landing platform.

That equipment requirement belongs to canonical Activity meaning because
removing the raised landing surface would materially change the
undertaking.

However, platform specifications do not automatically create separate
canonical Activities.

Tiizi should not create identities such as: - 30 cm Box Jump; - 45 cm
Box Jump; - 60 cm Box Jump;

merely because platform height differs.

The stable identity remains:

`FIT-PSA-001 — Box Jump`

## 3. Platform Height Is Not a Hidden Metric

The authoritative baseline supports:

`Box Jump → Repetitions → reps`

It does not currently establish platform height as a Tiizi core Metric.

Therefore Tiizi must not silently treat a higher platform as: - more
repetitions; - a higher score; - a competitive ranking dimension; - a
new measurement family.

If Challenge Definition later needs governed equipment parameters such
as platform height, that should be explicitly designed rather than
smuggled into Repetitions.

## 4. Repetitions Measurement

**Metric:** Repetitions\
**Unit:** reps

### Reporting meaning

The participant reports the number of complete Box Jump repetitions
performed.

### One repetition

For ordinary self-reporting, one repetition consists of: 1. beginning
from the applicable starting surface; 2. jumping onto the raised
platform; 3. establishing the applicable controlled landing on the
platform; 4. completing the repetition before beginning the next.

### Participant instruction

> Enter the number of Box Jump repetitions you completed.

## 5. Return to Starting Surface

Canonical Activity Knowledge should not invent a universal rule that the
participant must jump back down.

Stepping down or another controlled return may be used according to the
performed expression.

The return method is not itself a counted Box Jump repetition unless
separately governed.

## 6. Equipment / Environment Contract

A Box Jump occurrence requires: - a stable raised platform suitable for
the performed jump; - sufficient surrounding space; - a starting and
landing area that permit controlled execution.

The catalogue does not need to prescribe a brand, material or universal
platform dimension.

The important canonical truth is the presence of a stable raised landing
platform.

## 7. Setup

1.  Place or identify a stable raised platform.
2.  Ensure the landing surface and surrounding area are suitable for the
    Activity.
3.  Establish a starting position from which the jump can be performed
    under reasonable control.
4.  Begin the repetition sequence.

## 8. Execution

1.  initiate the jump from the starting surface;
2.  move onto the raised platform;
3.  establish a controlled landing;
4.  complete the repetition;
5.  return to the starting surface under control;
6.  repeat as applicable.

The Activity does not prescribe target repetitions, speed, rest,
platform height or competition rules.

## 9. Guidance

Useful ordinary cues include: - use a stable platform; - select an
execution that can be performed under reasonable control; - maintain
awareness of the landing area; - prioritize a controlled landing rather
than an uncontrolled attempt merely to complete a repetition.

These cues describe ordinary execution, not a Tiizi judging standard.

## 10. Variants and Related Activities

Differences in platform height, ordinary stance or equipment
construction do not automatically create separate canonical identities.

A materially different jumping undertaking may remain or become a
separate canonical Activity. The authoritative catalogue already treats
other jump Activities---such as Broad Jump, Squat Jump, Tuck Jump and
Lateral Jump---as separate identities because the undertaking differs.

## 11. Safety Boundary

Box Jump has a meaningful ordinary equipment/environment safety
dependency.

Use a stable platform and adequate surrounding space. Avoid attempting a
jump onto an unstable or unsuitable landing surface. Stop if the
Activity causes sharp pain or significant discomfort.

Tiizi should not invent: - universally safe platform heights; - age
thresholds; - medical eligibility rules; - individualized plyometric
prescriptions.

If future Challenge configuration exposes platform height or difficulty,
PF-03 should govern whether constraints are required.

## 12. Verification Boundary

Canonical Box Jump Knowledge defines: - the undertaking; - required
equipment/environment meaning; - what one repetition represents; -
ordinary execution and safety guidance.

It does not establish that Tiizi independently verified: - platform
height; - landing quality; - repetition count; - technique; - equipment
suitability.

Verification remains separately governed.

## 15. CLU-01 Finding

Box Jump establishes an important equipment rule:

> **Equipment can be essential to canonical Activity meaning without
> equipment specifications becoming separate Activity identities or
> hidden Metrics.**

This means Tiizi can state that Box Jump requires a raised landing
platform while leaving platform height and other configurable difficulty
parameters outside the Activity's current measurement contract.

PF-03 should decide later whether any equipment parameter needs governed
Challenge configuration. The catalogue should not invent it in advance.
