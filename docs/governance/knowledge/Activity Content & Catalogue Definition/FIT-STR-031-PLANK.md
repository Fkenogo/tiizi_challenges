# FIT-STR-031 --- Plank

**Working package:** Tiizi V2 Activity Content & Catalogue Definition\
**Document type:** Representative canonical Activity exemplar\
**Status:** Founder/Product working baseline\
**Activity Code:** `FIT-STR-031`\
**Domain:** Fitness\
**Category:** Strength\
**Classification:** Core / Trunk\
**Primary measurement:** Duration → seconds/minutes

## 1. Canonical Meaning

### Canonical title

**Plank**

### Description

A static bodyweight exercise in which the participant supports the body
in a controlled plank position and holds that position for a period of
time.

### Purpose / context

Plank gives Tiizi a canonical timed strength Activity based on
maintaining a supported body position rather than completing repeated
movement cycles.

The Activity defines the hold and its valid measurement. It does not
prescribe how long a participant should hold the position.

### Semantic distinction

Plank is a Duration-based static hold.

It is distinct from: - Push-Up, which uses repeated lowering-and-press
cycles; - Push-Up Hold, which has its own canonical identity in the
baseline; - Side Plank, which is separately represented because the
undertaking and body position materially differ.

## 2. Measurement Contract

### Duration → seconds/minutes

**Metric:** Duration\
**Valid Units:** seconds, minutes

**What the reported value means:**\
The elapsed time for which the participant reports maintaining the Plank
hold.

**Participant reporting instruction:**\
\> Enter how long you held the Plank.

Compatible units may be normalized deterministically:

`1 minute = 60 seconds`

The Challenge retains its configured participant-facing unit.

## 3. Timing Semantics

A static hold requires clearer start/end semantics than an ordinary
locomotion Activity.

### Timing begins

Timing begins when the participant has established the supported Plank
position and intentionally starts the hold.

### Timing continues

Timing continues while the participant maintains the Plank hold.

### Timing ends

Timing ends when the participant intentionally finishes the hold or can
no longer reasonably maintain the Plank position and stops the hold.

The participant reports the elapsed hold time.

These semantics explain what Duration refers to. They do not create a
Tiizi technique-verification system.

## 4. Setup

1.  Use a stable floor surface with sufficient clear space.
2.  Support the upper body on the forearms for the standard Plank
    expression.
3.  Extend the legs behind the body and support the lower body on the
    toes.
4.  Establish a controlled position with the trunk and hips supported
    rather than deliberately collapsed toward the floor.

The canonical Activity does not require specialist exercise equipment.

## 5. Execution

1.  Establish the supported Plank position.
2.  Maintain the position under control for the intended hold.
3.  Continue breathing normally rather than deliberately holding the
    breath.
4.  End the hold when the participant chooses to stop or can no longer
    reasonably maintain the position.

The Challenge may establish a required Duration. The canonical Activity
does not.

## 6. Form Guidance

Useful participant-facing cues include:

-   keep the body supported and controlled;
-   avoid deliberately allowing the hips to collapse toward the floor;
-   avoid raising the hips excessively away from the intended Plank
    position;
-   keep the forearms and lower-body support stable;
-   continue breathing during the hold.

These cues communicate the expected Activity. They are not a scoring or
verification rubric.

## 7. Common Mistakes

Common execution issues include:

-   allowing the hips to sag substantially;
-   raising the hips excessively;
-   losing stable upper-body support;
-   deliberately holding the breath through the exercise.

Tiizi does not claim to detect these issues during ordinary
self-reported participation.

## 8. Equipment and Environment

### Required equipment

No specialist exercise equipment is required.

### Environment

Use a stable surface with sufficient space to establish and exit the
position safely.

A mat may be used for comfort but is not part of canonical Plank
identity.

## 9. Adaptations and Variants

A participant may use an easier supported expression where appropriate,
provided the essential timed static-hold meaning remains recognizable.

However, Tiizi should not automatically collapse every plank-like
exercise into this Activity.

The baseline already treats **Side Plank** as a separate canonical
Activity. Other materially different support positions or movement-based
exercises should be assessed under the canonical-versus-variant rule.

The representative review should later determine which common Plank
adaptations need selectable variant metadata versus ordinary guidance.

## 10. Safety and Practical Caution

Use a stable surface and enter and exit the hold under control.

Stop the Activity if it causes sharp pain or significant discomfort.

Choose an appropriate easier expression where needed rather than forcing
a hold that cannot be maintained under reasonable control.

This is ordinary participation guidance, not injury diagnosis,
rehabilitation advice or individualized medical instruction.

## 11. Verification Boundary

Canonical Plank Knowledge defines: - the intended static hold; - when
reported Duration conceptually begins and ends; - valid Duration
units; - ordinary technique and safety guidance.

It does not establish that Tiizi independently verified: - body
alignment; - exact hold quality; - exact start/end time; - reported
Duration.

Any future verified Plank Challenge would require separately governed
evidence and Verification Authority.

## 12. Activity / Challenge Separation Check

The exemplar contains no canonical: - target hold time; - number of
holds; - rest interval; - frequency; - schedule; - Challenge duration; -
Challenge type; - streak rule; - scoring; - ranking; - recognition; -
reward.

Those remain downstream concerns.

## 13. KCS / Content Coverage

Plank exercises: - universal canonical meaning; - quantitative Duration
measurement; - static-hold timing semantics; - technique-bearing
physical guidance; - equipment/environment guidance; - adaptations; -
proportionate physical safety content.

It demonstrates that a Duration Activity may require explicit start/end
semantics even when its measurement unit is straightforward.
