# Tiizi V2 Activity Safety & Guidance Boundary

**Working package:** Tiizi V2 Activity Content & Catalogue Definition\
**Section:** 05\
**Status:** Founder/Product working baseline\
**Purpose:** Establish a proportionate cross-catalogue boundary for
practical guidance, safety content, health claims and publication
decisions across canonical Fitness and Wellness Activities.

## 1. Governing Principle

Tiizi may explain how an ordinary Fitness or Wellness Activity is
performed or practised and may provide proportionate practical cautions
needed to use that guidance responsibly.

Tiizi canonical Activity content is **not** a diagnosis, treatment plan,
rehabilitation programme, medical prescription or individualized
clinical recommendation.

Safety content should make the catalogue more useful and responsible
without turning Tiizi into a clinical authority.

## 2. Safety Is Part of Activity Meaning Where Needed

Safety should not be treated as a generic disclaimer pasted onto every
Activity.

For each Activity, Tiizi should determine whether a participant needs
practical information about: - safe setup; - environment; - equipment; -
ordinary discomfort or stop conditions; - a supported caution specific
to the practice; - an obvious circumstance in which the practice should
be avoided.

If no Activity-specific safety content is materially required, Tiizi
should not invent warnings simply to populate a field.

Where safety content is required by the applicable Knowledge Content
Standard (KCS) class, publication readiness must include that content.

## 3. Three Guidance Levels

### 3.1 Ordinary Practice Guidance --- Allowed

This is normal instructional content needed to understand or perform an
Activity.

Examples: - start in a stable position; - keep the movement
controlled; - practise in a quiet, comfortable setting; - use the
required equipment as intended; - follow the defined sequence; - report
the number of repetitions or duration completed.

This content explains the Activity and is part of ordinary canonical
Knowledge.

### 3.2 Proportionate Safety Caution --- Allowed Where Supported

This is practical caution reasonably connected to the Activity.

Examples: - use a stable surface; - stop if you feel sharp pain; - stop
and return to normal breathing if you feel dizzy or uncomfortable; - do
not perform an Activity in an unsafe environment; - use an appropriate
easier adaptation where one is part of the canonical guidance.

Cautions should be concise, relevant and understandable.

### 3.3 Clinical / Individualized Guidance --- Outside Ordinary Canonical Activity Content

Examples include: - diagnosing a condition; - prescribing treatment; -
directing medication use; - prescribing rehabilitation for an injury; -
asserting that a practice cures, treats or prevents a disease; -
providing individualized medical thresholds; - claiming a universal
physiological outcome that the canonical product has not governed and
supported.

If an Activity cannot be responsibly represented without this kind of
guidance, it should not simply be published through the ordinary
catalogue process.

## 4. Claims Boundary

Canonical Activity content should primarily describe:

> **what the participant does**

rather than promise:

> **what medical or psychological outcome Tiizi says will happen**

A practical purpose or ordinary context may be stated when appropriate,
but the wording must not depend on unsupported therapeutic claims.

For example:

Appropriate: \> A slow guided breathing practice using a repeated
inhale-and-exhale pattern.

Not appropriate as ordinary canonical content: \> Treats anxiety.

Likewise, Fitness content may describe strength, mobility, endurance or
movement context without making unsupported disease-prevention or
treatment claims.

## 5. Verification Boundary

Safety and technique guidance do not establish Verification Authority.

Tiizi may explain expected performance and what the participant should
report. Unless a separately governed verification capability applies,
Tiizi does not claim that it independently observed, assessed or
certified the participant's technique, quantity, duration, completion or
physical condition.

This preserves the distinction between: - guidance; - self-reporting; -
governed submission acceptance; - verification.

## 6. Pain, Discomfort and Stop Guidance

Canonical content may use ordinary, proportionate stop guidance where
relevant.

Examples: - stop if you feel sharp pain; - stop if a practice causes
significant discomfort; - return to normal breathing if dizziness
occurs.

Such wording is a practical safety boundary, not diagnosis.

Tiizi should avoid trying to classify symptoms, determine injury
severity or instruct the participant how to medically manage a
condition.

## 7. Adaptations and Safety

An adaptation may help make an Activity more accessible, but it must
remain within the canonical Activity's identity.

Safety content should not use "adaptation" as a way to prescribe
individualized rehabilitation.

Where a proposed adaptation materially changes: - the
movement/practice; - measurement semantics; - equipment requirements; -
safety profile; or - canonical meaning,

the catalogue should consider whether it is a separate Activity rather
than treating it as an adaptation.

## 8. Equipment and Environment

Where equipment or environment materially affects safe ordinary
performance, canonical content may state the relevant requirement.

Examples: - stable floor surface; - sufficient clear space; - required
ordinary equipment; - safe outdoor/indoor environment appropriate to the
Activity.

Tiizi should not attempt to certify third-party equipment or
environments merely by listing them.

## 9. Wellness Health-Adjacent Content

Wellness Activities require particular care where they touch hydration,
nutrition, fasting, sleep, mental wellbeing or other health-adjacent
areas.

Canonical content may define a concrete, reportable practice.

It should not automatically define a universal personal target or
clinical threshold.

For example, a hydration Activity may define: - what is being
reported; - a governed volume unit; - how the participant records the
quantity.

The canonical Activity should not invent one universal medically correct
daily intake for every participant.

Likewise, practices involving fasting or other potentially
higher-caution behavior must satisfy the applicable KCS safety
requirements before publication and should not be made Challenge
Eligible merely because a skeletal catalogue entry exists.

## 10. Physical Activity Safety

Physical Fitness Activities should carry safety content proportionate to
their ordinary risk and complexity.

The standard should avoid two extremes:

**Too little:** omitting a material ordinary caution needed to
understand safe performance.

**Too much:** turning every Activity into a medical warning sheet or
requiring clinical review for ordinary movement guidance.

The KCS safety class remains the publication gate where safety content
is applicable.

## 11. Avoidance Conditions

An avoidance condition is a concise circumstance in which the ordinary
practice should not be performed.

It should only be used when: - the condition is materially relevant; -
it can be stated responsibly; - it does not require Tiizi to diagnose
the participant.

Avoidance conditions must not become an improvised contraindication
database.

If responsible publication requires individualized clinical assessment,
the Activity should be escalated rather than filled with speculative
avoidance rules.

## 12. Publication Safety Gate

An Activity should **not** move from Draft to Published if:

-   applicable KCS safety content is missing;
-   its instructions create a material ambiguity about safe ordinary
    performance;
-   its meaning depends on an unsupported clinical/therapeutic claim;
-   a required caution has not been responsibly defined;
-   the Activity is inherently unsuitable for the ordinary catalogue
    without a separate product/safety decision.

A safety issue can therefore block publication even if identity,
taxonomy and measurement fields are technically complete.

## 13. Challenge Eligibility Safety Boundary

Published and Challenge Eligible remain distinct.

A Published Activity should not automatically become Challenge Eligible
if its use in a Challenge would require additional unresolved
constraints.

This matters because Challenges may introduce: - targets; -
repetition; - duration; - frequency; - competition; - streak pressure.

Those Challenge-level effects are not authored into the Activity itself.

Where a particular Activity requires Challenge-configuration constraints
for responsible use, those constraints should be governed at the
Challenge Definition/validation layer rather than hidden in
participant-facing Activity prose.

## 14. No Invented Safety Thresholds

Catalogue authors and coding agents must not invent: - maximum/minimum
medical values; - age-based medical thresholds; - disease-specific
restrictions; - universal hydration/nutrition targets; - clinical
heart-rate zones; - fasting limits; - injury rehabilitation schedules; -
other health thresholds merely to make an Activity look complete.

If such a threshold becomes genuinely necessary to the product, it
requires an appropriate supported product/content decision rather than
inference during catalogue authoring.

## 15. Source and Review Discipline

For ordinary low-risk guidance, Tiizi may use clear, established
practice descriptions appropriate to the Activity.

Where content becomes materially health-sensitive or depends on a
factual safety claim, the content-production process should identify the
need for stronger source/review support before publication.

The catalogue system should distinguish: - incomplete Draft content; -
publication-ready canonical content; - Challenge eligibility.

A coding agent may implement fields, validation and publication gates,
but must not independently invent authoritative health or safety policy
to satisfy those gates.

## 16. Authoring Checklist

Before publishing an Activity, the author/reviewer should ask:

-   Does the guidance explain the practice without making Tiizi a
    verification authority?
-   Is any Activity-specific ordinary safety caution actually needed?
-   If present, is the caution relevant and proportionate?
-   Does the content make a medical or therapeutic claim?
-   Does it attempt to diagnose, prescribe or rehabilitate?
-   Does it invent a universal health threshold?
-   Is an adaptation actually the same Activity?
-   Does equipment/environment require ordinary safety guidance?
-   Is there a material safety reason the Activity should remain Draft?
-   Could the Activity be Published but remain not Challenge Eligible
    pending Challenge-level constraints?

## 17. Product Rule

The Tiizi catalogue should be **useful enough to guide ordinary
participation and cautious enough not to pretend to be a clinical
system**.

Safety should be governed by the real needs of each Activity and the
applicable KCS class, not by blanket disclaimers, speculative warnings
or invented medical rules.

## 18. Challenge-Use Safety Can Be Stricter Than Publication Safety

A health-adjacent Activity may be safe and responsible to describe
canonically while some Challenge configurations remain unsuitable or
unresolved.

Challenge-use review may need to consider:

-   creator-selected target magnitude;
-   Duration;
-   frequency;
-   repeated Streak pressure;
-   Competitive pressure;
-   combinations with other requirements.

These concerns belong to Challenge Definition/eligibility and should not
be disguised as generic Activity safety copy.

## 19. Safety Uncertainty Means Draft, Not Invented Thresholds

Where responsible publication depends on safety/caution content that
Tiizi has not yet established with an appropriate basis, the correct
disposition is to keep the Activity Draft or not Challenge Eligible.

Authors and coding agents must not fill the gap by inventing:

-   medical thresholds;
-   age cut-offs;
-   disease-specific restrictions;
-   individualized limits;
-   universal health targets;
-   unsupported contraindication lists.

Fasting is the representative example: a stable candidate identity and
technically meaningful Duration do not require Tiizi to force
publication before the safety/product dependencies are resolved.
