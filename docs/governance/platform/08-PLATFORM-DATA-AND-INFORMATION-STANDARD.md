# Tiizi Platform Data and Information Standard

## 1. Purpose

Information governance exists to preserve truthful meaning, accountable authority and consistent use of information across Tiizi.

This standard governs:

- the constitutional categories of information;
- the distinction between evidence, authoritative state, derived truth and presentation;
- the authority responsible for each form of truth;
- the conceptual flow by which information gains meaning and authority;
- the boundaries among ownership, visibility, authority and access.

This standard does not define storage, technical structure, interfaces, entity inventories or lifecycle transitions.

## 2. Information Governance Principles

### 2.1 Governed Purpose

Information shall exist for a declared platform, domain, participation, knowledge, administrative or operational purpose. Information without a governed purpose shall not acquire authority merely because it is available.

### 2.2 One Authority Per Authoritative Fact

Every authoritative fact, state, calculation, knowledge item and decision shall have one declared authority. Multiple representations shall not create multiple truths.

### 2.3 Accepted Activity Information, Evidence Eligibility and Derived Truth Differ

An Accepted Activity Event records what Acceptance Authority accepted as authoritative Activity Information. Evidence Eligibility records the Policy-governed determination that the event may be used for a declared calculation. Derived Truth expresses what approved Policy and Calculation Authority determine from eligible evidence. None shall be presented as another.

### 2.4 Presentation Differs From Authority

Presentation communicates information. It does not establish, alter or replace the authority of the information presented.

### 2.5 Knowledge Differs From Participation

Knowledge defines approved meaning. Participation expresses a person's relationship to a group or challenge. Activity evidence records accepted performance. These concepts may reference one another but remain distinct.

### 2.6 Visibility Differs From Authorization

Visibility defines an approved audience. Authorization grants approved ability to access or act. Visibility does not establish ownership, authority or permission to change information.

### 2.7 Submission Differs From Acceptance

The Participant is the Attributable originator of Submission Intent, and Participant Authority establishes that expression. Acceptance Authority establishes the resulting Accepted Activity Event as authoritative Activity Information. Applicable Policy separately establishes Evidence Eligibility for a declared calculation.

### 2.8 Derivation Remains Attributable

Every derived value shall remain attributable to its authoritative evidence, governing policy and declared calculation authority.

### 2.9 No Silent Change of Meaning

Information shall not silently change category, authority, meaning or evidential status.

### 2.10 Minimum Necessary Information

Information access and use shall be limited to what is necessary for an approved purpose and visibility class.

## 3. Information Categories

An item of information shall have one primary category according to its governed purpose. Related categories may be referenced, but category overlap shall not create competing authority.

| Category | Purpose | Authority | Examples | Non-examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity Information | Represent a Person and support safe participation. | Identity Authority, constrained by the Person's rights and approved visibility classes. | Identity attributes, Profile information, consent records, participation-supporting preferences. | Challenge Progress, Activity Information, catalogue Knowledge. |
| Participation Information | Represent governed relationships to groups and challenges. | The participation authority designated by the applicable group or challenge policy. | Group membership relationship, challenge participation relationship, attributed contribution context. | Personal identity attributes, activity definitions, calculated ranking. |
| Knowledge Information | Define approved concepts, activities, metrics, units, relationships and constraints. | The governed knowledge authority; runtime availability belongs to the single governed runtime catalogue authority. | Knowledge Assets, activity definitions, metric meaning, unit meaning, approved policy knowledge. | Submission intent, accepted activity evidence, participant progress. |
| Activity Information | Represent a claimed or accepted occurrence of measurable Activity. | Participant Authority establishes Submission Intent; Acceptance Authority establishes Accepted Activity Events; Policy Authority establishes Evidence Eligibility for declared calculations. | Submission Intent, Accepted Activity Event, event provenance, Evidence Eligibility determination. | Activity definition, Progress total, presentation summary. |
| Derived Information | Express authoritative results calculated from accepted evidence under approved policy. | One declared calculation authority for each derived value. | Progress, completion, ranking, summaries, aggregates and governed calculated indicators. | Submission intent, accepted activity event, Knowledge Asset. |
| Administrative Information | Record the exercise of approved platform authority. | The authorized decision authority; high-impact decisions require trusted authority and immutable accountability. | Approval, restriction, administrative decision, authority record, sensitive-action audit record. | Personal preference, activity measurement, general presentation copy. |
| Operational Information | Support reliable, controlled and reviewable platform operation. | The declared platform-operations authority under later approved operational governance. | Operational condition, control outcome, incident evidence, continuity information. | Product goal, participant ranking, Knowledge Asset. |
| Analytical Information | Support interpretation of patterns, performance and outcomes without redefining source facts. | A declared analytical authority accountable for method, inputs, meaning and limitations. | Trend, cohort measure, evaluation, analytical report and clearly identified estimate. | Accepted activity event, administrative decision, unlabelled presentation claim. |
| Presentation Information | Communicate authoritative, derived or contextual information to an approved audience. | The underlying information retains authority; presentation has no independent authority. | Leaderboard presentation, progress display, feed presentation, notification content, summary wording. | The ranking calculation itself, accepted evidence, authoritative policy. |
| Temporary Information | Support a limited, declared working purpose before disposal or governed acceptance. | The designated custodian for that temporary purpose; temporary information is not authoritative unless accepted through a governed transition. | Working draft, preview, unaccepted working value, short-lived transfer material. | Accepted activity event, approved knowledge, final administrative decision. |

### Category rule

Information category describes purpose and authority, not sensitivity alone. Visibility classification is applied separately.

## 4. Authoritative Information

### 4.1 Authoritative Fact

An Authoritative Fact is a proposition accepted as true for a governed platform purpose by its declared authority. It has identifiable meaning, context, provenance and authority.

Availability, repetition or presentation does not make a proposition authoritative.

### 4.2 Authoritative State

Authoritative State is the current governed condition of an entity as established by its declared lifecycle authority.

This definition does not establish lifecycle states, transitions or correction rules.

### 4.3 Authoritative Calculation

An Authoritative Calculation is a governed determination produced from declared inputs under approved policy by one calculation authority.

Its result remains attributable to its evidence, policy and calculation basis.

### 4.4 Authoritative Knowledge

Authoritative Knowledge is approved meaning governed by the knowledge authority and made available for runtime use through the single governed runtime catalogue authority.

A local or temporary representation does not become Authoritative Knowledge merely by containing the same words.

### 4.5 Authoritative Decision

An Authoritative Decision is an attributable exercise of approved authority that establishes or changes governed state.

A high-impact Authoritative Decision requires trusted authority and an immutable audit record.

## 5. Derived Information

Derived Information is produced by applying approved policy or method to authoritative inputs.

### 5.1 Derived Values

A derived value expresses a governed result. It shall identify its calculation authority and remain traceable to its inputs and policy.

### 5.2 Projections

A projection is a subordinate representation of authoritative information for communication, access or analysis. It never becomes an independent authority.

### 5.3 Summaries

A summary condenses information without changing its meaning, category or authority.

### 5.4 Aggregates

An aggregate combines multiple authoritative inputs under a declared method. The aggregate is authoritative only when its calculation authority and method are approved.

### 5.5 Analytics

Analytics interprets governed information for an identified purpose. Analytical output shall disclose whether it is authoritative, estimated, provisional or descriptive.

Analytics does not alter the authority of its source facts.

### 5.6 Reports

A report assembles information for an approved audience and purpose. Each reported claim retains the authority and limitations of its source.

### 5.7 Leaderboards

A leaderboard presents governed ranking information. The ranking calculation remains authoritative; the leaderboard does not independently calculate or redefine rank.

### 5.8 Feeds

A feed presents group-relevant information or communication under approved access. A feed item does not become evidence, policy, progress or completion merely because it appears in a feed.

### Derived-information rule

Derived information never becomes an independent authority. Reuse, repetition, distribution or prominence does not change this rule.

## 6. Evidence Model

### Submission Intent

Submission Intent is a Participant's attributable expression that an Activity occurred in a governed context. The Participant is the Attributable originator, and Participant Authority establishes the expression.

Submission Intent is not an Accepted Activity Event, eligible evidence or Derived Truth.

### Accepted Activity Event

An Accepted Activity Event is the authoritative accepted record, established by Acceptance Authority, that a measurable Activity occurred in a governed context.

Acceptance establishes the event as authoritative activity information. It does not by itself determine progress, completion, ranking or recognition.

### Evidence

Evidence is authoritative information approved for use in a governed determination.

### Evidence Eligibility

Evidence Eligibility is the Policy-governed determination that an Accepted Activity Event may be used for a declared calculation. Acceptance establishes authoritative Activity Information; it does not establish universal eligibility for every calculation.

Knowledge informs the meaning of evidence but is not evidence of participation.

### Verification

Verification is a governed determination about evidential status. It may qualify how evidence may be used, but it does not replace the underlying event or policy.

Verification states, actors and effects remain deferred.

### Derived Truth

Derived Truth is an authoritative result calculated from accepted evidence under approved policy by the declared calculation authority.

Progress, completion and ranking are forms of Derived Truth when their governing requirements are satisfied.

### Historical Record

A Historical Record preserves an attributable account of an accepted fact, authoritative decision or derived truth as it was understood at a relevant time.

Historical record requirements do not establish correction, retention, deletion or archival rules.

### Evidence relationship

Submission Intent may be evaluated through an Acceptance Decision. Acceptance Authority may establish an Accepted Activity Event. Applicable Policy then determines Evidence Eligibility for a declared Calculation. Eligible evidence may support Derived Truth. Verification may qualify evidential use where later Policy permits. Historical Records preserve attributable prior truth without redefining it.

## 7. Information Relationships

| Concept | Information relationship |
|---|---|
| Knowledge | Defines approved meaning for activities, metrics, units and constraints. |
| Policy | Governs what information is permitted, how evidence may be used and how derived truth is determined. |
| Challenge | Provides the group-governed goal and policy context for participation. |
| Participant | Is the Attributable originator of Submission Intent within an approved participation relationship. |
| Activity Event | Governs the representation of an Activity occurrence; Acceptance Authority establishes its authoritative Accepted Activity Event form. |
| Evidence Eligibility | Determines under applicable Policy whether an Accepted Activity Event may be used for a declared calculation. |
| Progress | Expresses advancement toward a Goal established by Calculation Authority from eligible evidence under Policy. |
| Completion | Derives whether approved completion conditions are met. |
| Ranking | Derives an ordered result where an approved ranking policy exists. |
| Recognition | Truthfully acknowledges accepted participation or governed outcome. |
| Notification | Communicates an event, state or required attention without becoming authority for it. |
| Analytics | Interprets authoritative and derived information for a declared purpose without changing source authority. |

These relationships establish information meaning only. They do not establish lifecycle states or feature behavior.

## 8. Authority Flow

The constitutional authority flow is:

> **Governance Authority**
>
> ↓ approves boundaries
>
> **Governed Knowledge and Policy**
>
> ↓ define meaning and rules
>
> **Participation Context**
>
> ↓ provides governed relationships
>
> **Submission Intent**
>
> ↓ evaluated through an Acceptance Decision
>
> **Accepted Activity Event**
>
> ↓ assessed under applicable Policy
>
> **Evidence Eligibility**
>
> ↓ permits declared Calculation use
>
> **Calculation**
>
> ↓ establishes
>
> **Derived Truth**
>
> ↓ communicated without redefinition
>
> Presentation

### 8.1 Knowledge, Policy and Participation Context to Submission Intent

Knowledge and Policy define meaning and rules. Participation Authority supplies the governed Group and Challenge relationship context. Together they constrain, but do not create, the Submission Intent that Participant Authority establishes.

### 8.2 Submission Intent to Accepted Activity Event

Acceptance Authority evaluates Submission Intent against applicable governance and makes an Acceptance Decision. Acceptance establishes an Accepted Activity Event as authoritative Activity Information. This transition does not define acceptance states or procedures.

### 8.3 Accepted Activity Event to Evidence Eligibility and Calculation

Applicable Policy determines whether an Accepted Activity Event is eligible evidence for a declared Calculation. Calculation Authority applies approved Policy to eligible evidence. The resulting Progress, Completion, Ranking or other Derived Truth remains attributable.

### 8.4 Derived Truth to Presentation

Presentation communicates the authoritative result to an approved audience. Presentation may format or summarize, but shall not alter the result or its authority.

### 8.5 Flow boundary

No transition transfers authority silently. Each transition shall retain provenance, meaning and the identity of its governing authority.

## 9. Information Integrity Rules

1. **No competing authorities.** One authoritative concept or calculation shall not have equal independent authorities.
2. **No silent authority changes.** A copy, import, summary or presentation shall not acquire authority without explicit governance.
3. **Attributable derivation.** Derived values shall remain traceable to evidence, policy and calculation authority.
4. **Presentation preserves truth.** Presentation shall not change meaning, value, status or authority.
5. **Knowledge is not evidence.** A Knowledge Asset cannot prove participation.
6. **Evidence is not Policy.** An Accepted Activity Event cannot define the rules under which it is evaluated or its own Evidence Eligibility.
7. **Submission is not acceptance.** Submission Intent cannot be treated as an Accepted Activity Event without an Acceptance Decision by Acceptance Authority.
8. **Visibility is not authority.** Seeing information does not grant the right to establish or change it.
9. **Ownership is not unrestricted access.** Accountable stewardship remains constrained by purpose, privacy and least privilege.
10. **Temporary is not authoritative.** Temporary information cannot determine governed outcomes unless accepted under an approved transition.
11. **Historical truth is not silent replacement.** Material prior truth shall remain attributable when later governance permits change.
12. **Uncertainty is explicit.** Estimates, provisional information and unknown status shall be labelled truthfully.

## 10. Visibility and Access

Tiizi uses five constitutional visibility classes:

- **Public:** approved for access without a participation relationship.
- **Authenticated-discoverable:** approved for discovery by an authenticated person under governing policy.
- **Shared-group:** approved only within the relevant governed group relationship.
- **Private:** approved only for the person and specifically authorized purposes.
- **Privileged-operational:** approved only for defined operational authority under least privilege.

### Distinct concepts

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Visibility | The approved audience for information. |
| Ownership | Accountable stewardship of a Governed subject within declared constitutional boundaries. |
| Authority | The approved right and responsibility to establish or change governed truth. |
| Access | The approved ability to view, use or act upon information for a purpose. |

These concepts do not imply one another.

Minimal disclosure applies. Health, body, contact, birth-date and private preference information shall not be broadly readable. Group participants receive only information necessary for participation. Privileged-operational access requires defined authority. Access is deny-by-default outside the approved visibility class.

## 11. Information Lifecycle Layers

These layers describe how information may be understood over time. They are not lifecycle states and do not define transitions, timing or retention.

### Creation

Information is first expressed for a governed purpose by an Attributable originator or another declared source. Creation alone does not confer Authority to establish truth.

### Acceptance

A declared authority determines that information may become an authoritative fact, state, knowledge item or decision. Acceptance procedures remain deferred.

### Derivation

A declared calculation authority produces Derived Truth from authoritative inputs under policy.

### Presentation

Authoritative or derived information is communicated to an approved audience without changing its authority.

### Archival

Information may be removed from ordinary active use while preserving governed meaning and accountability. Archival rules remain deferred.

### Historical Reference

Information may be retained as an attributable account of prior accepted truth or decision. Retention, correction and access rules remain deferred.

## 12. Deferred Areas

This standard intentionally does not create or define:

- the Entity Ownership Register;
- the Security Standard;
- lifecycle standards or lifecycle states;
- a Database Model;
- an API Model;
- an Implementation Mapping;
- technical storage structures;
- interface contracts;
- retention periods;
- correction or deletion procedures;
- verification states or effects;
- challenge-type behavior;
- ranking, streak or reward rules;
- social, notification or operational lifecycles.

These areas require their own approved governance and shall conform to this standard.
