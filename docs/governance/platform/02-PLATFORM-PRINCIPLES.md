# Tiizi Platform Principles

## Purpose

These principles provide stable tests for every Tiizi standard and capability. They govern product decisions without prescribing implementation.

## 1. Group First

**Purpose:** Preserve the primary product model.

**Statement:** Version 2 centers group challenges, group accountability and shared progress. Supporting personal capabilities exist to enable group participation and do not create an individual-challenge mode.

**Rationale:** Shared pursuit is the defining platform context.

**Examples:**

- A profile capability is evaluated by how truthfully and safely it supports participation.
- A proposed individual challenge is future-only until separately governed.

**Affected domains:** Platform, Groups, Challenges, Identity, Analytics.

## 2. Meaningful Participation

**Purpose:** Keep participation connected to a clear purpose.

**Statement:** A participant's contribution shall have an understandable relationship to the challenge goal and approved activity definition.

**Rationale:** Activity without governed meaning cannot support trustworthy motivation.

**Examples:**

- A contribution identifies the activity and measurement it represents.
- Recognition does not substitute for actual participation evidence.

**Affected domains:** Challenges, Activity, Knowledge, Motivation.

## 3. Shared Progress

**Purpose:** Make collective understanding reliable.

**Statement:** Shared Progress shall be calculated under one declared Calculation Authority from Accepted Activity Events that applicable Policy makes eligible as evidence for that calculation.

**Rationale:** Groups need a common, stable account of progress.

**Examples:**

- Different views of the same challenge use the same authoritative result.
- A presentation summary does not independently redefine progress.

**Affected domains:** Groups, Challenges, Activity, Analytics.

## 4. Evidence Before Recognition

**Purpose:** Ensure recognition reflects governed facts.

**Statement:** Recognition, Completion and Ranking shall follow evidence made eligible under approved Policy and approved calculation rules.

**Rationale:** Motivation loses legitimacy when presentation precedes or contradicts evidence.

**Examples:**

- Submission Intent is not an Accepted Activity Event, and acceptance does not by itself establish Evidence Eligibility for every calculation.
- An unverified claim is not described as verified.

**Affected domains:** Activity, Challenges, Motivation, Administration.

## 5. Single Source of Truth

**Purpose:** Prevent competing authorities.

**Statement:** Every authoritative concept and every derived truth shall have one declared authority.

**Rationale:** Equal authorities create inconsistent history and decisions.

**Examples:**

- One governed runtime catalogue determines selectable knowledge.
- A projection communicates an authoritative result but does not become a second source.

**Affected domains:** Knowledge, Activity, Challenges, Analytics, Administration.

## 6. Immutable History

**Purpose:** Preserve accountability for important facts and changes.

**Statement:** Accepted facts and high-impact decisions shall not be silently rewritten. Material change shall remain attributable and reviewable.

**Rationale:** Trust requires a record of what was accepted and who changed governed state.

**Examples:**

- A sensitive administrative action leaves an immutable audit record.
- A later correction does not conceal that an earlier accepted state existed.

**Affected domains:** Activity, Challenges, Administration, Knowledge.

This principle does not decide the pending correction, retention or archival lifecycles.

## 7. Least Privilege

**Purpose:** Limit access and authority to approved need.

**Statement:** Every actor receives only the visibility and action authority required for an approved purpose. Access is deny-by-default outside an approved class.

**Rationale:** Convenience is not a sufficient basis for sensitive access.

**Examples:**

- Interface visibility never grants authority.
- Privileged-operational access requires a defined operational role.

**Affected domains:** Identity, Groups, Challenges, Administration, Operations.

## 8. Governed Knowledge

**Purpose:** Ensure selectable activities carry approved meaning.

**Statement:** Runtime knowledge shall come from one governed catalogue authority. Every use shall preserve the distinction between knowledge and participant activity.

**Rationale:** Consistent challenges require consistent definitions.

**Examples:**

- A local fixture cannot silently replace the governed authority.
- A participant event refers to knowledge; it does not become knowledge itself.

**Affected domains:** Knowledge, Fitness, Wellness, Challenges.

## 9. Domain Neutrality

**Purpose:** Keep the platform extensible without inventing scope.

**Statement:** Shared platform contracts shall avoid unnecessary Fitness- or Wellness-only assumptions while preserving domain-specific meaning through governed extensions.

**Rationale:** The platform may admit future domains, but only through governance.

**Examples:**

- The canonical activity-event concept is shared; Fitness and Wellness may extend it.
- A possible future domain is not listed as a launch capability.

**Affected domains:** Platform, Knowledge, Activity, Challenges.

## 10. Truthful User Experience

**Purpose:** Align every statement and control with enforceable reality.

**Statement:** Tiizi shall not describe display, privacy, verification, progress or authority more strongly than the governed platform can support.

**Rationale:** Trust depends on truthful expectations.

**Examples:**

- An unenforceable privacy control is removed or truthfully relabelled.
- A calculated estimate is not presented as authoritative fact.

**Affected domains:** Identity, Challenges, Analytics, Notifications, Administration.

## 11. Platform Before Feature

**Purpose:** Prevent local features from weakening shared foundations.

**Statement:** A feature shall conform to platform identity, terminology, ownership, authority and privacy rules before feature-specific convenience.

**Rationale:** Durable platform behavior requires common governance.

**Examples:**

- A feature may not create a competing catalogue authority.
- A feature may not assign itself authority over a derived total.

**Affected domains:** All domains.
