# Tiizi Challenge Domain Standard

## 1. Purpose

Challenges exist to give people a governed shared undertaking through which they pursue a defined goal together using measurable activities.

A Challenge supports:

- shared purpose within a Group;
- measurable participation connected to governed meaning;
- accountability among Participants;
- motivation through shared pursuit and truthful feedback;
- authoritative Progress derived from Accepted Activity Events that applicable Policy makes eligible as evidence for the declared calculation;
- truthful outcomes through approved Completion and Ranking authority;
- Recognition where later approved Policy permits it.

This standard defines the Challenge as a business domain. It does not define lifecycle states, permissions, types, formulas, technical structures or implementation behavior.

## 2. Constitutional Role

A Challenge is:

- the primary governed undertaking within a Group;
- a shared-goal context;
- a context for governed participation;
- a Policy context for accepted activity and Derived Truth;
- a domain-neutral foundation capable of using approved Fitness and Wellness Knowledge;
- the context in which measurable activity may contribute to a shared purpose.

A Challenge is not:

- a Group;
- a Profile;
- a Participant;
- an Activity Event;
- a Knowledge Asset;
- a Leaderboard;
- a Feed;
- an individual-challenge mode;
- an independent actor or authority.

A Challenge provides governed context and applies approved Policy. It does not itself accept Submission Intent, establish Accepted Activity Events or calculate Progress, Completion or Ranking.

## 3. Group-First Boundary

Version 2 Challenges are Group Challenges. Every Version 2 Challenge exists within a governed Group context.

The following boundaries apply:

- Group membership and Challenge participation are distinct relationships.
- Not every Group Member is automatically a Participant.
- A Profile does not establish membership or participation.
- Group administration does not automatically confer Challenge authority.
- Challenge participation requires a governed relationship whose exact rules remain deferred.
- Individual challenges are excluded from Version 2 launch and are not an active Challenge type.

This section does not define Challenge creation rights, eligibility, joining, leaving or participation transitions.

## 4. Domain Boundaries

### Included

The Challenge Domain includes the constitutional governance of:

- Challenge identity;
- Challenge purpose and shared-goal meaning;
- measurable activity context;
- references to approved Activity definitions, Metrics and Units;
- Challenge participation context;
- applicable Policy;
- the meaning of Progress and Completion;
- Ranking context where separately approved;
- Challenge presentation;
- Challenge-derived information;
- relationships to Group, Knowledge and accepted evidence.

### Excluded

The Challenge Domain does not define or approve:

- lifecycle states or transitions;
- Challenge creation rights;
- final Challenge types or taxonomy;
- activity submission workflow;
- acceptance workflow;
- correction workflow;
- Verification rules;
- scoring formulas;
- Ranking formulas;
- Streak formulas or qualification rules;
- Completion thresholds or formulas;
- Reward rules;
- moderation;
- technical implementation.

### Future Considerations

Future approved standards may define Challenge taxonomy, lifecycle, participation, amendment, evidence treatment, formulas, Ranking, Streaks, Recognition, Rewards and administration.

These possibilities establish no current type, state, transition, formula, threshold, permission or operational behavior. Future domain extensibility does not make an unimplemented domain active or weaken Fitness and Wellness as the Version 2 launch domains.

## 5. Domain Principles

### 5.1 Group Challenge First

Group Challenges are the primary and active Version 2 product model. Individual challenges remain excluded from Version 2 launch.

### 5.2 One Governed Purpose Per Challenge

Each Challenge shall have one declared governing purpose. Multiple activities or goals, if later permitted, shall remain attributable to that single governed purpose and shall not create competing Challenge meaning.

### 5.3 Goals Must Be Evaluable Truthfully

A Challenge Goal shall be measurable or truthfully evaluable under approved Knowledge and Policy. An undefined or ungoverned measure cannot establish authoritative Progress or Completion.

### 5.4 Meaning Comes From Knowledge and Policy

Knowledge defines approved Activities, Metrics, Units and relationships. Policy defines how those concepts may be used in the Challenge. The Challenge shall not silently redefine them.

### 5.5 Participation Differs From Membership

A Member has a governed relationship to a Group. A Participant has a governed relationship to a Challenge. Neither relationship silently creates the other.

### 5.6 Submission Differs From Acceptance

The Participant is the Attributable originator of Submission Intent, and Participant Authority establishes that expression. Acceptance Authority determines whether an Accepted Activity Event is established.

### 5.7 Evidence Eligibility Differs From Progress

An Accepted Activity Event is authoritative Activity Information. Applicable Policy separately establishes Evidence Eligibility for a declared calculation. Progress is Derived Truth established by Calculation Authority from eligible evidence under Policy.

### 5.8 Progress and Completion Are Derived Truth

Progress and Completion require declared Calculation Authority. Neither may be authored by a Participant, Group, Challenge, administrator or presentation.

### 5.9 Ranking Is Optional and Policy-Governed

Ranking exists only where later approved Policy permits and defines it. A Challenge does not imply Ranking merely by existing.

### 5.10 Presentation Cannot Redefine Challenge Truth

Presentation may communicate Challenge identity, context and authoritative outcomes. It shall not calculate, alter or replace them.

### 5.11 Challenge Administration Is Scoped

Challenge-related Administrative Authority shall be explicitly assigned, purpose-limited, reviewable and constrained by privacy, least privilege and auditability.

### 5.12 Type Labels Do Not Create Authority

A Challenge Type or classification label cannot silently change evidence, calculation, participation or administrative authority.

### 5.13 Domain Neutrality Preserves Launch Scope

Challenge foundations shall remain neutral enough to support governed domains while Fitness and Wellness remain the only Version 2 launch domains.

### 5.14 Individual Challenge Exclusion

Individual challenges remain a possible future governed capability only. Their possible future consideration creates no Version 2 requirement, authority or acceptance criterion.

## 6. Information Model

The Challenge Domain recognizes the following conceptual information groupings. They define purpose and authority without defining fields.

| Challenge information grouping | Constitutional category | Purpose | Primary authority | Authority boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Challenge Identity Information | Participation Information | Represent the governed undertaking distinctly and consistently. | Establishment authority remains deferred. Accountable stewardship remains deferred. | Identity does not establish Activity Information, participation or outcomes. |
| Challenge Purpose Information | Participation Information | State the shared undertaking and meaningful reason for participation. | Establishment authority remains deferred; Policy Authority may constrain permitted purpose under later approved governance. | Governance Authority approves constitutional boundaries but does not establish an individual Challenge's identity or purpose. Purpose is not evidence, Progress or a Completion rule by itself. |
| Goal Information | Participation Information | Define the outcome toward which participation is directed using governed Knowledge references. | Establishment authority remains deferred; Policy Authority constrains evaluation. | A Goal is not automatically a target, Progress value or Completion condition. |
| Challenge Policy Information | Knowledge Information | State approved rules constraining participation, evidence and calculation. | Policy Authority. | Policy approval and administrative audit remain separate; Policy does not prove Activity or calculate an outcome merely by existing. |
| Activity-reference Information | Knowledge Information | Identify approved Activities relevant to the Challenge. | Knowledge Authority; Runtime Catalogue authority governs runtime availability. | An Activity definition is not Submission Intent or evidence. |
| Metric and Unit Reference Information | Knowledge Information | Give governed meaning to measurement used by the Challenge. | Knowledge Authority. | A Metric or Unit reference is not a score, target or measured event. |
| Participation-context Information | Participation Information | Represent the governed relationship context between a Participant and Challenge. | Participation Authority under later lifecycle standards. | Membership, Profile or display does not establish participation. |
| Evidence-context Information | Activity Information | Relate an Accepted Activity Event and its Evidence Eligibility to the relevant Participant and Challenge context. | Acceptance Authority establishes the event; Policy Authority establishes Evidence Eligibility for a declared calculation. | Challenge information and Submission Intent do not establish accepted Activity Information or Evidence Eligibility. |
| Challenge-derived Information | Derived Information | Express authoritative Progress, Completion, Ranking or approved aggregates. | Calculation Authority. | Derived values require eligible evidence and Policy and remain attributable. |
| Challenge Administrative Information | Administrative Information | Record approved high-impact Challenge decisions or actions. | Administrative Authority within explicit scope. | Administrative access does not create evidence, Derived Truth or unrestricted authority. |
| Challenge Presentation Information | Presentation Information | Communicate Challenge context and authoritative information to an approved audience. | Presentation Authority. | Presentation cannot redefine Challenge, participation, evidence or outcomes. |
| Temporary Challenge Information | Temporary Information | Support a limited, declared working purpose before governed acceptance or disposal. | The authority designated for the temporary purpose. | Temporary information is not authoritative Challenge information unless later accepted through governed authority. |

## 7. Authority Model

The Challenge Domain conforms to the Tiizi Authority Chain. A Challenge is a governed context, not an independent actor or authority.

| Authority type | Challenge-domain authority | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Governance Authority | Approves Challenge-domain constitutional boundaries, standards, amendments and governed exceptions. | Does not establish an individual Challenge's identity or purpose and does not silently approve pending taxonomy, lifecycle or formula decisions. |
| Knowledge Authority | Defines approved Activities, Metrics, Units and Knowledge relationships used by Challenges. | Does not prove participation or establish Challenge outcomes. |
| Policy Authority | Governs how a Challenge may operate, use evidence and determine results where Policy is separately approved. | Does not create Submission Intent, accepted evidence or a calculated result. |
| Participation Authority | May establish Challenge participation relationships under later approved lifecycle standards. | This standard assigns no eligibility, joining, leaving, removal, state or transition rules. |
| Participant Authority | Establishes attributable Submission Intent within an approved Challenge participation context. | The Participant is the Attributable originator. Authority ends at Submission Intent and does not establish acceptance, Evidence Eligibility, Progress, Completion, Ranking or Verification. |
| Acceptance Authority | Evaluates Submission Intent and establishes Accepted Activity Events under approved Knowledge and Policy. | Does not create Policy or Derived Truth. |
| Calculation Authority | Establishes authoritative Progress, Completion, Ranking and other approved Derived Truth from eligible evidence under Policy. | Does not invent evidence, establish its own Evidence Eligibility, alter Knowledge or create Policy. |
| Administrative Authority | Performs only explicitly assigned Challenge actions under least privilege and durable accountability. | Does not receive unrestricted authority or bypass Knowledge, Policy, Acceptance or Calculation Authority. |
| Presentation Authority | Communicates approved Challenge information and authoritative results to an approved audience. | Does not calculate, change or independently establish Challenge truth. |
| Operational Authority | Supports reliable, controlled Challenge operation under later approved operational governance. | Does not acquire stewardship of Challenge truth or override constitutional authority. |

## 8. Ownership Model

This foundation describes Challenge ownership boundaries without creating the Entity Ownership Register.

| Constitutional relationship | Constitutional position |
|---|---|
| Governed subject | The Challenge is the Governed subject: a distinct undertaking with attributable identity, purpose and Group context. |
| Information subject | The Challenge is the Information subject of Challenge information; Persons remain the Information subjects of their Profile information. |
| Attributable originator | The Participant is the Attributable originator of Submission Intent; origin does not establish accepted or derived truth. |
| Authority to establish truth | Acceptance Authority establishes Accepted Activity Events. Policy Authority establishes Evidence Eligibility for declared calculations. Calculation Authority establishes Derived Truth. Establishment of Challenge identity and purpose remains deferred. |
| Accountable steward | Accountable stewardship remains deferred. |
| Custodian | A Custodian may preserve Challenge information within assigned scope without acquiring Authority to establish its truth. |
| Administrator | An Administrator may act only through explicitly assigned Administrative Authority and does not personally own Challenge information. |
| Operator | An Operator may support reliable Challenge operation only within explicitly assigned Operational Authority. |
| Presenter | A Presenter may communicate Challenge information and results without redefining them. |
| Downstream user | A Downstream user may use approved Challenge information only for a declared purpose without acquiring authority over it. |

The Group provides context but does not acquire authority over every Challenge decision. Challenge Administrators do not personally acquire stewardship or Authority to establish all Challenge information. Participants originate Submission Intent but do not establish Accepted Activity Events, Evidence Eligibility or calculated outcomes. Knowledge references, Accepted Activity Events and Derived Truth retain their distinct authorities.

| Related concept | Challenge relationship | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Group | Every Version 2 Challenge exists within a governed Group context. | The Group and Challenge remain distinct; Group administration does not automatically establish Challenge authority. |
| Profile | A Profile may supply approved identity and participation-supporting context. | A Profile does not establish Challenge participation or outcomes. |
| Person | A person may hold governed Group and Challenge relationships. | A person is not a Participant without the governed Challenge relationship. |
| Member | A Member has a governed relationship to the Group containing the Challenge. | Membership does not guarantee participation in the Challenge. |
| Participant | A Participant has a governed relationship to the Challenge and is the Attributable originator of Submission Intent. | Participation does not confer administrative, acceptance, evidence-eligibility or calculation authority. |
| Knowledge Asset | A Knowledge Asset defines approved meaning that may support Challenge configuration and measurement. | It is not a Challenge, participant claim or evidence. |
| Activity | An Activity is a governed measurable behavior that a Challenge may reference. | The Activity definition is not an Activity Event. |
| Metric | A Metric defines the governed dimension by which an Activity or Goal is measured. | A Metric is not a score unless later Policy explicitly defines that relationship. |
| Unit | A Unit expresses a Metric in an approved form. | A Unit is not a target or a display suffix chosen independently. |
| Submission Intent | A Participant's attributable expression that an Activity occurred in a governed context. | It is not an Accepted Activity Event, eligible evidence or Progress. |
| Accepted Activity Event | The authoritative accepted record, established by Acceptance Authority, that a measurable Activity occurred in a governed context. | It does not by itself establish Evidence Eligibility, Completion, Ranking or Recognition. |
| Evidence Eligibility | Applicable Policy determines whether an Accepted Activity Event may be used for a declared Challenge calculation. | It is not acceptance, Verification or Derived Truth. |
| Progress | The authoritative derived account of advancement toward the Challenge Goal. | It requires Calculation Authority and is not a participant-authored total. |
| Completion | The authoritative determination that approved conditions are met. | It requires approved Policy and Calculation Authority; thresholds remain deferred. |
| Ranking | The governed ordering of eligible results where approved Policy exists. | Ranking is optional and is not Recognition, Reward or arbitrary score. |
| Streak | A governed pattern of qualifying participation across defined periods where later approved. | This standard establishes no Streak rule, qualification or time boundary. |
| Feed | A Feed may present governed Challenge-related activity or communication. | Feed content does not become evidence, Progress or Challenge state. |
| Notification | A Notification may communicate a Challenge-related event, state or required attention. | It does not establish the underlying event or state. |
| Recognition | Recognition may truthfully acknowledge governed participation or outcome where later Policy permits. | It does not create evidence, Progress, Ranking or Reward authority. |
| Analytics | Approved analytics may interpret Challenge information for a declared purpose. | Analytics does not alter authoritative Challenge, evidence or Derived Truth. |
| Administration | Administration may exercise explicitly assigned Challenge authority under least privilege. | Administrative access is not ownership, unrestricted control or calculation authority. |

## 10. Goal and Measurement Model

The Challenge measurement model is conceptual and Policy-governed.

| Concept | Constitutional meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Challenge Goal | The stated outcome toward which Participants direct measurable activity. | A Goal is not automatically a target or Completion rule. |
| Measurable Activity | An approved Activity whose performance can be evaluated using governed meaning. | An Activity definition is not evidence that performance occurred. |
| Metric | The governed dimension by which Activity or Goal measurement is interpreted. | A Metric is not a score unless later Policy defines it as such. |
| Unit | The governed expression used to quantify the Metric. | A Unit cannot be selected independently of its Metric meaning. |
| Target | A Policy-governed reference value or condition toward which evaluation may be directed. | A target is not Progress, accepted evidence or Completion. |
| Time context | The governed temporal context relevant to measurement where applicable. | This standard defines no time boundary, cadence, grace rule or duration. |
| Evidence basis | Accepted Activity Events made eligible for a declared determination under applicable Policy. | Knowledge references and Submission Intent are not eligible evidence. |
| Evaluation basis | The approved Policy and method used by Calculation Authority. | This standard defines no formula, threshold or ranking method. |

Challenges need not share one measurement method. Every method shall use governed Knowledge, approved Policy and a declared authority. Targets, time boundaries and evaluation rules remain subject to later standards.

## 11. Evidence and Derived Truth Boundary

The canonical Challenge information chain is:

> **Governed Knowledge**
>
> ↓ defines meaning
>
> **Policy**
>
> ↓ defines rules
>
> **Participation Context**
>
> ↓ permits attributable expression
>
> **Submission Intent**
>
> ↓ evaluated through an Acceptance Decision
>
> **Accepted Activity Event**
>
> ↓ assessed under applicable Policy
>
> **Evidence Eligibility**
>
> ↓ permits declared Calculation use
>
> **Calculation**
>
> **Progress / Completion / Ranking**
>
> ↓ communicated without redefinition
>
> **Presentation**

The following boundaries apply:

- Challenge information does not prove that Activity occurred.
- Submission Intent is not an Accepted Activity Event or eligible evidence.
- Acceptance establishes authoritative Activity Information; it does not establish universal Evidence Eligibility.
- Accepted Activity Events do not by themselves establish Completion.
- Progress does not become authoritative through display or repetition.
- Ranking is Derived Truth only where approved Policy exists.
- Knowledge and Policy remain distinct from evidence.
- Corrections and Verification remain deferred.

## 12. Challenge Participation Context

| Concept | Meaning in Challenge participation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Person | The human subject who may hold governed Identity and participation relationships. | A Person is not a Member or Participant merely by existing. |
| Profile | The governed representation of Identity and participation-supporting information. | A Profile does not establish Group membership or Challenge participation. |
| Member | A person with a governed relationship to the Group. | Membership does not guarantee Challenge participation. |
| Participant | A person with a governed relationship to the Challenge. | Participation is Challenge-specific and grants no automatic administrative authority. |
| Group | The primary social and accountability context containing the Version 2 Challenge. | The Group is not the Challenge or a Participant. |
| Challenge | The governed shared undertaking in which Participants pursue a defined Goal. | The Challenge is not an actor and holds no independent authority. |

Exact eligibility, joining, leaving and removal rules remain deferred. A Participant's relationship does not create authority over other Participants or their information.

## 13. Challenge Types and Classification Boundary

This standard does not approve final Challenge Types or taxonomy.

Future classification may describe:

- participation structure;
- measurement pattern;
- motivational pattern;
- time structure;
- outcome model.

A classification label does not create a separate authority model. Every future Challenge Type shall conform to the same Knowledge, Policy, participation, evidence, acceptance, calculation, privacy and presentation boundaries.

Current implementation labels are not constitutionally approved merely because they exist. Final Challenge taxonomy remains deferred to a later founder decision and standard.

## 14. Information Integrity Rules

1. Challenge identity does not establish Activity evidence.
2. Group membership does not establish Challenge participation.
3. Submission Intent does not establish an Accepted Activity Event or Evidence Eligibility.
4. An Accepted Activity Event does not automatically establish Evidence Eligibility or Completion.
5. Progress, Completion and Ranking require declared Calculation Authority.
6. Challenge presentation cannot change authoritative results.
7. Challenge administrators cannot bypass Policy, authority boundaries or durable accountability.
8. Knowledge references shall not be silently redefined locally.
9. Targets shall not be changed silently after they have influenced participation; any later permitted change must preserve attribution and historical truth.
10. Challenge-derived values shall remain attributable to eligible evidence, approved Policy and Calculation Authority.
11. Feed content is not Challenge truth.
12. Notifications do not establish Challenge state.
13. Recognition does not create evidence or outcomes.
14. Challenge Type labels do not override constitutional rules.
15. Client-created authoritative totals, Completion or Rankings are prohibited.
16. Privacy, minimum necessary access and least privilege apply throughout the Challenge context.
17. Local copies, summaries and presentations shall not become competing Challenge authority.
18. A target, Metric or Unit shall not be treated as interchangeable with another concept merely for convenience.

## 15. Visibility

Visibility defines the approved audience for Challenge information. It does not establish participation, authority or access to all Participant information.

| Visibility class | Challenge-domain meaning | Constitutional constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Challenge information specifically approved for access without Group or participation relationship. | Public Challenge information does not make Activity evidence, Participant details or private Profile information public. |
| Authenticated-discoverable | Minimal Challenge information approved for governed discovery by an authenticated person. | Discovery differs from eligibility, participation and access; final discoverability rules remain deferred. |
| Shared-group | Challenge information approved within the relevant governed Group relationship. | Shared-group visibility does not automatically expose all Participant details or establish participation. |
| Private | Challenge or Participant-related information restricted to specifically authorized purposes or relationships. | Private Participant information remains separately governed from general Challenge visibility. |
| Privileged-operational | Challenge information available only to defined operational authority. | Access remains purpose-limited, reviewable, deny-by-default and constrained by least privilege. |

## 16. Administrative Boundary

Challenge administration:

- must be explicitly assigned;
- remains subordinate to Governance, Knowledge and Policy Authority;
- cannot establish Accepted Activity Events or Derived Truth unless separately acting through the declared Acceptance or Calculation Authority;
- cannot silently change targets, Metrics, Units, purpose or meaning;
- requires durable accountability for high-impact changes;
- does not imply unrestricted access or ownership;
- remains constrained by privacy, minimum necessary use and least privilege;
- does not establish final role vocabulary.

Title, interface access, Group administration or possession of Challenge information does not alone confer Challenge Administrative Authority.
