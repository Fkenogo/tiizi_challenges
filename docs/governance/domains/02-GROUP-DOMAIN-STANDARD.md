# Tiizi Group Domain Standard

## 1. Purpose

Groups exist as the governed social and participation context through which people pursue meaningful goals together in Tiizi.

The Group Domain supports:

- belonging through an attributable collective context;
- accountability through governed relationships and shared purpose;
- shared participation without erasing individual identity or authority boundaries;
- mutual encouragement through approved Group communication;
- Group Challenges as the primary Version 2 product model;
- truthful shared Progress derived from accepted evidence under approved authority.

This standard defines what a Group is as a business domain. It does not define technical structures, fields, interfaces, roles, workflows or lifecycle transitions.

## 2. Constitutional Role

A Group is the primary collective participation context in Tiizi's group-first model. It is:

- a governed relationship among people;
- the primary social and accountability context for Version 2 Challenges;
- a context within which Group Challenges may be governed;
- a context for approved communication, mutual encouragement and shared visibility;
- a boundary within which participation meaning, privacy and accountability must remain explicit.

A Group is not:

- a Challenge;
- a Participant;
- a Profile;
- a Leaderboard;
- an unrestricted social network;
- a public audience by default;
- an authority for Accepted Activity Events or Derived Truth;
- an individual-challenge mode;
- a substitute for the distinct relationships of Member and Participant.

The Group provides context for shared pursuit. It does not itself prove that activity occurred or determine Progress, Completion or Ranking.

## 3. Domain Boundaries

### Included

The Group Domain includes the constitutional governance of:

- Group identity;
- governed membership relationships;
- Group purpose and collective context;
- the participation context that may support Group Challenges;
- approved presentation of Group information;
- Group-level privacy and visibility meaning;
- the relationship between a Group and its governed Challenges;
- group-relevant communication context;
- attributable Group administration within approved authority boundaries;
- approved Group-derived information where a later authority and purpose are declared.

### Excluded

The Group Domain does not define or govern:

- final role vocabulary or role hierarchy;
- membership states or transitions;
- invitations, join requests or approvals;
- removal, leaving, suspension or stewardship transfer;
- moderation workflows;
- Challenge lifecycle, configuration or outcome rules;
- acceptance of Submission Intent or creation of Accepted Activity Events;
- calculation of Progress, Completion, Ranking or other Derived Truth;
- member Profile ownership;
- friendships or a social graph;
- unrestricted discovery or public access;
- technical implementation.

### Future Considerations

Future approved standards may define Group roles, stewardship, membership and invitation lifecycles, moderation, discovery, capacity, Challenge creation rights, communication lifecycle and archival behavior.

These possibilities establish no current role, permission, state, transition or operational obligation. Individual challenges remain excluded from Version 2 launch and are not created by Group-domain extensibility.

## 4. Domain Principles

### 4.1 Group First

Groups are the primary context for Version 2 Challenges, accountability and shared Progress. Personal capabilities support this context without creating an individual-challenge product mode.

### 4.2 A Group Is a Governed Relationship

A Group is not merely a list, label or audience. It is a governed collective context with attributable purpose and relationships among people.

### 4.3 Membership Differs From Profile Ownership

Membership expresses a governed relationship between a person and a Group. It does not transfer ownership of the person's Profile or grant access to all Profile information.

### 4.4 Group Context Does Not Establish Challenge Outcomes

A Group may provide the context for a Challenge, but it does not establish Accepted Activity Events, Progress, Completion, Ranking or Verification.

### 4.5 Shared Visibility Is Purpose-Limited

Information shared within a Group shall remain limited to an approved participation, communication or administrative purpose. Shared-group visibility does not apply automatically to every item.

### 4.6 Group Administration Is Scoped

Administrative Authority within a Group shall be explicitly assigned, purpose-limited, reviewable and constrained by privacy and least privilege. It is never unrestricted.

### 4.7 Group Communication Is Not Evidence

Communication may support belonging and encouragement. It does not become Submission Intent, an Accepted Activity Event, Progress or other Derived Truth merely because it appears in a Group context.

### 4.8 Group Identity Remains Distinct From Member Identity

Group identity represents the collective context. It neither replaces nor absorbs the identity or Profile of any Member.

### 4.9 Challenges Remain Separately Governed

A Group may host more than one governed Challenge only where later approved Policy and lifecycle standards permit. This principle does not approve concurrent Challenges or establish creation rights.

### 4.10 Convenience Does Not Override Governance

Group convenience shall not override privacy, authority, visibility, information integrity or domain boundaries.

## 5. Information Model

The Group Domain recognizes the following conceptual information groupings. They describe purpose and authority without defining fields.

| Group information grouping | Constitutional category | Purpose | Authority boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group Identity Information | Participation Information | Represent the governed collective context distinctly and consistently. | Establishment authority remains subject to later approved governance; Accountable stewardship remains deferred. Presentation or member usage cannot create competing Group truth. |
| Group Purpose Information | Participation Information | State the governed purpose and context that gives the Group meaning. | It may reference Knowledge but does not become Platform Policy, evidence or a Challenge Completion rule. |
| Membership Relationship Information | Participation Information | Represent the governed relationship between a person and a Group. | Participation Authority may establish this relationship only under later approved lifecycle rules; a Profile or display cannot establish membership. |
| Participation-context Information | Participation Information | Provide the Group context in which Challenge participation may later be governed. | It does not make every Member a Participant or establish Challenge state. |
| Group Privacy Information | Participation Information | Govern approved visibility and discovery meaning for the Group relationship context. | Administrative enforcement remains separate; privacy meaning must be enforceable and does not grant authority through presentation. |
| Group Administrative Information | Administrative Information | Record approved exercises of Group-related Administrative Authority. | Administrative information requires explicit authority and durable accountability; it does not imply unrestricted control. |
| Group Presentation Information | Presentation Information | Communicate approved Group identity, context and state to an approved audience. | Presentation Authority may format or summarize but cannot establish membership, Group state or Challenge truth. |
| Group Communication Information | Participation Information | Support attributable group-relevant communication and mutual encouragement. | Its presentation remains separate; communication is not evidence, Policy, Progress or authoritative membership state. |
| Group-derived Information | Derived Information | Express an approved aggregate or calculated summary about the Group. | It requires a declared Calculation Authority and remains attributable to approved inputs and Policy. |
| Group Analytical Information | Analytical Information | Interpret Group-related information for a declared analytical purpose. | It cannot establish Group, membership, Challenge or Activity truth. |
| Temporary Group Information | Temporary Information | Support a limited, declared working purpose before governed acceptance or disposal. | It is not authoritative Group information unless accepted through a later governed process. |

## 6. Authority Model

The Group Domain conforms to the Platform Authority Model.

| Authority type | Group-domain authority | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Participant Authority | May express permitted personal intent or communication within an approved Group or Challenge context. | Member or Participant actions do not automatically establish authority over Group state, membership state, Accepted Activity Events or Derived Truth. |
| Identity Authority | Establishes authoritative identity and Profile context used to attribute people in Group relationships. | Does not establish membership, Group state, Challenge participation or Group administration. |
| Participation Authority | May establish governed relationships among people, Groups and participation contexts under later approved lifecycle rules. | This standard assigns no membership states, transitions, final roles or Challenge participation rules. |
| Policy Authority | Establishes approved rules governing Group behavior, access and determinations where separately approved. | Does not create membership evidence, communication content, Group identity or Challenge outcomes. |
| Administrative Authority | May perform explicitly assigned high-impact Group actions under approved governance, least privilege and durable accountability. | A Group administrator receives no implied or unrestricted authority; final role names and powers remain deferred. |
| Presentation Authority | May communicate approved Group information to an approved audience. | Cannot establish or redefine membership, Group state, Challenge state, evidence or Derived Truth. |
| Governance Authority | Approves Group standards, authority boundaries, amendments and governed exceptions. | Does not silently convert pending Group decisions into approved rules. |
| Operational Authority | May support reliable and controlled Group-domain operation under later approved operational governance. | Does not acquire Group stewardship, override privacy or establish product truth outside explicit scope. |

Acceptance Authority and Calculation Authority remain outside authority over Group relationships and administration. The Group does not establish Accepted Activity Events, and Group administration does not calculate authoritative Progress, Completion, Ranking or Projections.

## 7. Ownership Model

This foundation describes Group ownership boundaries without creating the Entity Ownership Register.

| Constitutional relationship | Constitutional position |
|---|---|
| Governed subject | The Group is the Governed subject: a distinct collective context with attributable identity and purpose. |
| Information subject | The Group is the Information subject of Group information; each Person remains the Information subject of their Profile information. |
| Attributable originator | A Person may originate permitted Group expressions without thereby establishing Group state. |
| Authority to establish truth | Participation Authority may establish governed Group relationships only under later approved lifecycle rules. Other establishment authority remains deferred. |
| Accountable steward | Accountable stewardship remains deferred; membership alone does not establish it. |
| Custodian | A Custodian may preserve approved Group information within assigned scope but does not acquire Authority to establish truth. |
| Administrator | An Administrator may act only through explicitly assigned Administrative Authority and does not personally own Group information. |
| Operator | An Operator may support reliable Group operation within explicitly assigned Operational Authority. |
| Presenter | A Presenter may communicate Group information without establishing membership, Group state or Challenge truth. |
| Downstream user | A Downstream user may use approved Group information only for the declared purpose and does not acquire authority over it. |

Members do not acquire accountable stewardship of the Group merely through membership. Administrators do not personally acquire stewardship or Authority to establish all Group truth. Challenge information remains governed by the [Challenge Domain Standard](03-CHALLENGE-DOMAIN-STANDARD.md) and applicable Platform Authorities.

## 8. Relationships

| Related concept | Group relationship | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Profile | A Profile provides approved Identity and participation-supporting information relevant to a Group relationship. | The Group does not acquire stewardship of the Profile, Authority to establish Profile truth or access to all Profile information. |
| Person | A person may enter a governed relationship with a Group under later approved rules. | Being represented or discoverable does not establish membership. |
| Member | A Member is a person with a governed relationship to a Group. | Membership does not confer every administrative authority or automatic Challenge participation. |
| Participant | A Participant is a person with a governed relationship to a Challenge in the Group context. | Member and Participant are distinct relationships. |
| Challenge | A Challenge belongs to a Group in Version 2 and provides a governed shared undertaking. | The Group is not the Challenge and does not determine its lifecycle or outcomes. |
| Activity Event | An Accepted Activity Event may be associated with a Participant and Challenge in a Group context. | Group information and communication are not Submission Intent, an Accepted Activity Event or Evidence Eligibility. |
| Progress | Shared Progress may be presented within the Group context. | The Group does not calculate or establish authoritative Progress. |
| Ranking | A governed Ranking may be relevant to a Group Challenge where approved. | Group identity, administration or presentation does not establish Ranking. |
| Feed | A Feed may present governed group-relevant activity or communication. | Feed content is not an independent source of membership, activity or Progress truth. |
| Notification | A Notification may communicate a Group-related event, state or required attention. | Notification delivery or display does not establish the underlying event or state. |
| Knowledge | Governed Knowledge may define activities, Metrics, Units or constraints used by Group Challenges. | Group preferences or communication do not alter Knowledge or the Runtime Catalogue. |
| Administration | Administration may exercise explicitly approved Group-related authority under least privilege. | Administrative access is not ownership, unlimited authority or constitutional governance. |
| Analytics | Approved analytics may interpret Group-related authoritative information for a declared purpose. | Analytics does not alter Group, membership, Challenge or activity truth. |

## 9. Group Participation Context

The Group establishes the collective context in which relationships and Group Challenges may be governed. The following concepts remain distinct:

| Concept | Meaning in Group participation | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Person | The human subject who may hold governed Identity and participation relationships. | A Person is not a Group relationship merely by existing in the platform context. |
| Profile | The governed representation of Identity and participation-supporting information. | A Profile does not automatically establish membership or Challenge participation. |
| Member | A person with a governed relationship to a Group. | A Member is not automatically a Participant in every Challenge. |
| Participant | A person with a governed relationship to a Challenge. | Participation is Challenge-specific and is not identical to membership. |
| Group | The primary social and accountability context for Version 2 Challenges. | A Group is not a person, Profile, Member, Participant or Challenge. |
| Challenge | A governed shared undertaking through which Participants pursue a defined goal using measurable activities. | A Challenge is hosted in the Group context but remains a distinct governed concept. |

Being a Member does not necessarily establish participation in every Challenge. A Profile does not establish membership. Challenge participation is a governed relationship. Exact membership and participation rules, states, actors and transitions remain deferred.

## 10. Information Integrity Rules

1. Group membership does not create Profile ownership.
2. Group information does not establish Accepted Activity Events.
3. Group communication is not Submission Intent or activity evidence.
4. Group presentation does not establish membership, Group state or Challenge state.
5. Administrative access is not unrestricted ownership or authority.
6. Group-derived information shall remain attributable to approved inputs, Policy and Calculation Authority.
7. Group visibility does not imply public access.
8. Member count and similar values require a declared authority and attributable basis when represented as authoritative.
9. Local copies, summaries or presentations shall not become competing Group truth.
10. Group privacy controls shall be enforceable and truthfully described.
11. Authority over Group information or state shall not transfer silently.
12. Pending role names shall not be treated as approved authority.
13. Membership does not establish Challenge participation.
14. Communication, applause or other social expression shall not redefine Group, membership or Challenge truth.
15. Group convenience shall not override minimum necessary access or least privilege.

## 11. Visibility

Visibility defines the approved audience for Group information. It does not establish membership, authority or editing rights.

| Visibility class | Group-domain meaning | Constitutional constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Group information specifically approved for access without a membership relationship. | Public access is not the default and does not establish membership or access to member information. |
| Authenticated-discoverable | Minimal Group information approved for governed discovery by an authenticated person. | Discoverability differs from Group access and does not resolve final discovery or joining rules. |
| Shared-group | Group information approved for an applicable governed Group relationship. | This is the normal candidate class for participation information, but it does not automatically apply to every item or grant access to all Member information. |
| Private | Group information restricted to specifically authorized purposes or relationships. | Private classification does not create a hidden membership or unrestricted administrative right. |
| Privileged-operational | Group information available only to defined operational authority. | Access remains purpose-limited, reviewable, deny-by-default and constrained by least privilege. |

Shared-group visibility is relationship-dependent, not a general audience label. Final Group discoverability and access rules remain deferred.

## 12. Administrative Boundary

Group administration is a governed exercise of explicitly assigned authority. It:

- must be explicitly assigned;
- remains limited to an approved purpose and scope;
- remains constrained by privacy, minimum necessary use and least privilege;
- requires durable accountability for high-impact actions;
- remains subordinate to Platform, Policy and Governance Authority;
- does not create technical superuser rights;
- does not establish Accepted Activity Events or Derived Truth;
- does not establish final Group role vocabulary through this standard.

Membership, title, interface access or possession of Group information does not alone confer Administrative Authority.

## 13. Deferred Areas

This standard explicitly defers:

- canonical Group roles;
- Group ownership designation;
- membership lifecycle;
- invitations;
- join requests;
- removals;
- suspension;
- leaving;
- transfer of Group stewardship;
- Group deletion and archival;
- moderation;
- Group communication lifecycle;
- Group discovery rules;
- Group capacity;
- Challenge creation rights;
- concurrent Challenge rules;
- Member permissions;
- security controls;
- the Implementation Mapping.

No deferred role, authority, state, transition, rule or permission is approved by this standard.
