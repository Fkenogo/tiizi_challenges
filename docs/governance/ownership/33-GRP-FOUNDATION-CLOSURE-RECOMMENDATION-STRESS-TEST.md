# GRP Foundation Closure Phase 3 — Recommendation Stress Test

**Status:** Adversarial stress test; recommendations remain pending founder approval

**Date:** 2026-07-21

## 1. Purpose

This test subjects each Phase 2 recommendation to failure-oriented constitutional scenarios. It complements the full [Independent Constitutional Challenge](33-GRP-FOUNDATION-CLOSURE-INDEPENDENT-CONSTITUTIONAL-CHALLENGE.md) and does not approve any outcome.

## 2. Stress-test Scale

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pass | The model preserves the approved constitutional boundary without new doctrine. |
| Conditional pass | The model remains preferred, but a stated later-governance dependency is essential. |
| Fail | The model contradicts approved governance or is weaker than an existing viable alternative. |

## 3. Recommendation Results

| Recommendation | Primary attack | Result | Decisive reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| GFC-01-B | Administration becomes general Group truth Authority. | Conditional pass | EOG-02 expressly permits narrow creation authentication; continuing Group Authority must be excluded. |
| GFC-02-B | Charter cannot act or resolve contested succession. | Conditional pass | Charter supplies the rule, while later governance must separately establish reassignment truth. |
| GFC-03-A | Creator-authored terms become binding before Community deliberation. | Conditional pass | Founding applicability is simpler and continuity-supporting if Policy, Authority and amendment boundaries remain separate. |
| GFC-04-B | Collective responsibility becomes shared stewardship or unattributable duty. | Conditional pass | EOG-01 permits bounded responsibility assignment; later Council governance must preserve attribution and non-inference. |
| GFC-05-A | Permanent Entity ID implies allocation or premature classification. | Pass | EOG-02 already establishes a distinct Governed Subject and EOG-05 architecture separates inventory from allocation. |

## 4. Detailed Failure Conditions

### GFC-01-B fails if

- Administrative Authority is described as continuing Group Authority;
- authentication is used to establish later identity, purpose, Membership or Charter changes;
- creator origin, first Membership or initial Stewardship is treated as administrative allocation; or
- an Administrator role is treated as the Authority without an approved relationship.

### GFC-02-B fails if

- a Charter rule is treated as self-executing Authority;
- relinquishment can create a stewardship vacancy;
- Council membership, delegation or Administration can substitute for reassignment;
- exceptional succession can create more than one Accountable Steward; or
- inactivity alone is treated as stewardship failure.

### GFC-03-A fails if

- “binding” implies permissions, technical enforcement or unrestricted Member consent;
- creator authorship establishes every Charter truth;
- Charter content overrides Platform governance or applicable Policy;
- amendment silently reinterprets prior participation; or
- Charter applicability is used as a lifecycle state.

### GFC-04-B fails if

- Council responsibility implies Authority, Accountable Stewardship, Custody or Administration;
- a Council member acquires a relationship by membership alone;
- the Council becomes a second point of ultimate answerability;
- responsibility lacks declared purpose, scope, attribution or review boundary; or
- the Council's existence is treated as an allocation.

### GFC-05-A fails if

- permanent classification is treated as relationship allocation;
- Council information silently absorbs Group or member information;
- the permanent row settles composition, lifecycle or responsibility; or
- the EOG-05 amendment occurs without an attributable approval and validation trace.

## 5. Ten-year Stress Test

| Evolution pressure | Preferred package behavior | Residual burden |
|---|---|---|
| Large increase in Group volume | Creation scope remains uniform and attributable. | Administrative authentication oversight must scale without expanding Authority. |
| Creator departure becomes common | Successor-continuous reassignment remains the ordinary rule. | Charter quality and exceptional reassignment governance must mature. |
| Charters evolve substantially | One Charter identity can preserve historically intelligible amendments. | Applicability and interpretation governance become essential. |
| Councils become common | Stable Council identity and bounded responsibility permit consistent treatment. | Composition and attribution standards must prevent collective-accountability drift. |
| Some Groups remain inactive | Existence and governance integrity remain separate from activity volume. | No new constitutional burden. |
| Organizational roles change | EOG-01 relationships remain independent of titles. | Allocation records must remain separately declared. |
| Council model becomes unnecessary for a Group | Permanent entity treatment does not require a Council instance for every Group. | Later lifecycle governance must distinguish constitutional entity type from actual establishment. |
| Governance philosophy changes | Attributable amendment remains possible. | Creation Authority and Charter applicability changes would have material historical impact. |

## 6. Stress-test Verdict

**No recommendation fails constitutional challenge.** Four require express conditions in any future approval or drafting phase. Those conditions refine boundaries already present in EOG-01 and EOG-02; they do not add new Platform Authorities, allocations, lifecycle or implementation.
