# EOG-01 — Platform Accountability Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework defines the universal accountability relationships Tiizi may later assign to Governed subjects, information and actions.

It establishes what each relationship means, why it exists and where its constitutional boundary ends. It enables later allocation work to distinguish accountability from truth-establishment, preservation, operation, contribution, participation, delegation and presentation.

This framework does not assign any relationship to a Group, Challenge, Activity Event, Knowledge Asset, Verification, Recognition or any other Governed subject.

## 2. Constitutional Context

The [Constitutional Ontology](../00-TIIZI-CONSTITUTIONAL-ONTOLOGY-AND-FOUNDATIONAL-PRODUCT-CONCEPTS.md) explains what exists in the Tiizi world. The [Founder Philosophy and Design Doctrine](../00A-TIIZI-FOUNDER-PHILOSOPHY-AND-DESIGN-DOCTRINE.md) guides choices among constitutionally valid futures. The [Platform Constitution](../platform/01-TIIZI-PLATFORM-CONSTITUTION.md) and [Platform Authority Model](../platform/10-PLATFORM-AUTHORITY-MODEL.md) govern the authority boundaries within which accountability relationships operate.

The [Entity Ownership Foundation](../platform/06-ENTITY-OWNERSHIP-FOUNDATION.md) establishes that ownership means accountable stewardship and that Authority, stewardship, custody, administration, operation, presentation, origin and use remain distinct.

This framework supplies universal relationship semantics. Later governance may allocate those relationships, but may not redefine them for local convenience.

## 3. Scope and Non-Allocation Boundary

### Included

- canonical accountability-relationship definitions;
- the purpose and boundary of each relationship;
- distinctions among relationships;
- rules for combining relationships without conflating them;
- delegation and assignment principles at a constitutional level;
- terminology required by later registers and standards.

### Excluded

- assignment of any relationship to any Governed subject;
- designation of any person, organization, role, team or system;
- EOG-02 through EOG-06 outcomes;
- lifecycle states or transitions;
- permissions or access rules;
- privacy and security controls;
- operating procedures;
- technical architecture or implementation.

### Interpretive Rule

Examples in this framework are abstract. They illustrate relationship boundaries only. They do not constitute an allocation, delegation, permission or approval.

## 4. Foundational Accountability Principles

### 4.1 One Relationship, One Declared Meaning

Every relationship shall use one canonical meaning. A local title, role label or organizational custom shall not silently redefine it.

### 4.2 One Authoritative Fact, One Declared Authority

Every authoritative fact, state, calculation, Knowledge item or decision shall have one declared Platform Authority. Accountability relationships do not create a competing source of truth.

### 4.3 One Governed Subject, One Accountable Steward

Each Governed subject shall have one declared Accountable steward when later allocated. Collaboration, delegation, custody and administration do not create equal stewardship by implication.

### 4.4 Stewardship Is Not Authority

An Accountable steward is answerable for the coherent governance of a subject. The steward does not establish every truth concerning that subject unless a separate Platform Authority is explicitly assigned.

### 4.5 Custody Is Not Stewardship

A Custodian preserves or handles information for an approved purpose. Possession, storage or protection does not make the Custodian the Accountable steward or source Authority.

### 4.6 Operation Is Not Truth

An Operator supports reliability and continuity within approved scope. Successful operation does not establish product, participation, Knowledge or Derived Truth.

### 4.7 Origin Is Not Acceptance

An Attributable originator creates an expression or source material. Origin preserves authorship and accountability for the expression; it does not establish accepted or derived truth.

### 4.8 Contribution Is Not Approval

A Contributor supplies effort, content, expertise or source material. Contribution does not approve, accept, govern or calculate the resulting information.

### 4.9 Participation Is Contextual

A Participant holds a governed relationship to an Undertaking. Participation may provide context for expression and contribution, but does not confer general Authority, stewardship, custody or administration.

### 4.10 Presentation Is Not Source Truth

A Presenter communicates information without acquiring authority over the truth communicated.

### 4.11 Use Does Not Transfer Accountability

A Downstream user consumes information for an approved purpose. Use does not transfer Authority, stewardship, custody or canonical meaning.

### 4.12 Delegation Must Be Explicit

Delegation or assignment shall identify its source, recipient, purpose, scope and review boundary. No relationship transfers silently.

### 4.13 Relationship Stacking Remains Explicit

One actor may later hold more than one relationship where governance permits, but each relationship must be declared separately. Holding one does not imply another.

### 4.14 Visibility, Access and Possession Create No Relationship

Seeing, accessing, holding, storing, invoking or presenting information does not by itself create Authority, stewardship, custody, administration or any other accountability relationship.

## 5. Canonical Accountability Relationships

The framework recognizes the following universal relationships. It does not create a new Platform Authority type.

### 5.1 Authority

**Definition:** Authority is the approved right and responsibility to establish, accept, calculate, govern, change or communicate platform information or state for a declared purpose.

**Constitutional purpose:** Ensure that governed truth and consequential action have a legitimate, attributable source.

**Boundary:** Authority exists only within declared purpose and scope. It does not arise from ownership, stewardship, contribution, participation, access, visibility, possession, role title or technical capability.

**Relationship to other concepts:** Authority is exercised through one of the eleven canonical Platform Authority types. This framework does not add, remove or allocate a Platform Authority.

### 5.2 Authority to Establish Truth

**Definition:** Authority to establish truth is the specific approved relationship through which a Platform Authority establishes a particular authoritative fact, state, calculation, Knowledge item or decision for a declared purpose.

**Constitutional purpose:** Identify the source of each authoritative truth without treating every exercise of Authority as interchangeable.

**Boundary:** It applies only to the named truth and context. It does not confer stewardship, custody, access to unrelated information or Authority over adjacent facts.

**Relationship to Authority:** It is a specific application of a canonical Platform Authority, not a twelfth Authority type.

### 5.3 Steward — Canonical Formal Term: Accountable Steward

**Definition:** An Accountable steward is the declared party answerable for the coherent stewardship of a Governed subject within approved constitutional boundaries.

**Constitutional purpose:** Ensure that every Governed subject has one durable point of accountability for its integrity, purpose, governance coherence and unresolved risks.

**Boundary:** Stewardship does not itself confer Authority to establish truth, custody, administration, operation, presentation, unrestricted access or personal ownership. The steward need not perform every task concerning the subject.

**Terminology rule:** “Accountable steward” is the formal governance term. “Steward” may be used as plain-language shorthand only when the accountable relationship is unambiguous. Formal registers and allocations should use “Accountable steward.”

### 5.4 Custodian

**Definition:** A Custodian is a party entrusted to preserve, protect or handle information within an approved purpose and scope.

**Constitutional purpose:** Preserve continuity, integrity, availability and appropriate handling without transferring meaning or source Authority.

**Boundary:** Custody does not confer Authority to establish truth, Accountable stewardship, authorship, administration or unrestricted use. Mere possession or storage is not custody unless governance declares the custodial purpose and responsibility.

**Multiplicity boundary:** Later governance may assign one or more purpose-limited custodial relationships where necessary. Multiple Custodians do not create multiple Accountable stewards or source Authorities.

### 5.5 Operator

**Definition:** An Operator is an actor explicitly assigned Operational Authority for a declared purpose and scope.

**Constitutional purpose:** Support reliable, controlled and continuous Platform Operation.

**Boundary:** Operation does not establish product truth, Policy, Knowledge, accepted evidence, Derived Truth or permanent governance exceptions. Operational urgency does not expand Authority.

**Relationship to custody:** An Operator may also receive a separate custody assignment, but operation and custody remain distinct.

### 5.6 Administrator

**Definition:** An Administrator is an actor explicitly assigned Administrative Authority for a declared purpose and scope.

**Constitutional purpose:** Perform approved high-impact administrative decisions or actions under least privilege and durable accountability.

**Boundary:** Administration does not confer unrestricted Authority, Accountable stewardship, custody, source truth or immunity from review. An administrative action cannot silently replace the Authority responsible for the underlying domain fact.

**Relationship to operation:** Administration governs approved consequential action; operation supports continuity. The same actor may hold both only through separate assignments.

### 5.7 Contributor

**Definition:** A Contributor is an attributable party that supplies effort, content, expertise, source material or other input to a governed purpose.

**Constitutional purpose:** Preserve the origin and value of input without overstating its approval or Authority.

**Boundary:** Contribution does not establish canonical meaning, acceptance, Evidence Eligibility, calculation, stewardship, custody or administrative control. A contribution may be accepted, rejected, transformed or used only under the applicable governance.

**Relationship to origin:** A Contributor is normally an Attributable originator of the supplied input. An Attributable originator need not be a Contributor to the wider governed purpose.

### 5.8 Participant

**Definition:** A Participant is a Person with a governed relationship to a Challenge or other future approved Undertaking.

**Constitutional purpose:** Identify voluntary participation context and preserve attribution for permitted participant expression.

**Boundary:** Participation does not automatically confer Authority over accepted facts, other Participants, the Undertaking, Progress, administration, stewardship or custody. Membership and participation remain distinct.

**Relationship to contribution:** A Participant may contribute or originate permitted expressions, but participation and contribution remain separate relationships.

### 5.9 Subject

“Subject” is not sufficiently precise for formal allocation. Tiizi distinguishes two canonical relationships.

#### Governed Subject

**Definition:** A Governed subject is an entity, relationship or information object governed as a distinct subject under Tiizi standards.

**Constitutional purpose:** Identify what governance, accountability and later lifecycle standards concern.

**Boundary:** Being the Governed subject does not make that subject an actor, Authority, Accountable steward or Custodian.

#### Information Subject

**Definition:** An Information subject is the Person, entity or governed matter to which information relates.

**Constitutional purpose:** Identify whom or what information concerns without confusing the subject with its originator, steward, Custodian or Authority.

**Boundary:** Being the Information subject does not confer Authority, possession, access or control over every use of that information.

**Terminology rule:** Formal governance shall use “Governed subject” or “Information subject.” Unqualified “Subject” may appear only in explanatory prose where the meaning cannot be mistaken.

### 5.10 Attributable Originator

**Definition:** An Attributable originator is the identifiable source of an expression or submitted information.

**Constitutional purpose:** Preserve provenance and responsibility for what was expressed or supplied.

**Boundary:** Origin does not establish authoritative truth, acceptance, Evidence Eligibility, stewardship or custody.

**Relationship to Contributor and Participant:** A Contributor or Participant may be an Attributable originator, but the relationships are not synonymous.

### 5.11 Delegate

**Definition:** A Delegate is a party that receives an explicit, limited delegation of Authority or assignment of responsibility from a party entitled to confer it.

**Constitutional purpose:** Permit accountable distribution of action while preserving scope, source and reviewability.

**Boundary:** A Delegate receives only what is explicitly delegated or assigned. Delegation does not create a new Authority type, exceed the source's scope, transfer unrelated Authority, confer stewardship automatically or erase the source of the delegation.

**Terminology rule:** The delegated relationship must state whether it concerns Authority, responsibility, custody, administration or operation. “Delegate” alone is not a sufficient allocation.

### 5.12 Delegating Party

**Definition:** A Delegating party is the attributable party that explicitly delegates Authority or assigns responsibility within its own approved scope.

**Constitutional purpose:** Preserve the legitimacy and provenance of delegated action.

**Boundary:** A party cannot delegate what it does not hold. Delegation does not remove the delegating party's accountability for the validity and scope of the delegation. Stewardship can change only through separately governed reassignment, not ordinary delegation.

### 5.13 Presenter

**Definition:** A Presenter is an actor or Authority that communicates authoritative, derived or contextual information without acquiring Authority over the underlying truth.

**Constitutional purpose:** Make information understandable to an approved audience while preserving source, status and limitations.

**Boundary:** Presentation cannot create or alter accepted facts, Derived Truth, Policy, Knowledge, stewardship or custody. Prominence and repetition do not increase Authority.

### 5.14 Downstream User

**Definition:** A Downstream user is an approved consumer of information for a declared purpose.

**Constitutional purpose:** Enable governed reuse while preserving the source's meaning, Authority, privacy and accountability boundaries.

**Boundary:** Use does not transfer Authority, stewardship, custody, authorship or permission for a different purpose. A Downstream user may derive new information only where separately governed.

## 6. Relationships That Are Not Interchangeable

| Relationship pair | Constitutional distinction |
|---|---|
| Authority and Accountable steward | Authority establishes a declared truth or action; the steward is answerable for the coherent governance of the subject. |
| Accountable steward and Custodian | The steward is accountable for the subject; the Custodian preserves or handles information for a purpose. |
| Custodian and Operator | Custody concerns preservation and handling; operation concerns continuity and controlled performance. |
| Administrator and Operator | Administration performs approved consequential action; operation supports reliable continuity. |
| Contributor and Authority | Contribution supplies input; Authority determines or establishes only within declared scope. |
| Participant and Contributor | Participation is a governed relationship; contribution is attributable input. Either may exist without the other where later governance permits. |
| Participant and Authority | Participation may support personal expression but creates no general source Authority. |
| Attributable originator and Authority | Origin identifies who expressed or supplied information; Authority determines its authoritative status. |
| Governed subject and Information subject | The Governed subject is what standards govern; the Information subject is whom or what the information concerns. |
| Delegate and Accountable steward | A Delegate receives limited Authority or responsibility; the steward relationship does not transfer unless separately reassigned. |
| Presenter and Authority to establish truth | A Presenter communicates source truth; presentation does not create that truth. |
| Downstream user and Custodian | Use consumes information for a purpose; custody carries preservation or handling responsibility. |

## 7. Responsibility and Answerability

Each relationship carries a different form of answerability:

| Relationship | Answerable for |
|---|---|
| Authority | Legitimacy, basis, scope and attribution of the authoritative action or truth established. |
| Accountable steward | Coherent governance, integrity, unresolved risk and accountable coordination concerning the Governed subject. |
| Custodian | Preservation, protection and handling within the declared custodial purpose. |
| Operator | Reliable and controlled operation within declared scope. |
| Administrator | Legitimacy, scope and attribution of the administrative action performed. |
| Contributor | Provenance and integrity of the contribution as supplied. |
| Participant | Attribution and integrity of permitted participant expressions and commitments. |
| Attributable originator | Authorship or source of the originating expression or information. |
| Delegate | Action within the exact delegated or assigned boundary. |
| Delegating party | Legitimacy, clarity and scope of the delegation or assignment. |
| Presenter | Faithful communication of source, status, meaning and limitations. |
| Downstream user | Use within approved purpose without changing source meaning or Authority. |

Answerability does not make these relationships equal. It describes the distinct concern for which each may later be held accountable.

## 8. Delegation and Assignment Boundaries

### Delegation of Authority

Authority may be delegated only explicitly and within the delegating Authority's own scope. The delegation must name purpose, scope, source, recipient and review boundary. It remains attributable and revocable under approved governance.

### Assignment of Responsibility

A task, custody duty, operational duty, administrative responsibility or presentation responsibility may be assigned where later governance permits. Assignment does not grant Authority unless Authority is separately and explicitly delegated.

### Stewardship Reassignment

Ordinary delegation does not transfer Accountable stewardship. A change of Accountable steward is a governed reassignment of accountability and remains subject to later allocation and lifecycle standards.

### No Silent Substitution

A Delegate, Custodian, Operator, Administrator or Presenter does not become the source Authority merely because the original Authority is unavailable or because the delegated party performs the visible action.

## 9. Relationship Composition

Later governance may permit one actor to hold several relationships. Composition is valid only when:

1. every relationship is named separately;
2. each relationship has its own purpose and scope;
3. conflicts of interest are governed where applicable;
4. the actor does not use one relationship to expand another;
5. attribution identifies which relationship justified each consequential action;
6. least privilege and privacy continue to constrain the combined assignments.

No role title creates a relationship bundle automatically.

## 10. Prohibited Accountability Patterns

The following patterns are prohibited:

1. treating Accountable stewardship as unrestricted Authority;
2. treating Authority as personal ownership of the Governed subject;
3. treating custody or storage as source truth;
4. treating operational control as Policy or product truth;
5. treating administration as universal Authority;
6. treating contribution as approval;
7. treating participation as stewardship or Authority over others;
8. treating origin as acceptance;
9. treating presentation as calculation or source truth;
10. treating downstream use as permission to redefine meaning;
11. using an unqualified “Subject” where Governed subject and Information subject could differ;
12. allowing delegation to exceed or obscure its source;
13. allowing a role name, title, access level, interface or technical capability to imply an undeclared relationship;
14. silently assigning an accountability relationship through custom or repeated practice.

## 11. Relationship Declaration Standard

A later allocation should declare:

- the relationship type;
- the Governed subject or information concerned;
- the declared purpose;
- the scope and boundary;
- the attributable party or governed role;
- any source Authority or delegating party;
- its review or reassignment dependency;
- its relationship to other declared accountability relationships.

This is a governance declaration standard, not a permissions, lifecycle or implementation specification.

## 12. Deferred Areas

This framework defers:

- every entity-level allocation;
- the EOG-01 allocation-pattern decision across register rows;
- EOG-02 through EOG-06;
- named organizational roles;
- Group and Challenge stewardship;
- Verification, correction and Recognition accountability;
- lifecycle, permission, privacy and security standards;
- delegation procedures and role mechanics;
- custody operations and retention;
- implementation mapping.

## 13. Governance

### Status

**Status:** Draft for founder review. No accountability relationship is allocated by this framework.

### Precedence

Document 00, approved constitutional governance and the Platform Authority Model take precedence. This framework refines relationship semantics only and cannot create a new Platform Authority or override an existing boundary.

### Decision Dependency

Founder approval of the framework vocabulary and distinctions is required before they become the mandatory basis for later allocation. Approval of this framework would not approve any entity allocation.
