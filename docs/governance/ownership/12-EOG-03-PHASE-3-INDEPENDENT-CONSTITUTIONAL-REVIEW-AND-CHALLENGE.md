# EOG-03 Phase 3 — Independent Constitutional Review and Challenge

**Review status:** Complete

**Subject reviewed:** EOG-03 Founder Review Draft 1

**Review date:** 2026-07-20

**Review posture:** Adversarial constitutional review; no amendment or approval

## 1. Executive Summary

EOG-03 Draft 1 has a sound constitutional architecture. Its strongest contribution is the separation of Platform Knowledge, Policy, Group context, runtime representation and historical use through the doctrine of Constitutional Composition. It also correctly subordinates Templates, Creation Mechanisms and Runtime Projections and preserves the distinction between contribution and Knowledge Authority.

The draft is not yet ready for approval without refinement. The review found no architectural failure, no newly created Platform Authority and no formal allocation of an Accountable Steward, Custodian, Administrator, Operator or Delegate. It did identify several sentences that could be interpreted as hidden authority or responsibility allocations, despite the draft's express disclaimers.

Four matters require attention before approval:

1. actor-like wording says Platform Knowledge or a Knowledge Asset “establishes” Authoritative Meaning, while higher-order governance assigns that act to Knowledge Authority;
2. the future-contribution chain omits Knowledge Authority between Governed Review and Authoritative Meaning, creating an avoidable implication that review or Platform governance establishes canonical truth;
3. Historical Meaning is presented as the fourth level of an ordered meaning stack even though it is better understood, under the draft's own provisions, as preservation of the other applicable meanings across time;
4. the blanket exclusion of Tags and Categories is not fully reconciled with the approved Knowledge Asset Domain Standard's recognition of Taxonomy or Controlled-Vocabulary Knowledge Assets.

These are bounded constitutional-precision problems. They can be corrected without redesigning EOG-03, reopening EOG-01 or EOG-02, allocating accountability, or resolving KNW-02 through KNW-04.

**Independent verdict:** **Ready after minor refinements.**

## 2. Constitutional Strengths

### 2.1 Clear constitutional purpose

The draft consistently treats Platform Knowledge as reusable governed meaning supporting truthful collective participation rather than as catalogue records or implementation data. Sections 1, 3 and 5 maintain constitutional scope and exclude technical design.

### 2.2 Strong composition doctrine

Sections 10 and 11 establish a durable distinction among Platform Knowledge, applicable Policy, Group configuration, Community context and the resulting Group Challenge. The explicit rejection of copying, inheritance, modification and forking materially reduces future source-of-truth ambiguity.

### 2.3 Strong negative boundaries

Templates, Creation Mechanisms, Runtime Projections, Historical Representations, local representations and discovery concepts are denied independent Knowledge Authority. These negative definitions are constitutionally useful because each concept could otherwise become a competing source through convenience or repeated use.

### 2.4 Preservation of higher-order authority separation

The authority matrix in section 8 correctly preserves the eleven Platform Authorities and their principal boundaries. Knowledge does not prove occurrence; Policy does not manufacture evidence; Acceptance does not calculate outcomes; Calculation does not invent Knowledge; Presentation does not redefine truth.

### 2.5 Accountability restraint

Section 9 explicitly constrains “Groups own their governed experiences” as a domain boundary rather than an EOG-01 allocation. Sections 3, 8, 16 and 18 repeatedly defer Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration, Operation, roles and permissions.

### 2.6 Historical-integrity restraint

Sections 14 and 15 preserve historical intelligibility without approving versions, snapshots, retention rules or lifecycle states. KNW-02 and KNW-03 remain expressly pending.

### 2.7 Future contribution without decentralised truth

Section 16 correctly states that a Contributor is an Attributable Originator and that contribution alone does not create canonical meaning, approval or accountability relationships. This is aligned with EOG-01.

### 2.8 Status truthfulness

The draft, notes, checklist and self-validation all state that Draft 1 is non-governing until separately approved. Self-validation is not represented as founder approval.

## 4. Consistency Findings

### 4.1 Internal consistency

The main doctrines are mutually compatible:

- Platform Knowledge supplies reusable governed meaning.
- Knowledge Authority establishes canonical meaning.
- Policy governs permitted behavior and use.
- Group context contributes local configuration and purpose.
- Constitutional Composition forms a distinct Challenge without transferring source Authority.
- runtime and historical representations remain subordinate.

The principal internal tensions are CW-01, CW-03 and CW-09. They concern who establishes meaning and how meaning is preserved, not the validity of the underlying architecture.

### 4.2 Alignment with the Platform Constitution and Platform Authority Model

The draft substantially aligns with the canonical authority chain. Its section 8 matrix mirrors the Platform Authority Model's separation among Knowledge, Policy, Acceptance, Calculation, Participation and Presentation.

CW-01 and CW-02 are the exceptions. Higher-order governance states that **Knowledge Authority establishes approved meaning**. A Knowledge Asset is the Governed Subject of that meaning, not the authority actor. Governed Review may provide an approval basis but cannot replace Knowledge Authority.

### 4.3 Alignment with EOG-01

EOG-01 relationship meanings are preserved in express provisions. No formal Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration, Operation, Delegate or Presenter allocation is made.

The phrases identified in CW-05 and CW-06 are nevertheless accountability leakage risks. They use capability or responsibility language without declaring whether the statement concerns a Governed Subject, Authority, Accountable Steward, Administrator, Operator or Contributor.

### 4.4 Alignment with EOG-02

The draft preserves Group persistence, the separation of Group existence from participation, and the Group-versus-Challenge boundary. It does not reopen Group creation authority, stewardship succession, Charter governance, moderation or membership lifecycle.

The Group Experience wording is compatible with EOG-02 only because the draft immediately denies formal accountability allocation. CW-05 should be addressed so the supporting verbs cannot be misread as permissions granted to a Group as actor.

### 4.5 Alignment with the Knowledge Asset Domain Standard

Runtime Projection subordination, Knowledge/Policy separation, historical intelligibility and the launch-domain boundaries align well.

The unresolved exception is the distinction between discovery-oriented Tags and Categories and constitutionally governed taxonomy or controlled-vocabulary meaning. DN-10 identifies the problem but Draft 1 still contains both positions without a final constitutional boundary.

## 5. Authority Boundary Review

### 5.1 Formal allocation result

No formal allocation was found for:

- Accountable Steward;
- Custodian;
- Administrator;
- Operator;
- Delegate;
- Presenter.

No twelfth Platform Authority is explicitly introduced.

### 5.2 Implied allocation risks

Three risks remain:

1. **Knowledge-as-actor:** “Platform Knowledge establishes” and asset definitions using “establishes” may assign Authority to a Governed Subject by grammar.
2. **Platform responsibility:** section 16.1 may assign undefined accountability to “the Platform.”
3. **Group capability:** section 9 may imply that the Group itself has action authority rather than providing the governed context in which later-approved actors act.

These do not amount to completed allocations because sections 3, 8, 9 and 18 disclaim them. Constitutional text should not depend on a later disclaimer to neutralise an earlier actor-like statement.

## 6. Composition Doctrine Review

### 6.1 Stress-test result

The doctrine passes the principal tests:

- it is technology-neutral;
- no field, record, interface or storage structure is prescribed;
- composition is explicitly not copying;
- composition is explicitly not inheritance;
- composition is explicitly not modification;
- composition is explicitly not forking;
- no Authority transfer is permitted;
- the result has distinct constitutional identity.

### 6.2 Edge cases requiring clarification

**Partial representation.** A Challenge may consume a Runtime Projection rather than a rich Knowledge Asset in practice. The doctrine must continue to regard the source Platform Knowledge and Authoritative Meaning—not the projection—as the Knowledge input. Draft 1 implies this but does not connect sections 10 and 14 expressly.

**Multiple Knowledge Assets.** The doctrine uses “Platform Knowledge” collectively and therefore can support one or more assets without specifying cardinality. No amendment is required.

**Conflicting inputs.** The integrity rules prohibit Community context from overriding Knowledge or Policy, but the draft does not state what constitutional treatment applies when inputs conflict. Deferral is appropriate because resolution belongs to Policy and later creation governance; a Creation Mechanism must not become the decision authority by default.

**Policy applicability.** A Template may reference Policy, but cannot make that Policy applicable merely by selection. The draft should make this boundary unmistakable without defining an applicability workflow.

**Future undertaking forms.** Constitutional Composition is durable for Group Challenges but is not yet fully durable for a future community undertaking with a different product classification. This is a scope formulation risk, not a flaw in the four-input model.

## 7. Authoritative Meaning Review

### 7.1 Precedence result

The document structurally places Authoritative Meaning before Platform Knowledge Asset types, Templates, runtime representations and Historical Representation. Subordinate concepts consistently depend on source meaning and are prohibited from redefining it.

The exception is grammatical rather than structural: sections 4, 6 and 7 alternate between Knowledge Authority as the establisher and Platform Knowledge or the Asset as the establisher. This should not remain in a constitutional standard.

### 7.2 “Meaning precedes representation”

An explicit constitutional statement that **meaning precedes representation** would be genuinely beneficial.

The principle is already supported by:

- the one-meaning rule;
- Runtime Projection subordination;
- Template and Creation Mechanism subordination;
- historical non-fork rules;
- presentation's inability to redefine truth.

Making the dependency explicit would prevent future records, projections, snapshots, labels or interfaces from becoming authoritative merely because they are the visible representation. It would refine existing doctrine rather than create new governance.

The principle should not be interpreted as a lifecycle sequence, publication workflow or claim that meaning can exist without an attributable Governed Subject and Knowledge Authority.

## 8. Layered Meaning Review

### 8.1 Completeness

No additional substantive meaning layer is required for the present composition doctrine.

Activity Event, Evidence Eligibility, Calculation and Presentation should not be added as meaning layers:

- an Activity Event establishes occurrence in context, not canonical Knowledge meaning;
- Evidence Eligibility is a Policy determination for a declared calculation;
- Calculation establishes Derived Truth;
- Presentation communicates existing truth.

Adding any of them would weaken the authority separation the draft is designed to preserve.

### 8.2 Internal structure

Platform Meaning, Policy Meaning and Community Meaning can coexist as distinct contextual contributions if “Meaning” is not treated as identical Authority.

Historical Meaning is different. It is not an additional source input alongside the first three. It is the historically applicable preservation of those meanings and their context. Treating all four as a descending stack risks implying that Historical Meaning may add or override meaning.

### 8.3 Future-proofing

The model is future-proof if understood as distinct, non-overriding dimensions with a temporal preservation view. It is less durable if treated as a strict hierarchy. No fifth layer should be added; the relationship among the four should be clarified.

## 9. Constitutional Dependency Assessment

Draft 1 contains two legitimate dependency structures:

1. the higher-order Information Authority Chain, in which Knowledge and Policy precede participation, acceptance and calculation;
2. the composition relationship, in which source meaning and Policy constrain Group configuration and Community context without transferring Authority.

The proposed broad dependency—Platform Knowledge → Groups → Challenges → Participation → Activity → Historical Understanding—should **not** be adopted as a literal constitutional chain. It collapses distinct types of relationship:

- a Group does not derive its existence from Platform Knowledge;
- participation is voluntary and does not establish Group existence under EOG-02;
- Activity occurrence is established through Acceptance Authority, not by the Challenge alone;
- historical understanding preserves prior context rather than becoming the terminal source of truth.

What should become explicit is a narrower dependency principle:

- downstream representation and use depend on the applicable source meaning;
- dependency does not transfer Authority;
- historical preservation does not reverse or recreate source Authority.

This materially improves governance without creating a lifecycle or process chain.

If future community undertakings remain Challenges, no amendment is needed. If Tiizi later introduces a governed undertaking that is not a Challenge, EOG-03 would require amendment unless the doctrine distinguishes the general composition principle from its Version 2 Group Challenge application.

## 12. Founder Checklist Review

### 12.1 Strengths

The checklist covers the principal doctrines, all four composition negatives, the eleven-authority boundary, runtime subordination, contribution restraint, Discovery separation and pending KNW decisions. It correctly requires a separate approval record.

### 12.2 Missing confirmations

The checklist should additionally require founder confirmation that:

1. Knowledge Authority—not Platform Knowledge as a subject, record or corpus—establishes Authoritative Meaning;
2. Governed Review supplies a governed basis but does not itself establish Authoritative Meaning;
3. Runtime Catalogue “authority” is not a new Platform Authority, role or accountability allocation;
4. Historical Meaning preserves other applicable meaning and is not an independent source layer;
5. discovery Tags/Categories are distinguished from any governed controlled-vocabulary meaning retained under the Knowledge Asset Domain Standard;
6. a Template or Creation Mechanism cannot establish which Policy is constitutionally applicable merely by reference, selection or validation;
7. the Challenge-specific scope of Constitutional Composition is intentional or should be generalised before approval;
8. “Meaning precedes representation” is accepted as a constitutional dependency principle.

### 12.3 Duplication

The checklist deliberately decomposes composite decisions, but several items restate the same no-authority-transfer proposition across sections 5, 6, 7, 9 and 10. This is not harmful, though a founder review agenda could group them under one boundary decision while retaining the detailed checks as evidence.

### 12.4 Items that belong in EOG-03 rather than only the checklist

If approved, “Meaning precedes representation,” the Governed Review/Knowledge Authority boundary, and the Historical Meaning topology should be stated in EOG-03 itself. A checklist cannot cure ambiguity in the constitutional instrument it reviews.

## 14. Final Constitutional Verdict

**Ready after minor refinements.**

The draft's architecture, philosophy and core boundaries are constitutionally defensible. Constitutional Composition is coherent and implementation-neutral; EOG-01 and EOG-02 are preserved; no new Platform Authority or formal accountability allocation is created; Templates, Creation Mechanisms and runtime representations remain subordinate; future contribution does not decentralise truth.

Approval should wait until CR-01 through CR-03 are addressed and the recommended meaning/history/runtime clarifications are resolved. These changes are local to constitutional precision and do not require substantive architectural revision.
