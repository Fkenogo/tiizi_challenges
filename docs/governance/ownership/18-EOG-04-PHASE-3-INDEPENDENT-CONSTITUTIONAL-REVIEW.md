# EOG-04 Phase 3 — Independent Constitutional Review

**Document reviewed:** [EOG-04 Constitutional Governance of Challenges — Founder Review Draft 1](17-EOG-04-CONSTITUTIONAL-GOVERNANCE-OF-CHALLENGES-DRAFT-1.md)

**Review status:** Independent constitutional critique complete

**Review date:** 2026-07-20

**Review posture:** Draft 1 was treated as unproven. This report identifies constitutional defects and risks without rewriting the draft.

## 1. Executive Summary

EOG-04 Founder Review Draft 1 is constitutionally coherent, architecturally complete and faithful to the approved Challenge drafting contract. It preserves the group-first product identity, inherits Constitutional Composition from EOG-03, keeps the Challenge distinct from its inputs and adjacent subjects, uses only the eleven approved Platform Authorities, allocates no accountability relationship, and defers every pending lifecycle, calculation, role, permission and implementation decision.

The draft's strongest contribution is its treatment of Challenge identity after composition. It correctly describes the Challenge as a distinct Governed Subject and Information Subject while refusing to make it an actor or Authority. Its bounded treatment of Challenge Identity Continuity is disciplined: representations do not alter identity, but material-change and replacement tests remain deferred.

No critical structural defect, hidden accountability allocation or substantive contradiction was found.

Five narrow constitutional refinements are recommended before founder approval:

1. remove two unsupported Information Subject assertions in section 7.2;
2. expand the compressed information chain in section 11.5 so Participation Context cannot appear to exercise Authority and Participant Authority remains visible;
3. align Runtime Catalogue wording precisely with EOG-03 so the Catalogue cannot appear to govern as an actor;
4. restore the Authority Model's explicit Governance Authority escalation rule for unresolved representation conflicts; and
5. make the identity-continuity attribution requirement explicit for every representation claiming to concern the same Challenge.

These are precision corrections, not architectural changes. The draft does not require redesign.

**Final verdict:** **Ready after minor constitutional refinements.**

## 3. Constitutional Weaknesses

### 3.1 Unsupported subject classifications in section 7.2

Draft section 7.2 states:

> Platform Knowledge remains the Governed Subject and Information Subject of its own Knowledge meaning.

Approved EOG-03 defines Platform Knowledge as the body of authoritative reusable meaning. It defines Knowledge Assets as governed units with distinct identity. It does not establish Platform Knowledge itself as one Governed Subject or Information Subject.

The same paragraph states:

> Derived Truth retains the information subjects declared by its governing calculation and Policy.

The approved Authority Model authorizes Calculation Authority to establish Derived Truth and Policy Authority to govern applicable rules and Evidence Eligibility. It does not establish “governing calculation and Policy” as actors that declare Information Subjects.

Both sentences reach beyond the source corpus. They are local precision defects rather than defects in the draft's central Challenge identity.

### 3.2 Authority compression in the information chain

Draft section 11.5 combines **Applicable Policy and Participation Context** and says they “govern expression and evaluation of” Submission Intent.

Participation Context is not an Authority. Applicable Policy supplies rules, Participation Authority establishes relationship context where approved, and Participant Authority establishes Submission Intent. Compressing them into one node obscures those distinctions even though adjacent sections state them correctly.

The chain is labelled constitutional dependence rather than workflow, but it should still preserve every source Authority accurately.

### 3.3 Runtime Catalogue grammatical agency

Draft section 16.2 says:

> The Runtime Catalogue governs availability of approved Knowledge representations within the existing Knowledge Authority boundary.

The same paragraph correctly says the Runtime Catalogue is not an Authority, actor or role. EOG-03 is more precise: the Runtime Catalogue is a governed runtime-availability function through which Knowledge Authority establishes availability.

The current grammar can make a non-actor appear to exercise governance. This is a narrow authority-language inconsistency.

### 3.4 Conflict escalation is incomplete

Draft section 14.6 says conflicting representations must be resolved “through the applicable source Authorities and later approved governance.”

The Platform Authority Model is explicit that unresolved conflict must be escalated to Governance Authority and that lower layers may not select whichever source is convenient. Draft 1 contains the convenience prohibition but omits the explicit escalation destination.

The omission does not create competing authority, but restoring the exact hierarchy would remove ambiguity.

### 3.5 Identity-continuity attribution is distributed rather than explicit

Draft sections 7.4, 7.5, 14.1, 15.2 and 15.3 collectively require identity attribution and forbid silent substitution. No wording expressly states that every representation claiming continuity must remain attributable to one source Challenge identity and the historical context it represents.

The current protection is substantively strong. One explicit statement would make the anti-drift test easier to apply and audit without creating a lifecycle or replacement rule.

## 5. Recommended Refinements

These refinements should be applied before founder approval. They are narrow and preserve the accepted architecture.

### RR-01 — Remove unsupported Information Subject assertions

**Affected location:** Draft section 7.2; consequential checklist and self-validation references.

**Issue:** The draft classifies Platform Knowledge as a Governed Subject and Information Subject and says calculation and Policy declare the Information Subjects of Derived Truth. Neither allocation is established by the approved corpus.

**Required treatment:** Preserve only the necessary boundary:

- Knowledge Assets retain their governed identity and Knowledge information remains governed by EOG-03; and
- reference by a Challenge does not transfer or collapse the Information Subjects associated with personal, Activity or Derived Information.

Do not assign a new Information Subject through EOG-04.

**Constitutional basis:** EOG-01 subject distinctions; EOG-03 Platform Knowledge and Knowledge Asset definitions; Phase 1 non-allocation gate.

**Effect:** Removes two unapproved classifications without weakening Challenge Information Subject status.

### RR-02 — Restore the full Authority separation in the information chain

**Affected location:** Draft section 11.5; self-validation principle traces E4-P21, E4-P23 and E4-P25.

**Issue:** Applicable Policy and Participation Context appear jointly to govern expression and evaluation.

**Required treatment:** Keep the chain conceptual while making clear that:

- Knowledge supplies governed meaning;
- applicable Policy supplies approved rules;
- Participation Authority supplies governed relationship context where later standards permit;
- Participant Authority establishes Submission Intent;
- Acceptance Authority establishes Accepted Activity Events;
- Policy Authority establishes Evidence Eligibility for a declared calculation; and
- Calculation Authority establishes Derived Truth.

No workflow or lifecycle order should be added.

**Constitutional basis:** Platform Authority Model sections 4–6; Entity Ownership Foundation; EOG-01.

**Effect:** Prevents Participation Context from appearing to exercise Authority and keeps Participant Authority visible.

### RR-03 — Align Runtime Catalogue wording with EOG-03

**Affected location:** Draft section 16.2; Founder Review Checklist FR-28; self-validation E4-B12.

**Issue:** “Runtime Catalogue governs availability” gives grammatical agency to a function that the same draft denies is an actor.

**Required treatment:** State that, within the governed Runtime Catalogue function, Knowledge Authority establishes whether an approved Knowledge representation is available for a declared purpose.

**Constitutional basis:** EOG-03 sections 4 and 14.

**Effect:** Preserves one runtime availability boundary without creating a twelfth Authority or accountability allocation.

### RR-04 — Add explicit unresolved-conflict escalation

**Affected location:** Draft section 14.6.

**Issue:** Conflict resolution is attributed generally to source Authorities and later governance without naming the approved escalation rule.

**Required treatment:** Preserve source-authority review and state that any unresolved authority conflict requires escalation to Governance Authority under the approved precedence hierarchy.

**Constitutional basis:** Platform Authority Model section 12.

**Effect:** Removes discretion to leave equal conflicting interpretations unresolved or to select a convenient source.

### RR-05 — Make continuity attribution explicit

**Affected location:** Draft sections 7.4 or 15.3; Founder Review Checklist FR-25 and FR-26.

**Issue:** Attribution is present across several sections but not expressed as one direct continuity test.

**Required treatment:** State that a representation claiming to concern the same Challenge must remain attributable to one source Challenge identity and to the historical context it claims to represent.

The refinement must not define materiality, replacement, amendment, lifecycle, storage or identity matching.

**Constitutional basis:** Entity Ownership Foundation historical-integrity rule; EOG-03 composition and Historical Representation integrity; Phase 1 continuity assessment.

**Effect:** Strengthens protection against identity drift without creating a replacement test.

## 6. Optional Improvements

### OI-01 — Consolidate repeated continuity negatives

Continuity protections appear in sections 4.8, 7.4, 7.5, 14.1, 14.7, 15.3 and 15.4. The repetition is constitutionally consistent, but a later precision pass could reduce duplication while retaining the vocabulary definition, identity rule and historical rule.

This is editorial only. No consolidation is required for correctness.

### OI-02 — Clarify Challenge Information as an organising term

Draft section 4.6 already says Challenge Information creates no field set or storage model. It could also state expressly that Challenge Information is an organising domain term, not an eleventh Platform Information Category.

The Drafting Notes already explain this. Moving the clarification into the draft would reduce later classification confusion but is not required if the note remains authoritative review context.

### OI-03 — Add an explicit no-representation-primacy sentence

Sections 6.4 and 16 collectively establish meaning-before-representation. A concise integrity rule could say that no representation gains priority merely because it is the most recent, visible or convenient.

The existing no-competing-truth and presentation-integrity provisions substantially cover this point.

### OI-04 — Narrow the Founder Checklist after refinement

The checklist is comprehensive but long. After RR-01 through RR-05 are applied, overlapping checks concerning representations, integrity and continuity could be consolidated for the final founder session.

This is a review-efficiency improvement, not constitutional correction.

## 7. Identity Drift Assessment

### 7.1 Assessment standard

Identity drift would occur if Challenge identity could change, merge, substitute or become historically ambiguous without attributable governance.

The review tested whether Draft 1 permits drift through:

- presentation;
- runtime representation;
- local copying or labelling;
- interpretation;
- Community context;
- Policy or Knowledge change;
- historical representation;
- calculated outcomes; or
- implied continuity after material change.

### 7.2 Drift pathway assessment

| Potential drift pathway | Draft protection | Residual risk | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| One Challenge silently presented as another | Sections 7.5 and 14.7 prohibit silent substitution. | No replacement test exists, by design. | Pass |
| Presentation changes identity | Sections 6.4, 7.4, 14.5 and 16.1 make presentation subordinate. | None constitutionally material. | Pass |
| Runtime representation becomes identity | Sections 6.4, 15.3 and 16.2 deny runtime primacy. | RR-03 should improve grammatical precision. | Pass with refinement |
| Local label or copy becomes identity | Sections 7.4 and 14.6 deny it. | Attribution test is distributed. | Pass with refinement |
| Community context silently changes source meaning | Sections 8.4, 9.4 and 14.2 preserve layered meaning. | Material-context effect remains intentionally deferred. | Pass |
| Later Knowledge or Policy rewrites prior participation | Sections 14.4 and 15.4 prohibit retrospective application. | Historical mechanism remains deferred. | Pass |
| Historical Representation becomes a new Challenge | Sections 15.2 and 15.3 deny it. | RR-05 would improve source-identity trace. | Pass with refinement |
| Derived Truth replaces Challenge identity | Sections 7.1, 11.4 and 17.3 separate them. | None found. | Pass |
| Material change automatically preserves identity | Sections 7.4 and 15.3 expressly refuse that inference. | Later governance must supply the test. | Pass |
| Material change automatically creates a new Challenge | Sections 7.4 and 15.3 also refuse that inference. | Later governance must supply the test. | Pass |

### 7.3 Identity Drift conclusion

Draft 1 does not permit identity drift as a constitutional default.

It establishes three effective controls:

1. **identity distinction:** the Challenge remains separate from its Group, inputs, participants, events, results and representations;
2. **representation subordination:** presentation, runtime and history communicate or preserve but do not establish identity; and
3. **deferral of material-change tests:** the draft does not pretend to know whether a substantive change preserves or replaces the Challenge.

RR-05 is recommended because it converts distributed attribution into one explicit continuity test. It strengthens auditability without changing the doctrine or creating lifecycle behavior.

No constitutional basis was found for adding a Platform-wide Constitutional Continuity doctrine. The bounded Challenge Identity Continuity treatment remains correct.
