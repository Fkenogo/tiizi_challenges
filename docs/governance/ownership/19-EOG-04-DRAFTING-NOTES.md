# EOG-04 Founder Review Draft 2 — Drafting Notes

**Companion to:** [EOG-04 Constitutional Governance of Challenges — Founder Review Draft 2](19-EOG-04-CONSTITUTIONAL-GOVERNANCE-OF-CHALLENGES-DRAFT-2.md)

**Status:** Review companion; not constitutional governance

**Date:** 2026-07-20

## 1. Purpose

These notes record architectural observations, deferred constitutional questions and assumptions deliberately avoided while preparing EOG-04 Founder Review Draft 2.

They do not amend the draft, approve a recommendation, allocate accountability or resolve a pending founder decision.

## 2. Architectural Observations

### DN-01 — Constitutional handoff from EOG-03

EOG-03 fully governs Constitutional Composition. EOG-04 therefore begins constitutionally with the resulting Challenge rather than with a separate creation theory.

Draft 2 incorporates the composition inputs and integrity boundaries only to explain why the resulting Challenge is distinct. It does not redefine Platform Knowledge, Constitutional Composition, Layered Meaning or meaning-before-representation.

**Review significance:** Founder review should confirm that the handoff is clear enough to avoid duplication while making Challenge identity understandable on its own.

### DN-02 — Challenge as both Governed Subject and Information Subject

The draft treats the Challenge as a Governed Subject requiring coherent governance and as the Information Subject of Challenge Information. These are separate EOG-01 relationships.

This does not make the Challenge an actor, Authority or person. It also does not absorb information whose subject remains a Person, Knowledge Asset, Activity Event or derived determination.

**Review significance:** Future Entity Ownership Register reconciliation will require exact row-level treatment, but no allocation is appropriate in this draft.

### DN-03 — One governed purpose

The Challenge Domain Standard already requires one governed purpose per Challenge. Draft 2 preserves this as an identity-coherence principle.

It does not determine whether multiple Goals, Activities, Metrics, Units, participant structures or motivational patterns may exist under one purpose.

**Review significance:** Founder confirmation is appropriate because the principle affects future-domain coherence, but it does not require a pre-drafting decision.

### DN-04 — Identity continuity treatment

The Phase 1 assessment found implicit constitutional support for Challenge identity continuity but rejected a new standalone Platform-wide doctrine.

Draft 2 uses **Challenge Identity Continuity** as a bounded interpretive principle inside Challenge Identity and Challenge History. It says that presentation, runtime representation and local copying do not themselves change identity. A representation claiming continuity must remain attributable to one source Challenge identity and to the historical context represented. The draft does not determine the effect of contextual or substantive change.

**Review significance:** Founder review should confirm the label and boundary. Material amendment, replacement and lifecycle tests remain outside EOG-04.

### DN-05 — Challenge Information boundary

The draft introduces Challenge Information as information whose primary Information Subject is the Challenge. The term organises constitutional understanding without creating a category that competes with the approved ten Platform Information Categories.

Challenge Information may still be classified as Participation, Knowledge-reference, Administrative, Presentation, Temporary or other approved information according to its primary constitutional category.

Draft 2 does not assign Platform Knowledge itself a Governed Subject or Information Subject classification, and it does not imply that Policy or a calculation declares the Information Subjects of Derived Information. Knowledge Assets retain their governed identities; reference by a Challenge neither determines nor transfers adjacent Information Subject relationships.

**Review significance:** Later information-register work should map each subject to one primary constitutional category. Draft 2 does not perform that mapping.

### DN-06 — Group Experience language

The founder-approved statement that Groups own their governed experiences is preserved only as a domain boundary. Draft 2 translates its consequences using EOG-01 precision and does not treat it as Accountable Stewardship or Authority.

**Review significance:** This distinction is essential because CHL-01 and Challenge stewardship remain pending.

### DN-07 — Policy applicability

Draft 2 preserves the EOG-03 rule that Templates and Creation Mechanisms may consume an approved determination of Policy applicability but cannot establish that applicability.

The draft does not determine who establishes applicability for an individual Challenge or how that determination is made.

**Review significance:** A later Policy Governance Standard must address authorship, scope, applicability and amendment without collapsing Policy into Challenge configuration.

### DN-08 — Historical intelligibility without historical mechanism

Draft 2 identifies the constitutional relationships that must remain historically intelligible. It deliberately avoids snapshots, version identity, retention, archival, correction and reopening.

**Review significance:** The listed relationships are an integrity test for later governance, not a required historical payload.

### DN-09 — Authority interaction versus allocation

The Platform Authority interaction table applies all eleven approved Authorities to the Challenge boundary. It does not assign an actor, role or mechanism.

The conceptual information chain separately preserves Knowledge meaning, Policy rules, Participation Authority relationship context, Participant Authority Submission Intent, Acceptance Authority Accepted Activity Events, Policy Authority Evidence Eligibility and Calculation Authority Derived Truth. Unresolved authority conflicts escalate to Governance Authority under the approved Authority Model. The chain and escalation statement create no workflow.

The Authority establishing an individual Challenge's identity, purpose and Goal remains pending. The draft does not infer Participation Authority, Administrative Authority, Group stewardship or creator status as the answer.

**Review significance:** Founder decision is still required before the Entity Ownership Register can receive a final allocation for those truths.

### DN-10 — Future-domain neutrality

The Challenge definition is sufficiently neutral to support future approved domains. Draft 2 names examples only to deny present activation.

**Review significance:** Future domains require their own approved Knowledge, Policy, evidence meaning and scope. This draft cannot be used as evidence that they are current capabilities.

### DN-11 — Identifier collision

Historical planning used EOG-03 for Challenge governance and EOG-04 for Verification. Current approved and founder-directed sequencing uses EOG-03 for Platform Knowledge and the full title EOG-04 — Constitutional Governance of Challenges for this programme.

**Review significance:** Draft 2 records the collision but does not rename or modify any historical artefact. Future decision-register reconciliation remains separate.

## 4. Assumptions Deliberately Avoided

Draft 2 does not assume that:

1. a Challenge is proposed rather than created;
2. the Challenge creator becomes its Accountable Steward;
3. Group Accountable Stewardship transfers to a Challenge;
4. Participation Authority establishes Challenge identity or purpose;
5. Administrative Authority may establish Challenge truth by default;
6. every Group Member participates in every Challenge;
7. a Challenge requires Ranking, competition, Streaks or Recognition;
8. one Goal means one Activity or one Metric;
9. a target is Progress or a Completion condition;
10. every Accepted Activity Event is eligible for every calculation;
11. presentation or runtime representation preserves identity after every contextual change;
12. historical integrity requires snapshots or version identifiers;
13. current Challenge types are constitutionally approved;
14. Fitness-specific measurement applies to future domains;
15. future domains or individual challenges are active product capabilities;
16. current implementation behavior supplies constitutional governance.

## 5. Drafting Notes Status

No note in this document is an approved constitutional rule. DN-01 through DN-11 explain drafting treatment. DQ-01 through DQ-12 remain open. The assumptions in section 4 remain deliberately unmade.

Founder review may confirm the draft's treatment, request refinement or preserve a question for later governance. No response becomes governance without an explicit approval record.
