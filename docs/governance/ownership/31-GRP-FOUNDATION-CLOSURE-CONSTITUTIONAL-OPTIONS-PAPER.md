# GRP Foundation Closure Phase 1 — Constitutional Options Paper

**Status:** Objective options analysis; no recommendation or approval

**Date:** 2026-07-21

## 1. Purpose

This paper presents constitutionally viable alternatives for GFC-01 through GFC-05 under the [Phase 0 Scope](30-GRP-FOUNDATION-CLOSURE-CONSTITUTIONAL-SCOPE.md), [Decision Agenda](30-GRP-FOUNDATION-CLOSURE-FOUNDER-DECISION-AGENDA.md) and [Founder Decision Blueprint](30-GRP-FOUNDATION-CLOSURE-FOUNDER-DECISION-BLUEPRINT.md).

It does not rank or recommend an option, allocate a relationship, resolve CG-08 or amend EOG-01, EOG-02 or EOG-05.

## 3. GFC-01 — Group Creation Authentication

### 3.1 Constitutional question

Which existing Platform Authority authenticates the attributable Group-creation event and establishes authoritative Group existence, initial identity and founding-purpose truth?

### 3.2 Approved baseline

- A Group is created, not proposed.
- Creation establishes existence, initial identity and founding purpose.
- The creator becomes first Member and first Accountable Steward.
- Participation is voluntary and is not the constitutional basis of existence.
- Creator origin is attributable but does not itself establish authoritative truth.

### 3.3 Governing constraints

- one Authority to Establish Truth for Group creation;
- only an existing Platform Authority may be used;
- Identity, Policy, Administration and Participation contributions remain distinct;
- no interface, mechanism, role title or possession creates Authority; and
- first Membership and initial Accountable Stewardship remain consequences distinct from existence authentication.

### 3.4 Viable constitutional options

| Option | Model | Constitutional strengths | Constitutional weaknesses | EOG-01 consistency | EOG-02 consistency | EOG-05 impact | Dependency consequences | Reversibility | Amendment burden | Long-term implications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GFC-01-A | Participation Authority establishes authoritative Group existence and initial Group truth after attributable creator expression; Identity supplies creator context, Policy supplies rules and Administration may perform a separately authorized action. | Uses the Authority already associated with Group and membership relationships; preserves one source for Group truth and first Membership context. | The Authority name may be misread as making later participation the basis of existence; wording must state that creation expression, not activity, is the basis. | Consistent if origin, Policy, Administration and Authority remain separately declared. | Consistent if Group existence remains independent of participation. | Clarifies GRP-01 and may inform GRP-04 without changing either row. | Supports GFC-02 and later GRP work; does not by itself resolve roles or lifecycle. | Moderate; changing the source Authority later would require truth-source migration in governance. | Medium; requires clarification of Participation Authority's creation scope. | Establishes one domain-aligned Group truth source while requiring durable protection against semantic confusion over “participation.” |
| GFC-01-B | Administrative Authority establishes authoritative Group existence as an approved high-impact creation decision; Participation Authority separately establishes first Membership context. | Makes trusted, attributable creation action explicit and sharply separates existence from later participation. | Risks elevating Administration into a general domain-truth source and may blur administrative action with underlying Group identity. | Consistent only if the creation declaration is narrowly scoped and does not confer stewardship, custody or unrestricted Administration. | Consistent with creation independence, subject to preserving creator consequences. | Clarifies GRP-01 and GRP-06; requires a strict boundary between Group and administrative-action truth. | Increases dependence on later administrative and audit governance. | Constitutionally difficult if later changed because the source of Group existence would move between Authority types. | High; requires enduring limits preventing Administrative Authority expansion. | Centralizes creation truth within high-impact Administration and may create precedent for other domain-entity creation decisions. |

### 3.5 Models excluded as non-viable under the current baseline

- Participant Authority cannot establish Group existence because the creator need not yet be a Challenge Participant and participant expression does not establish accepted truth.
- Identity Authority may establish creator identity context but not Group truth.
- Policy Authority may establish creation rules but not an individual Group's existence.
- Governance Authority approves constitutional boundaries and cannot routinely establish individual product subjects.
- Acceptance, Calculation, Knowledge, Operational and Presentation Authorities do not govern Group creation truth.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| GFC-01-B | Supported. | Supported if Administration does not imply stewardship. | Conditional on GFC-02. | Pressure: administrator terminology may invite role-based inference. |

## 4. GFC-02 — Stewardship Continuity

### 4.1 Constitutional question

What constitutional model preserves exactly one Accountable Steward relationship through voluntary reassignment, relinquishment, succession and stewardship failure without treating delegation, Administration or Council membership as stewardship?

### 4.2 Approved baseline

- The creator becomes first Accountable Steward.
- Exactly one Accountable Steward relationship exists per Group at any time.
- Stewardship may be reassigned or voluntarily relinquished through approved governance.
- A Group may outlive its creator.
- Delegation of Authority, Assignment of Responsibility and Stewardship Reassignment are distinct.

### 4.3 Governing constraints

- no shared or vacant Accountable Steward relationship may be assumed;
- a successor must hold the singular relationship whenever reassignment becomes authoritative;
- the Council cannot become steward by implication;
- a current steward may originate a change without necessarily establishing its authoritative truth; and
- this phase cannot define workflow, eligibility, states or technical procedure.

### 4.4 Viable constitutional options

| Option | Model | Constitutional strengths | Constitutional weaknesses | EOG-01 consistency | EOG-02 consistency | EOG-05 impact | Dependency consequences | Reversibility | Amendment burden | Long-term implications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GFC-02-A | Successor-continuous reassignment: relinquishment becomes constitutionally effective only when one successor relationship is established in the same governed change. | Preserves unbroken singular accountability and avoids a constitutional vacancy concept. | Does not independently address incapacity, abandonment or unresolved disputes where no voluntary successor is available. | Cleanly distinguishes stewardship reassignment from delegation and ordinary Administration. | Preserves exactly one relationship and continuity beyond the creator where a successor exists. | Clarifies future allocations concerning GRP-01 and Group stewardship; no row change. | Requires later failure governance for non-voluntary cases. | Moderate; additional succession paths can later supplement the model. | Low to medium; core rule is simple but incomplete for failure. | Produces a stable default while leaving exceptional continuity to separate governance. |
| GFC-02-B | Dual-path continuity: voluntary successor-continuous reassignment plus a Charter-governed succession principle for incapacity, abandonment or failure, with authoritative effect established separately. | Covers ordinary and exceptional continuity while keeping one relationship at a time. | Depends on a sufficiently binding and historically intelligible Charter; may make Charter governance more complex. | Consistent if Charter rule, origin, Authority and reassignment are separately declared. | Preserves singular stewardship and gives the Group an internal continuity basis. | Couples future stewardship allocation to C-GRP-07 Charter treatment. | Requires GFC-03 and later Charter governance before the exceptional path is usable. | Moderate; Charter succession can be amended only under later approved Charter rules. | Medium; requires coherent Charter truth and amendment boundaries. | Embeds continuity within Group constitutional meaning and reduces reliance on external intervention. |
| GFC-02-C | Governance-protected continuity: voluntary successor-continuous reassignment remains primary, while an exceptional governed determination establishes a successor when the ordinary path cannot operate. | Addresses orphaning, incapacity and dispute without making the Council or an Administrator the steward by implication. | Creates a recurring need to distinguish constitutional intervention from routine Group control and may increase founder or delegated-governance burden. | Consistent only if the deciding Authority, stewardship relationship and administrative execution remain separate. | Preserves Group continuity and singular stewardship but introduces an external constitutional backstop. | Clarifies future Group stewardship allocations; no entity classification change. | Requires a later decision naming the existing Authority and scope for exceptional determination. | Constitutionally difficult to remove once relied upon for continuity. | High; exception boundaries and reviewability require durable governance. | Provides resilience for failure cases while increasing central governance dependency. |

### 4.5 Models excluded as non-viable

- a vacant Accountable Steward relationship contradicts the approved “exactly one at any time” principle;
- multiple Accountable Stewards contradict singular answerability;
- automatic Council replacement contradicts the Council non-replacement doctrine; and
- ordinary delegation cannot transfer stewardship.

### 4.6 Scenario analysis

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GFC-02-A | Supported. | Pressure: relinquishment cannot become effective. | Conditional on later dispute governance. | Supported; delegation has no stewardship effect. | Supported only when successor is established. |
| GFC-02-B | Supported. | Conditional on an applicable Charter succession principle. | Conditional on Charter interpretation and Authority governance. | Supported. | Supported through voluntary or Charter-governed path. |
| GFC-02-C | Supported. | Supported through exceptional determination, subject to later Authority decision. | Supported conceptually through governed determination. | Supported. | Supported through primary or exceptional path. |

## 5. GFC-03 — Charter Foundations

### 5.1 Constitutional question

What constitutional status should the Group Charter have at founding and afterward, including binding meaning, amendment boundary and historical intelligibility?

### 5.2 Approved baseline

- The Charter is a Governed Subject.
- It is distinct from Platform governance, role bundles and implementation configuration.
- Group identity and purpose may evolve through approved governance.
- Charter governance remains deferred.

### 5.3 Governing constraints

- Charter authorship, Authority, Accountable Stewardship, Custody and Administration remain distinct;
- binding effect cannot create permissions by itself;
- amendment cannot silently reinterpret prior participation;
- historical intelligibility is required without defining versioning; and
- Charter treatment cannot override higher constitutional governance or Policy.

### 5.4 Viable constitutional options

| Option | Model | Constitutional strengths | Constitutional weaknesses | EOG-01 consistency | EOG-02 consistency | EOG-05 impact | Dependency consequences | Reversibility | Amendment burden | Long-term implications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GFC-03-A | Founding binding Charter: an initial Charter becomes constitutionally applicable at Group creation; later amendments require declared Authority and preserved historical meaning. | Supplies immediate internal constitutional meaning and can support continuity from the first Member onward. | Increases the significance of creation-time choices and requires careful separation between creator authorship and authoritative Charter truth. | Consistent if origin, Authority and stewardship are separately declared. | Aligns with creation establishing founding purpose and Charter subject status. | Strengthens C-GRP-07 as an independently governed Charter; may inform GRP-04 but changes no row now. | Supports GFC-02-B and requires later amendment governance. | Moderate; founding applicability remains historical even when amended. | Medium; initial and amended meaning require durable traceability. | Gives every Group an immediate constitutional instrument while increasing founding-governance discipline. |
| GFC-03-B | Adopted binding Charter: a Group may exist before the Charter becomes binding; a later governed adoption establishes applicability, with later amendments separately governed. | Separates Group existence from Charter adoption and permits broader considered input before binding effect. | Leaves an early period governed without a binding Charter and cannot independently support immediate Charter-based succession. | Consistent if proposal, adoption Authority and stewardship remain distinct. | Consistent because Group existence does not depend on Charter adoption or participation. | Retains C-GRP-07 but makes its proposed and applicable meaning important future distinctions. | GFC-02-B remains unavailable until adoption; later membership governance becomes influential. | Easy to moderate before adoption; moderate after applicability. | Medium; adoption and amendment meanings require separate governance. | Enables deliberate community constitutional development while accepting an initial Charter gap. |

- a Charter that overrides Platform governance or Policy;
- a Charter that automatically grants permissions or Platform Authority;
- silent amendment without historical intelligibility; and
- Charter ownership by the Accountable Steward as personal property.

## 6. GFC-04 — Stewardship Council Constitutional Treatment

### 6.1 Constitutional question

What enduring supporting treatment may a Stewardship Council receive without becoming a Platform Authority, Accountable Steward, Administrator or Custodian?

### 6.2 Approved baseline

- The Council is a governed body supporting the Accountable Steward.
- Council membership does not create Accountable Stewardship.
- The Council is not a Platform Authority, Administration or Custody.
- It does not replace the singular Accountable Steward relationship.
- Composition, appointment, decision-making and responsibilities remain deferred.

### 6.3 Governing constraints

- no collective stewardship by implication;
- no Council Authority, Administration or Custody by status;
- any relationship stacking must be declared separately;
- member relationships remain distinct from Council treatment; and
- no composition, voting, appointment or procedural governance.

### 6.4 Viable constitutional options

| Option | Model | Constitutional strengths | Constitutional weaknesses | EOG-01 consistency | EOG-02 consistency | EOG-05 impact | Dependency consequences | Reversibility | Amendment burden | Long-term implications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GFC-04-A | Advisory-only Council: the body provides deliberation and advice but receives no formal Assignment of Responsibility or delegation as a body. | Maximizes separation from stewardship and Authority; low risk of role leakage. | May provide limited constitutional value beyond informal collaboration and does not distribute declared supporting responsibility. | Consistent; advice is not Authority, stewardship or assignment. | Fully preserves non-replacement and non-Administration boundaries. | Supports either permanent or consolidated CG-08 treatment. | Leaves all formal Council responsibility to future governance or separate member relationships. | Easy. | Low. | Creates a narrow, stable institution with limited formal governance utility. |
| GFC-04-B | Assigned-responsibility Council: the body may later receive expressly bounded Assignments of Responsibility supporting stewardship, but never Authority, Accountable Stewardship, Administration or Custody. | Gives the Council a distinct constitutional function while preserving the singular steward. | Collective responsibility may be mistaken for collective stewardship unless declarations remain precise. | Consistent if Assignment of Responsibility is explicit and no other relationship is inferred. | Consistent with “supports but never replaces.” | Favors a permanent C-GRP-08 row if later allocations must reference the Council body. | Requires later responsibility, composition and review governance. | Moderate. | Medium; every assigned responsibility requires scope discipline. | Enables durable collaborative support while maintaining one answerable steward. |
| GFC-04-C | Coordination-context Council: the Council body coordinates support, while any Authority, responsibility, Custody, Administration or other relationship may attach only to separately identified members or actors through independent declarations. | Makes relationship stacking explicit and prevents collective-body ambiguity. | Council identity may become constitutionally thin if all substantive relationships exist outside the body. | Strongly preserves separate declarations and role neutrality. | Preserves Council support and singular stewardship. | May support consolidation or permanent row treatment depending whether coordination identity warrants an entity. | Later member-role governance becomes necessary for any substantive assignment. | Easy to moderate. | Low to medium. | Supports flexible organizational forms while keeping the Council itself non-operational. |

### 6.5 Models excluded as non-viable

- Council as joint Accountable Steward;
- Council as a twelfth Platform Authority;
- automatic Administrative Authority or Custody for the body or its members; and
- Council power derived solely from role title or membership.

## 7. GFC-05 — CG-08 Inventory Readiness

### 7.1 Constitutional question

What permanent inventory treatment, if any, should apply to C-GRP-08 Stewardship Council while preserving its approved supporting Governed Subject status and unresolved Information Subject boundary?

### 7.2 Approved baseline

- Stewardship Council is an approved supporting Governed Subject.
- C-GRP-08 is Provisional under CG-08.
- Council, Group and later Council relationships must not be collapsed without analysis.
- Inventory classification does not allocate a relationship.

### 7.3 Governing constraints

- EOG-05 entity-admission and non-duplication boundaries;
- stable cross-register reference only for independently admitted entities;
- no permanent row merely to imply accountability;
- no consolidation that erases approved Governed Subject status; and
- no CG-08 resolution in this phase.
