# Tiizi Entity & Operational Governance Standard

## Stage E1 Integrated Operational Governance Instrument

**Document ID:** EOG-E1-01\
**Version:** Approved Entity & Operational Governance\
**Approved source version:** Founder Approved Substantive Baseline v0.2\
**Stage:** E1 --- Entity & Operational Governance\
**Status:** Founder Approved --- Effective\
**Approval date:** 2026-09-03\
**Authority:** Tiizi Platform Governance\
**Purpose:** Establish the minimum operational governance necessary for
Groups, Membership, Challenges, Participation, Evidence, operational
authority, visibility, Recognition and historical integrity before Stage
F product definition.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Purpose and governing boundary

This Standard establishes the operational governance needed to apply
Tiizi's approved constitutional, authority, entity, domain and knowledge
foundations to Groups and Challenges.

It does not replace or redefine approved Platform Authority, Evidence,
visibility, Knowledge or domain standards. Where this Standard uses a
governed term, the approved constitutional meaning remains controlling.

Detailed product calculations, exact Challenge behaviour, user
experience, technical permissions, data structures, V1→V2 migration and
implementation remain downstream unless expressly stated otherwise.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Part I --- Community, Group and Challenge Structure

## 2. Group-first operating model

Tiizi is a group-accountability platform.

Every Challenge belongs to exactly one Group. A Challenge cannot be
established outside a Group.

The normal relationship progression is:

``` text
Tiizi Member
    ↓
Group Membership
    ↓
Challenge Participation
```

Platform Membership, Group Membership and Challenge Participation are
distinct governed relationships. One does not automatically establish
another.

## 3. Group establishment and stewardship

A Tiizi Member may establish a Group. The establishing Member becomes
the Group's first Member and initial Accountable Steward.

Every Group has exactly one Accountable Steward at a time.

Accountable Stewardship provides singular accountability and continuity.
It does not constitute unrestricted ownership or control over Members,
Challenges, Participation, Evidence, Recognition or Group information.

## 4. Stewardship continuity

Stewardship may transfer to another eligible Group Member through an
attributable transfer and acceptance under applicable governance.

Relinquishment should ordinarily establish a willing successor. Where
succession cannot immediately be completed, stewardship-dependent
actions may be restricted until continuity is restored.

Tiizi shall not silently assign Accountable Stewardship because a person
is active, senior, administratively privileged or technically capable.

## 5. Group Charter and optional governance support

Every Group has a Group Charter governing Group-specific operating rules
within Platform governance.

Tiizi may provide standard or prefilled Charter provisions from which
applicable rules are selected, with permitted Group-specific provisions
where supported.

The governing hierarchy is:

``` text
Platform Governance
        ↓
Group Charter
        ↓
Group Operations
```

A Charter cannot override Platform governance or create prohibited
authority.

A Governance Body or Stewardship Council may support a Group where
useful, but is not required for ordinary operation and cannot replace or
duplicate the singular Accountable Steward.

# Part II --- Membership, Discovery and Participation

## 6. Group admission

A Tiizi Member becomes a Group Member by establishing a Group or
entering an existing Group under its applicable admission rule.

Permitted admission modes may include Open, Approval or Invitation,
subject to Platform governance and the Group Charter.

Group Members may invite eligible Tiizi Members by default unless the
Charter validly restricts invitation capability. An invitation does not
itself establish Membership.

## 7. Voluntary membership lifecycle

Group Membership is voluntary. A Member may leave a Group.

Suspension, removal and restoration require appropriately authorized
action under Platform governance and the Group Charter.

Departure, suspension or removal operates prospectively and does not by
itself erase historically attributable Membership, Participation,
Evidence or valid prior acts.

## 8. Governed discoverability

Group, Challenge, Profile, Participation and Evidence information shall
use Tiizi's approved constitutional visibility classes rather than a new
binary visibility taxonomy.

The approved classes remain:

-   Public;
-   Authenticated-discoverable;
-   Shared-group;
-   Private; and
-   Privileged-operational.

A Group intended for discovery by signed-in Tiizi Members may expose the
minimum Group information permitted by the Authenticated-discoverable
class. Purpose-specific information may be Public where separately
authorized.

## 9. Challenge discovery

Where the governing visibility class permits it, a Tiizi Member who is
not a Member of a Group may discover the Group and sufficient
information about its discoverable Challenges to decide whether to seek
Membership or Participation.

Discovery does not establish Membership, Challenge eligibility,
Participation or authority.

For an authenticated-discoverable Group Challenge:

``` text
Discover Group / Challenge
        ↓
Become Group Member under applicable admission rule
        ↓
Satisfy Challenge-specific eligibility
        ↓
Affirmatively choose to join
        ↓
Challenge Participation
```

## 10. Challenge establishment

Any Group Member may create a Challenge by default unless the Group
Charter validly restricts Challenge-creation capability.

The eligible Member completing establishment is the attributable
Challenge creator.

Challenge establishment must comply with Platform governance, the Group
Charter, applicable Challenge-type rules and the valid configuration of
the particular Challenge.

The Challenge Creation Wizard and Templates may facilitate or configure
creation but do not independently establish a Challenge or constitute an
Authority source.

## 11. Voluntary Challenge Participation

Only a current Member of the relevant Group may join its Challenge.

Where restrictions apply, the particular Challenge configuration
determines Challenge eligibility.

Group Membership determines community belonging. Challenge configuration
determines eligibility. The Member determines Participation.

Eligibility, discovery, recommendation, invitation, notification, feed
exposure or other presentation does not create Participation. A Member
must affirmatively join.

## 12. Participation ending

A Participant may withdraw voluntarily. Removal from continuing
Participation requires appropriately authorized action under applicable
governance and Challenge rules.

Withdrawal or removal does not by itself erase legitimate historical
Participation or Evidence.

Whether historically eligible Evidence contributes to later Progress,
Completion, Ranking, streak or other calculations after Participation
ends is determined by the applicable Stage F calculation rules, not by
Membership governance alone.

# Part III --- Challenge Lifecycle and Administration

## 13. Common lifecycle

Every Challenge has attributable establishment, an Active period and an
Ended state.

The conditions producing the Ended state depend on the governed
Challenge type and valid Challenge configuration. Tiizi shall not impose
one universal completion rule across all Challenge types.

## 14. Collective Challenge

A Collective Challenge ends when its configured collective Goal is
reached or its configured period expires, whichever occurs first.

While the Challenge remains Active, a validly authorized
Challenge-administration action may extend its configured period.

An extension is attributable, preserves the original timing
historically, retains the same Challenge identity and does not reset
existing Participation, Evidence or progress.

This Standard does not allocate a new extension Authority or prescribe a
universal maximum number or duration of extensions.

## 15. Streak Challenge

A Streak Challenge ends when its configured Challenge period expires.

A Participant's streak may reset under the applicable Stage F Challenge
rules without ending the Challenge itself.

Exact streak calculations, time boundaries, grace conditions and reset
mechanics remain Stage F matters.

## 16. Competitive Challenge

A Competitive Challenge ends when its configured competition window
expires.

A Participant may individually finish earlier by reaching the configured
competitive Goal without ending the Challenge for other Participants.

Exact finishing, ranking and leaderboard calculations remain Stage F
matters.

## 17. Repeat after ending

An Ended Challenge remains historically intelligible as the undertaking
that ended.

Where the product permits Members to "run again", repeat or recreate
that undertaking, the action establishes a new Challenge rather than
continuing the ended Challenge's operational history.

The new Challenge has its own identity, lifecycle, Participation,
Evidence, progress and history. The ended Challenge may be used as a
configuration or Template basis where supported.

This rule governs the V2 repeat/recreate product behaviour only. It does
not resolve any broader deferred constitutional question concerning
reopening.

## 18. Challenge creator and administrative continuity

Challenge Creator is historical attribution and does not create
perpetual independent ownership or Authority.

Challenge establishment may carry bounded administration capability
while valid under applicable governance.

If the creator ceases to hold the relationship or capability required
for continuing administration, creator attribution remains unchanged and
administration must continue only through an actor holding valid
authority or delegation.

This Standard does not invent a separate Challenge-owner role or a new
Authority merely to provide administrative continuity.

# Part IV --- Evidence, Acceptance and Self-Accountability

## 19. Controlled Evidence chain

This Standard preserves Tiizi's approved Evidence authority vocabulary
and distinctions.

The governed chain includes:

``` text
Participation Authority
        ↓
Participant Authority → Submission Intent
        ↓
Acceptance Authority → Accepted Activity Event
        ↓
Policy Authority → Evidence Eligibility for a declared calculation
        ↓
Calculation Authority → Derived Truth
```

Submission Intent, acceptance, Evidence Eligibility and Derived Truth
are distinct. No term in this Standard creates a parallel Evidence gate.

## 20. Self-accountability and Submission Intent

Tiizi is a self-accountability platform.

A Participant is the attributable source of their Submission Intent and
is accountable for truthful recording of their own Activity.

Submission Intent is not itself an Accepted Activity Event, Evidence
Eligibility or Derived Truth.

## 21. Acceptance is not factual certification

Acceptance Authority establishes an Accepted Activity Event according to
the approved Platform Authority model.

Acceptance does not by itself certify or verify that the Participant's
reported real-world physical or wellness Activity actually occurred.

An Accepted Activity Event may therefore be authoritative as the
governed accepted event without Tiizi claiming infallibility concerning
the underlying real-world behaviour.

## 22. Evidence Eligibility and calculation

Policy Authority determines whether an Accepted Activity Event is
eligible Evidence for a declared calculation.

Calculation Authority applies the declared governed calculation to
eligible Evidence to establish the applicable Derived Truth.

Structural, semantic, Metric, Unit, timing, Participation and
Challenge-policy conditions shall be expressed through these approved
Authority boundaries rather than through a new independent
"admissibility" authority.

## 23. Verification boundary

This Standard does not create a general Tiizi duty to verify every
Participant's physical or wellness Activity.

It also does not abolish, redefine or prematurely allocate the
separately deferred Verification authority and downstream-effect
questions preserved by existing governance.

Where future governed circumstances require Verification, including
potentially higher-integrity contexts, those requirements must be
established without collapsing Verification into Submission Intent,
Acceptance, Evidence Eligibility or Calculation Authority.

## 24. Anomalies and community accountability

Tiizi may support identification or flagging of anomalous or implausible
records under future governed product rules.

A flag does not establish falsity, and absence of a flag does not
establish factual verification.

Groups may establish Charter expectations concerning honest Activity
recording and may take appropriately authorized community-accountability
action concerning Member standing or Participation.

Such action does not itself authorize a Group actor to impersonate,
silently rewrite or erase another Participant's historical assertion.

## 25. Correction and dispute boundary

Participants should ordinarily correct their own erroneous self-reported
information within applicable lifecycle and integrity rules.

Material correction or dispute shall preserve sufficient attribution and
historical intelligibility.

This Standard does not create an Evidence Verifier role and does not
resolve deferred correction, deletion or Verification Authority
questions beyond the operational principles stated here.

# Part V --- Roles, Delegation and Operational Authority

## 26. Contextual authority and minimal role model

Operational capability derives from the governed relationship, Authority
or delegation applicable to the context, not merely from User identity
or technical privilege.

Tiizi shall avoid unnecessary role proliferation.

The principal operating relationships are Tiizi Member, Group Member,
Accountable Steward and Challenge Participant. Challenge Creator remains
attributable historical information rather than a permanent independent
Authority class.

## 27. Ordinary Member capability

Ordinary Group Membership carries the normal community capabilities
permitted by Platform governance and the Group Charter, including
Challenge creation and Member invitation where not validly restricted.

Ordinary community activity should not require an administrative role
merely for administrative convenience.

## 28. Delegated Group administration

The Accountable Steward may delegate bounded administrative capabilities
to eligible Group Members where permitted.

Delegation shall be explicit, scoped, attributable and revocable.

Delegation does not transfer Accountable Stewardship, create a second
Steward or create an independent Authority source.

Revocation or expiry ends future exercise of the delegated capability
but does not erase historically valid acts performed while the
capability was legitimately held.

## 29. Participant and Platform administration boundaries

A Participant controls their own voluntary Participation and Submission
Intent within applicable governance and does not gain authority over
another Participant's Evidence merely by participating.

Administrator or Super Admin status is an operational mechanism and does
not independently confer unrestricted Platform Authority.

Any exceptional Platform intervention must derive from valid Authority
and remain constrained, attributable and reviewable.

# Part VI --- Privacy, Visibility and Consent

## 30. Visibility follows approved classes

Visibility determines who may discover or access information; it does
not create or alter the underlying governed Truth.

Group discoverability does not automatically make Member, Profile,
Participation, Evidence or administrative information visible under the
same class.

Each information use remains subject to the approved constitutional
visibility classes, purpose and minimum-disclosure rules.

## 31. Profile and Evidence protection

Joining a discoverable Group or Challenge does not make a Member's
entire Profile public or authenticated-discoverable.

Accepted Activity evidence is not public by default merely because Group
or Challenge discovery information is visible.

Raw Evidence, Derived Truth, progress, ranking, leaderboard and feed
representations may have different governed visibility classifications
according to purpose.

## 32. Consent and minimum necessary access

Participation in Tiizi, a Group or a Challenge does not constitute
blanket consent for unrelated uses of personal information.

Where specific consent is required, it applies to the relevant governed
use. Withdrawal ordinarily operates prospectively and does not
automatically rewrite legitimate historical records or override another
valid preservation obligation.

Access shall be limited to information reasonably necessary for the
legitimate governed function. Stewardship, delegated administration or
Platform administrative status does not itself confer unrestricted
access.

A Group Charter may select or establish privacy rules only within
Platform-permitted boundaries.

# Part VII --- Derived Truth, Recognition and Community Acknowledgement

## 33. Derived Truth and Recognition remain distinct

Progress, Completion, Ranking, streak state and other declared
calculations are forms of Derived Truth or downstream product
interpretation according to their approved governance.

Recognition follows Truth and must not create the underlying result it
purports to acknowledge.

This Standard does not reclassify Recognition or resolve the
still-pending MOT-01 final Recognition Authority/classification
boundary.

## 34. Recognition qualification principle

Platform Policy determines the governed conditions under which Derived
Truth may qualify for Platform Recognition.

Those conditions may legitimately differ by Challenge type and valid
particular Challenge configuration.

Tiizi may issue or record Platform Recognition according to those
governed conditions once the applicable Recognition
authority/classification boundary is resolved.

No Accountable Steward, Challenge creator, Group Administrator or other
Member is made a routine Recognition Authority by this Standard.

## 35. Challenge Engine boundary

The intended product architecture may use Challenge-type-specific
Challenge Engines to execute calculations and Recognition-related rules
configured for a Challenge.

"Challenge Engine" is not established by this Standard as a Platform
Authority, constitutional entity or final Recognition Authority.

Its exact definition, behaviour and implementation belong to Stage F and
subsequent technical translation, subject to existing Authority
boundaries and MOT-01.

The Challenge Creation Wizard and Templates may carry or configure
permitted Challenge rules but are not Authority sources.

## 36. Community Acknowledgement

Community Acknowledgement is distinct from Platform Recognition.

Tiizi may support social mechanisms such as Kudos through which Members
acknowledge Activity, achievement, participation, contribution,
financial contribution to a cause or other socially meaningful Group
events.

Community Acknowledgement does not establish or alter Accepted Activity
Events, Evidence Eligibility, Derived Truth, Ranking, Challenge results
or Platform Recognition.

# Part VIII --- Future Rewards

## 37. Reward separation and deferral

Recognition and Reward are distinct.

Tiizi governance preserves the future possibility for a Group Challenge
to include a financial, material or other Reward associated with a
qualifying result where permitted by future governed product rules.

Recognition or a Challenge result does not itself create or promise a
Reward unless a separate governed Reward arrangement exists.

Reward functionality is not required for initial V2/Phase 1.

Future Reward-bearing Challenges may require additional Evidence,
Verification, qualification or fulfilment conditions without changing
Tiizi's ordinary self-accountability model.

Reward declaration, funding, fulfilment, payments, sponsorship,
disputes, taxation, regulated promotions and related legal, commercial
and technical mechanisms remain deferred pending separate assessment.

# Part IX --- Historical Integrity

## 38. Material historical intelligibility

Tiizi shall preserve sufficient attributable history for material Group,
Challenge, Membership, Participation, Evidence and authority states and
actions to remain historically intelligible.

This includes, where materially necessary, Group establishment and
stewardship changes; material Charter or visibility changes; Challenge
identity, creator, original undertaking, material amendments or
extensions, ending and relevant result; Participation changes; Evidence
corrections or disputes; and consequential exercises of governance or
operational authority.

Historical changes shall not be represented as though the later state
had always been the original state.

## 39. Preservation, visibility and superior obligations

Historical preservation does not create public visibility, unrestricted
administrative access or perpetual access entitlement.

This Standard establishes no arbitrary universal retention period and
does not require preservation of every technical event forever.

Privacy, legal or superior governance obligations may require deletion,
anonymisation, restriction or other treatment. Historical
intelligibility should be preserved to the legitimate extent possible
without overriding those obligations.

# Part X --- Downstream Boundary and Sufficiency

## 40. Downstream allocation and Stage E1 sufficiency

This Standard establishes the minimum operational governance required
from Stage E1 without converting governance into product or
implementation design.

The following remain downstream:

**Stage F --- Product Definition & Technical Translation** - Progress,
Completion, Ranking, streak and projection formulas; - exact
Challenge-type and Challenge Engine behaviour; - exact Recognition
qualification and presentation subject to MOT-01; - feeds, discovery,
recommendation, notifications and social interaction behaviour; -
moderation product behaviour; - exact visibility fields and user
experience; - detailed capability and product permission requirements; -
Wizard and Template product behaviour; - exact Challenge administration
workflows.

**Stage G --- V1→V2 Alignment** - migration, carry-forward, remediation,
replacement or retirement of V1 implementation.

**Stage H / technical architecture and implementation** - IAM/RBAC
implementation; - API authorization; - Firestore/security rules; - data
schemas; - persistence/versioning mechanisms; - credentials, encryption
and audit infrastructure; - Challenge Engine code architecture and
runtime enforcement.

**Preserved deferrals** - ACT-03/ACT-04 correction, deletion and
Verification Authority questions to the extent not already resolved; -
MOT-01 final Recognition classification/Authority; - detailed Reward
system; - broader future governance maturity and any non-Group Challenge
model not separately authorized.

Additional governance instruments should not be created merely to
elaborate matters already sufficiently governed. A new instrument is
warranted only where its absence would materially impair authority,
accountability, traceability, historical intelligibility, dependency
control or safe execution.
