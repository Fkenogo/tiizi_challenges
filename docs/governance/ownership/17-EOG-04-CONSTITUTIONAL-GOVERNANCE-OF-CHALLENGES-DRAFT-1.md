# EOG-04 — Constitutional Governance of Challenges

**Version:** Founder Review Draft 1

**Status:** Draft for founder review; not approved governance

**Date:** 2026-07-20

## 1. Purpose

This standard defines the constitutional identity, relationships, integrity and historical boundaries of a Challenge in Tiizi.

A Challenge gives governed product expression to the ontological concept of an Undertaking. It provides the distinct collective context through which Participants may voluntarily pursue a defined objective within a persistent Group, using governed meaning, applicable Policy and truthful evidence.

This standard exists so that a Challenge remains intelligible as one governed undertaking without being confused with its Group, its composition inputs, its Participants, its Activity Events, its calculated outcomes or its representations.

It governs what a Challenge constitutionally is. It does not govern lifecycle, accountability allocation, roles, permissions, calculations, implementation or product behavior.

## 3. Constitutional Scope and Non-Allocation Boundary

### 3.1 Included constitutional scope

This standard governs:

- the Challenge as Tiizi's governed expression of a collective Undertaking;
- Challenge identity as a distinct Governed Subject;
- the Challenge as an Information Subject of Challenge information;
- Challenge identity after Constitutional Composition;
- the Challenge's constitutional relationships with Group, Platform Knowledge, Policy, Community context, participation, Activity Information, calculation, runtime and history;
- integrity of Challenge identity, meaning, Authority boundaries, provenance, history and presentation;
- bounded identity continuity across subordinate representations;
- the interaction of the eleven approved Platform Authorities with Challenge contexts;
- the distinction between a Challenge and its calculated or presented outcomes;
- future domain neutrality within approved platform scope.

### 3.2 Excluded constitutional scope

This standard does not establish:

- Challenge creation, creation workflow or creation Authority;
- lifecycle states, transitions, timing or effects;
- Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration or Operation allocations;

- the Authority or actor establishing an individual Challenge's identity, purpose or Goal;

Definition of the Challenge domain does not allocate an EOG-01 accountability relationship.

This standard names no Challenge Accountable Steward, Custodian, Administrator, Operator, Delegate or Presenter. It assigns no actor to a Platform Authority. Where an approved Authority already establishes a class of truth, this standard preserves that constitutional boundary without naming the actor, role or mechanism through which it is exercised.

## 4. Canonical Vocabulary

The definitions in this section apply only to the Challenge-domain precision required by this standard. Existing EOG-01, EOG-02 and EOG-03 terms retain their approved meanings.

### 4.1 Challenge

A Challenge is Tiizi’s governed, time-bounded collective undertaking through which members of a Group voluntarily pursue a defined objective using agreed participation rules, measurement criteria and completion conditions.

Time-boundedness distinguishes the Challenge from the persistent Group. It does not establish a duration, state, transition, deadline or lifecycle rule.

### 4.2 Undertaking

Undertaking is the ontological collective pursuit for which the Challenge is Tiizi's governed product expression. Undertaking does not create a second Challenge entity, Authority or product mode.

### 4.3 Challenge Identity

Challenge Identity is the authoritative account by which one Challenge is distinguishable and historically intelligible as the same governed Undertaking.

The Authority and actor establishing or changing an individual Challenge's identity remain pending.

### 4.4 Challenge Purpose

Challenge Purpose is the declared reason the collective Undertaking exists. It gives the Challenge coherent direction without becoming Policy, a Goal, evidence, Progress or a Completion condition merely by being declared.

### 4.5 Challenge Context

Challenge Context is the bounded set of constitutional relationships through which the Challenge, Group, Participants, applicable meaning, Policy and relevant activity can be understood together without merging their identities or Authorities.

### 4.6 Challenge Information

Challenge Information is information whose primary Information Subject is the Challenge. It may concern Challenge identity, purpose, Goal, composition context, applicable Policy references, participation context, administration, history or presentation while remaining distinct from personal, Knowledge, Activity Event and Derived Information.

This definition creates no field set, record type, visibility rule or storage model.

### 4.7 Challenge Integrity

Challenge Integrity is the constitutional condition in which Challenge identity, meaning, Authority boundaries, provenance, history and presentation remain coherent, attributable and distinguishable.

It is not a metric, score, lifecycle state, operational status or implementation control.

### 4.8 Challenge Identity Continuity

Challenge Identity Continuity is the bounded interpretive principle that subordinate presentation, runtime representation or local copying does not, by itself, create a new Challenge or alter Challenge identity.

It is not a Platform-wide doctrine, lifecycle doctrine, state machine, amendment test or replacement test. Later approved governance must determine whether a material contextual or substantive change preserves the same Challenge or establishes a new one.

## 5. Challenge in the Constitutional Ontology

The Challenge applies the ontology established in Document 00 without redefining it.

| Ontological concept | Challenge relationship | Constitutional boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | Supplies the governed conceptual environment in which the Challenge is intelligible. | Platform existence does not establish an individual Challenge. |
| Community | Receives governed product expression through the persistent Group. | The Community is not the Challenge. |
| Undertaking | Receives governed product expression through the Challenge. | The Undertaking remains distinct from the persistent Community. |
| Commitment | Receives governed product expression through voluntary Challenge participation. | Membership is not Commitment to every Challenge. |
| Evidence | May support truthful understanding of participation where accepted information is eligible under Policy. | Challenge identity and participant expression are not Evidence Eligibility. |
| Truth | Includes authoritative Challenge information and Derived Truth established through declared Authorities. | Popularity, display, repetition and local interpretation do not establish truth. |
| Motivation | May be supported through shared purpose, truthful participation and appropriate encouragement. | Motivation does not establish outcomes or Recognition. |
| Growth | May be supported through meaningful participation over time. | A Challenge does not guarantee or establish human growth. |

The Challenge is therefore an Undertaking through which a Community may organise voluntary Commitment around a shared purpose. Evidence may later support truthful understanding of that participation. Truth may support motivation and growth. None of these relationships transfers Authority or guarantees an outcome.

## 6. Constitutional Composition Dependency

### 6.1 Inherited doctrine

Constitutional Composition is governed by EOG-03. This standard does not redefine Platform Knowledge, Constitutional Composition, Layered Meaning or the principle that meaning precedes representation.

EOG-03 establishes the four constitutionally distinct composition inputs:

1. Platform Knowledge;
2. applicable Policy;
3. Group configuration; and
4. Community context.

EOG-03 also establishes that composition is not copying, inheritance, modification or forking, and that composition transfers no source Authority.

### 6.2 Resulting Challenge

The constitutional concern of this standard begins with the resulting Challenge.

After Constitutional Composition, the Challenge is:

- a distinct Governed Subject;
- a distinct Information Subject of Challenge Information;
- situated within a Group context;
- attributable to its applicable composition basis;
- distinct from every composition input;
- incapable of exercising Authority merely because it is governed;
- neither a representation of one input nor a merged Authority over all inputs.

### 6.3 Input integrity after composition

Every composition input retains its identity, provenance, Authority and declared scope after the Challenge is formed.

- Platform Knowledge continues to bear Authoritative Meaning established by Knowledge Authority.
- Applicable Policy continues to express approved rules within its declared scope.
- Group configuration continues to contribute the arrangement permitted by later governance.
- Community context continues to contribute local purpose, framing and shared meaning.

The resulting Challenge does not inherit the Authority, lifecycle or accountability relationships of an input. Use within the Challenge does not make an input owned by the Challenge or Group.

### 6.4 Meaning precedes representation

Every representation of a Challenge remains subordinate to the governed meaning and source truths it represents. A Template, Creation Mechanism, Runtime Projection, Historical Representation, interface, record, summary or local copy cannot establish Challenge identity or redefine its composition by presentation alone.

## 7. Challenge Identity

### 7.1 Distinct Governed Subject

Each Challenge is one distinct Governed Subject. It is not its Group, creator, Participants, Knowledge references, Policy, Activity Events, calculations or presentations.

The existence of a distinct Governed Subject requires coherent governance. It does not allocate Authority, Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration, Operation or presentation responsibility.

### 7.2 Distinct Information Subject

The Challenge is the Information Subject of information concerning the Challenge itself.

This boundary does not absorb adjacent information:

- a Person remains the Information Subject of personal and Profile information;
- Platform Knowledge remains the Governed Subject and Information Subject of its own Knowledge meaning;
- an Accepted Activity Event remains authoritative Activity Information concerning its event subject and context;
- Derived Truth retains the information subjects declared by its governing calculation and Policy.

Reference by a Challenge does not transfer information-subject status from another subject.

### 7.3 Identity, purpose and Goal

Challenge Identity distinguishes the undertaking. Challenge Purpose explains why it exists. A Goal directs participation toward an objective. These concepts are related but not interchangeable.

A Goal is not automatically:

- Platform Knowledge;
- applicable Policy;
- a target;
- Progress;
- a Completion condition; or
- a calculated outcome.

One Challenge shall remain coherent around one governed purpose. Whether later governance permits multiple Goals, Activities or measurement patterns does not create competing Challenge purposes.

The Authority and actor establishing Challenge Identity, Challenge Purpose and Goal remain pending founder decision.

### 7.4 Identity continuity

Challenge Identity Continuity protects the distinction between identity and representation.

A change in presentation, runtime representation, local label, summary or copy does not, by itself:

- create a new Challenge;
- change Challenge Identity;
- replace the governed undertaking;
- amend applicable Policy; or
- rewrite prior participation.

This principle does not determine whether a contextual or substantive change preserves identity. It does not define materiality, permissible amendment, replacement, succession or lifecycle effect. Those determinations remain for later approved governance.

### 7.5 No silent identity substitution

No representation may silently substitute one Challenge for another while communicating that the same undertaking continues. No later interpretation may treat a different undertaking as the original merely because a label, Group, Template or presentation is similar.

This rule preserves identity integrity. It does not establish a test for when a new Challenge must be created.

## 8. Challenge Context and Group Relationship

### 8.1 Group-first context

Every Version 2 Challenge exists within a governed Group context.

The constitutional relationship is:

> **Group**
>
> ↓ provides persistent Community context for
>
> **Challenge**
>
> ↓ provides a governed Undertaking context for
>
> **Participation**

The arrows express constitutional context, not ownership, Authority, lifecycle, permission or workflow.

### 8.2 Group and Challenge distinction

The Group is Tiizi's persistent governed Community. The Challenge is a time-bounded collective Undertaking within that Community.

The Group may exist without a Challenge. It may persist across multiple Challenges. One Challenge does not absorb the Group's identity, membership, Charter, governance health, Community growth or broader history.

A Challenge cannot acquire Group identity merely because it uses Group purpose, configuration or Community context. The Group does not automatically establish every Challenge fact merely because the Challenge exists within it.

### 8.3 Group Experience boundary

The EOG-03 statement that Groups own their governed experiences establishes a domain boundary. It means that the Challenge and its local Community context belong to the governed Group Experience rather than to Platform Knowledge.

It does not allocate Challenge Authority, Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration, Operation, presentation responsibility or permission. Group Accountable Stewardship does not become Challenge Accountable Stewardship by implication.

### 8.4 Context does not establish truth outside scope

Group configuration and Community context contribute bounded local meaning. They do not:

- redefine Platform Knowledge;
- determine Policy applicability merely by expression;
- prove participation or Activity occurrence;
- establish Evidence Eligibility;
- calculate Progress or outcomes; or
- transfer Authority from the Group to the Challenge.

## 9. Knowledge, Policy and Community Meaning

### 9.1 Layered meaning applied

EOG-03 governs Layered Meaning. In a Challenge context:

- Platform Meaning supplies the Authoritative Meaning of Platform Knowledge;
- Policy Meaning supplies the approved rules governing use in the declared context;
- Community Meaning supplies local purpose, configuration and framing;
- Historical Meaning preserves the historically applicable relationship among those layers.

This standard applies those layers to Challenge identity and context. It does not redefine them.

### 9.2 Platform Knowledge boundary

A Challenge may refer to approved Platform Knowledge. It does not become Platform Knowledge and cannot modify, fork or locally redefine Authoritative Meaning.

Repeated use, local preference, Community framing, Template selection or runtime availability does not establish new Knowledge meaning or equivalence.

### 9.3 Policy boundary

Applicable Policy governs permitted use and authoritative determinations within declared scope. It remains distinct from:

- Platform Knowledge;
- Challenge Identity and Purpose;
- Goal and target;
- Group configuration;
- Community context;
- Submission Intent;
- Accepted Activity Events;
- Evidence Eligibility as applied to a declared calculation; and
- Derived Truth.

A Template or Creation Mechanism may consume an approved determination of Policy applicability. Neither may establish that applicability merely by presenting, recommending or arranging a choice.

### 9.4 Community Meaning boundary

Community Meaning makes the Challenge locally intelligible within its Group. It may express purpose, framing and shared context where later governance permits.

Community Meaning may not override Platform Meaning or applicable Policy. It may not establish canonical Knowledge, prove participation or calculate outcomes.

## 10. Challenge Participation Boundary

### 10.1 Member and Participant

A Member has a governed relationship to a Group. A Participant has a governed relationship to a Challenge.

> **Member ≠ Participant**

Membership does not establish participation in every Challenge. A Profile does not establish membership or participation. Challenge presentation does not establish either relationship.

### 10.2 Voluntary and Challenge-specific participation

Participation is the voluntary relationship through which a Person commits to a particular governed Challenge. It is Challenge-specific and does not become general Group governance.

This standard does not define eligibility, invitation, joining, withdrawal, removal, consent effect or participation history.

### 10.3 Participation is not governance

> **Participation ≠ Governance**

Participant status does not, by itself, establish:

- Authority over the Challenge;
- Accountable Stewardship;
- Custody;
- Administration;
- Operation;
- permission over another Participant; or
- Authority over accepted or calculated truth.

If one actor later holds more than one EOG-01 relationship, every relationship must be declared separately with its own purpose and scope.

### 10.4 Participant expression boundary

Participant Authority establishes attributable Submission Intent within a governed participation context. It ends at that expression.

> **Submission Intent ≠ Accepted Activity Event**

Submission does not guarantee acceptance, Evidence Eligibility, Verification, Progress or any outcome.

## 11. Activity, Evidence and Calculation Boundary

### 11.1 Challenge context is not Activity occurrence

A Challenge may identify approved Activities, Metrics, Units, Goals and evaluation context. Those references make the undertaking intelligible. They do not prove that an Activity occurred.

Group communication, Feed content, Notification, participant claim, target or presentation is not an Activity Event merely because it concerns the Challenge.

### 11.2 Acceptance boundary

Acceptance Authority evaluates Submission Intent under approved Knowledge, Policy and participation context. Acceptance Authority establishes an Accepted Activity Event as authoritative Activity Information.

> **Accepted Activity Event ≠ Evidence Eligibility**

The Challenge does not accept an event. A creator, Participant, Group, Administrator, Template, mechanism or presentation cannot substitute for Acceptance Authority by implication.

### 11.3 Evidence Eligibility boundary

Policy Authority establishes whether an Accepted Activity Event is eligible for one declared calculation under applicable Policy.

Evidence Eligibility may depend on the declared calculation. It is not universal merely because an event was accepted.

> **Evidence Eligibility ≠ Derived Truth**

This standard defines no eligibility criterion, Verification effect, evidence weighting or calculation use.

### 11.4 Calculation boundary

Calculation Authority establishes authoritative Derived Truth from eligible evidence under approved Policy. The Challenge is the governed context for that truth; it is not Calculation Authority and does not calculate itself.

This standard does not define Progress, Completion, Ranking, Streak, Recognition, a formula, target effect, comparison method, finalization rule or correction effect. It preserves only the Authority and information boundaries already approved.

### 11.5 Information chain

The Challenge context conforms to the approved information chain:

> **Governed Knowledge**
>
> ↓ supplies meaning to
>
> **Applicable Policy and Participation Context**
>
> ↓ govern expression and evaluation of
>
> **Submission Intent**
>
> ↓ evaluated through an attributable Acceptance Decision into
>
> **Accepted Activity Event**
>
> ↓ made eligible for a declared calculation by Policy Authority as
>
> **Evidence Eligibility**
>
> ↓ used by Calculation Authority to establish
>
> **Derived Truth**
>
> ↓ communicated without redefinition through
>
> **Presentation**

The arrows express constitutional dependence, not workflow, timing, lifecycle or implementation.

## 14. Challenge Integrity

### 14.1 Identity integrity

Challenge Identity remains attributable and distinguishable from Group identity, composition inputs, Participants, Activity Events, outcomes and representations.

No label, copy, state description or presentation may silently substitute a different Challenge for the governed undertaking it claims to represent.

### 14.2 Meaning integrity

Platform Meaning, Policy Meaning and Community Meaning remain distinguishable within the Challenge context. No lower layer may silently redefine or override the meaning established within the scope of a higher governing source.

The Challenge remains attributable to the meanings applicable to its governed context without becoming their source Authority.

### 14.3 Authority integrity

Every authoritative fact retains one declared Authority to establish its truth. Authority does not arise from Challenge existence, creation access, stewardship, administration, participation, possession, visibility, repetition or presentation.

Composition and relationship stacking transfer no Authority by implication.

### 14.4 Historical integrity

Challenge identity, purpose, applicable meaning, Policy context and material authoritative change must remain historically intelligible and attributable. Later interpretation cannot silently rewrite the basis on which prior participation or Derived Truth was understood.

This requirement establishes no version, snapshot, retention, archival, correction or reopening rule.

### 14.5 Presentation integrity

Presentation may format, summarise and arrange Challenge information for an approved audience. It cannot establish, calculate, amend or replace the underlying truth.

Visibility, prominence, repetition or apparent completeness does not increase constitutional Authority.

### 14.6 No competing truth

A local copy, Template, Runtime Projection, Historical Representation, Feed item, Notification, leaderboard, summary or analytical interpretation cannot become an equal Challenge authority.

Where two representations conflict, the conflict must be resolved through the applicable source Authorities and later approved governance. Convenience does not choose the governing truth.

### 14.7 No silent substitution or reinterpretation

No actor, representation or downstream use may:

- replace one Challenge with another without attributable governance;
- change source meaning through presentation;
- present later meaning as though it governed earlier participation;
- collapse accepted Activity Information into Derived Truth;
- convert administrative or operational action into source Authority; or
- conceal the constitutional basis of a material change.

## 15. Challenge History and Identity Continuity

### 15.1 Historically intelligible Challenge identity

Challenge history must make the identity and constitutional context applicable to prior participation intelligible.

Historical understanding may require preservation of the relationship among:

- Challenge Identity and Purpose;
- the Group and Community context;
- Platform Meaning;
- applicable Policy and Policy Meaning;
- Group configuration;
- participation context;
- authoritative Activity Information;
- Evidence Eligibility; and
- Derived Truth.

This list defines constitutional intelligibility, not historical storage or a required representation.

### 15.2 Historical Meaning and Historical Representation

Historical Meaning is governed by EOG-03 as the historically applicable preservation of Platform Meaning, Policy Meaning and Community Meaning. It is not an independent Authority.

A Historical Representation may preserve the broader constitutional composition and Challenge context applicable to a historical use. It remains subordinate to the source truths and Authorities it represents. It is not:

- the live Challenge;
- a lifecycle state;
- a new Knowledge Asset;
- a competing source of truth; or
- an approved snapshot or versioning model.

### 15.3 Identity continuity in history

Challenge Identity Continuity means that a subordinate representation does not create or replace the constitutional undertaking it represents.

Presentation, runtime projection, analytical interpretation or local copying cannot, by itself, establish that:

- a new Challenge exists;
- the original Challenge has ended;
- the same Challenge continues after a material change; or
- prior participation now has different meaning.

Later governance must determine the effect of contextual or substantive change. This standard supplies no materiality threshold, amendment class, replacement test, reopening rule or state transition.

### 15.4 No silent historical reinterpretation

Later Knowledge, Policy, Community context, presentation or calculation treatment must not be applied retrospectively as though it governed prior participation when it did not.

This principle preserves truthful history while leaving correction, recalculation, dispute, retention, archival and access governance unresolved.

## 16. Presentation, Runtime and Analytical Boundaries

### 16.1 Presentation boundary

Presentation Authority may communicate Challenge information to an approved audience. It may format, summarise, order and contextualise information without changing its source meaning or Authority.

Presentation does not establish:

- Challenge Identity or Purpose;
- participation;
- Accepted Activity Events;
- Evidence Eligibility;
- Derived Truth; or
- lifecycle state.

### 16.2 Runtime boundary

Runtime Catalogue and Runtime Projection retain the meanings approved in EOG-03.

The Runtime Catalogue governs availability of approved Knowledge representations within the existing Knowledge Authority boundary. It is not a Platform Authority, actor, role or Challenge accountability relationship.

A Runtime Projection remains a subordinate representation of a Knowledge Asset for a declared runtime purpose. It does not become Challenge identity, Policy, evidence or calculation merely because the Challenge consumes it.

Runtime presence, absence, local availability or display cannot silently create, replace or redefine a Challenge.

### 16.3 Feed and Notification boundary

A Feed item may present Challenge-related communication or activity context. A Notification may communicate an event, information or need for attention.

Neither establishes the underlying Challenge fact. Neither is evidence, participation, Progress, lifecycle state or Authority merely because it is presented in connection with a Challenge.

### 16.4 Leaderboard and analytical boundary

A leaderboard is a presentation of underlying Ranking where later Policy permits Ranking. Analytical interpretation may describe or contextualise approved Challenge information for a declared purpose.

Neither becomes an independent Calculation Authority or source truth. Presentation and analysis must remain attributable to the authoritative information and limitations on which they depend.

## 17. Domain Neutrality, Deferred Governance and Prohibited Inferences

### 17.1 Domain neutrality

The Challenge is constitutionally domain-neutral. Its identity as a governed, time-bounded collective Undertaking does not depend on Fitness-specific terminology, exercise-only metrics or one measurement pattern.

Fitness and Wellness remain the approved Version 2 launch domains. Future domains such as education, nutrition, rehabilitation or mindfulness are not activated, promised or governed by this standard.

A future domain may use the Challenge model only after separate approved governance establishes its Knowledge, Policy, evidence meaning and scope. Future extensibility does not authorize individual, non-Group or indefinite Challenge modes.

### 17.2 Deferred governance

The following remain intentionally deferred:

- Challenge creation and the Authority authenticating individual Challenge identity, purpose and Goal;
- Challenge Accountable Stewardship and every other accountability allocation;
- Challenge taxonomy and type meaning;
- lifecycle, states, transitions, timing and recovery;
- participation eligibility, entry, withdrawal, removal and history;
- Policy authorship, applicability, amendment and effect;
- Goal, target and configuration amendment;
- activity submission, acceptance criteria, Verification and correction;
- late activity, early Completion, finalization and reopening;
- Progress, Completion, Ranking and Streak formulas or qualification;
- tie, fairness, comparison and scoring rules;
- Recognition and Reward governance;
- Template and Creation Mechanism governance;
- historical versions, snapshots, retention, archival and access;
- roles, permissions, privacy and security;
- operations and implementation mapping.

CHL-01 through CHL-04 and related pending ACT, GRP, KNW and RSK decisions remain pending.

### 17.3 Prohibited inferences

No person, document or system may infer from this standard that:

- a Challenge exercises Authority;
- a Group, Group Accountable Steward, creator or Administrator automatically owns or governs every Challenge fact;
- “Groups own their governed experiences” allocates an EOG-01 relationship;
- membership establishes Challenge participation;
- participation establishes governance;
- Challenge creation or configuration modifies Platform Knowledge;
- a Template or Creation Mechanism establishes Policy, permission, identity or truth;
- an Accepted Activity Event is automatically eligible for every calculation;
- Challenge identity, Goal or target is Progress, Completion or Ranking;
- presentation, runtime, Feed, Notification, leaderboard or analytics establishes source truth;
- historical integrity approves snapshots, versions, retention or reopening;
- identity continuity determines whether a material amendment preserves the same Challenge;
- current implementation behavior is constitutional governance;
- future domains or individual challenges are active capabilities.
