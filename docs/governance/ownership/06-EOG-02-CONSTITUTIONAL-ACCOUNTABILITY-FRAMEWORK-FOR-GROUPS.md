# EOG-02 — Constitutional Accountability Framework for Groups

## 1. Purpose

This framework applies the approved EOG-01 accountability vocabulary to the Group as Tiizi's persistent governed Community.

It establishes constitutional analysis and boundaries for Group existence, identity, purpose, stewardship, custody, administration, membership, Charter governance, moderation and inactivity. It does not approve a final Group accountability allocation except for the founder-established consequences of Group creation.

## 2. Status and Decision Boundary

**Status:** Draft for founder review.

The following founder clarifications are authoritative inputs rather than recommendations:

1. A Group is created, not proposed.
2. Creation establishes the Group's existence, initial identity and founding purpose.
3. The creator becomes the first Accountable Steward.
4. The creator becomes the first Member.
5. Stewardship is not permanent and may later be reassigned, voluntarily relinquished or otherwise changed through approved governance.
6. A Group must be capable of outliving its original creator.
7. Group identity may evolve through approved governance without that evolution becoming a lifecycle state.

All other authority, stewardship, custody, administration, Charter and moderation models in this document remain analysis pending founder approval.

## 3. Constitutional Definition of a Group

A Group is Tiizi's persistent Governed Subject through which the ontological concept of Community receives a governed product expression.

The founder-approved definition remains controlling:

> A Group is Tiizi’s persistent governed community through which people organise, participate, collaborate and pursue shared fitness, wellness and future governed purposes. It is the enduring social foundation of collective participation within the platform.

A Group is therefore:

- a persistent collective context rather than a temporary Undertaking;
- a governed identity and purpose distinct from the identities of its Members;
- a context in which membership, communication and Group Challenges may be governed;
- capable of continuity across changes in membership, stewardship, purpose and presentation;
- an Information Subject of information concerning the Group itself.

A Group is not:

- a Challenge or other Undertaking;
- a collection of Profiles;
- its creator, Accountable Steward, Administrator or Members;
- evidence that any person participated in an Undertaking;
- an Authority merely because it is governed;
- a lifecycle state, interface audience or role bundle.

## 4. Relationship to the Constitutional Ontology

This framework applies, but does not redefine, the ontology in Document 00.

| Ontological concept | Group relationship | Constitutional boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Community | The Group is Tiizi's governed product expression of Community. | The Group is more enduring than any one Undertaking. |
| Undertaking | A Group provides the Community context in which a Challenge may exist. | The Group is not the Challenge and does not itself establish Challenge outcomes. |
| Commitment | A Member may voluntarily become a Participant in an Undertaking. | Membership is not Commitment to every Undertaking. |
| Evidence | Governed participation within a Group context may give rise to information later eligible as Evidence. | Group identity, membership, communication and administration are not activity Evidence. |
| Truth | Authoritative Group facts must be established by a declared Platform Authority and remain attributable. | Popularity, display, possession or stewardship does not establish truth. |
| Motivation | The Group supports belonging, encouragement and truthful collective participation. | Motivation does not authorize invented progress or Recognition. |
| Growth | Persistent Community may support personal and collective growth over time. | The Group does not guarantee or independently establish human outcomes. |

## 5. Constitutional Purpose

The constitutional purpose of a Group is to provide an enduring, governed Community in which people may:

- belong in relation to a shared purpose;
- organise and collaborate without losing individual identity;
- support and encourage one another;
- participate voluntarily in governed Undertakings;
- understand collective participation truthfully;
- sustain continuity beyond one Challenge or one founding person.

This purpose concerns why the Group exists. It does not prescribe product behavior, communication features, workflows, interfaces or technical structures.

### 5.1 Constitutional Existence and Participation Principles

**A Group may exist without participation.** Participation is voluntary. Absence of participation does not affect the Group's constitutional existence.

**A Group may be inactive without being constitutionally unhealthy.** Activity level and governance integrity are different constitutional concepts.

**Participation concerns Community life. Existence concerns Community identity.** These concepts shall not be conflated.

## 6. Group Creation and Founding Consequences

### 6.1 Creation, Not Proposal

Constitutional Group existence begins with creation. A concept, draft, invitation to imagine a Group or proposal for a future Group is not a Group.

### 6.2 Founding Consequences

At creation:

- the Group exists as a Governed Subject;
- its initial identity is established;
- its founding purpose is established;
- its creator becomes its first Accountable Steward;
- its creator becomes its first Member.

These consequences apply to the constitutional relationship created, not to a named individual in this framework.

### 6.3 Creator Relationship

The creator is the Attributable Originator of the creation event and founding expression. By creating the Group, the creator establishes it as a Community identity. Founder clarification separately establishes the creator as the initial Accountable Steward and first Member.

Attributable origin does not, by itself, establish a general Authority, permanent stewardship, custody, administration or authority over later Members. The Platform Authority that authenticates the creation event and its founding facts remains for founder decision.

## 7. Group Identity and Purpose

### 7.1 Identity

Group identity is the authoritative account by which the Group remains recognizable as the same persistent Community. It is distinct from:

- the identities and Profiles of Members;
- presentation labels or local copies;
- stewardship and administration;
- lifecycle status;
- Challenge identity.

### 7.2 Purpose

Group purpose is the attributable reason the Community exists and the shared context within which its governed activities are intelligible.

Purpose is not automatically Platform Policy, Knowledge, a Challenge Goal or proof of Member Commitment.

### 7.3 Evolution Without Replacement

Group identity may evolve through approved governance. Goals, interests, Charter, description, visibility meaning and participation rules may change while the Group remains the same constitutional entity.

Material evolution must remain attributable and historically intelligible. Presentation changes cannot silently redefine identity or purpose. Exact amendment, retention and lifecycle rules remain deferred.

## 8. Authority to Authenticate Group Creation

The Group exists because the creator creates it. Constitutional authentication requires a declared Platform Authority to authenticate:

- the creation event;
- the Group existence established by that event;
- the initial Group identity and founding purpose expressed through creation.

Authentication makes the creation event and its founding facts authoritative Platform truth. It does not cause the Group to exist, convert creation into a proposal or require later participation.

The creator supplies attributable origin and establishes the Group by creating it. The creator does not identify the authenticating Platform Authority merely by being the origin.

The constitutionally viable Authority models are evaluated in the [Group Accountability Options Paper](06-EOG-02-GROUP-ACCOUNTABILITY-OPTIONS-PAPER.md). No model is approved by this framework.

The following boundaries apply to every viable model:

1. Governance Authority approves standards and boundaries; it does not establish an individual Group's identity or purpose.
2. Identity Authority establishes Person and Profile identity context; it does not silently absorb Group identity.
3. Participation Authority governs participation relationships and context; it does not provide the constitutional basis for Group existence.
4. Administrative Authority may authenticate a creation event only if founder-approved governance assigns that declared purpose and scope.
5. Presentation Authority communicates Group information but does not authenticate creation or establish Group truth.
6. Accountable Stewardship does not itself confer Authority to authenticate creation or establish later Group truth.
7. Creation and participation remain constitutionally separate even when participation follows immediately after creation.

## 9. Accountable Stewardship

### 9.1 Initial Stewardship

The creator becomes the initial Accountable Steward at Group creation. This is the sole accountability allocation established for Groups by the founder clarifications in this phase.

Initial stewardship does not confer every Authority, custody, administrative power or operational responsibility concerning the Group.

### 9.2 Constitutional Responsibilities

The Accountable Steward is answerable for coherent governance of the Group, including:

- preserving fidelity to the Group's governed purpose;
- ensuring accountability relationships are declared rather than inferred;
- ensuring custody and administration are governed;
- ensuring unresolved constitutional risks are escalated;
- supporting continuity beyond the original creator;
- preserving separation between Group truth and presentation;
- remaining answerable for stewardship even when responsibilities are assigned.

These are stewardship responsibilities, not automatic permissions or Authority allocations.

### 9.3 Singular Stewardship, Reassignment and Succession

Every Group shall have exactly one Accountable Steward relationship at any given time. That relationship is singular and remains ultimately answerable for coherent Group governance.

The Accountable Steward relationship may be reassigned through approved governance. Its holder may voluntarily relinquish stewardship under later approved governance. Reassignment and relinquishment must support continuity beyond the creator without becoming Delegation of Authority or Assignment of Responsibility.

### 9.4 Stewardship Council

A **Stewardship Council** is a governed body established to support the Accountable Steward in fulfilling the constitutional stewardship of a Group.

- Council membership does not itself create Accountable Stewardship.
- Council members are not individually Accountable Stewards merely because they belong to the Council.
- The Council is not a Platform Authority.
- The Council is not Administration.
- The Council is not Custody.
- The Council does not replace the Accountable Steward.

Future governance may define Council composition, appointment, decision-making and responsibilities. No such governance is created here.

### 9.5 Stewardship Failure

Stewardship failure means that the required answerability for coherent Group governance is absent, unavailable, conflicted or materially unfulfilled. It does not automatically terminate the Group, transfer stewardship or grant another actor Authority.

A later standard must govern continuity, review, temporary protection and valid Stewardship Reassignment without treating inactivity, absence or operational convenience as an automatic transfer.

## 10. Custody

### 10.1 Group Information Requiring Custodial Treatment

The following conceptual Group information requires preservation and protection under purpose-limited custody when it exists:

- Group identity and purpose information;
- founding and later Charter expressions;
- authoritative membership relationship information;
- stewardship declarations and reassignment records;
- administrative and moderation decisions;
- Group communication retained under approved governance;
- privacy, visibility and participation-context information;
- attributable historical Group information;
- records necessary to explain material change.

This list identifies information requiring a custodial model. It does not name a Custodian or define retention.

### 10.2 Purpose-Limited Custody

Custody must state the information, purpose and scope being preserved. Access, storage or possession alone creates no Custodian relationship.

### 10.3 Shared Custody

Custodial duties may be shared through separate, explicit assignments where later governance permits. Shared custody does not create Accountable Stewardship, duplicate source Authority or unrestricted access.

### 10.4 Stewardship Versus Custody

The Accountable Steward is answerable for ensuring custody is governed. A Custodian preserves and protects assigned information. Neither relationship implies the other.

## 11. Group Administration

### 11.1 Meaning

Group Administration is the exercise of explicitly assigned Administrative Authority for a declared Group purpose and scope.

An Administrator may carry out approved high-impact Group decisions or actions only where later governance authorizes the action. The title “Administrator” alone creates no Authority or other accountability relationship.

### 11.2 Boundaries

Group Administration is distinct from:

- **Authority to establish truth:** administration cannot silently establish Group identity, purpose, membership or Charter truth;
- **Accountable Stewardship:** administration does not make the Administrator answerable for the Group as a whole;
- **Custody:** administrative access or possession does not create preservation responsibility;
- **Operation:** performing administrative action does not confer general Operational Authority;
- **Presentation:** the ability to edit what is displayed does not establish the underlying fact.

### 11.3 Constitutionally Appropriate Administrative Action Classes

Later governance may authorize Administrative Authority to perform purpose-limited actions concerning:

- execution of membership decisions established under applicable Participation Authority and Policy;
- execution of conduct decisions established under applicable Policy;
- administration of approved Group information changes;
- appointment or removal of Administrators under a separately approved authority model;
- execution of a valid Stewardship Reassignment without becoming the source of stewardship legitimacy;
- protection of Group integrity where approved governance supplies the basis.

This classification identifies possible constitutional action classes. It grants no permission and names no actor.

## 12. Membership

### 12.1 Meaning

Membership is the governed participation relationship by which a Person belongs to a Group as a Member.

The creator becomes the first Member at creation. Later membership relationships require separately governed establishment and lifecycle rules.

### 12.2 Constitutional Consequences

Membership provides Community belonging and a relationship context. It may support communication, shared visibility and eligibility for later Undertakings where separate governance permits.

Membership does not, by itself, create:

- Authority;
- Accountable Stewardship;
- Custody;
- Administration;
- participation in every Challenge;
- approval power over another Member;
- access to all Group or Member information.

Membership is a participation relationship, not a governance bundle. A Member may hold another relationship only when it is separately declared with its own purpose and scope.

## 13. Group Charter Governance

### 13.1 Constitutional Status

A Group Charter is a Governed Subject that expresses the Group's attributable constitutional commitments concerning its identity, purpose and the rules by which its Community intends to remain coherent.

The Charter is distinct from:

- the Group itself;
- Platform constitutional governance;
- a Challenge Policy;
- a presentation or description;
- a role or permission matrix;
- a lifecycle standard.

### 13.2 Information Character

A Charter may contain different kinds of authoritative information. Identity and purpose expressions remain Group Participation Information. Rules intended to govern conduct, membership or amendment require applicable Policy Authority. Presentation of the Charter remains subordinate to its authoritative content.

The Charter's composite nature does not permit one relationship to establish every statement merely because the statements appear in one document.

### 13.3 Initial Charter

Creation establishes a founding purpose but does not, without founder approval, settle whether every Group must have a separate formal Charter at creation. Where an initial Charter exists, the creator may be its Attributable Originator; the Authority establishing its different authoritative contents remains subject to the approved Charter model.

### 13.4 Amendment and Evolution

A Charter may evolve only through later approved governance that preserves:

- the applicable Authority for each changed statement;
- attribution of the amendment;
- the relationship to Group purpose;
- historically intelligible prior meaning;
- the distinction between an amendment and presentation editing.

Whether amendments require formal versions remains pending. Historical integrity is required; a particular versioning method is not yet approved.

### 13.5 Binding Effect

A Charter may become binding on Members only where approved Policy defines its scope, entry effect, amendment effect and relationship to voluntary membership. Membership alone cannot be treated as consent to undisclosed or silently changed terms.

### 13.6 Charter and Stewardship

The Accountable Steward is answerable for ensuring the Charter remains coherently governed. Stewardship does not automatically establish or amend Charter truth. Charter amendment Authority and stewardship remain distinct.

## 14. Moderation Governance

### 14.1 Constitutional Meaning

Moderation is a purpose-limited governance response intended to protect the integrity, safety, purpose and truthful participation of the Group Community.

Moderation is not an independent Platform Authority. A moderation action must identify the applicable Policy basis, Administrative Authority, affected participation relationship and accountable attribution.

### 14.2 Constitutional Relationship Map

| Potential action | Constitutional relationship that may justify the action | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Invite a prospective Member | Attributable Originator or Contributor may express the invitation; applicable Policy governs its meaning. | Invitation does not establish membership. |
| Approve entry | Administrative Authority may establish the approved administrative decision; Participation Authority establishes the membership relationship under Policy. | Administration cannot invent membership criteria. |
| Suspend, restore or remove a Member | Administrative Authority may establish the approved moderation decision under Policy; Participation Authority establishes the resulting relationship truth under later lifecycle governance. | No state or transition is approved here. |
| Issue a warning | Administrative Authority may establish an attributable conduct-management decision under Policy. | A warning is not evidence of activity and does not silently change membership. |
| Apply a temporary restriction | Administrative Authority may establish the approved restriction under Policy. | Scope, duration and effect remain for later standards. |
| Apply permanent removal | Administrative Authority may establish the approved decision under Policy; Participation Authority establishes the membership consequence. | Permanence, appeal and lifecycle effect remain deferred. |
| Edit Group identity, purpose, goals or interests | A Contributor or Administrator may originate a proposed change; the declared Authority for the underlying Group truth must establish it. | Administrative ability to edit presentation is insufficient. |
| Amend the Charter | A Contributor or Administrator may originate an amendment; applicable Group-truth and Policy Authorities establish its authoritative contents. | Stewardship or administration alone does not approve amendment. |
| Appoint an Administrator | Administrative Authority may establish an appointment only under approved governance. | Appointment does not confer stewardship, custody or unrestricted Authority. |
| Establish a Stewardship Council | Later governance must establish the Council as a supporting governed body. | Administration alone cannot create Accountable Stewardship or make Council members Accountable Stewards. |
| Transfer stewardship | A valid Stewardship Reassignment must establish the new stewardship relationship. | It is not Delegation of Authority or Assignment of Responsibility. |

This map identifies relationship justification, not permissions, roles, actors, workflows or lifecycle transitions.

### 14.3 Moderation Boundaries

Every constitutionally valid moderation action must be:

- grounded in approved Policy;
- exercised through a declared Platform Authority;
- limited to purpose and scope;
- attributable to the actor and Authority;
- distinguishable from stewardship and custody;
- historically intelligible;
- constrained by privacy and least privilege.

Appeal, review, duration, thresholds and procedural safeguards remain deferred.

## 15. Dormant Membership

Inactivity is an observed condition, not misconduct by itself.

A Group may be inactive without being constitutionally unhealthy. Activity level does not determine governance integrity, continued identity or constitutional existence.

An approved Group Charter or Policy may later establish participation expectations and may identify inactivity as a basis for a governed membership decision. Such a rule must remain truthful, knowable and purpose-related. It must not retrospectively convert ordinary inactivity into misconduct.

Where later governance permits removal for inactivity:

- the action must have an attributable reason and Policy basis;
- Administrative Authority must remain distinct from Participation Authority;
- the resulting membership truth must be established through the declared Participation Authority;
- prior membership and the moderation action must remain historically intelligible;
- inactivity must not be represented as a moral or evidential judgment beyond what Policy supports.

This framework does not approve an inactivity threshold, removal rule, notice, appeal or lifecycle transition.

## 16. Future Governance Direction (Non-Governing)

### 16.1 Group Governance Health

**Group Governance Health** is the constitutional condition of a Group determined by the integrity of its governance relationships rather than by its activity level.

Possible future governance considerations may include:

- a valid Accountable Steward;
- stewardship continuity;
- a Stewardship Council, where established;
- governance orphan status;
- Charter governance;
- unresolved constitutional issues;
- moderation governance;
- governance review status.

This is future governance only. EOG-02 creates no Group Governance Health standard, metric, implementation, score or determination.

### 16.2 Community Growth and Governance Maturity

**Community Growth** concerns healthier personal or collective change supported through meaningful participation, motivation and time.

**Governance Maturity** concerns the developing constitutional resilience of a Group's governance relationships. Conceptual examples may include successful Stewardship Reassignment, Charter evolution, improved governance, governance continuity and constitutional resilience.

Governance Maturity is conceptual only. No maturity model, level, metric, score, assessment or implementation is approved.

Community Growth and Governance Maturity are distinct. Neither is inferred from activity volume alone.

| Attributable Originator | The creator originates the founding expression; other changes retain their origin. | Founding relationship established; later origin depends on context. |
| Authority to establish truth | A declared Platform Authority must authenticate the creation event and founding facts; later material Group truth retains its applicable declared Authority. | Creation-authentication model pending founder decision. |
| Accountable Steward | The creator becomes the initial Accountable Steward. | Founder-established initial allocation; later model pending. |
| Custodian | Preserves purpose-limited Group information. | Allocation pending. |
| Administrator | Exercises explicitly assigned Administrative Authority. | Powers and allocation pending. |
| Operator | Supports continuity under Operational Authority. | Allocation pending. |
| Contributor | Supplies attributable Group content or expertise without approval power. | Contextual relationship only. |
| Participant | Holds a governed relationship to an Undertaking, not merely to the Group. | Distinct from Member. |
| Delegate | Receives only explicit, limited Authority or responsibility. | Any Group delegation pending. |
| Delegating Party | May confer only what it is entitled to confer. | Any Group delegation pending. |
| Presenter | Communicates Group information without establishing underlying truth. | Allocation pending. |
| Downstream User | Uses Group information for an approved purpose without acquiring Authority. | Allocation pending. |

The Stewardship Council is a supporting Governed Subject, not an EOG-01 accountability relationship and not a substitute for the singular Accountable Steward relationship.

## 18. Deferred Areas

This framework defers:

- the Platform Authority that authenticates Group creation and founding facts;
- Stewardship Reassignment procedures;
- voluntary relinquishment and succession procedures;
- succession and stewardship-failure procedures;
- Stewardship Council composition, appointment, decision-making and responsibilities;
- Custodian allocation and retention;
- Administrator allocation and specific powers;
- membership lifecycle, invitations, suspension, restoration, removal and departure;
- Charter requirement, initial form, amendment Authority, binding effect and versioning treatment;
- moderation thresholds, process, appeal and remedy;
- inactivity thresholds and consequences;
- Group Governance Health governance;
- any Governance Maturity model;
- roles and permissions;
- privacy and security controls;
- technical implementation.

## 19. Governance and Precedence

This framework is subordinate to Document 00, Document 00A's interpretive doctrine, the Platform Constitution, Platform Principles, Platform Authority Model, Entity Ownership Foundation, Group Domain Standard and approved EOG-01 semantics.

The singular Accountable Steward relationship remains fully consistent with EOG-01. A Stewardship Council may support that relationship only under later governance and can never replace or multiply it.

This framework does not amend any governing source.
