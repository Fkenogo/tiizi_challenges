# GRP Foundation Closure Phase 2 — Cross-model Consistency Assessment

**Status:** Consistency assessment of unapproved recommendations

**Date:** 2026-07-21

## 1. Purpose

This assessment tests the five preferred models as one constitutional package. It identifies remaining tensions and downstream consequences without approving decisions, resolving CG-08, allocating relationships or amending approved governance.

## 2. Preferred-model Chain

```text
GFC-01-B — bounded creation authentication
        ↓
GFC-02-B — successor-continuous reassignment plus Charter succession
        ↓
GFC-03-A — founding binding Charter
        ↓
GFC-04-B — Council with bounded supporting responsibilities
        ↓
GFC-05-A — permanent independent Council inventory identity
```

The arrows show constitutional dependency, not workflow, Authority transfer or automatic approval.

## 3. Compatibility Findings

| Combined models | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| GFC-01-B + GFC-02-B | Coherent | Creation authentication and continuing stewardship remain different constitutional facts and relationships. |
| GFC-02-B + GFC-03-A | Strongly coherent | A Charter applicable from creation can supply a succession rule without itself establishing reassignment truth. |
| GFC-03-A + GFC-04-B | Coherent | Charter meaning may later bound Council support, but Council responsibility does not become Charter Authority or stewardship. |
| GFC-04-B + GFC-05-A | Strongly coherent | A stable Council entity can later receive explicit responsibility allocations without inventory classification implying them. |
| All five | Coherent with later-governance dependencies | The package preserves one Group truth boundary, one steward, one Charter identity and one Council identity while deferring procedure and allocation. |

## 4. Authority Consistency

The package uses no new Platform Authority.

- Administrative Authority is recommended only for narrowly scoped Group-creation authentication.
- Participation Authority remains responsible for governed relationship context, including first Membership under later approved treatment.
- Policy Authority supplies applicable rules but does not establish individual Group or Charter truth.
- Governance Authority continues to approve standards and resolve unresolved Authority conflicts; it does not establish ordinary Group facts.
- The Charter, Group and Council are Governed Subjects, not actors exercising Authority.
- A later Charter succession principle cannot establish reassignment truth without a separately declared existing Platform Authority.

No Authority recommendation in this package allocates an actor, role, mechanism or technical system.

## 5. Accountability Consistency

The preferred models preserve:

- exactly one Accountable Steward relationship per Group at any time;
- Stewardship Reassignment as distinct from Delegation of Authority and Assignment of Responsibility;
- the Council as support rather than shared stewardship;
- Council-body responsibility as separately declared and bounded;
- creator origin as distinct from creation authentication; and
- Membership as distinct from Group governance.

The package does not identify who performs a future reassignment, Charter amendment, Council appointment or administrative action.

## 6. Remaining Constitutional Tensions

| Tension | Why it remains | Required later governance |
|---|---|---|
| Administrative creation boundary | GFC-01-B could be over-read as general Group Authority. | A founder-approved creation-authentication rule with express exclusions and attribution. |
| Charter rule versus reassignment truth | GFC-02-B relies on Charter meaning, but a Charter cannot exercise Authority. | Identification of the existing Authority establishing exceptional reassignment and later Charter interpretation governance. |
| Binding Charter versus Policy | Binding internal commitments cannot contradict Platform governance or applicable Policy. | Charter hierarchy, applicability and interpretation standard. |
| Council responsibility versus stewardship | Collective support could be mistaken for shared answerability. | Council responsibility standard preserving the singular steward and separate declarations. |
| Permanent entity versus allocation inference | C-GRP-08 classification could be misread as granting relationships. | EOG-05 amendment language preserving the cross-register non-allocation contract. |
| Information Subject boundary | Council information may concern the body, its Group or a Council relationship. | CG-08 classification amendment with an explicit Information Subject treatment. |

These tensions do not make the package internally inconsistent. They explain why founder approval would unlock later governance rather than complete it.

## 7. Implementation-neutral Consequences

If approved, the package would constitutionally require later governance to preserve:

1. an attributable distinction between the creator's expression and authoritative creation authentication;
2. unbroken singular stewardship through any effective reassignment;
3. a Charter whose applicable meaning remains historically intelligible;
4. separate declarations for Council responsibility and every other EOG-01 relationship; and
5. inventory classification that never implies allocation.

It would not require any interface, workflow, data structure, permission, role, approval mechanism, state, transition or technical service.

## 8. Effect on Later Governance

### 8.1 GRP work

- GRP creation governance could proceed from a bounded authentication Authority.
- GRP stewardship work could define reassignment and exceptional continuity without reconsidering singular answerability.
- Charter governance could define applicability, amendment and historical integrity.
- Council governance could define composition and responsibilities without reconsidering whether the Council may be substantively useful.
- Membership governance would remain separate and would not inherit stewardship or Council relationships.

### 8.2 CHL work

A constitutionally stable Group context would reduce ambiguity in CHL-01. The package would not decide Challenge creation, purpose, stewardship or lifecycle.

### 8.3 Allocation work

Approval would remove or materially reduce these blockers:

- the Group creation Authority blocker;
- the ordinary and exceptional stewardship-continuity model blocker;
- the Charter constitutional-effect blocker; and
- the CG-08 classification direction blocker.

Allocation drafting would still wait for the attributable EOG-05 amendment, later Group roles and membership governance, Charter allocation detail, Council responsibility detail and other milestones identified in the approved roadmap.

## 9. Cross-model Verdict

**The preferred package is constitutionally coherent and suitable for founder review.**

Its dependencies are explicit, its Authorities remain bounded, and its Council and Charter models reinforce rather than weaken singular stewardship. The package creates no implementation rule and makes no allocation. All recommendations remain pending founder approval.
