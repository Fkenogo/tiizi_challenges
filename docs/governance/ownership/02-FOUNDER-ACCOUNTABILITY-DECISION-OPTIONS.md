# Founder Accountability Decision Options

## 1. Purpose and Method

This document provides the constitutional analysis supporting the [Founder Accountability Decision Agenda](02-FOUNDER-ACCOUNTABILITY-DECISION-AGENDA.md). It evaluates all models that can operate within the approved 11-authority architecture and separately identifies alternatives that would require constitutional change.

The analysis follows five tests:

1. Does the option preserve one Authority to establish each fact?
2. Does it keep Accountable stewardship, custody, administration, operation and presentation separate?
3. Does it preserve domain boundaries and historically intelligible truth?
4. Can it scale without assigning technical systems or named individuals as constitutional actors?
5. Does it leave lifecycle, permission, privacy, security and implementation choices to later standards?

Recommendations emerge after comparison. Every recommendation is labelled **Recommended — pending founder approval** and has no constitutional effect until explicit founder approval.

## 2. EOG-01 — Stewardship and Custody Allocation Framework

### 2.1 Decision Question

How should Tiizi allocate one Accountable steward and appropriate custody across 63 governed subjects without confusing stewardship with Authority, administration or operation?

### 2.2 Option A — Individual Allocation for Every Register Row

**Description:** The founder separately approves the Accountable steward and Custodian for each of the 63 rows.

**Advantages:**

- maximum row-level precision;
- every exception is visible;
- no reliance on broad domain inference;
- straightforward audit of one row at a time.

**Disadvantages:**

- high founder oversight burden;
- repeated decisions create inconsistency risk;
- future subjects require repeated constitutional review;
- related subjects may receive fragmented accountability.

**Constitutional clarity:** High at row level, medium across the system because patterns may diverge.

**Operational complexity:** High. Sixty-three separate allocations must be maintained and reconciled.

**Founder oversight burden:** High initially and high during amendments.

**Scalability:** Low to medium. Every future domain expands the decision inventory directly.

**Accountability clarity:** High if maintained perfectly; low if similar rows drift.

**Maintenance effort:** High.

**Governance impact:** Produces a detailed register but no reusable allocation rule.

**Implementation neutrality:** High; the model does not depend on technology.

**Register impact:** All 63 rows become individually allocated.

**Future implications:** Lifecycle and permission standards must interpret many independent allocations.

**Why not recommended:** Precision does not justify the permanent constitutional maintenance burden when durable domain and capability patterns can provide the same clarity with explicit exceptions.

### 2.3 Option B — Foundational-Domain Allocation

**Description:** Each subject inherits both Accountable stewardship and custody from its foundational domain, with limited exceptions.

**Advantages:**

- simple mental model;
- strong domain accountability;
- low founder review burden;
- new domain subjects receive a default allocation.

**Disadvantages:**

- custody often crosses domains, especially audit, operations, presentation and Runtime Catalogue preservation;
- cross-platform subjects do not fit one foundational domain cleanly;
- domain steward could appear to control source authority or technical operation;
- exceptions may become hidden.

**Constitutional clarity:** High for stewardship; low to medium for custody and cross-platform subjects.

**Operational complexity:** Low initially, medium when exceptions accumulate.

**Founder oversight burden:** Low.

**Scalability:** High for domain growth, weaker for platform capabilities spanning domains.

**Accountability clarity:** High for domain outcomes, ambiguous for preservation and audit.

**Maintenance effort:** Low to medium.

**Governance impact:** Strengthens Domain Standards but risks treating domain accountability as operational custody.

**Implementation neutrality:** High.

**Register impact:** Most rows inherit a domain allocation; cross-platform rows require exceptions.

**Future implications:** Operational, audit and presentation standards would need repeated boundary corrections.

**Why not recommended:** It places preservation responsibilities into domain accountability even where the constitutional work is capability-specific.

### 2.4 Option C — Governance-Capability Allocation

**Description:** Stewardship and custody are allocated by capabilities such as Identity and Profile, Group and Membership, Challenge, Activity Integrity, Knowledge, Derived Truth, Analytics, Administration, Audit, Presentation and Communication.

**Advantages:**

- aligns accountability with specialized governance work;
- handles cross-platform subjects well;
- makes audit, analytics and presentation responsibilities explicit;
- supports organizational specialization.

**Disadvantages:**

- one domain may be split among many stewards;
- capability boundaries may overlap;
- Group-first accountability can become fragmented;
- future organizational changes can destabilize allocations.

**Constitutional clarity:** Medium to high if capability boundaries are precise.

**Operational complexity:** Medium to high because cross-capability coordination is constant.

**Founder oversight burden:** Medium.

**Scalability:** High for specialized functions, medium for coherent domain accountability.

**Accountability clarity:** High for technical and governance capabilities, weaker for end-to-end domain outcomes.

**Maintenance effort:** Medium.

**Governance impact:** Requires a mature capability taxonomy before the register can be completed.

**Implementation neutrality:** Medium to high; capability names must remain organizational rather than technical.

**Register impact:** Rows group by governance function rather than constitutional domain.

**Future implications:** Domain Standards may need extensive cross-capability accountability maps.

**Why not recommended:** It solves custody well but fragments the single accountable view of Profile, Group, Challenge, Activity Event and Knowledge outcomes.

### 2.5 Option D — Constitutional Hybrid

**Description:** Accountable stewardship is domain-led; custody is capability-led; cross-platform subjects and material exceptions receive explicit row-level allocation. Each subject still has exactly one Accountable steward.

**Advantages:**

- preserves one accountable domain perspective;
- assigns preservation to the capability suited to it;
- supports audit, presentation and operational custody without transferring stewardship;
- scales through defaults while retaining row-level precision;
- makes exceptions visible.

**Disadvantages:**

- requires an explicit exception rule;
- needs a maintained stewardship-to-custody relationship map;
- careless drafting could create overlapping stewardship;
- slightly more complex than a single-axis model.

**Constitutional clarity:** High. Stewardship and custody are deliberately separated.

**Operational complexity:** Medium.

**Founder oversight burden:** Medium initially, low after allocation rules are established.

**Scalability:** High across new domains and cross-platform capabilities.

**Accountability clarity:** High when each exception names one steward and one or more purpose-limited Custodians.

**Maintenance effort:** Medium.

**Governance impact:** Enables a reusable Entity Accountability and Custody Standard.

**Implementation neutrality:** High. Durable organizational roles are used; technical systems remain execution mechanisms only.

**Register impact:** All 63 rows receive a domain or capability default plus explicit exceptions.

**Future implications:** Simplifies lifecycle, permission and operational standards because each can reference a stable steward/custody pattern.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option D.

**Trade-off:** Tiizi accepts moderate mapping discipline in return for scalable, explicit constitutional separation.

### 2.6 EOG-01 Consequences

**Affected register rows:** All 63.

**Affected governance documents:** Entity Ownership Foundation; Platform Authority Model clarification; five Domain Standards; Entity Ownership Register; future Entity Accountability and Custody Standard.

**Unlocked phases:** Final register allocation, lifecycle stewardship drafting, Roles and Permissions scoping, Privacy accountability, Security control accountability and operational custody mapping.

**Remaining blocked:** Named domain and capability roles still require EOG-02 through EOG-06 and later role governance.

## 3. EOG-02 — Group Identity, Purpose and Stewardship

### 3.1 Facts That Must Remain Separate

- creator expression proposing a Group;
- accepted Group identity;
- accepted Group purpose;
- compliance of that purpose with Policy;
- Accountable stewardship;
- administrative action;
- membership relationships.

### 3.2 Authority Model A — Unified Participation Establishment

**Description:** A permitted actor originates Group identity and purpose. Participation Authority establishes both as the accepted collective participation context under Policy.

**Strengths:**

- Group is constitutionally a governed participation context;
- one source Authority establishes coherent Group identity and purpose;
- Identity Authority remains focused on Persons and Profiles;
- Administration remains execution and recordkeeping rather than source truth;
- Policy constrains without authoring the individual Group.

**Weaknesses:**

- Participation Authority's current relationship language requires clarification to include establishment of the collective context itself;
- later standards must prevent the exercising actor from gaining unrestricted Group control;
- purpose amendment boundaries require later lifecycle governance.

**Constitutional consequence:** Refinement of Participation Authority and Group Domain wording; no new Authority.

**Operational consequence:** A trusted establishment action must be attributable to the exercising Participation Authority, but no execution mechanism is prescribed.

**Scalability:** High. The pattern applies to future collective contexts.

**Founder oversight:** Low after the constitutional rule is established.

### 3.3 Authority Model B — Participation Identity With Separate Policy Compliance

**Description:** Participation Authority establishes Group identity and the declared purpose. Policy Authority separately establishes whether that purpose is permitted. The compliance determination is a separate fact and does not become the purpose itself.

**Strengths:**

- preserves Policy as a constraint rather than author;
- makes purpose compliance review explicit;
- avoids Administrative Authority becoming source truth;
- supports different Group-purpose policies without changing identity authority.

**Weaknesses:**

- creates two related facts that presentation could easily collapse;
- does not materially change the authority establishing Group purpose compared with Model A;
- increases governance and audit complexity where purpose review is unnecessary.

**Constitutional consequence:** Refinement defining the compliance fact and its Policy Authority.

**Operational consequence:** Requires separate attribution for purpose and compliance determination.

**Scalability:** High where regulated or restricted Group purposes exist; unnecessary overhead for general Groups.

**Founder oversight:** Medium because Policy categories require later review.

### 3.4 Administrative-Establishment Alternative — Not Viable Without Constitutional Change

Administrative Authority could record an accepted Group-creation action, but making it the source Authority for Group identity or purpose would conflict with the approved boundary that Administrative Information does not become domain truth. This alternative is not constitutionally viable within Phase E1A and is not recommended.

Identity Authority is also not a viable source for Group identity: its approved scope concerns a Person's governed Identity and Profile context, while Group identity is Participation Information.

Governance Authority is expressly excluded from establishing individual Group purpose.

### 3.5 Stewardship Models

#### Model 1 — Creator as Permanent Steward

**Advantages:** Immediate clarity and low initial administration.

**Disadvantages:** Confuses origin with stewardship, fails when the creator departs, and imposes permanent accountability without transfer governance.

**Scalability:** Low.

**Founder oversight:** Low initially, high when exceptions occur.

#### Model 2 — Creator as Initial Transferable Steward

**Advantages:** Preserves early accountability, separates creator status from permanent constitutional status, and supports continuity.

**Disadvantages:** Requires later transfer governance and an explicit acceptance of the initial allocation.

**Scalability:** High.

**Founder oversight:** Low after lifecycle governance exists.

#### Model 3 — Institutionally Designated Steward From Establishment

**Advantages:** Strong organizational continuity and no dependency on the creator.

**Disadvantages:** Heavy for ordinary Groups and presumes an institutional actor before Tiizi has approved Group role governance.

**Scalability:** Medium; appropriate as an exception for managed Groups.

**Founder oversight:** Medium.

#### Model 4 — Multiple Equal Stewards

This conflicts with the Entity Ownership Foundation's one-steward rule. Collaborative administration remains viable, but equal constitutional stewardship is not.

### 3.6 Recommended Group Model

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Unified Participation Establishment with one durable Group Steward. The accepted creator may become initial steward only through the governed establishment decision; creator status alone is insufficient. Stewardship is transferable under a later Group Lifecycle Standard. Administrators receive explicitly delegated administrative responsibility and do not share or inherit stewardship automatically. Institutionally designated stewardship remains a permitted row-level exception under EOG-01.

**Why alternatives were not recommended:** Model B adds a compliance fact that should be used only where Policy needs it. Permanent creator stewardship is fragile. Institutional designation is too heavy as the default. Multiple equal stewardship violates the baseline.

**Trade-off:** Participation Authority needs a clarified establishment boundary, while later role and lifecycle governance must identify who may exercise it.

### 3.7 EOG-02 Consequences

**Direct rows:** GRP-01 and GRP-04.

**Consequential rows:** GRP-02, GRP-03 and GRP-06.

**Affected documents:** Platform Authority Model; Entity Ownership Foundation; Group Domain Standard; Group validation; Entity Ownership Register; later Group Lifecycle and Roles and Permissions Standards.

**Future phases unlocked:** Group stewardship allocation, Group Lifecycle drafting, role separation and Challenge stewardship dependency.

**Remaining blocked:** GRP-03 and GRP-04 lifecycle details; GRP-01 role vocabulary; Privacy and Security Standards.

## 4. EOG-03 — Challenge Identity, Purpose, Goal and Stewardship

### 4.1 Facts That Must Remain Separate

- creator expression proposing a Challenge;
- accepted Challenge identity;
- accepted Challenge purpose;
- Goal as desired outcome;
- Activity, Metric and Unit Knowledge meaning;
- Target and evaluation Policy;
- Accountable stewardship;
- administrative action;
- Progress, Completion and Ranking.

### 4.2 Authority Model A — Unified Participation Undertaking

**Description:** Participation Authority establishes Challenge identity, purpose and Goal as the accepted shared undertaking. Knowledge Authority supplies referenced meaning. Policy Authority separately establishes evaluation rules, Target treatment, Evidence Eligibility and amendment constraints.

**Strengths:**

- matches the Challenge Domain's definition as a participation context;
- keeps Goal distinct from Policy and Knowledge;
- creates one coherent source for the shared undertaking;
- prevents Administration from becoming Challenge truth;
- preserves Calculation Authority for outcomes.

**Weaknesses:**

- requires clarification of Participation Authority beyond relationship establishment;
- exercising actors remain dependent on CHL-01 and later role governance;
- Goal changes after reliance require later constitutional amendment rules.

**Constitutional consequence:** Refinement of Platform Authority Model and Challenge Domain Standard; no new Authority.

**Operational consequence:** Challenge establishment must attribute the exercising actor and approved Participation Authority without prescribing a technical process.

**Scalability:** High across Fitness, Wellness and future governed domains.

**Founder oversight:** Low after approval.

### 4.3 Authority Model B — Participation Undertaking With Separate Goal Compliance

**Description:** Participation Authority establishes Challenge identity, purpose and the declared Goal. Policy Authority separately establishes whether the Goal and evaluation basis are permitted. Knowledge Authority supplies meaning.

**Strengths:**

- explicitly separates desired outcome from permitted evaluation;
- supports safety, fairness or domain-specific constraints;
- prevents Policy from becoming the Goal itself.

**Weaknesses:**

- adds a compliance fact and review burden;
- presentation may conflate Goal with Policy approval;
- does not replace the need for Participation Authority to establish the Goal.

**Constitutional consequence:** Refinement defining Goal-compliance determination.

**Operational consequence:** Separate attribution for Goal establishment and Policy compliance.

**Scalability:** High for constrained Challenge types; medium as a universal default.

**Founder oversight:** Medium.

### 4.4 Policy-Authored Goal Alternative — Not Preferred and Constitutionally Weak

Making Policy Authority establish the individual Goal would collapse the desired outcome into the rule governing it. Policy may constrain, approve or reject a Goal's permitted evaluation, but it should not originate the Challenge's purpose. This alternative would require material amendment of the current Challenge definition.

### 4.5 Administrative-Establishment Alternative — Not Viable Without Constitutional Change

Administrative Authority may perform and record an approved Challenge-establishment action. It cannot become the source Authority for Challenge identity, purpose or Goal without contradicting the approved Administrative boundary. Governance Authority is also expressly excluded from establishing an individual Challenge.

### 4.6 Stewardship Models

#### Model 1 — Group Steward Automatically Stewards Every Challenge

**Advantages:** Simple accountability and strong Group alignment.

**Disadvantages:** Poor delegation, overload at scale, and silent transfer of Group stewardship into another Governed subject.

**Scalability:** Low to medium.

#### Model 2 — Distinct Challenge Steward, Initially the Accepted Creator

**Advantages:** Direct accountability, clear separation from Group stewardship and natural handling of multiple Challenges.

**Disadvantages:** Requires later designation and transfer governance; creator cannot become steward merely through origin.

**Scalability:** High.

#### Model 3 — Distinct Challenge Steward Designated by Group Governance

**Advantages:** Strong Group accountability and deliberate delegation.

**Disadvantages:** Depends on unresolved Group roles and may slow ordinary Challenge creation.

**Scalability:** High for managed Groups, medium as a universal default.

#### Model 4 — Multiple Equal Challenge Stewards

This conflicts with the one-steward constitutional rule. Multiple Administrators or delegated contributors may exist later without becoming equal Accountable stewards.

### 4.7 Meaning-Defining Amendment Boundary

All viable models must preserve the existing principle that a Target, Metric, Unit, purpose or Goal cannot be silently changed after it has influenced participation. The decision pack does not define a transition, edit window or correction workflow. It establishes only that later Challenge governance must distinguish presentation amendments from meaning-defining amendments and preserve prior reliance.

### 4.8 Recommended Challenge Model

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Unified Participation Undertaking with one distinct Challenge Steward accountable within the Group context. The accepted creator may initially serve only through later approved participation governance. Group Steward may hold or delegate the role but does not acquire it automatically. Policy Authority retains evaluation rules and meaning-defining amendment constraints. Model B's separate compliance fact may be used where specific Policy requires it rather than as the universal default.

**Why alternatives were not recommended:** Automatic Group stewardship scales poorly; institution-only designation is too restrictive as a default; Policy-authored Goal collapses purpose into rules; Administrative establishment violates the source-truth boundary.

**Trade-off:** The model creates clear Challenge accountability but requires later role and lifecycle standards to govern designation, delegation and transfer.

### 4.9 EOG-03 Consequences

**Direct rows:** CHL-01, CHL-02 and CHL-03.

**Consequential rows:** CHL-04 through CHL-08.

**Affected documents:** Platform Authority Model; Entity Ownership Foundation; Challenge Domain Standard; Group Domain reciprocal relationship; Entity Ownership Register; future Challenge Lifecycle, Policy and Roles and Permissions Standards.

**Future phases unlocked:** Challenge stewardship allocation, Challenge Lifecycle drafting, Challenge Policy and amendment governance.

**Remaining blocked:** CHL-02 through CHL-04 details; KNW-04 Goal relationship; Ranking, Privacy and Security Standards.

## 5. EOG-04 — Verification Authority

### 5.1 Constitutional Distinctions

Verification is a governed determination about corroboration, trust basis or evidential confidence. It is not:

- Submission Intent;
- Acceptance Decision;
- Accepted Activity Event;
- Evidence Eligibility;
- Progress, Completion or Ranking;
- a presentation badge;
- administrative opinion without declared authority.

### 5.2 Option A — Acceptance Authority, Pre-Acceptance Only

**Description:** Acceptance Authority establishes Verification only while evaluating Submission Intent. Once an event is accepted, later information must use correction governance rather than Verification.

**Advantages:** Simple boundary; Verification and acceptance are evaluated together; limited historical ambiguity.

**Disadvantages:** Cannot accommodate later device evidence, review or fraud discovery without correction; risks making Verification appear to be acceptance itself.

**Constitutional consequence:** Refinement of Acceptance Authority within the pre-acceptance context.

**Operational consequence:** Verification must be available before acceptance when required.

**Scalability:** Medium.

**Founder oversight:** Low after Policy is defined.

### 5.3 Option B — Acceptance Authority, Pre- and Post-Acceptance

**Description:** Acceptance Authority establishes a distinct Verification determination under Policy before or after an Accepted Activity Event exists. Timing and source are attributable. A post-acceptance determination does not silently rewrite the event.

**Advantages:** Supports manual, device, external and later review; keeps one authority for evidential trust determination; preserves historical facts; allows Policy to decide effect separately.

**Disadvantages:** Requires strong distinction among event truth, Verification and Evidence Eligibility; later correction rules must govern adverse determinations; more complex presentation.

**Constitutional consequence:** Refinement of Acceptance Authority and Activity Event Domain; no new Authority.

**Operational consequence:** Systems must preserve timing and attribution later, but this pack defines no mechanism.

**Scalability:** High.

**Founder oversight:** Medium because Verification Policy needs governance by context.

### 5.4 Administrative Verification Alternative — Not Viable Without Constitutional Change

**Description:** A scoped Administrator establishes Verification under Policy, while Acceptance and Calculation Authorities consume it only where separately approved.

**Advantages:** Clear human-review accountability; straightforward for exceptional disputes and moderation-like review.

**Disadvantages:** Ties Verification to administration; does not scale to automatic or device corroboration; risks granting Administrative Authority evidential truth; can bypass Acceptance Authority.

**Constitutional consequence:** Material refinement and possibly constitutional change to the Administrative boundary.

**Operational consequence:** Requires a privileged review function and strict scope.

**Scalability:** Low to medium.

**Founder oversight:** High due to abuse and consistency risk.

**Assessment:** Administrative Authority may administer or record a Verification decision, but establishing Verification as source truth would expand the approved Administrative boundary. This is not a viable Phase E1A option without a separate constitutional-change decision.

### 5.5 Option D — No Authoritative Verification Yet

**Description:** Policy may state Verification is not required. Source attestations remain provenance rather than authoritative Verification. The row remains pending until a later founder decision.

**Advantages:** Truthful launch posture; avoids dead or misleading status; minimal governance burden.

**Disadvantages:** Does not resolve EOG-04; cannot support policies requiring corroboration; delays competitive and device-evidence governance.

**Constitutional consequence:** None; the register remains incomplete.

**Operational consequence:** No feature may imply verified status.

**Scalability:** Low if Verification becomes important.

**Founder oversight:** Low now, higher later.

### 5.6 Effects of Verification

Under every viable active model:

- Policy defines when Verification is required and what source basis is acceptable;
- Verification does not automatically establish or reverse acceptance;
- Policy Authority determines whether Verification affects Evidence Eligibility;
- Calculation Authority recalculates only when eligible evidence changes;
- a post-acceptance adverse determination cannot erase history silently;
- preserved records must distinguish source attestation, Verification determination, event acceptance and downstream effects.

These are authority boundaries, not a workflow.

### 5.7 Recommended Verification Model

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option B. Acceptance Authority establishes the distinct Verification determination under Policy before or after acceptance. Policy may declare Verification not required. Any effect on acceptance, Evidence Eligibility or Derived Truth remains separately governed by the responsible Authority.

**Why alternatives were not recommended:** Pre-only Verification is too restrictive; Administration-centered Verification overextends administrative truth; deferral leaves the register blocked.

**Trade-off:** The model is more complex, but it supports future evidence sources without collapsing constitutional facts.

### 5.8 EOG-04 Consequences

**Direct row:** AEV-08.

**Consequential rows:** AEV-05, AEV-06, AEV-07, DRV-01 and DRV-03.

**Affected documents:** Platform Authority Model; Activity Event Domain Standard; Entity Ownership Register; future Verification, Evidence and Competition Fairness Standards.

**Future phases unlocked:** Verification Standard, ACT-04 resolution, evidence-source governance and competitive verification policy.

**Remaining blocked:** Verification lifecycle, roles, permissions, privacy, security and technical evidence contracts.

## 6. EOG-05 — Correction Authority

### 6.1 Correction Facts

Correction may concern different constitutional facts. A single edit concept cannot replace their distinct authorities:

- Submission Intent expression;
- Acceptance Decision;
- Accepted Activity Event truth;
- Evidence Eligibility;
- Derived Truth;
- presentation;
- administrative or audit record.

### 6.2 Option A — Fact-Authority Correction

**Description:** The Authority that establishes each fact establishes its correction. Participant Authority establishes corrected or withdrawn intent expression; Acceptance Authority establishes accepted-event correction, reversal or supersession; Policy Authority establishes corrected Evidence Eligibility; Calculation Authority establishes corrected Derived Truth. Administrative Authority coordinates and records without redefining source facts.

**Advantages:** Preserves one authority per fact; maintains historical attribution; scales across domains; prevents administrative overwrite; cleanly drives downstream recalculation.

**Disadvantages:** Requires coordinated decisions; disputes may involve several authorities; later standards must define relationships among corrections without creating circularity.

**Constitutional consequence:** Clarification of existing Authority responsibilities, not a new Authority.

**Operational consequence:** Later implementation must orchestrate several authoritative effects deterministically.

**Scalability:** High.

**Founder oversight:** Medium initially, low after standards exist.

### 6.3 Option B — Acceptance-Centered Activity Correction

**Description:** Acceptance Authority establishes corrections to Acceptance Decisions and Accepted Activity Events. Participant Authority remains responsible for intent expressions; Policy and Calculation Authorities remain responsible for their separate downstream facts.

**Advantages:** One focal authority for event truth; simpler than an undifferentiated multi-authority model; preserves Policy and Calculation boundaries.

**Disadvantages:** It is only a narrower expression of Option A, not a complete correction model; cannot correct Profile, Policy or presentation truth; may be mistaken for authority over downstream outcomes.

**Constitutional consequence:** Clarification of Acceptance Authority.

**Operational consequence:** Event correction has a clear source but still requires coordinated downstream effects.

**Scalability:** Medium to high within Activity Event; low as a platform-wide correction rule.

**Founder oversight:** Medium.

### 6.4 Administrative Correction Alternative — Not Viable as General Authority

Administrative Authority may authorize or record a high-impact correction action. It cannot establish corrected Submission Intent, Accepted Activity Event, Evidence Eligibility or Derived Truth without acquiring Participant, Acceptance, Policy or Calculation Authority. A general Administrative correction model contradicts the baseline and is excluded.

### 6.5 Separate Correction Authority — Outside Current Constitution

A twelfth Correction Authority could centralize decisions but would contradict the instruction to preserve the approved Authority Model and would require a separate constitutional-change process. It is not a viable Phase E1A option.

### 6.6 Request, Relationship and Downstream Boundaries

Under either viable model:

- a Participant may originate a correction request without establishing the correction;
- an Administrator or Operator may originate an attributable concern without establishing source truth;
- reversal and supersession preserve their relationship to prior truth;
- deletion cannot conceal authoritative history;
- Calculation Authority establishes revised calculations when Policy makes corrected evidence eligible or ineligible;
- presentation, Feed, Leaderboard, Notification, recap and Recognition must remain attributable to the corrected source truth;
- disputes and appeal authority remain for later standards;
- audit preserves who requested, decided, administered and presented the effect.

No workflow, state vocabulary or timing rule is created here.

### 6.7 Recommended Correction Model

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option A, with Option B recognized as its Activity Event specialization. No separate correction Authority is created.

**Why alternatives were not recommended:** Administration-centered correction violates source authority. A twelfth Authority contradicts the current constitution. Acceptance-centered correction alone is incomplete outside accepted-event truth.

**Trade-off:** Coordination is more complex, but Tiizi avoids a competing universal editor and preserves historical integrity.

### 6.8 EOG-05 Consequences

**Direct row:** AEV-09.

**Consequential rows:** AEV-04 through AEV-07; AEV-10; DRV-01 through DRV-04; DRV-06 through DRV-09.

**Affected documents:** Platform Authority Model clarification; Entity Ownership Foundation; Activity Event Domain Standard; Challenge Domain derived-use boundary; Entity Ownership Register; future Correction, Audit, Dispute and lifecycle standards.

**Future phases unlocked:** ACT-03/ACT-04 correction governance, historical-integrity standard, downstream recalculation policy and audit design.

**Remaining blocked:** Correction lifecycle, dispute authority, time rules, permissions, privacy, security and implementation.

## 7. EOG-06 — Recognition Authority

### 7.1 Recognition Sources

Formal Recognition may be grounded in:

- accepted participation;
- Progress;
- Completion;
- Ranking;
- consistency or Streak;
- attributable Group contribution.

The source fact retains its existing Authority. Recognition qualification is distinct from source truth, social acknowledgement, presentation and Reward.

### 7.2 Option A — Calculation-Centered Formal Recognition

**Description:** Motivation Policy defines qualifying conditions. Calculation Authority establishes formal Recognition qualification from authoritative sources. Presentation Authority communicates the result.

**Advantages:** Objective, consistent and attributable; works across multiple source types; preserves Policy/calculation separation; correctable when source truth changes.

**Disadvantages:** Does not by itself govern participant applause or discretionary acknowledgement; can over-formalize motivation if applied too broadly.

**Constitutional consequence:** Refinement defining Recognition qualification as Derived Truth.

**Operational consequence:** Later calculations need declared source and Policy; no formula is approved here.

**Scalability:** High.

**Founder oversight:** Medium for Policy, low for individual determinations.

### 7.3 Option B — Policy-Centered Recognition

**Description:** Policy Authority establishes both qualification rules and the individual Recognition determination.

**Advantages:** Central governance and strong policy consistency.

**Disadvantages:** Collapses rule and application; turns Policy Authority into Calculation Authority; weakens correction attribution.

**Constitutional consequence:** Conflicts with the existing Policy/Calculation boundary unless materially amended.

**Assessment:** Not constitutionally preferred and not viable without changing the current Authority boundary.

### 7.4 Option C — Participant-Authored Recognition Only

**Description:** Recognition is limited to attributable encouragement or acknowledgement established by Participant Authority. No platform-qualified Recognition exists.

**Advantages:** Strongly aligned with mutual encouragement; simple; avoids formula governance.

**Disadvantages:** Cannot truthfully establish Completion, consistency or contribution distinctions; social popularity may masquerade as achievement; no authoritative Recognition row is resolved.

**Constitutional consequence:** Participant expression remains communication; DRV-09 would need reclassification or remain pending.

**Operational consequence:** No formal recognition capability.

**Scalability:** Medium for social interaction, low for governed motivation.

**Founder oversight:** Low.

### 7.5 Option D — Split Formal Recognition and Social Acknowledgement

**Description:** Calculation Authority establishes formal Recognition qualification under Motivation Policy. Participant Authority establishes attributable encouragement and acknowledgement as communication. Presentation Authority communicates each truthfully. Neither becomes Reward.

**Advantages:** Preserves mutual encouragement and objective formal acknowledgement; prevents applause from becoming achievement truth; supports correction; scales across domains; cleanly separates Recognition from Reward.

**Disadvantages:** Requires precise terminology; users may confuse the two if presentation is poor; two governance contexts must remain coordinated.

**Constitutional consequence:** Refinement of Recognition and communication definitions; new Motivation and Recognition governance, not a new Authority.

**Operational consequence:** Later products must label formal and social expressions distinctly.

**Scalability:** High.

**Founder oversight:** Medium for Policy, low for individual formal determinations.

### 7.6 Stewardship, Correction and Withdrawal

Every viable formal model requires one durable Recognition Accountable steward under the EOG-01 framework. The steward governs meaning and Policy accountability but does not calculate individual qualification.

Where source truth changes, Calculation Authority establishes the corrected qualification under Policy. Presentation Authority withdraws or updates communicated Recognition only as a consequence of authoritative change. Discretionary withdrawal, dispute and retention remain for later Recognition governance.

This pack creates no Reward, value, balance, entitlement or redemption system.

### 7.7 Recommended Recognition Model

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option D.

**Why alternatives were not recommended:** Formal-only Recognition does not govern mutual encouragement; Policy-centered determination collapses Authority boundaries; participant-only acknowledgement cannot resolve formal qualification.

**Trade-off:** Tiizi must govern two clearly named concepts, but gains truthful social encouragement and authoritative Recognition without a reward economy.

### 7.8 EOG-06 Consequences

**Direct row:** DRV-09.

**Consequential rows:** DRV-01 through DRV-04; DRV-07; DRV-08; AEV-09.

**Affected documents:** Platform Domain and Terminology Standard; Constitutional Glossary; Platform Authority Model clarification; Challenge Domain relationships; Entity Ownership Register; future Motivation and Recognition Framework.

**Future phases unlocked:** MOT-01 resolution, Recognition Standard, motivation terminology, notification and Feed relationship governance.

**Remaining blocked:** Reward policy, Recognition lifecycle, correction detail, privacy, permissions, analytics and implementation.
