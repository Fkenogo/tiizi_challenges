# EOG-03 — Constitutional Governance of Platform Knowledge

**Version:** Founder Review Draft 1

**Status:** Draft for founder review

**Date:** 2026-07-20

## 1. Purpose

Tiizi requires authoritative Platform Knowledge so that people may pursue meaningful goals together through concepts whose meaning remains coherent, attributable and trustworthy.

This standard governs the constitutional meaning and boundary of Platform Knowledge. It establishes how authoritative meaning supports Group experiences without being redefined by them, how a Group Challenge is formed through Constitutional Composition, and how Templates, Creation Mechanisms, runtime representations and future contributions remain subordinate to governed truth.

This standard governs what these concepts mean. It does not prescribe how they are implemented.

## 2. Constitutional Basis and Precedence

This standard is subordinate to and shall be interpreted consistently with:

- the Tiizi Constitutional Ontology and Foundational Product Concepts;
- the Founder Philosophy and Design Doctrine;
- the Platform Constitution;
- the Platform Principles;
- the Entity Ownership Foundation;
- the Platform Data and Information Standard;
- the Platform Authority Model;
- EOG-01 — Platform Accountability Framework;
- EOG-02 — Constitutional Accountability for Groups;
- the Profile, Group, Challenge, Activity Event and Knowledge Asset Domain Standards.

EOG-01 supplies the mandatory meanings of Authority, Authority to Establish Truth, Accountable Steward, Custodian, Administrator, Operator, Contributor, Participant, Governed Subject, Information Subject, Attributable Originator, Delegate, Presenter and Downstream User. This standard applies those meanings and does not redefine them.

EOG-02 establishes the persistent constitutional identity of a Group, the distinction between Group existence and participation, and the approved Group accountability boundaries. This standard does not reopen them.

Where a lower-order document, legacy catalogue document, Template, local representation or current mechanism conflicts with this standard, the approved constitutional hierarchy prevails.

## 3. Constitutional Scope

### 3.1 Included

This standard governs:

- Platform Knowledge;
- Authoritative Meaning;
- primary Platform Knowledge Asset types;
- the boundary of Knowledge Authority;
- the boundary between Platform Knowledge and Group Experience;
- Constitutional Composition;
- the integrity of composition inputs;
- Templates as governed composition guides;
- Creation Mechanisms as subordinate means of forming Group Challenges;
- Runtime Catalogue and Runtime Projection boundaries;
- Historical Representation at the level of constitutional integrity;
- current Platform-generated Knowledge and future governed contribution direction;
- the boundary between Platform Knowledge and Discovery and Personalisation;
- the constitutional restraints that later governance must preserve.

### 3.2 Excluded

This standard does not define:

- individual Exercise or Wellness Activity content;
- catalogue membership, identifiers, counts or rationalisation outcomes;
- Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration, Operation or role allocations;
- lifecycle states or transitions;
- publication, revision, deprecation, retirement, deletion or retention;
- version formats, snapshot contents or historical storage;
- contributor eligibility, appointment, qualification or workflow;
- permissions, privacy rules or security controls;
- taxonomy dictionaries, tags, categories or recommendation vocabularies;
- Group, Challenge, Template or participation lifecycle;
- Challenge types, eligibility or completion rules;
- validation, scoring, Progress, Completion, Ranking, Streak or Recognition calculations;
- interfaces, schemas, services, storage or other implementation structures;
- migration, seeding, synchronisation, deployment or operational procedure.

### 3.3 Scope discipline

EOG-03 establishes constitutional concepts and boundaries that remain stable when features, processes or technologies change. A later standard may refine these boundaries but may not contradict them.

## 4. Canonical Vocabulary

### Platform Knowledge

The body of authoritative, reusable meaning governed by Tiizi for declared platform purposes.

### Governed Knowledge

Knowledge whose identity, meaning, provenance and authority are subject to approved governance. Governed Knowledge is a constitutional condition, not a separate Authority or competing asset class.

### Knowledge Asset

A governed unit of Knowledge with distinct identity, authoritative meaning, declared purpose and defined relationships.

### Platform Knowledge Asset

A Knowledge Asset whose authoritative meaning is established for shared platform use. Use by a Group, Challenge, Template, mechanism or runtime representation does not transfer Authority over that meaning.

### Authoritative Meaning

The canonical meaning established through Knowledge Authority for a declared Knowledge Asset and purpose.

### Exercise Asset

A primary Fitness-domain Platform Knowledge Asset that establishes the authoritative meaning of an Exercise.

### Wellness Activity Asset

A primary Wellness-domain Platform Knowledge Asset that establishes the authoritative meaning of a Wellness Activity.

### Group Experience

A governed community experience situated within a Group context, including Group Challenges, their configuration, Community context, participation and local objectives where later governance permits.

### Constitutional Composition

The governed formation of a Group Challenge from Platform Knowledge, applicable Policy, Group configuration and Community context, while preserving the identity, authority and provenance of every input.

### Applicable Policy

Approved Policy whose declared scope governs permitted use or behavior within a particular constitutional context.

### Group Configuration

The purpose-limited configuration contributed by a Group context to a governed Group Experience. It does not establish Platform Knowledge, Policy or Derived Truth.

### Community Context

The local purpose, framing and shared context contributed by a Group to its governed experience. It does not redefine Platform Knowledge or prove participation.

### Template

A governed composition guide that assists in arranging eligible inputs for the formation of a Group Challenge.

### Creation Mechanism

A governed means through which approved inputs may be brought together to form a Group Challenge. The Challenge Wizard is one present example; no particular mechanism is constitutionally required.

### Runtime Catalogue

The single governed authority function through which approved Knowledge representations become available for declared runtime purposes. It is not a twelfth Platform Authority.

### Runtime Projection

A subordinate governed representation of a Knowledge Asset containing the approved meaning required for a declared runtime purpose.

### Historical Representation

A representation that preserves the meaning and composition context applicable to a governed historical use without becoming an independent Knowledge Asset or competing source of truth.

### Contributor

The EOG-01 relationship through which an attributable party supplies effort, expertise, source material or proposed content to a governed purpose. Contribution does not establish canonical meaning, approval, Accountable Stewardship or Custody.

### Governed Review

The constitutional requirement that proposed contribution be subject to approved review before it may support Authoritative Meaning. This term creates no review role, stage, workflow or allocation.

## 5. Platform Knowledge

Platform Knowledge exists to preserve reusable meaning upon which truthful participation can depend.

It provides a common constitutional foundation across Groups. A person in one Group and a person in another Group should not encounter competing canonical meanings merely because their Community contexts differ.

Platform Knowledge is:

- authoritative within its declared scope;
- attributable to governed sources and decisions;
- reusable across approved Group experiences;
- distinct from participant expression and evidence of occurrence;
- distinct from Policy governing use;
- distinct from calculations and Derived Truth;
- distinct from discovery, personalisation and presentation;
- capable of supporting historically intelligible participation.

Platform Knowledge is not made authoritative by being stored, copied, displayed, selected, frequently used or included in a Template. Its authority arises only through the existing constitutional Knowledge Authority boundary.

No Group, Challenge, Participant, Template, Creation Mechanism, Runtime Projection, local representation or presentation becomes an equal source of Platform Knowledge through use.

## 6. Authoritative Meaning

### 6.1 Foundational doctrine

Platform Knowledge does not merely contain information. It establishes **Authoritative Meaning**.

Authoritative Meaning determines what a governed Knowledge concept means for its declared purpose. Everything downstream that references a Knowledge Asset depends upon that meaning remaining coherent and attributable.

Authoritative Meaning may address, where separately governed and appropriate:

- identity and purpose;
- Activity meaning;
- Metric and Unit meaning;
- instructional and safety meaning;
- approved relationships;
- semantic constraints and compatibility meaning;
- provenance and historical intelligibility.

Authoritative Meaning is not:

- authorship alone;
- a display label;
- a preference or discovery classification;
- a claim that an Activity occurred;
- Policy governing every permitted use;
- a calculation or result;
- a universal statement of individual suitability;
- runtime availability;
- a local interpretation created for convenience.

### 6.2 Dependence on meaning

A Template depends on Authoritative Meaning when it refers to Platform Knowledge. A Creation Mechanism depends on it when it presents or validates governed inputs. A Group Challenge depends on it when it incorporates a Knowledge Asset through Constitutional Composition. An Activity Event depends on it when an occurrence is interpreted. Policy and Calculation depend on it when approved rules use Knowledge meaning.

Dependence does not transfer Authority. A downstream use may add context or apply Policy, but it may not silently change the Authoritative Meaning upon which it relies.

### 6.3 One meaning, no competing equal authority

One canonical Knowledge concept shall not have competing equal authorities. A local copy, historical representation, Template configuration, Group description or Runtime Projection may preserve or communicate meaning, but none establishes a second canonical meaning.

## 7. Primary Platform Knowledge Assets

The primary Platform Knowledge Assets for the current constitutional scope are:

- Exercise Assets;
- Wellness Activity Assets.

Exercise Assets support the Fitness launch domain. Wellness Activity Assets support the Wellness launch domain. They conform to the same constitutional requirements for authoritative meaning, provenance, authority, runtime subordination and historical intelligibility, while retaining domain-appropriate meaning.

Supporting Knowledge Assets may establish governed Metric, Unit, instructional, safety or relationship meaning where existing constitutional governance recognises them. Their detailed classification and content contracts remain outside this standard.

Interests, Goals, Preferences, Tags and Categories are not Platform Knowledge Assets. Similar wording between one of those concepts and an Exercise Asset or Wellness Activity Asset does not establish identity.

Future domains may introduce additional Platform Knowledge Asset types only through approved governance. Extensibility does not make a future domain active.

## 8. Knowledge Authority Boundaries

EOG-03 creates no new Platform Authority and allocates no accountability relationship.

The eleven approved Platform Authorities apply at the Knowledge boundary as follows:

| Platform Authority | Constitutional relationship to Platform Knowledge | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Governance Authority | Approves constitutional boundaries, standards and amendments. | Does not establish the meaning of an individual Knowledge Asset merely by governing the domain. |
| Identity Authority | Supplies authoritative identity context for attributable actions where required. | Does not approve Knowledge meaning. |
| Participation Authority | Supplies governed Group, Challenge and Participant relationships where applicable. | Does not establish Knowledge or Group participation merely through Knowledge use. |
| Participant Authority | Establishes permitted participant expressions, including Submission Intent where applicable. | Does not establish Platform Knowledge. |
| Knowledge Authority | Establishes Authoritative Meaning for governed Knowledge Assets. | Does not establish participation, occurrence, Evidence Eligibility or Derived Truth. |
| Policy Authority | Establishes approved rules governing permitted use and behavior. | Does not redefine Authoritative Meaning. |
| Acceptance Authority | Establishes Accepted Activity Events under approved Knowledge and Policy. | Does not create or modify Platform Knowledge. |
| Calculation Authority | Establishes approved Progress, Completion, Ranking and other Derived Truth. | Does not invent Knowledge, Policy or evidence. |
| Administrative Authority | Performs explicitly assigned high-impact actions within approved scope. | Administration does not confer Knowledge Authority or unrestricted control. |
| Operational Authority | Supports continuity, reconciliation and reliable operation within approved scope. | Operation does not establish Platform Knowledge or product truth. |
| Presentation Authority | Communicates approved meaning and representations. | Presentation cannot establish or redefine Authoritative Meaning. |

Runtime Catalogue authority is the approved single authority function for governed runtime availability. It is not an additional Platform Authority and does not replace Knowledge Authority.

Authorship, contribution, Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration, Operation, presentation and downstream use remain distinct from Authority to Establish Truth.

## 9. Platform Knowledge and Group Experience

The founder doctrine is:

> **Groups own their governed experiences.**

This statement establishes a constitutional domain boundary. It means that Group Challenges, Group configuration, Community context, participation and local objectives belong to the governed Group experience rather than to Platform Knowledge.

It does not, by itself, allocate Authority, Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration, Operation or permission. Those allocations remain subject to later governance under EOG-01.

A Group may:

- select approved Platform Knowledge for a governed experience;
- compose that Knowledge with applicable Policy;
- contribute Group configuration;
- contribute Community context and local objectives;
- present the resulting Group Challenge within its governed context.

A Group may not:

- redefine the canonical meaning of an Exercise Asset or Wellness Activity Asset;
- fork Platform Knowledge into a local competing authority;
- modify Platform Knowledge through configuration;
- convert Community context into canonical Knowledge;
- treat repeated use or local preference as Knowledge approval;
- remove applicable Policy by local interpretation.

Group Experience and Platform Knowledge are mutually related but constitutionally distinct. Platform Knowledge supplies reusable Authoritative Meaning. The Group Experience supplies governed local context. Neither silently acquires the constitutional relationships of the other.

## 10. Constitutional Composition

### 10.1 Central doctrine

Constitutional Composition is the governed formation of a Group Challenge from four distinct inputs:

1. Platform Knowledge;
2. applicable Policy;
3. Group configuration;
4. Community context.

The resulting Group Challenge is constitutionally distinct from every input. It is a governed undertaking situated within a Group context. It is not a Knowledge Asset, a Policy, a Group, a Template or a mere representation of any one input.

### 10.2 What composition is not

Constitutional Composition is not copying. A Group Challenge does not become a duplicate Knowledge Asset merely because it preserves Knowledge meaning.

Constitutional Composition is not inheritance. A Group Challenge does not acquire the identity, lifecycle or Authority of its Knowledge or Policy inputs.

Constitutional Composition is not modification. Group configuration and Community context do not alter the Authoritative Meaning of Platform Knowledge.

Constitutional Composition is not forking. A Group does not create a competing local Knowledge authority by using Platform Knowledge differently within an approved context.

### 10.3 Separation of inputs

Each input contributes a different constitutional kind of meaning:

- Platform Knowledge contributes authoritative reusable meaning;
- applicable Policy contributes governed rules for permitted use and behavior;
- Group configuration contributes the declared arrangement of the Group experience;
- Community context contributes local purpose, framing and shared meaning.

No input transfers its Authority to another. No input becomes authoritative outside its declared scope merely because the composition uses it.

### 10.4 Layered meaning

Tiizi recognises that a governed Challenge may be understood through distinct layers of meaning:

1. **Platform Meaning** — the Authoritative Meaning established for Platform Knowledge;
2. **Policy Meaning** — the approved rules governing how that meaning may be used in the particular context;
3. **Community Meaning** — the Group’s local purpose, configuration and framing;
4. **Historical Meaning** — the meaning and context applicable to the governed participation as it occurred.

These layers support a complete understanding without becoming competing truths.

- Policy Meaning may constrain or permit use but may not redefine Platform Meaning.
- Community Meaning may contextualise the experience but may not override Platform Meaning or applicable Policy.
- Historical Meaning preserves the layers applicable to past participation but may not rewrite them using later meaning.

Layered meaning is a constitutional interpretation principle. It is not a storage model, record structure, lifecycle or calculation hierarchy.

## 11. Composition Integrity

Every Constitutional Composition shall preserve the following principles:

1. **Identity integrity:** each input remains distinguishable from the resulting Challenge.
2. **Authority integrity:** composition transfers no Authority by implication.
3. **Meaning integrity:** Group configuration and Community context do not silently alter Authoritative Meaning.
4. **Policy integrity:** applicable Policy remains distinguishable from Knowledge and local context.
5. **Provenance integrity:** the origin and constitutional role of each input remain attributable.
6. **Historical integrity:** later changes do not silently reinterpret the meaning applicable to prior participation.
7. **Presentation integrity:** labels and summaries do not redefine the composition or its inputs.
8. **No competing truth:** a copied, cached, local or displayed representation does not become an equal authority.

A Challenge classification, Template label, local objective or mechanism default cannot override these principles.

## 12. Templates as Governed Composition Guides

Templates are governed composition guides.

A Template may help a Group understand or arrange a possible Challenge composition. It may refer to Platform Knowledge, applicable Policy and configurable Group elements. It may support consistency and reduce avoidable complexity.

A Template is not:

- a Platform Knowledge Asset;
- a source of Authoritative Meaning;
- Policy merely because it recommends a configuration;
- a Group Challenge;
- evidence of participation;
- proof that a proposed composition is permitted;
- Authority over a Group Experience;
- a replacement for governed review or applicable Policy.

Template use does not transfer Knowledge Authority to the Group or Template. A Template cannot redefine the Knowledge it consumes.

Template authorship, approval, availability, amendment, lifecycle, historical treatment, Administration and presentation remain deferred.

## 13. Challenge Creation Mechanisms

A Creation Mechanism is a governed means through which Platform Knowledge, Templates and Group configuration may be brought together to form a Group Challenge. The Challenge Wizard is one present example.

A Creation Mechanism may communicate choices, arrange inputs or apply approved constraints. Its visible behavior does not make it an Authority.

A Creation Mechanism:

- consumes governed inputs;
- remains subordinate to Platform Knowledge and applicable Policy;
- does not establish Authoritative Meaning;
- does not convert a Template into Knowledge;
- does not create permission merely by offering a choice;
- does not establish Group or Challenge accountability;
- does not establish Progress, Completion, Ranking or Recognition;
- does not become part of the resulting Challenge’s constitutional identity.

The mechanism through which a Challenge is formed may evolve without changing the constitutional meaning of composition.

## 14. Runtime Knowledge

### 14.1 Runtime authority chain

Runtime use shall preserve the following constitutional relationship:

> **Platform Knowledge Asset**
>
> ↓ Authoritative Meaning made available through
>
> **Runtime Catalogue**
>
> ↓ represented for a declared purpose through
>
> **Runtime Projection**
>
> ↓ preserved for governed historical understanding through
>
> **Historical Representation**

### 14.2 Runtime Catalogue

Tiizi shall have one governed Runtime Catalogue authority for runtime availability. This authority establishes whether an approved Knowledge representation is available for a declared runtime purpose. It does not establish canonical Knowledge meaning.

No local source, fallback, Template, cache, Group selection or presentation may silently become an equal runtime authority.

### 14.3 Runtime Projection

A Runtime Projection exposes the approved subset of a Knowledge Asset required for a declared purpose. It remains attributable and subordinate to:

- the source Platform Knowledge Asset;
- the Authoritative Meaning established by Knowledge Authority;
- the Runtime Catalogue authority through which it is made available.

A Runtime Projection may simplify communication. It may not redefine identity, measurement, safety meaning, provenance or applicable constraints. It is not a Knowledge Asset classification or a competing source of truth.

This standard defines no projection structure, field set, interface or synchronization behavior.

## 15. Historical Knowledge Integrity

Historical Meaning must remain intelligible.

A Historical Representation may preserve the Platform Meaning, applicable Policy, Group configuration and Community context relevant to prior governed participation. Its purpose is truthful historical understanding, not creation of a new Knowledge authority.

A Historical Representation:

- remains attributable to its source meaning and context;
- does not become a Platform Knowledge Asset;
- does not make superseded meaning available for new use by implication;
- does not permit later Knowledge, Policy or Community changes to rewrite prior participation silently;
- does not establish Progress, Completion, Ranking or Recognition;
- remains subordinate to the constitutional authorities that established the source truths it represents.

This integrity boundary does not approve a snapshot minimum, version scheme, checksum, amendment process, retention period, deletion rule or lifecycle state. KNW-02 and KNW-03 remain pending.

## 16. Knowledge Contribution Direction

### 16.1 Current model

The present constitutional direction recognises Platform-generated authoritative Knowledge. The Platform remains responsible for ensuring that Authoritative Meaning is established through approved governance.

### 16.2 Future direction

Tiizi may later permit trusted contribution through the following constitutional relationship:

> **Platform governance**
>
> ↓ permits contribution within approved scope
>
> **Trusted Contributors**
>
> ↓ supply attributable proposed material
>
> **Governed Review**
>
> ↓ supports establishment of
>
> **Authoritative Meaning**
>
> ↓ forms part of
>
> **Platform Knowledge**

Contribution may decentralise. Truth remains governed.

A Contributor is an Attributable Originator of the material contributed. Contribution does not, by itself, establish Authoritative Meaning, Knowledge Authority, Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration, runtime availability or publication.

“Trusted” does not name a role or create Authority. It describes a possible future governed condition whose eligibility and consequences remain deferred.

This standard does not define contributor eligibility, qualifications, appointment, review stages, approval workflow, permissions, disputes, withdrawal, lifecycle or implementation.

## 17. Discovery and Personalisation Boundary

The following are not Platform Knowledge Assets:

- Interests;
- Goals;
- Preferences;
- Tags;
- Categories.

These concepts belong to a future Discovery and Personalisation Governance Programme concerning discovery, recommendation, filtering, matching, classification and personalisation.

Discovery and Personalisation may later reference Platform Knowledge. Reference does not transfer Knowledge Authority or establish identity.

- An Interest does not become an Exercise Asset.
- A Goal does not become Platform Knowledge, Policy or a Challenge target merely through expression.
- A Preference does not establish canonical meaning.
- A Tag does not prove identity, suitability or compatibility.
- A Category does not replace the identity of a Knowledge Asset.

This boundary does not create the future programme, allocate its accountability, approve its vocabularies or resolve KNW-04.

- taxonomy and controlled dictionaries;

- Template governance;
- Challenge and participation lifecycle;

- calculation and Recognition rules;
- content, evidence and safety review standards;
- roles, permissions, privacy and security;
- operations and implementation mapping.

KNW-02, KNW-03 and KNW-04 remain pending.

### 18.2 Prohibited inferences

No person or system may infer from this standard that:

- Platform Knowledge owns every downstream fact;
- a Group acquires Knowledge Authority over Platform Knowledge through use;
- “Groups own their governed experiences” allocates an Accountable Steward or other EOG-01 relationship;
- a Template is a Knowledge Asset;
- a Creation Mechanism is an Authority;
- a Runtime Projection is an independent source of truth;
- Historical Representation approves a versioning or lifecycle model;
- Contributor status establishes trust, approval or Authority;
- Knowledge Authority establishes Policy, evidence, calculations or Recognition;
- a discovery concept is Platform Knowledge;
- current implementation behavior is constitutional governance.
