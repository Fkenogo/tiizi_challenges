# Founder Accountability Decision Agenda

## 1. Purpose

This agenda presents the six register-blocking accountability decisions identified by the [Phase E1 Entity Ownership Register](01-TIIZI-ENTITY-OWNERSHIP-REGISTER.md) in concise, approval-ready form.

Every recommendation is analysis only. Nothing in this agenda changes constitutional governance, the Entity Ownership Register, or a pending founder decision unless the founder explicitly approves it and the approval is recorded later.

Detailed alternatives and comparative analysis are in the [Founder Accountability Decision Options](02-FOUNDER-ACCOUNTABILITY-DECISION-OPTIONS.md). The effects of possible approval are mapped in the [Entity Ownership Resolution Plan](02-ENTITY-OWNERSHIP-RESOLUTION-PLAN.md).

## 3. Constitutional Guardrails

The founder is deciding allocations within the approved constitutional system, not reopening that system. Every decision must preserve:

- one Accountable steward per Governed subject;
- one Authority to establish each authoritative fact;
- stewardship distinct from Authority, custody, administration, operation and presentation;
- only the 11 approved Platform Authority types;
- Participant Authority ending at Submission Intent;
- Acceptance Authority establishing Accepted Activity Events;
- Policy Authority establishing Evidence Eligibility for a declared calculation;
- Calculation Authority establishing approved Derived Truth;
- Knowledge Authority establishing canonical Knowledge meaning;
- Runtime Catalogue authority establishing availability without redefining Knowledge;
- Presentation Authority communicating without redefining truth;
- durable attribution and historically intelligible correction;
- no technical system becoming an Accountable steward or Authority to establish truth.

## 4. Session 1 Decisions

### EOG-01 — Stewardship and Custody Allocation Framework

**Decision required:** Choose the constitutional allocation method used to complete Accountable steward and Custodian columns across all 63 register rows.

**Viable models:**

- A — Allocate each relationship independently for every row.
- B — Allocate primarily by foundational domain, including stewardship and custody.
- C — Allocate primarily by governance capability, including stewardship and custody.
- D — Hybrid: domain-led accountable stewardship, capability-led custody, explicit row-level exceptions.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option D.

**Recommendation summary:** Each Governed subject receives exactly one durable Accountable steward anchored in its constitutional domain or expressly approved cross-platform capability. Custody is allocated by the capability responsible for preservation and continuity. Exceptions are recorded at row level. Administrator, Operator and Presenter remain separate assignments.

**Why this emerges from the analysis:** It preserves domain accountability while recognizing that preservation, audit, presentation and operational custody cross domain boundaries. It avoids 63 unrelated mini-decisions without allowing broad domain labels to conceal exceptions.

**Decision difficulty:** Medium — the model is conceptually clear, but it establishes the pattern for every later allocation.

**Decision risk:** High — a weak framework would reproduce ambiguity across all 63 subjects.

**Decision reversibility:** Moderate — individual allocations can later change through governance, but changing the allocation method would require register-wide reconciliation.

**Long-term platform impact:** High — the decision determines how Tiizi scales accountability across future domains.

**Directly affected rows:** All 63 register rows.

**Trade-off accepted if approved:** The hybrid requires an exception discipline and a maintained custody map; it is more structured than a single domain label.

- [ ] Select Option A
- [ ] Select Option B
- [ ] Select Option C

### EOG-02 — Group Identity, Purpose and Stewardship

**Decision required:** Determine which existing Authority establishes individual Group identity and Group purpose, and define the constitutional stewardship pattern without creating lifecycle transitions or permissions.

**Authority models assessed:**

- A — Participation-centered: Participation Authority establishes accepted Group identity and purpose as collective participation context under Policy.
- B — Participation with separate Policy compliance: Participation Authority establishes Group identity and the declared purpose; Policy Authority separately establishes whether that purpose is permitted.
- C — Administrative establishment: Administrative Authority establishes the accepted Group identity and purpose from attributable creator intent under Policy. This would conflict with the current Administrative boundary and is not viable without separate constitutional change.

**Viable stewardship models:**

- one durable Group Steward, initially filled by the accepted creator and transferable under later lifecycle governance;
- one durable institutional Group Steward independent of the creator;
- shared stewardship among several actors.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Authority Option A with exactly one durable Group Steward. The accepted creator may be the initial steward, but creator status alone does not establish stewardship. Stewardship may later transfer under a Group Lifecycle Standard. Administrators receive delegated administrative responsibility and do not become co-stewards by default.

**Why this emerges from the analysis:** Group identity and purpose describe the collective participation context rather than personal Identity, platform Policy or an administrative record. Participation Authority is therefore the narrowest existing Authority consistent with the Group Domain. Exactly one steward preserves the Entity Ownership Foundation; transferability prevents permanent constitutional dependence on the original creator.

**Decision difficulty:** High — the decision connects domain truth, stewardship and later Group roles while keeping them separate.

**Decision risk:** High — an incorrect allocation can turn administrators or creators into unrestricted constitutional actors.

**Decision reversibility:** Constitutionally difficult — changing the authority responsible for existing Group truth would require historical and register reconciliation.

**Long-term platform impact:** High — Group is the primary collective context for Version 2.

**Directly affected rows:** GRP-01 Group; GRP-04 Group purpose.

**Consequential rows:** GRP-02 Membership relationship; GRP-03 Member; GRP-06 Group administrative action.

**Trade-off accepted if approved:** Participation Authority receives a clarified establishment scope for the collective context. Later governance must prevent this clarification from becoming unrestricted Group control.

**Founder decision:**

- [ ] Select the split participation/policy model
- [ ] Request separate constitutional-change analysis for the administrative-establishment model
- [ ] Amend the authority allocation
- [ ] Amend the stewardship pattern

### EOG-03 — Challenge Identity, Purpose, Goal and Stewardship

**Decision required:** Determine which existing Authority establishes Challenge identity, Challenge purpose and Goal, and define Challenge stewardship in relation to the Group and Challenge Policy.

**Authority models assessed:**

- A — Participation-centered: Participation Authority establishes Challenge identity, purpose and Goal as the governed participation undertaking; Policy Authority separately establishes evaluation rules.
- B — Participation with separate Policy compliance: Participation Authority establishes Challenge identity, declared purpose and Goal; Policy Authority separately establishes whether the Goal and evaluation basis are permitted.
- C — Administrative establishment: Administrative Authority establishes the accepted Challenge configuration from attributable creator intent under Knowledge and Policy constraints. This would conflict with the current Administrative boundary and is not viable without separate constitutional change.

**Viable stewardship models:**

- one Challenge Steward, initially the accepted creator and accountable within the Group context;
- Group Steward automatically serves as Challenge Steward;
- one Challenge Steward designated independently by the Group's later governance.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Authority Option A with one Challenge Steward accountable within the Group context. The accepted creator may serve initially only when later participation governance establishes that allocation. Group stewardship and Challenge stewardship remain distinct; the Group Steward may hold or delegate Challenge stewardship but does not acquire it automatically. Policy Authority retains Challenge Policy, evaluation and amendment constraints.

**Why this emerges from the analysis:** Challenge identity, purpose and Goal form the shared undertaking in which participation occurs. Treating them as Policy would collapse desired outcome into governing rule; treating them as Administrative truth would elevate administration over product meaning. A distinct Challenge Steward preserves accountability without making every Group Steward responsible for every Challenge.

**Decision difficulty:** High — the allocation must preserve Group context, Challenge independence, Knowledge references and Policy boundaries.

**Decision risk:** High — ambiguity affects every later Challenge lifecycle, participation and amendment standard.

**Decision reversibility:** Constitutionally difficult — changing source authority after participation would threaten historical intelligibility.

**Long-term platform impact:** High — Challenge is Tiizi's central governed undertaking.

**Directly affected rows:** CHL-01 Challenge; CHL-02 Challenge purpose; CHL-03 Goal.

**Consequential rows:** CHL-04 Challenge Policy; CHL-05 Challenge participation relationship; CHL-06 Participant; CHL-07 Target; CHL-08 Challenge administrative action.

**Trade-off accepted if approved:** Participation Authority receives a clarified Challenge-context scope, while later standards must specify who may exercise it and how reliance limits amendments. KNW-04 remains pending for the Goal–Knowledge relationship.

## 5. Session 2 Decisions

### EOG-04 — Verification Authority

**Decision required:** Determine which existing Authority establishes Verification and whether Verification may occur before acceptance, after acceptance, or both, without collapsing it into acceptance, Evidence Eligibility or calculation.

**Models assessed:**

- A — Pre-acceptance only: Acceptance Authority establishes Verification as part of evaluating Submission Intent.
- B — Pre- and post-acceptance: Acceptance Authority establishes a distinct Verification determination under Policy before or after an Accepted Activity Event exists.
- C — Administrative review outside the current baseline: making Administrative Authority the source of Verification would require separate constitutional-change analysis.
- D — No authoritative Verification at launch: preserve source attestations and omit platform Verification until later governance activates it.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option B as the constitutional model, while Policy may declare Verification not required for a particular Activity or Challenge. Acceptance Authority establishes the Verification determination; Verification remains a distinct fact and does not automatically accept, reject, qualify or recalculate anything.

**Why this emerges from the analysis:** Verification evaluates the credibility or corroboration of participation evidence and is closest to trusted acceptance expertise. Supporting both timing positions avoids making later corroboration impossible. Separating its effects preserves Policy Authority over eligibility and Calculation Authority over outcomes.

**Decision difficulty:** High — timing, source truth and downstream effects must remain separable.

**Decision risk:** High — an ambiguous Verification flag could silently distort fairness or imply trust that does not exist.

**Decision reversibility:** Constitutionally difficult — moving Verification to another Authority would require reinterpretation of past determinations.

**Long-term platform impact:** High — the model affects competition, device evidence, manual review and future domains.

**Directly affected row:** AEV-08 Verification.

**Consequential rows:** AEV-05 Acceptance Decision; AEV-06 Accepted Activity Event; AEV-07 Evidence Eligibility; DRV-01 Progress; DRV-03 Ranking.

**Trade-off accepted if approved:** Acceptance Authority gains a distinct Verification determination responsibility. Later standards must rigorously prevent Verification from silently mutating acceptance or outcomes.

**Founder decision:**

- [ ] Approve the recommended pre- and post-acceptance model
- [ ] Select pre-acceptance only
- [ ] Request separate constitutional-change analysis for administrative review
- [ ] Select no authoritative Verification at launch

### EOG-05 — Correction Authority

**Decision required:** Determine how correction authority is allocated across Submission Intent, Accepted Activity Event, Evidence Eligibility and Derived Truth without creating a twelfth Authority or permitting administrative rewriting.

**Viable models:**

- A — Fact-authority model: the Authority that established each fact establishes its correction; Administrative Authority coordinates and records but does not redefine source truth.
- B — Acceptance-centered model: Acceptance Authority establishes all Activity-event corrections; Participant, Policy and Calculation Authorities handle only their separate expressions or downstream effects.
- C — Administrative correction model: Administrative Authority establishes corrections to activity and derived records under Policy, with source authorities consuming the result. This conflicts with current source-authority boundaries and is not viable as a general constitutional model.

Creating a separate Correction Authority is not viable within the approved 11-authority model and would require a separate constitutional-change decision.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option A. Participant Authority establishes a corrected or withdrawn Submission Intent expression; Acceptance Authority establishes correction, reversal or supersession of accepted-event truth; Policy Authority establishes corrected Evidence Eligibility; Calculation Authority establishes corrected Derived Truth. Administrative Authority supports attributable review, custody and audit only.

**Why this emerges from the analysis:** It preserves one authority per fact and prevents a correction mechanism from becoming a competing source of truth. It also makes downstream recalculation an authority consequence rather than an administrative edit.

**Decision difficulty:** High — several authorities must remain coordinated without being collapsed.

**Decision risk:** High — incorrect correction authority could erase history or create inconsistent Progress, Ranking and recap truth.

**Decision reversibility:** Constitutionally difficult — correction provenance must remain intelligible for historical records.

**Long-term platform impact:** High — the model governs errors, disputes, duplicates, late evidence and future integrations.

**Directly affected row:** AEV-09 Correction reference.

**Consequential rows:** AEV-04 through AEV-07; AEV-10; DRV-01 through DRV-04; DRV-06 through DRV-09.

**Trade-off accepted if approved:** Coordination is more demanding than a single administrative editor, but constitutional authority remains intact and auditable.

**Founder decision:**

- [ ] Select the Acceptance-centered model
- [ ] Request separate constitutional-change analysis for an Administrative correction model

### EOG-06 — Recognition Authority

**Decision required:** Determine what constitutes authoritative Recognition, which source truth qualifies it, which existing Authority establishes qualification, and how formal Recognition differs from participant encouragement and Reward.

**Models assessed:**

- A — Calculation-centered formal Recognition: Calculation Authority establishes qualification under Motivation Policy; Presentation Authority communicates it.
- B — Policy-centered Recognition: Policy Authority establishes both qualification rule and individual Recognition determination. This would collapse Policy and Calculation Authority and is not viable without constitutional change.
- C — Participant-authored Recognition only: encouragement and acknowledgement are Participant expressions with no authoritative qualification.
- D — Split model: formal Recognition qualification is Derived Truth established by Calculation Authority; participant-authored encouragement remains communication, not formal Recognition.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option D. Formal participation, Completion, Ranking, consistency and Group-contribution Recognition is established by Calculation Authority from the relevant authoritative source under Motivation Policy. Participant encouragement remains attributable communication. Presentation Authority communicates either without redefining it. Recognition remains distinct from Reward.

**Why this emerges from the analysis:** Tiizi needs truthful formal acknowledgement without turning social encouragement into a calculated claim or turning calculated qualification into a participant opinion. The split preserves both mutual encouragement and authoritative recognition.

**Decision difficulty:** Medium — the source distinctions are clear, but the boundary must be maintained across several motivation concepts.

**Decision risk:** Medium — poor governance would mislead users or allow recognition to become an accidental reward economy, but it does not alter accepted activity truth directly.

**Decision reversibility:** Moderate — recognition Policy can evolve prospectively, but historical qualification must remain attributable.

**Long-term platform impact:** High — the model shapes motivation across Fitness, Wellness and future governed domains.

**Directly affected row:** DRV-09 Recognition.

**Consequential rows:** DRV-01 Progress; DRV-02 Completion; DRV-03 Ranking; DRV-04 Streak; DRV-07 Feed item; DRV-08 Notification; AEV-09 Correction reference.

**Trade-off accepted if approved:** The split requires clear terminology for formal Recognition versus social acknowledgement. It avoids creating Reward rules or conflating applause with qualification.

**Founder decision:**

- [ ] Select Calculation-centered formal Recognition only

- [ ] Select participant-authored Recognition only
