# EOG-01 — Accountability Relationship Matrix

## 1. Purpose

This matrix compares the universal relationships defined by the [Platform Accountability Framework](04-PLATFORM-ACCOUNTABILITY-FRAMEWORK.md).

It is a definition matrix, not an allocation matrix. A statement that a relationship “may” perform something describes its conceptual boundary only. It does not assign that relationship to any Governed subject, actor, role or system.

## 2. Reading Rules

1. A relationship may act only where later governance declares purpose and scope.
2. “No” means the relationship does not confer the capability by itself.
3. “Separate assignment required” means the same actor could act only through another explicitly declared relationship.
4. Storage or possession never creates custody, stewardship or Authority by itself.
5. Creation of an expression or contribution is distinct from establishment of authoritative truth.
6. Approval is a form of Authority only where the applicable Platform Authority and Policy permit it.
7. No row names an accountable party for an entity.

## 4. Key-Dimension Matrix

This condensed view shows which relationship is primary for each accountability dimension. “Primary” describes meaning, not an allocation.

| Dimension | Primary relationship meaning | Supporting relationships | Constitutional exclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Establishing authoritative truth | Authority to establish truth | Attributable originator, Contributor, Participant and Policy may supply source or context where separately governed. | Stewardship, custody, storage, operation and presentation cannot substitute for Authority. |
| Coherent subject accountability | Accountable steward | Authorities, Custodians, Administrators, Operators and Contributors may support. | The steward does not acquire all supporting relationships. |
| Protection and preservation | Custodian | Accountable steward ensures custody is governed; Operator may support continuity. | Possession alone is not custody. |
| Reliable operation | Operator | Custodian, Administrator and Delegate may support within separate scope. | Operation does not establish product truth. |
| High-impact administration | Administrator | Authority, Delegate, Custodian and Operator may support within declared scope. | Administration is not unrestricted Authority. |
| Source contribution | Contributor | Attributable originator identifies source; Participant may contribute in context. | Contribution is not approval or acceptance. |
| Participation context | Participant | Attributable originator and Contributor may describe participant expression. | Participation is not stewardship or Authority over others. |
| Subject of governance | Governed subject | Accountable steward and Authorities later relate to it. | The Governed subject is not necessarily an actor. |
| Subject of information | Information subject | Custodian, Presenter and Downstream user may handle information under governance. | The Information subject is not automatically the Authority or Custodian. |
| Provenance | Attributable originator | Contributor, Participant, Delegating party and Authority action attribution may support. | Origin is not authoritative status. |
| Delegated action | Delegate | Delegating party and applicable Platform Authority define scope. | Delegation cannot exceed or conceal its source. |
| Communication | Presenter | Source Authority and Policy supply meaning and constraints. | Presentation does not establish underlying truth. |
| Purpose-limited consumption | Downstream user | Presenter and Custodian may enable approved access. | Use does not transfer meaning or accountability. |

## 5. Creation, Approval and Acceptance Distinctions

| Action | Relationship that may originate it | Relationship that may make it authoritative | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Create an expression | Attributable originator, Participant or Contributor | Applicable Authority only where governance accepts or establishes a related truth | Expression is not authoritative merely because it exists. |
| Supply source material | Contributor | Applicable Authority may approve a governed result based on it | Source material remains attributable and may be rejected. |
| Approve a rule or decision | An authorized actor acting through the applicable Platform Authority | The same declared Authority within scope | Steward, Custodian or Administrator title alone is insufficient. |
| Preserve information | Custodian | Preservation does not establish substantive truth | Integrity of custody and authority of content are separate. |
| Perform an administrative action | Administrator acting through Administrative Authority | Administrative Authority establishes only the approved administrative decision | Underlying domain truth retains its source Authority. |
| Operate a supporting process | Operator acting through Operational Authority | Operational Authority establishes only approved operational determinations | Operation cannot become permanent Policy or product truth. |
| Communicate information | Presenter | Source Authority remains authoritative | Presentation may create wording, not underlying truth. |
| Reuse information | Downstream user | A new truth requires a separate declared Authority | Reuse must preserve source meaning and purpose constraints. |

## 6. Delegation Comparison

| Relationship | Can the relationship itself be delegated or assigned? | What remains with the source? | What must remain explicit? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authority | May be delegated only where constitutionally permitted. | Legitimacy of the source and accountability for valid delegation. | Authority type, truth or action, scope, purpose, recipient and review boundary. |
| Accountable stewardship | Not transferred by ordinary delegation; reassignment requires later governance. | Stewardship until a valid reassignment becomes effective. | Governed subject, outgoing and incoming accountability, basis and historical attribution. |
| Custody | Duties may be assigned or shared where governed. | Responsibility defined by each continuing custodial assignment. | Information, purpose, handling scope and boundaries among Custodians. |
| Operation | Duties or Operational Authority may be assigned within approved scope. | Source responsibility for valid assignment and any retained Operational Authority. | Operational purpose, scope, conditions and review. |
| Administration | Administrative Authority or tasks may be delegated only explicitly. | Source accountability for valid delegation and any retained Authority. | Decision class, action scope, purpose and attribution. |
| Contribution | Work may be assigned; attribution must remain truthful. | Responsibility for the contribution each party actually supplies. | Contributor identity, source material and any editorial transformation. |
| Participation | Participation is a governed personal relationship and is not transferred by ordinary delegation. | The original relationship remains personal to its subject. | Any representative action requires separate governance. |
| Presentation | Work may be assigned without transferring source Authority. | Source Authority remains unchanged. | Audience, purpose, source status and limitations. |
| Downstream use | Use may not be passed to another purpose or party without governance. | Original use remains accountable to its approved purpose. | Recipient, purpose and source restrictions. |

## 7. Non-Allocation Confirmation

This matrix does not:

- name a Steward, Custodian, Operator, Administrator, Contributor, Delegate or Presenter for any entity;
- allocate any Platform Authority;
- determine who may create, approve, protect, operate, store, delegate, administer or present a specific Governed subject;
- change the Entity Ownership Register;
- resolve EOG-02 through EOG-06;
- define lifecycle, permission, privacy, security or implementation rules.
