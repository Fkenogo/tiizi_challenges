# EOG-02 — Group Accountability Options Paper

## 1. Purpose

This paper evaluates constitutional alternatives for Authority concerning Group truth, Accountable Stewardship, custody, administration, membership, Charter governance, moderation and dormant membership.

It applies the [EOG-01 Platform Accountability Framework](04-PLATFORM-ACCOUNTABILITY-FRAMEWORK.md). It does not allocate a relationship, approve an option, define a permission or create a lifecycle rule.

Every recommendation is **Recommended — pending founder approval**.

## 2. Fixed Founder Context

The following matters are not options in this paper:

- a Group is created rather than proposed;
- creation establishes existence, initial identity and founding purpose;
- the creator becomes the first Accountable Steward and first Member;
- membership remains distinct from stewardship;
- exactly one Accountable Steward relationship exists for every Group at any given time;
- stewardship may later be reassigned or voluntarily relinquished under approved governance;
- a Stewardship Council may support but never replace the Accountable Steward;
- Group existence is constitutionally independent of participation;
- the Group can outlive its creator;
- Group identity can evolve without identity evolution becoming lifecycle state.

## 3. Evaluation Criteria

| Criterion | Constitutional test |
|---|---|
| Authority clarity | Does one declared Platform Authority authenticate the creation event without being mistaken for the creator? |
| EOG-01 consistency | Are Authority, stewardship, custody, administration, operation and membership distinct? |
| Community continuity | Can the Group remain coherent beyond one person or Undertaking? |
| Founder-context fidelity | Does the option preserve creation and founding consequences? |
| Historical integrity | Can material change remain attributable and historically intelligible? |
| Least privilege | Does the option avoid implied or bundled power? |
| Voluntary participation | Does the model preserve the distinction between belonging and Commitment? |
| Implementation neutrality | Can the model remain valid across changing technologies and organizational structures? |
| Future governance clarity | Can lifecycle, permission, privacy and security standards refine it without contradiction? |

## 4. Authentication of Group Creation

The creator establishes the Group by creating it. The constitutional decision is which existing Platform Authority authenticates that creation event and its founding facts after creation.

Participation may never occur. Participation Authority therefore cannot be the constitutional basis for Group existence.

### Option A — Administrative Authority Authenticates Creation

Administrative Authority authenticates the creation event and its founding facts for a declared, limited purpose after the creator establishes the Group.

**Strengths**

- distinguishes human creation from authoritative authentication;
- treats authentication as an attributable high-impact administrative determination;
- does not make participation a prerequisite for Community identity;
- uses an existing Platform Authority without expanding Identity or Governance Authority.

**Weaknesses**

- requires careful wording so authentication is not mistaken for prior approval or administrative creation of the Group;
- must remain limited to authenticating the event and founding facts rather than acquiring general Group Authority.

**Long-term consequence:** Supplies an authoritative record of creation while leaving participation, membership, stewardship and later Group change separately governed.

### Option B — Identity Authority Authenticates Creation

Identity Authority authenticates the creation event because it establishes a new collective identity.

**Strengths**

- emphasizes Community identity rather than later activity;
- keeps participation separate from existence.

**Weaknesses**

- the approved Identity Authority concerns Person and Profile identity context;
- extending it to Group identity would blur human identity and collective identity boundaries.

**Long-term consequence:** Would require a material refinement of Identity Authority's constitutional scope.

### Option C — Participation Authority Authenticates Creation

Participation Authority authenticates creation because the Group will provide a participation context if participation later occurs.

**Strengths**

- uses the Authority already associated with Group and membership relationships;
- keeps the Group near its future participation context.

**Weaknesses**

- makes potential participation the basis for an already existing Community identity;
- conflicts with the founder principle that a Group may exist without participation;
- conflates Community identity with Community life.

**Long-term consequence:** Would preserve the conceptual error this refinement is intended to remove.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason:** Administrative Authority can authenticate the attributable creation event without causing, proposing or approving the Group's existence. The creator creates; Administrative Authority authenticates. Participation Authority remains responsible only for governed participation relationships and context.

## 5. Singular Accountable Stewardship and Stewardship Council

### Founder-Directed Constitutional Model

Every Group has exactly one Accountable Steward relationship at any given time. The relationship is singular, remains ultimately answerable, may be reassigned through approved governance and may be voluntarily relinquished under later approved governance. It supports continuity beyond the creator.

This model replaces the previous collective-holder recommendation and requires no EOG-01 amendment.

### Stewardship Council

A Stewardship Council is a governed body established to support the Accountable Steward in fulfilling the constitutional stewardship of a Group.

The Council:

- does not replace or multiply Accountable Stewardship;
- is not a Platform Authority;
- is not Administration;
- is not Custody;
- gives no Council member Accountable Stewardship merely through membership.

Council composition, appointment, decision-making and responsibilities remain for future governance. No Council allocation or operating model is approved in EOG-02.

## 6. Stewardship Reassignment, Succession and Failure

### Option A — Incumbent-Controlled Reassignment

The current Accountable Steward alone determines succession and originates reassignment.

**Strengths:** Clear source; protects continuity of declared accountability.

**Weaknesses:** Fails if the steward is absent, conflicted or unwilling; may prevent the Group from outliving its creator.

### Option B — Policy-Governed Reassignment With Separate Establishment

Approved Policy defines valid bases for voluntary transfer, relinquishment, succession and stewardship failure. A separately declared Authority establishes the reassignment; an Administrator may execute it only where separately assigned.

**Strengths:** Distinguishes Policy, Stewardship Reassignment and administration; supports continuity and failure cases; preserves attribution.

**Weaknesses:** Requires later standards to determine the establishing Authority and safeguards.

### Option C — Automatic Succession by Role or Membership

A role holder, oldest Member or other preset category automatically becomes Accountable Steward.

**Strengths:** Operational simplicity.

**Weaknesses:** Treats role or membership as implied accountability; conflicts with EOG-01; may create unwilling or unsuitable stewardship.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option B.

**Reason:** It permits the Group to outlive its creator without treating Delegation of Authority, Assignment of Responsibility or role inheritance as Stewardship Reassignment.

## 7. Custody Model

### Option A — Single Group-Wide Custodian

One Custodian preserves all Group information.

**Strengths:** Simple declaration and accountability surface.

**Weaknesses:** Treats information with different purposes and sensitivity as one custody scope; may create excessive access assumptions.

### Option B — Purpose-Limited Custody by Information Class

Custody is assigned separately for identity, Charter, membership, administration, communication and historical records. Compatible duties may be shared, but each assignment retains purpose and scope.

**Strengths:** Applies EOG-01 directly; supports least privilege; permits continuity without conflating storage, stewardship or Authority.

**Weaknesses:** More declarations and coordination are required.

### Option C — Accountable Steward Is Automatic Custodian

The Accountable Steward automatically holds custody of all Group information.

**Strengths:** Appears to align accountability and preservation.

**Weaknesses:** Directly conflates stewardship and custody; risks unrestricted possession and access; conflicts with EOG-01.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option B.

**Reason:** Group information has materially different privacy, continuity and historical purposes. Purpose-limited custody preserves those distinctions without naming a technical or organizational Custodian.

## 8. Administration Model

### Option A — Stewardship Implies Administration

The Accountable Steward automatically possesses every Group administrative power.

**Strengths:** Simple founding model.

**Weaknesses:** Conflates answerability with Administrative Authority; creates unrestricted power; impedes shared or transferred administration.

### Option B — Separately Declared Administrative Action Classes

Administrative Authority is assigned by action class and scope. Accountable Steward, Member or other status creates no administration unless separately declared.

**Strengths:** Preserves least privilege and EOG-01; enables different administrative responsibilities without changing stewardship; supports attributable moderation.

**Weaknesses:** Requires later action, role and permission governance.

### Option C — All Members Share Administration

Membership confers equal administrative power.

**Strengths:** Maximizes direct Community participation.

**Weaknesses:** Collapses membership into Authority; makes high-impact actions hard to attribute; conflicts with EOG-01 and least privilege.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option B.

**Reason:** Administration is a distinct exercise of Administrative Authority and should never be inferred from stewardship, membership or interface access.

## 9. Membership Model

### Option A — Membership Is a Participation Relationship Only

Membership establishes belonging to the Group and no default Authority, Accountable Stewardship, custody, administration or Challenge participation.

**Strengths:** Applies EOG-01; preserves Member versus Participant; supports relationship stacking only through separate declaration.

**Weaknesses:** Requires other governance for every consequential Group power.

### Option B — Membership Includes Limited Governance by Default

Every Member receives a baseline approval or administrative relationship.

**Strengths:** Supports participatory governance.

**Weaknesses:** Treats membership as a role bundle; creates implied Authority; makes boundaries difficult to govern.

### Option C — Charter Defines Whatever Membership Implies

The Charter may attach any Authority or accountability relationship to membership.

**Strengths:** Maximum Group autonomy.

**Weaknesses:** Allows local Charter terms to override Platform Authority boundaries and EOG-01; risks undisclosed or unstable power.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option A.

**Reason:** Community belonging should remain meaningful without becoming a source of governance power. Other relationships may be added only by separate, explicit governance.

## 10. Group Charter Status and Initial Establishment

### Fixed Charter Position

The Group Charter is treated as a Governed Subject. The options concern its constitutional form and establishment.

### Option A — Mandatory Founding Charter

Every Group is created with a Charter containing a minimum governed statement of purpose and participation principles.

**Strengths:** Makes founding commitments explicit and reviewable from the beginning.

**Weaknesses:** Converts a separate Charter into a universal creation requirement not established by the founder clarification; risks conflating founding purpose with a full policy instrument.

### Option B — Founding Declaration With Optional Formal Charter

Creation always establishes identity and founding purpose. A separate formal Charter may be established where the Group adopts governed rules beyond that founding declaration.

**Strengths:** Preserves the founder-established creation facts without inventing a universal document requirement; allows Charter governance to scale with Community complexity.

**Weaknesses:** Requires later governance to distinguish the founding declaration from the formal Charter and determine when a Charter is required.

### Option C — Charter Is Presentation Only

The Charter is an editable explanation without independent governed status.

**Strengths:** Simple and flexible.

**Weaknesses:** Conflicts with the founder instruction to treat the Charter as a governed constitutional entity; cannot support binding or historically intelligible rules.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option B.

**Reason:** It preserves creation as constitutionally complete while allowing a distinct governed Charter to carry rules when required.

## 11. Authority for Charter Content, Amendment and Binding Effect

### Option A — Accountable Steward Establishes and Amends the Charter

Stewardship itself authorizes Charter creation and amendment.

**Strengths:** Direct answerability.

**Weaknesses:** Conflates stewardship with Authority; permits unilateral rule changes by relationship status alone.

### Option B — Composite Authority by Charter Content

The creator or another Contributor may originate Charter text. The declared Platform Authority for Group truth establishes identity and purpose content; Policy Authority establishes binding participation and conduct rules. Amendments follow the same content-specific Authority boundaries.

**Strengths:** Respects the Charter's composite information; preserves Knowledge, Policy, Group truth and presentation boundaries; prevents one actor or relationship from acquiring universal Authority over Charter content.

**Weaknesses:** Requires clear classification of Charter provisions and later amendment governance.

### Option C — Member Consensus Establishes All Charter Content

A defined Member consensus establishes and amends the Charter directly.

**Strengths:** Strong Community legitimacy.

**Weaknesses:** Membership does not itself create Authority; consensus method, thresholds and lifecycle would need extensive new governance; some content remains constrained by higher Policy.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option B.

**Reason:** A Charter may express both Group truth and binding rules. Those contents require their applicable existing Authorities rather than a new Platform Authority for the Charter.

### Binding Effect Options

- **Binding from disclosed entry:** Members are bound only to Charter rules disclosed under approved membership governance.
- **Binding upon each amendment automatically:** Every valid amendment binds all Members immediately.
- **Binding according to governed amendment impact:** Policy distinguishes clarifications from material changes and governs their effect without silent retroactivity.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Binding according to governed amendment impact.

**Reason:** It preserves voluntary Community relationship and prevents silent material changes from being treated as prior consent. Exact notice, acceptance and transition rules remain deferred.

### Historical Treatment Options

- **Formal versioning required:** Every amendment produces a declared Charter version.
- **Attributable amendment history required:** Historical integrity is mandatory, while the mechanism remains for later governance.
- **Current text only:** Prior Charter meaning may be replaced.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Attributable amendment history required, with formal versioning deferred.

**Reason:** Historical intelligibility is constitutional; a specific versioning method is not yet approved and should not be invented here.

## 12. Moderation Model

### Option A — Inherent Steward Moderation

The Accountable Steward may invite, suspend, remove, restrict and amend Group governance by virtue of stewardship.

**Strengths:** Clear visible leadership.

**Weaknesses:** Conflates stewardship, Policy, Administrative Authority and Participation Authority; creates unrestricted power.

### Option B — Policy-Grounded, Separately Authorized Moderation

Policy defines valid moderation bases. Administrative Authority establishes an attributable moderation decision within assigned scope. Participation Authority establishes any resulting membership relationship truth. Stewardship remains answerability for coherent governance rather than the source of each action.

**Strengths:** Preserves all EOG-01 distinctions; supports least privilege and attribution; separates decision, relationship truth and stewardship.

**Weaknesses:** Requires later moderation, lifecycle, role and permission standards.

### Option C — Member-Majority Moderation

Members collectively determine all moderation actions.

**Strengths:** Community participation and perceived legitimacy.

**Weaknesses:** Membership is not Authority; may expose sensitive matters; risks popularity replacing Policy and due governance.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option B.

**Reason:** Moderation protects Community integrity only when its Policy basis, administrative decision and membership consequence remain distinct and attributable.

## 13. Dormant Membership

### Option A — Inactivity Is Misconduct

Inactivity automatically constitutes a breach and justifies removal.

**Strengths:** Encourages an active membership list.

**Weaknesses:** Converts absence into moral or conduct judgment without Policy; conflicts with truthful treatment of Members.

### Option B — Inactivity Is Neutral but May Be Policy-Relevant

Inactivity is not misconduct. An approved Charter or Policy may make a defined period or pattern of inactivity relevant to a purpose-limited membership decision. Any action requires an attributable reason and authority basis.

**Strengths:** Preserves truth and Community purpose; allows Groups to govern genuine capacity or participation needs; supports accountable moderation.

**Weaknesses:** Requires later definitions, safeguards and lifecycle governance.

### Option C — Inactivity Can Never Support Removal

Dormant membership must continue indefinitely unless the Member acts.

**Strengths:** Strong protection from involuntary removal.

**Weaknesses:** May prevent legitimate Community continuity or capacity governance; treats one rule as appropriate for every Group purpose.

**Recommended — pending founder approval:** Option B.

**Reason:** Inactivity should be described truthfully and may matter only where disclosed, approved Policy connects it to a legitimate Group purpose.

## 14. Future Governance Concepts (Non-Governing)

Group Governance Health may later describe the constitutional condition of a Group by reference to governance integrity rather than activity level. Possible considerations remain non-governing and may include a valid Accountable Steward, continuity, a Stewardship Council where established, governance orphan status, Charter governance, unresolved constitutional issues, moderation governance and governance review status.

Governance Maturity is conceptually distinct from Community Growth. Possible signs may include successful Stewardship Reassignment, Charter evolution, improved governance, governance continuity and constitutional resilience.

These are future governance concepts only. No metric, score, maturity model, assessment, implementation or lifecycle is approved in EOG-02.

## 16. Constitutional Amendments or Refinements Potentially Required

No source is amended by this paper.

If approved, the recommendations may require later refinement of:

- the Group Domain Standard, which currently leaves creation authentication and Accountable Stewardship deferred;
- the Entity Ownership Register Group and Charter rows;
- future Charter, membership, stewardship, Stewardship Council, moderation and Group lifecycle standards.

The Authority model itself need not gain a new Authority type under the recommended options.
