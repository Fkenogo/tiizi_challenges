# GRP Foundation Closure Phase 2 — Preferred Constitutional Models Report

**Status:** Recommendations prepared for founder review; no decision approved

**Date:** 2026-07-21

## 1. Purpose

This report evaluates every viable alternative established by the [Phase 1 Constitutional Options Paper](31-GRP-FOUNDATION-CLOSURE-CONSTITUTIONAL-OPTIONS-PAPER.md). It identifies the constitutionally strongest model for each GFC question without approving a decision, allocating an EOG-01 relationship, resolving CG-08 or amending EOG-01, EOG-02 or EOG-05.

The evaluation gives greatest weight to constitutional consistency, one-source truth, bounded Authority, singular accountability, historical intelligibility, community continuity and implementation neutrality. Allocation readiness is considered only as a downstream consequence; it does not determine constitutional meaning.

## 2. Evaluation Method

Each option was tested against:

1. the mandatory relationship distinctions in [EOG-01](04-PLATFORM-ACCOUNTABILITY-FRAMEWORK.md);
2. the approved Group principles and explicit deferrals in the [EOG-02 Founder Approval Record](07-EOG-02-FOUNDER-APPROVAL-RECORD.md);
3. the inventory boundaries and classification gates in the [EOG-05 Approved Register](27-EOG-05-ENTITY-OWNERSHIP-REGISTER-APPROVED.md);
4. constitutional simplicity, resilience and amendment burden;
5. dependencies identified in the [Phase 1 Dependency Impact Summary](31-GRP-FOUNDATION-CLOSURE-DEPENDENCY-IMPACT-SUMMARY.md); and
6. compatibility with the preferred models for the other GFC questions.

“Recommended” means constitutionally preferred for founder consideration. It does not mean approved.

## 3. GFC-01 — Group Creation Authentication

### 3.1 Comparative evaluation

| Criterion | GFC-01-A — Participation Authority | GFC-01-B — Administrative Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional strength | Keeps Group context near Participation Authority. | Gives the attributable creation event a narrowly bounded, reviewable authentication source. |
| Constitutional weakness | The approved EOG-02 framework states that Participation Authority does not provide the constitutional basis for Group existence. Repeated clarification would be needed to prevent semantic drift. | Could expand Administration into general Group truth unless its purpose is expressly confined to authenticating creation. |
| Long-term governance | Creates enduring ambiguity between Community existence and participation relationships. | Creates a clear creation-only boundary that later Group governance can preserve. |
| Dependencies | Requires a durable reinterpretive clarification of Participation Authority before later GRP work. | Requires later administrative accountability and audit treatment, without deciding mechanism or role. |
| Amendment burden | Medium; future standards must repeatedly separate creation from participation. | Medium at foundation, then lower if the creation-only scope remains stable. |
| Allocation readiness | Clarifies a source Authority but risks contaminating membership and participation allocations. | Clarifies the creation truth boundary while keeping Membership and Stewardship consequences separately allocable. |
| EOG-01 | Viable only if Authority scope is expressly extended without implying participation. | Strong if authentication, stewardship, custody and execution remain separate. |
| EOG-02 | Under tension with the express creation-versus-participation separation. | Directly matches the approved analytical boundary allowing Administrative Authority to authenticate creation if founder approved. |
| EOG-05 | Informs GRP-01 and GRP-04; no row or classification changes. | Informs GRP-01 and GRP-06; no row or classification changes. |

### 3.2 Founder trade-off

The founder is choosing between domain proximity and semantic clarity. GFC-01-A places creation beside later Group relationships but must continually explain why “Participation” does not mean participation. GFC-01-B accepts a tightly constrained administrative authentication event while preventing Administration from establishing Membership, Stewardship, Charter truth or general Group identity changes.

### 3.3 Recommendation

**Recommended — pending founder approval: GFC-01-B, narrowly bounded Administrative Authority authentication.**

The creator remains the Attributable originator of the creation expression. Administrative Authority should authenticate only the creation event and thereby establish the authoritative creation record within declared scope. Identity Authority supplies identity context; Policy Authority supplies applicable rules; Participation Authority separately establishes the first Membership relationship. The initial Accountable Steward consequence remains distinct from every Authority exercise.

This option is preferred because it most directly preserves the founder-approved distinction: existence concerns Community identity, while participation concerns Community life. It also follows EOG-01 by separating origin, Authority, stewardship and administration rather than using one relationship as shorthand for the others.

### 3.4 Why GFC-01-A is not recommended

GFC-01-A is constitutionally possible only through careful scope refinement, but its name creates persistent interpretive pressure. Because EOG-02 already states that Participation Authority does not provide the constitutional basis for Group existence, selecting it would weaken rather than clarify the approved separation. Its domain proximity does not outweigh the long-term risk of treating participation context as existence truth.

## 4. GFC-02 — Stewardship Continuity

### 4.1 Comparative evaluation

| Criterion | GFC-02-A — Successor-continuous | GFC-02-B — Dual path with Charter | GFC-02-C — Governance-protected |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constitutional strength | Simple rule; no vacancy; clean reassignment boundary. | Combines the simple voluntary path with a Group-grounded exceptional continuity basis. | Provides the strongest external backstop for orphaning and disputes. |
| Constitutional weakness | Does not address incapacity, abandonment or failure without a willing successor. | Depends on a binding, historically intelligible Charter and a separately declared source of authoritative reassignment truth. | Risks normalizing external intervention and increasing central governance burden. |
| Long-term governance | Stable default but constitutionally incomplete for failure. | Supports durable Community self-governance while preserving singular answerability. | Resilient but structurally dependent on exceptional central determination. |
| Dependencies | Later stewardship-failure governance remains a blocker. | Depends on GFC-03-A or another binding Charter model, then later Charter interpretation and reassignment governance. | Requires a later decision identifying the existing Authority and exceptional scope. |
| Amendment burden | Low for ordinary cases; further governance required for failure. | Medium; Charter applicability and historical integrity must remain coherent. | High; intervention limits, attribution and reviewability require durable controls. |
| Allocation readiness | Opens ordinary stewardship allocation but leaves failure relationships unresolved. | Provides the clearest basis for ordinary and exceptional allocation analysis once Charter governance matures. | Delays allocation until exceptional Authority and controls are defined. |
| EOG-01 | Strong separation of reassignment from delegation. | Strong if Charter rule, Authority and reassignment remain separately declared. | Viable only if intervention does not merge Governance, Administration and Stewardship. |
| EOG-02 | Preserves one steward but only conditionally supports continuity beyond the creator. | Best combines exactly-one stewardship, transferability and Community continuity. | Preserves continuity but places more constitutional weight outside the Group. |
| EOG-05 | No row change; informs GRP-01 stewardship allocation. | No row change; creates a traceable dependency between GRP-01 and C-GRP-07. | No row change; future exceptional-decision subjects may require separate analysis. |

### 4.2 Founder trade-off

The trade-off is simplicity versus constitutional resilience, and internal Community governance versus external intervention. GFC-02-A is clean but incomplete. GFC-02-C is comprehensive but risks making exceptional central control part of ordinary constitutional expectation. GFC-02-B makes continuity a property of the Group's governed constitutional instrument while still requiring a separately declared Authority to establish any actual reassignment truth.

### 4.3 Recommendation

**Recommended — pending founder approval: GFC-02-B, dual-path continuity.**

Voluntary stewardship change should use successor-continuous reassignment. Incapacity, abandonment or failure should be addressable through a constitutionally applicable Charter succession principle. The Charter supplies the governed rule; it does not exercise Authority. Later governance must still identify the Authority that establishes an authoritative exceptional reassignment and must preserve attribution, reviewability and exactly one Accountable Steward relationship.

This option is preferred because it permits a persistent Group to outlive its creator without converting Stewardship Council membership, delegation or Administration into stewardship. Paired with a founding binding Charter, it gives continuity an internal Community basis while avoiding the routine centralization risk of GFC-02-C.

### 4.4 Why the alternatives are not recommended

- **GFC-02-A:** It is the correct ordinary rule but not a sufficient constitutional model by itself. It cannot resolve the approved requirement for continuity where voluntary succession is unavailable.
- **GFC-02-C:** It is a legitimate exceptional model, but its constitutional and founder-oversight burden is disproportionate when a binding Charter can supply a Group-grounded succession basis. It should not be selected as the primary model merely because later dispute governance will still be needed.

## 5. GFC-03 — Charter Foundations

### 5.1 Comparative evaluation

| Criterion | GFC-03-A — Founding binding | GFC-03-B — Adopted binding | GFC-03-C — Layered | GFC-03-D — Declarative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Constitutional strength | Supplies immediate, attributable constitutional meaning and continuity. | Allows considered adoption after Group creation. | Combines immediate core with later community provisions. | Maximizes separation between expression and binding rules. |
| Constitutional weakness | Gives creation-time choices lasting significance and requires clear origin/Authority separation. | Leaves an initial Charter gap and cannot support immediate Charter-based succession. | Creates the greatest risk of competing meaning and applicability ambiguity. | Weakens the Charter's function and cannot support binding succession or Member expectations. |
| Long-term governance | Stable foundation with governable evolution. | Flexible but introduces a constitutionally incomplete founding period. | Flexible but persistently complex to interpret and preserve historically. | Simple but shifts constitutional work into Policy and other standards. |
| Dependencies | Requires later amendment, interpretation and historical-governance standards. | Requires adoption governance before binding effect. | Requires layer applicability, adoption and historical-governance standards. | Requires all binding continuity and conduct rules elsewhere. |
| Amendment burden | Medium and attributable. | Medium after adoption, plus adoption boundary. | High. | Low for Charter; higher across displaced governance. |
| Allocation readiness | Supplies one clear Charter subject and truth boundary for later relationship analysis. | Keeps Charter allocation conditional until adoption. | Requires allocation to distinguish layers without creating multiple sources. | Narrows Charter-related allocations but leaves continuity allocations elsewhere unresolved. |
| EOG-01 | Strong if creator authorship, Authority, stewardship and custody remain distinct. | Strong under the same separation after adoption. | Viable only with unusually precise one-truth treatment. | Strong separation, but at the cost of reduced constitutional effect. |
| EOG-02 | Best reflects creation establishing founding purpose and Charter Governed Subject status. | Compatible with independent Group existence, but less supportive of continuity. | Compatible with evolution but risks doctrinal complexity. | Compatible only as a weak expression of constitutional commitments. |
| EOG-05 | Strengthens the existing C-GRP-07 row without changing classification. | Makes applicable/proposed treatment more significant later. | Increases C-GRP-07 Information Subject and history complexity. | Retains C-GRP-07 with a narrower boundary. |

### 5.2 Founder trade-off

The founder is choosing how much constitutional work the Charter should perform. Immediate binding effect supports coherent founding meaning and succession, but demands disciplined later amendment governance. Delayed or layered applicability offers flexibility at the price of ambiguity. A declarative Charter is easy to maintain but cannot carry the continuity function identified in EOG-02.

### 5.3 Recommendation

**Recommended — pending founder approval: GFC-03-A, founding binding Charter.**

The initial Charter should become constitutionally applicable at Group creation. Its binding effect should remain subordinate to Platform governance and applicable Policy and should not itself create permissions, Authority or operational enforcement. Creator authorship must remain distinct from the Authority that establishes authoritative Charter truth. Later amendment must preserve attributable, historically intelligible meaning without this phase defining lifecycle or versioning.

This option provides the strongest, simplest companion to GFC-02-B. It gives the Group an immediate constitutional instrument, avoids an ungoverned adoption gap and preserves one Charter identity rather than a layered set of potentially competing meanings.

### 5.4 Why the alternatives are not recommended

- **GFC-03-B:** The adoption model creates an initial period in which the Charter cannot support continuity or binding Member expectations. The benefit of wider consideration does not outweigh the constitutional gap.
- **GFC-03-C:** The layered model is capable but imposes the highest historical and interpretive burden. Its flexibility can be achieved through attributable amendment of one founding Charter without creating quasi-independent layers.
- **GFC-03-D:** A purely declarative Charter does not provide enough constitutional effect for Community continuity and cannot coexist with the preferred Charter-governed succession path.

## 6. GFC-04 — Stewardship Council Constitutional Treatment

### 6.1 Comparative evaluation

| Criterion | GFC-04-A — Advisory only | GFC-04-B — Assigned responsibility | GFC-04-C — Coordination context |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constitutional strength | Lowest leakage risk; unmistakably non-governing. | Gives the Council a durable supporting function through separately bounded responsibility. | Makes all substantive member relationships individually attributable. |
| Constitutional weakness | May reduce the Council to an informal forum with little constitutional value. | Collective responsibility may be mistaken for collective stewardship unless carefully bounded. | Makes the Council body constitutionally thin and shifts all substance into later member relationships. |
| Long-term governance | Stable but limited. | Scalable collaborative support while one steward remains answerable. | Flexible across organizational forms but relationship-heavy. |
| Dependencies | Leaves all formal responsibility for later governance. | Requires later Council responsibility, composition and review governance. | Requires later individual or actor relationship declarations for substantive action. |
| Amendment burden | Low. | Medium. | Medium through repeated separate allocations. |
| Allocation readiness | Offers little reason for Council-body allocation and weakens the case for permanent C-GRP-08 treatment. | Supplies a credible future allocation target while allocating nothing now. | Enables member-level allocations but leaves the Council row's purpose uncertain. |
| EOG-01 | Strong by exclusion. | Strong if Assignment of Responsibility remains explicit and does not imply any other relationship. | Strong through separate-declaration discipline. |
| EOG-02 | Preserves support but may under-realize the approved Council concept. | Best expresses “supports but never replaces” the Accountable Steward. | Preserves boundaries but makes support mainly a coordination label. |
| EOG-05 | Supports either consolidation or permanent treatment, with limited independent need. | Strongly supports permanent C-GRP-08 treatment. | Leaves permanent versus consolidated treatment ambiguous. |

### 6.2 Founder trade-off

The trade-off is leakage risk versus constitutional usefulness. Advisory treatment is safest but least consequential. Coordination-only treatment protects individual attribution but makes the Council body largely descriptive. Assigned responsibility permits genuine collaborative governance while requiring the strictest language protecting the singular Accountable Steward relationship.

### 6.3 Recommendation

**Recommended — pending founder approval: GFC-04-B, assigned-responsibility Council.**

The Council should be constitutionally capable of receiving expressly bounded Assignments of Responsibility that support the Accountable Steward. No assignment should imply Authority, Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration or a relationship for any Council member. Composition, appointment, decision-making, responsibility content and review remain deferred.

This option is preferred because it gives the founder-approved Council a durable constitutional purpose without weakening singular answerability. EOG-01 already provides the semantic control needed: Assignment of Responsibility is neither Delegation of Authority nor Stewardship Reassignment.

### 6.4 Why the alternatives are not recommended

- **GFC-04-A:** It preserves boundaries but supplies too little constitutional substance to justify a durable Council body or meaningfully support stewardship continuity.
- **GFC-04-C:** It has strong attribution discipline, but treating every substantive relationship as external to the Council undermines the Council's identity as a governed supporting body and complicates later allocation.

## 7. GFC-05 — CG-08 Inventory Readiness

### 7.1 Comparative evaluation

| Criterion | GFC-05-A — Permanent independent entity | GFC-05-B — Consolidate under Group | GFC-05-C — Continue provisional |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constitutional strength | Preserves the Council's distinct governed-body identity and stable traceability. | Keeps inventory lean and avoids premature entity status. | Maximizes restraint until later Council governance exists. |
| Constitutional weakness | Could be mistaken for an allocation or over-classification if boundaries are weak. | Risks erasing EOG-02 Governed Subject treatment and prevents Council-body allocation. | Leaves CG-08 and affected allocation readiness unresolved. |
| Long-term governance | Stable Entity ID supports later Council governance and history. | Likely requires readmission if the Council receives body-level responsibility. | Defers the same classification question and may prolong programme blockage. |
| Dependencies | Requires an attributable EOG-05 classification amendment and a resolved Information Subject boundary. | Requires careful preservation of the Council concept; conflicts with GFC-04-B if body-level responsibility is later allocated. | Requires a later revisit after Council governance; no blocker is removed. |
| Amendment burden | Medium once, with future amendments attributable. | Medium now and potentially high through later readmission. | Low now but cumulative delay and later analysis remain. |
| Allocation readiness | Closes CG-08 and provides a stable future allocation reference without making an allocation. | Closes classification only by eliminating the independent target. | Does not satisfy the Group classification-closure gate. |
| EOG-01 | Neutral; inventory identity implies no relationship. | Neutral if consolidation implies no relationship. | Neutral. |
| EOG-02 | Directly preserves the approved supporting Governed Subject. | Compatible only if conceptual status remains explicit outside the register row. | Preserves conceptual status but not permanent register treatment. |
| EOG-05 | Requires a later approved change from Provisional to permanent treatment; no other row changes follow automatically. | Requires a later approved consolidation amendment. | Leaves EOG-05 unchanged and CG-08 open. |

### 7.2 Founder trade-off

The founder is choosing between stable constitutional traceability and inventory minimalism. Permanent treatment creates no allocation, but it acknowledges that the Council has an identity capable of bearing its own governed information. Consolidation reduces rows but becomes costly if the Council receives body-level responsibility. Continued provisional treatment preserves optionality while defeating the closure purpose of this programme.

### 7.3 Recommendation

**Recommended — pending founder approval: GFC-05-A, permanent independent inventory entity.**

Subject to a later attributable EOG-05 amendment, C-GRP-08 should receive permanent inventory treatment with Council information, Group information and Council-relationship information expressly distinguished. Permanent classification must state that it allocates no Authority, Accountable Stewardship, Custody, Administration, Operation or Assignment of Responsibility.

This option is preferred because it is the only model fully compatible with GFC-04-B and the approved cross-register separation. It closes the classification gate while leaving all relationship allocation for the deferred Entity Relationship Allocation Register.

### 7.4 Why the alternatives are not recommended

- **GFC-05-B:** Consolidation is incompatible with the preferred Council-body responsibility model unless the Council is later readmitted, producing avoidable traceability and amendment cost. It also creates a material risk of obscuring the Council's approved Governed Subject status.
- **GFC-05-C:** Continued provisional treatment is constitutionally safe but does not perform foundation closure. It leaves CG-08 and the associated allocation-readiness dependency unresolved despite Phase 2 now having sufficient Council-boundary evidence to recommend a permanent treatment.

## 9. Decision Status

All five recommendations remain **pending founder approval**. They are evidence-based proposals only. EOG-01, EOG-02 and EOG-05 remain unchanged; CG-08 remains unresolved; no relationship is allocated; and no implementation, lifecycle, role or permission rule is created.
