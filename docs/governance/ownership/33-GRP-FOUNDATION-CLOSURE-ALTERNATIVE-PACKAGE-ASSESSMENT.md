**Status:** Comparative package challenge; no package approved

## 1. Purpose

This assessment tests whether a combination of Phase 1 alternatives is constitutionally stronger than the Phase 2 package. It uses only previously identified options and introduces no hybrid decision, Authority, allocation or implementation.

## 4. Package Challenges

### 4.1 P2 — Domain-aligned minimal

P2 is the strongest low-documentation alternative. It fails as a ten-year foundation because it combines a semantically strained creation Authority, no complete failure-continuity model, a Charter unable to supply binding succession and no durable Council allocation target. Its low initial burden becomes later repair work.

### 4.2 P3 — Central resilience

P3 is the strongest substantive competitor. It handles orphaning and dispute more directly than P1. It is not preferred because it constitutionalizes external intervention before demonstrating that a binding Group Charter and separately governed reassignment Authority are inadequate. The ongoing scope, review and central-oversight burden is higher.

### 4.3 P4 — Deliberative restraint

P4 best protects against premature founding commitments. It leaves too many linked questions unresolved: succession before Charter adoption, exceptional continuity, Council allocation meaning and CG-08. It is suitable as an interim posture, not as foundation closure.

### 4.4 P5 — Lean Charter continuity

P5 preserves the strongest continuity and Charter pair while minimizing Council governance. It is coherent, but it underuses the founder-approved Council concept and likely creates a later readmission cycle if the Council needs formal supporting responsibilities. Its inventory savings are not constitutionally decisive.

## 5. Dependency Concentration

P1 contains two deliberate concentrations:

- **GFC-02-B depends on GFC-03-A.** The dependency is material but not excessive because a binding Charter has independent constitutional value for purpose, commitments and historical coherence. If GFC-03-A is rejected, GFC-02 must be reconsidered.
- **GFC-04-B supports GFC-05-A.** The dependency is reinforcing rather than constitutive. EOG-02 already establishes the Council as a Governed Subject, so permanent inventory identity remains defensible even if GFC-04-B is rejected.

GFC-01-B is independent of the Charter and Council choices. No recommendation is being used to create a new Authority or automatic allocation for another.

## 7. Ten-year Amendment Assessment

P1 is likely to require later standards for creation attribution, stewardship reassignment, Charter interpretation and history, Council governance and EOG-05 classification. Those are expected refinements of declared boundaries.

P2 and P4 appear lighter but defer foundational defects that would likely require constitutional reopening. P3 supplies resilience earlier but requires continuous control of exceptional central Authority. P5 reduces Council work initially but may require later reclassification and cross-register repair.

P1 therefore has the lowest expected **corrective** amendment burden even though it does not have the lowest total governance volume.

## 8. Alternative-package Verdict

No tested alternative package is constitutionally stronger than P1 across EOG fidelity, Community continuity, Authority separation, allocation readiness and ten-year maintenance. P3 is the closest competitor and should remain visible if later Charter-based continuity proves unable to handle exceptional failure without excessive ambiguity.
