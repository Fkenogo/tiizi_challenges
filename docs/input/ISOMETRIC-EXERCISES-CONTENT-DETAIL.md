## CORE

### 1. Hollow Body Hold
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Core, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the entire core for intermediate to advanced fitness enthusiasts. This full-body tension hold builds the foundation for advanced core control and gymnastics-style movements.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 15–30 sec

**Setup:** Lie flat on your back. Press lower back firmly into the floor. Extend arms overhead.
**Execution:** Lift shoulders and legs off the floor simultaneously, forming a shallow banana shape. Keep lower back pinned to the floor throughout. Hold the position without letting the back arch.

**Cues:**
- Keep lower back glued to the floor
- Point toes, squeeze legs together
- Breathe steadily, don't hold your breath

**Common Mistakes:**
- Lower back arching off the floor
- Lifting legs too high, losing tension
- Holding breath during the hold

**Safety First:** Stop if you feel sharp lower back pain — regress to bent knees.

### 2. Arch Hold (Superman Hold)
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Core, Back, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the lower back, glutes, and shoulders. Builds posterior chain endurance and counters the effects of prolonged sitting.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 15–30 sec

**Setup:** Lie face down, arms extended overhead, legs straight.
**Execution:** Lift arms, chest, and legs off the floor at the same time. Hold at the top with glutes and lower back engaged.

**Cues:**
- Squeeze glutes throughout
- Reach long through fingers and toes
- Keep neck neutral, look at the floor

**Common Mistakes:**
- Craning the neck up
- Bouncing instead of holding still
- Shrugging shoulders toward ears

**Safety First:** Ease off if lower back discomfort occurs; reduce range of lift.

### 3. Bird Dog Hold (Left / Right)
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Core, Balance, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the core and back for beginner to intermediate fitness enthusiasts. Builds anti-rotation strength and spinal stability.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 15–20 sec per side

**Setup:** Start on hands and knees, hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
**Execution:** Extend one arm forward and the opposite leg back until both are parallel to the floor. Hold without letting hips or shoulders rotate.

**Cues:**
- Keep hips square to the floor
- Brace core like expecting a light punch
- Reach through the extended hand and heel

**Common Mistakes:**
- Hips rotating open
- Lower back sagging
- Rushing into position instead of controlled extension

**Safety First:** Stop if you feel instability or pain in the supporting wrist or knee.

### 4. Dead Bug Hold (Left / Right)
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Core, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the deep core and hip flexors for beginner fitness enthusiasts. A safe, back-friendly way to build core control.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 10–20 sec per side

**Setup:** Lie on your back, arms extended toward ceiling, knees bent at 90 degrees.
**Execution:** Extend one arm overhead and the opposite leg straight out, hovering just above the floor. Hold without letting your lower back lift off the ground.

**Cues:**
- Press lower back into the floor
- Move slowly into position, then hold still
- Keep extended limbs just off the floor

**Common Mistakes:**
- Lower back arching up
- Letting the extended leg touch the floor
- Holding breath

**Safety First:** Reduce the hold height if lower back lifts off the floor.

### 5. V-Sit Hold
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Core, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the core and hip flexors for intermediate to advanced fitness enthusiasts. Builds the balance and core strength used in advanced bodyweight skills.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 10–20 sec

**Setup:** Sit on the floor, knees bent, feet flat.
**Execution:** Lean back slightly, lift feet off the floor, and extend arms forward for balance. Hold the balanced V position.

**Cues:**
- Keep chest lifted, don't round the back
- Engage core to stay balanced
- Start with bent knees before progressing to straight legs

**Common Mistakes:**
- Rounding the lower back
- Holding breath while balancing
- Progressing to straight-leg version too early

**Safety First:** Keep knees bent if you feel strain in the lower back or hip flexors.

### 6. Hollow Rock Hold
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Core, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the entire core for advanced fitness enthusiasts. An intensified hollow hold that increases the lever arm for greater core demand.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 10–20 sec

**Setup:** Lie flat on your back, lower back pressed to the floor.
**Execution:** Lift shoulders and legs into the hollow position, then extend arms fully overhead past your head. Hold with maximum body tension.

**Cues:**
- Squeeze entire body tight, like a plank on your back
- Keep lower back pinned down
- Extend arms fully for the full lever effect

**Common Mistakes:**
- Arching the lower back
- Losing tension in the extended arms
- Attempting this before mastering the basic hollow hold

**Safety First:** Master the basic Hollow Body Hold first before progressing to this variation.

### 7. Star Plank Hold (Left / Right)
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Core, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the obliques, shoulders, and hip abductors for advanced fitness enthusiasts. Adds a balance challenge to the standard side plank.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 10–20 sec per side

**Setup:** Start in a side plank position on your forearm.
**Execution:** Extend the top arm and top leg outward simultaneously to form a star shape. Hold the position while keeping hips lifted.

**Cues:**
- Keep hips high throughout
- Extend limbs fully for maximum stretch and tension
- Stack shoulders directly over the supporting elbow

**Common Mistakes:**
- Hips dropping toward the floor
- Rotating the torso forward or backward
- Rushing to extend limbs before hips are stable

**Safety First:** Master a standard side plank hold before attempting this variation.

### 8. Copenhagen Plank Hold (Left / Right)
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Core, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the inner thigh (adductors) and core for advanced fitness enthusiasts. Highly effective for groin injury prevention, used widely in football/athletic conditioning.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 10–15 sec per side
**Equipment:** Bench or sturdy chair

**Setup:** Lie on your side, top leg resting on a bench or chair, propped up on your forearm.
**Execution:** Lift your bottom leg and hips off the floor, holding your body in a straight line supported by the top leg and forearm.

**Cues:**
- Keep hips lifted and squared
- Engage the inner thigh of the supporting leg
- Start with the bent-knee regression if full extension is too difficult

**Common Mistakes:**
- Letting hips sag toward the floor
- Rotating the torso forward
- Attempting the full straight-leg version too soon

**Safety First:** Regress to a bent-knee variation if you feel strain in the groin.

## GLUTES & HIPS

### 9. Glute Bridge Hold
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Lower Body, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the glutes, hamstrings, and lower back for beginner fitness enthusiasts. A foundational hip-strengthening hold, ideal for beginners and rehab contexts.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 20–40 sec

**Setup:** Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor hip-width apart.
**Execution:** Lift hips toward the ceiling until knees, hips, and shoulders form a straight line. Hold at the top, squeezing the glutes.

**Cues:**
- Squeeze glutes hard at the top
- Avoid overextending the lower back
- Keep feet flat and knees tracking forward

**Common Mistakes:**
- Overarching the lower back instead of using glutes
- Feet too far from or too close to the hips
- Letting hips sag mid-hold

**Safety First:** Stop if you feel lower back pain rather than glute engagement.

### 10. Single Leg Glute Bridge Hold (Left / Right)
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Lower Body, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the glutes unilaterally for intermediate fitness enthusiasts. Builds single-leg hip strength and corrects side-to-side imbalances.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 15–20 sec per side

**Setup:** Lie on your back in bridge position, then extend one leg straight out.
**Execution:** Lift hips off the floor keeping both sides level, holding the position on the single grounded leg.

**Cues:**
- Keep hips level, avoid tilting to one side
- Drive through the heel of the grounded foot
- Keep extended leg in line with your torso

**Common Mistakes:**
- Hips tilting toward the lifted leg
- Overarching lower back to compensate
- Extended leg dropping below hip height

**Safety First:** Master the two-leg bridge hold first if you feel any hip or lower back strain.

### 11. Clamshell Hold (Left / Right)
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Lower Body, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the glute medius and hip stabilizers for beginner fitness enthusiasts. Commonly used for hip and knee injury prevention.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 15–20 sec per side

**Setup:** Lie on your side, knees bent at roughly 45 degrees, feet stacked together.
**Execution:** Keeping feet touching, lift the top knee open as far as comfortable, and hold at end range without rotating your hips back.

**Cues:**
- Keep hips stacked, don't roll backward
- Keep feet together throughout
- Focus on the outer hip doing the work

**Common Mistakes:**
- Rolling the hips backward to gain range
- Feet separating during the movement
- Using momentum instead of a controlled hold

**Safety First:** Reduce range if hip discomfort occurs.

### 12. Fire Hydrant Hold (Left / Right)
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Lower Body, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the glute medius and hip abductors for beginner to intermediate fitness enthusiasts. Improves hip stability for running and lateral movement.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 15–20 sec per side

**Setup:** Start on hands and knees, hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
**Execution:** Lift one bent knee out to the side to hip height, keeping the knee bent at 90 degrees, and hold.

**Cues:**
- Keep hips square, don't rotate the torso
- Lift only to hip height, not higher
- Keep supporting core engaged

**Common Mistakes:**
- Rotating the torso to gain height
- Arching the lower back
- Lifting the knee too high, losing form

**Safety First:** Keep the supporting wrist aligned under the shoulder to avoid strain.

### 13. Standing Hip Abduction Hold (Left / Right)
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Lower Body, Balance, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the glute medius for beginner fitness enthusiasts. Builds standing hip stability and single-leg balance.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 15–20 sec per side
**Equipment:** Wall (optional, for balance)

**Setup:** Stand tall, optionally holding a wall for balance.
**Execution:** Lift one leg out to the side, keeping it straight, and hold at end range without leaning the torso.

**Cues:**
- Keep standing leg slightly soft, not locked

- Keep hips facing forward throughout

**Common Mistakes:**
- Leaning the torso to lift the leg higher

## LOWER BODY / LEGS

### 14. Single Leg Wall Sit (Left / Right)
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Lower Body, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the quads and glutes unilaterally for advanced fitness enthusiasts. An intensified progression from the standard Wall Sit.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 10–15 sec per side
**Equipment:** Wall

**Setup:** Start in a standard wall sit position with thighs parallel to the floor.
**Execution:** Lift one foot slightly off the floor, holding your body weight on the standing leg.

**Cues:**
- Keep hips level, don't drop to one side
- Keep the lifted foot relaxed, not pointed
- Maintain 90-degree knee angle on the standing leg

**Common Mistakes:**
- Hips dropping toward the lifted leg side
- Knee of the standing leg caving inward
- Rising up out of position to compensate for fatigue

**Safety First:** Master a full wall sit hold of 30+ seconds before attempting the single-leg version.

### 20. Pistol Squat Hold (Left / Right)

## UPPER BODY

### 21. Isometric Push-Up Hold — Top
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Upper Body, Isometric
**Benefits:** A strength exercise targeting the shoulders, core, and triceps for beginner to intermediate fitness enthusiasts. Builds the lockout strength and core bracing needed for full push-ups.
**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 15–30 sec

**Setup:** Start in a high plank / top push-up position, arms fully extended.
**Execution:** Hold the top position without sagging hips or rounding the upper back.

**Cues:**
- Keep body in a straight line from head to heels
- Engage core and glutes throughout
- Keep shoulders away from ears

**Common Mistakes:**
- Hips sagging toward the floor
- Head dropping forward
- Locking elbows too aggressively

**Safety First:** Drop to knees if wrist or shoulder discomfort occurs.

### 22. Isometric Push-Up Hold — Mid
**Category:** STRENGTH · **Tags:** Upper Body, Isometric

**Metric Unit:** Seconds · **Recommended:** 10–20 sec

**Setup:** Lower from a high plank to the halfway point of a push-up, elbows around 90 degrees.
**Execution:** Hold the midpoint position without sinking lower or pressing back up.

**Cues:**
- Keep elbows at roughly 45 degrees from the body

- Maintain a straight body line

**Common Mistakes:**
- Hips sagging below shoulder line
- Elbows flaring out to 90 degrees

- Keep shoulders down, away from ears

- Holding breath during the push

## NEW SUGGESTIONS — How to Capture This Better

A few structural ideas beyond just filling in the existing template, based on what isometric-specific content needs that dynamic exercises don't:

### 1. Add a "Difficulty" field (separate from category)
Right now difficulty is buried inside the Benefits paragraph ("for intermediate-level fitness enthusiasts"). For isometric holds, difficulty scales mostly through *hold duration* and *lever length* rather than reps — worth pulling into its own field (`Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced`) so it can be filtered and used to auto-suggest progressions.

### 2. Add an "Equipment" field
Several isometric moves need a wall, chair, or bench (Wall Sit, Copenhagen Plank, Doorway Chest Press). Right now equipment is buried in the exercise name or absent. A dedicated `equipment: none | wall | chair | bench` field would let you filter "no-equipment only" workouts cleanly — important for your Kenyan bodyweight-first audience.

### 3. Split "Instructions" into three phases instead of two
Your current schema has Setup + Execution. For isometric moves specifically, a third phase — **Hold** — is useful because "getting into position" and "maintaining the position" involve different cues (e.g., "lower into position" vs. "keep pressing your palm into the wall the entire time"). This also sets up cleaner UI for a workout timer: a short "get into position" countdown, then the main hold timer.

### 4. Progression linking
Several of these exercises are natural progressions of ones already in your list (e.g., Wall Sit → Single Leg Wall Sit, Glute Bridge → Single Leg Glute Bridge Hold, Push Up Hold → Isometric Push-Up Hold Mid). A `progression_from` / `progression_to` field would let the app suggest "you've mastered this, try this next" — a nice retention feature for a fitness PWA.

### 5. Rep-based vs. Hold-based flag (you may already need this for the timer logic)
Since Tiizi mixes reps (Push-ups) and time (Push Up Hold) even within the same movement family, a boolean like `hold_based: true` (mentioned in the schema note from before) becomes essential — it determines whether the workout screen shows a rep counter or a single countdown timer, and whether "Recommended: 10-20" means reps or seconds.
