# Tiizi_Canonical_Exercise_Families_v2.2

**Status:** Draft for approval

**Purpose:** Define the authoritative family structure used to classify every fitness exercise in the Tiizi Knowledge Catalogue.

This phase refines the earlier Exercise Family Specification into a usable classification standard.

A key correction is required before catalogue production begins:

> **Domains describe the broad training purpose. Families describe the primary movement or activity pattern.**
>

For example:

- **Strength & Conditioning** is a domain.
- **Push** is a family.
- **Horizontal Push** is a sub-family.
- **Push-up** is a canonical exercise.
- **Knee Push-up** is a variant.

The same exercise may have several goals and tags, but it must have one primary family.

---

# 1. Canonical Classification Structure

```
Exercise
  └── Primary Domain
        └── Primary Family
              └── Sub-family
                    └── Canonical Exercise
                          └── Variant
```

Example:

```
Exercise
  └── Strength & Conditioning
        └── Push
              └── Horizontal Push
                    └── Push-up
                          ├── Knee Push-up
                          ├── Incline Push-up
                          └── Decline Push-up
```

---

# 2. Approved Exercise Domains

The following domains are approved for Version 2:

1. Strength & Conditioning
2. Cardiovascular
3. Mobility
4. Flexibility
5. Balance & Stability
6. Power & Athletic Movement
7. Recovery

Domains are intentionally broad. They must not be confused with challenge categories, user goals or body-part labels.

# 4. Family 01 — Push

## Definition

Movements in which the participant applies force away from the body or moves the body away from a surface through pressing action.

## Primary domain

Strength & Conditioning

## Approved sub-families

- Horizontal Push
- Vertical Push
- Incline Push
- Decline Push
- Dip
- Isometric Push

## Canonical exercise candidates

- Push-up
- Bench Press
- Chest Press
- Shoulder Press
- Dip
- Wall Press Hold

## Common variants

- Knee Push-up
- Incline Push-up
- Decline Push-up
- Diamond Push-up
- Dumbbell Bench Press
- Machine Chest Press
- Arnold Press

## Inclusion rule

Use Push when the primary training action is pressing.

## Exclusion rule

Do not classify an exercise as Push merely because the chest, shoulders or triceps are active.

A plank, for example, may load the shoulders but its primary intent is trunk stability.

## Typical metrics

- Repetitions
- Sets
- Load
- Duration for isometric variations
- Completion

## Typical challenge compatibility

- Collective repetition target
- Competitive highest total
- Streak session completion
- Workout completion

## Safety considerations

- Shoulder position
- Wrist loading
- Elbow control
- Excessive lumbar extension during overhead pressing

# 6. Family 03 — Squat

## Definition

Knee-dominant lower-body movements where the hips and knees flex and extend while the torso remains comparatively upright.

## Primary domain

Strength & Conditioning

## Approved sub-families

- Bilateral Squat
- Unilateral Squat
- Front-loaded Squat
- Back-loaded Squat
- Overhead Squat
- Isometric Squat

## Canonical exercise candidates

- Bodyweight Squat
- Goblet Squat
- Back Squat
- Front Squat
- Pistol Squat
- Wall Sit

## Common variants

- Box Squat
- Sumo Squat
- Jump Squat
- Chair Squat
- Heel-elevated Squat

## Inclusion rule

Use Squat when the movement is primarily a coordinated hip-and-knee descent and ascent.

## Exclusion rule

A split squat remains within the Lunge family because of its staggered stance and unilateral loading pattern.

A jump squat is primarily classified as Jump & Plyometric when explosive output is the main purpose.

## Typical metrics

- Repetitions
- Sets
- Load
- Duration for wall sits
- Completion

## Safety considerations

- Knee alignment
- Foot pressure
- Depth suitability
- Lumbar and pelvic control
- Load selection

# 8. Family 05 — Lunge

## Definition

Staggered-stance or split-stance lower-body movements involving controlled lowering and rising, usually with unilateral emphasis.

## Primary domain

Strength & Conditioning

## Approved sub-families

- Forward Lunge
- Reverse Lunge
- Lateral Lunge
- Walking Lunge
- Split Squat
- Curtsy Lunge

## Canonical exercise candidates

- Forward Lunge
- Reverse Lunge
- Walking Lunge
- Lateral Lunge
- Split Squat

## Inclusion rule

Use Lunge where the stance and movement create a predominantly unilateral lower-body pattern.

## Exclusion rule

Do not classify Step-ups as Lunges. Step-ups have a distinct elevation and stepping demand and belong to the Step family.

## Typical metrics

- Repetitions
- Repetitions per side
- Sets
- Load
- Distance for walking lunges
- Completion

## Safety considerations

- Knee alignment
- Balance
- Step length
- Hip control
- Unequal loading between sides

# 9. Family 06 — Step

## Definition

Movements where the participant ascends, descends or repeatedly moves onto an elevated surface.

## Primary domains

- Strength & Conditioning
- Cardiovascular

## Approved sub-families

- Step-down
- Stair Climb
- Lateral Step

## Canonical exercise candidates

- Step-up
- Step-down
- Stair Climbing
- Lateral Step-up

## Inclusion rule

Use Step when elevation change through stepping is central to the exercise.

## Exclusion rule

## Typical metrics

- Flights
- Steps
- Duration
- Distance or elevation where supported
- Completion

## Challenge compatibility

- Collective step or flight target

- Timed completion
- Streak participation

## Safety considerations

- Step height
- Surface stability
- Balance
- Knee tolerance
- Controlled descent

# 22. Family 19 — Recovery

## Definition

Low-intensity physical practices primarily intended to support restoration, reduce stiffness or promote readiness rather than produce a direct training stimulus.

## Primary domain

Recovery

## Approved sub-families

- Active Recovery
- Self-Myofascial Release
- Gentle Movement
- Recovery Flow
- Post-session Cooldown

## Canonical exercise candidates

- Recovery Walk
- Foam Rolling
- Gentle Mobility Flow
- Cooldown Walk
- Light Recovery Cycle

## Important overlap rule

Recovery is used only when restoration is the principal purpose.

Normal walking remains in Locomotion.

A Recovery Walk may be a separate prescribed Knowledge Item only where its intensity, instructions, safety and challenge compatibility materially differ from ordinary Walking. Otherwise, it should be a Walking prescription rather than a duplicate exercise.

## Typical metrics

- Duration
- Sessions
- Completion

Competitive ranking should generally be disabled for recovery activities.

## Safety considerations

- Avoid turning recovery into high intensity
- Pain awareness
- Equipment use
- Clinical conditions

# 23. Secondary Movement Demands

An exercise may record secondary patterns without changing its primary family.

Examples:

| Exercise | Primary family | Secondary demands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Renegade Row | Pull | Anti-Rotation, Push |
| Turkish Get-up | Carry or Functional composite review | Lunge, Push, Rotation, Balance |
| Suitcase Carry | Carry | Anti-Lateral Flexion |
| Jump Squat | Jump & Plyometric | Squat |
| Bear Crawl | Locomotion | Push, Anti-Rotation |
| Kettlebell Swing | Hinge | Power |
| Single-leg Deadlift | Hinge | Balance |

Secondary demands support recommendations, search and workout balancing. They must not create duplicate catalogue records.

---

# 24. Composite Exercise Rule

Some exercises contain several movement patterns.

Examples:

- Burpee
- Turkish Get-up
- Clean and Press
- Thruster
- Mountain Climber
- Man Maker

A composite exercise must be assigned according to its **dominant prescribed purpose**, not whichever movement appears first.

Each composite record must include:

- primary family
- component patterns
- technical complexity
- required prerequisite exercises
- approved metric
- safety tier
- whether it is suitable for general public challenges

Highly technical composite exercises may be restricted or deferred from the initial launch catalogue.

---

# 25. Body-Part Names Are Not Families

The following must remain tags, filters or anatomical metadata:

- Chest
- Back
- Arms
- Shoulders
- Core
- Abs
- Legs
- Glutes
- Full Body

They must not be used as primary exercise families.

For example:

```
Incorrect:
Family = Chest

Correct:
Family = Push
Primary muscles = Chest
```

Similarly:

```
Incorrect:
Family = Core

Correct:
Family = Anti-Extension
Primary region = Trunk
```

# 26. Exercise Versus Wellness Overlap Rules

Phase 2D remains embedded in this production process through the following rules.

## Remains an Exercise

- Walking
- Running
- Stretching
- Mobility drills
- Yoga postures
- Breathing drills used as physical training
- Recovery movement
- Balance exercises

## Usually remains a Wellness Activity

- Drink water
- Sleep routine
- Screen-free bedtime
- Gratitude practice
- Meal planning
- Medication adherence
- Blood pressure check
- Mindfulness check-in
- Social connection
- Healthy meal choice

## May reference an Exercise without duplicating it

- Daily walking habit
- Morning mobility habit
- Stretch before bed
- Recovery walk routine
- Five-minute breathing break

These are prescriptions, routines or challenge templates that reference canonical exercises.

They are not necessarily separate Knowledge Items.

# 27. Family Assignment Decision Test

For every legacy exercise, reviewers should answer:

1. What is the exercise primarily trying to train?
2. What is the dominant movement pattern?
3. Is it a canonical exercise or a variant?
4. Is its distinction technical, behavioural or merely editorial?
5. Does it require a separate metric contract?
6. Does it require materially different safety guidance?
7. Is it actually a workout, routine or wellness prescription rather than an exercise?
8. Would retaining it separately create duplication?

The answers determine its canonical family and rationalisation decision.

---

# 28. Version 2 Governance Freeze

The following are frozen for the first catalogue-production pass:

- There are seven approved exercise domains.
- There are nineteen approved primary exercise families.
- Every exercise has one primary family.
- Secondary movement demands may be recorded separately.
- Muscles and body parts are not exercise families.
- Variants inherit from canonical parent exercises.
- Composite exercises are classified by dominant prescribed purpose.
- Exercise–wellness overlap is resolved through references and prescriptions rather than duplication.
- New families must not be introduced during rationalisation without a documented taxonomy exception.
