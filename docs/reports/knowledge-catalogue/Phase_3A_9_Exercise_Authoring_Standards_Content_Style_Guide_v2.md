# Phase_3A_9_Exercise_Authoring_Standards_Content_Style_Guide_v2

**Status:** Constitutional Editorial Standard

---

# 1. Purpose

This standard defines how every Exercise Knowledge Record is authored.

It applies to:

- Human editors
- Fitness experts
- Medical reviewers
- Translators
- AI generation
- Future automated knowledge pipelines

This standard governs **content quality**, not software implementation.

---

# 2. Editorial Philosophy

Every exercise should be:

- technically accurate
- beginner friendly
- medically responsible
- internationally understandable
- culturally neutral
- easy to translate
- reusable across every Tiizi product

The catalogue is educational, not promotional.

---

# 3. Audience

Every exercise must work for:

- complete beginners
- recreational users
- experienced exercisers
- coaches
- healthcare professionals reviewing content
- AI systems

Content should never assume prior expertise.

---

# 4. Reading Level

Target:

**CEFR B1–B2 equivalent**

Sentences should be concise.

Prefer:

> "Keep your back straight."
>

Avoid:

> "Maintain an optimal spinal alignment throughout the concentric and eccentric phases."
>

Technical detail belongs in dedicated fields, not general instructions.

---

# 5. Tone of Voice

The catalogue is:

- calm
- encouraging
- factual
- respectful
- inclusive

Never:

- aggressive
- military
- shaming
- exaggerated
- sales-oriented

Avoid phrases such as:

- "Destroy your abs"
- "Burn fat fast"
- "No pain, no gain"

---

# 6. Canonical Naming Rules

Exercise names should:

- use internationally recognised terminology
- avoid slang
- avoid marketing language
- avoid dosage
- avoid punctuation unless required

Preferred:

- Push-up
- Walking Lunge
- Romanian Deadlift

Avoid:

- Killer Push-up
- Ultimate Lunge
- 100 Rep Push-up

---

# 7. Description Standards

## Short Description

Maximum:

25 words.

Purpose:

Identify the exercise.

Example:

> "A bodyweight pushing exercise that strengthens the chest, shoulders and triceps."
>

---

## Long Description

Maximum:

150–250 words.

Should explain:

- purpose
- major benefits
- typical use
- important considerations

Not a scientific article.

---

# 8. Execution Standards

Every execution section follows the same structure:

1. Starting position
2. Movement
3. Finish position
4. Return
5. Repeat

Never mix these steps.

---

# 9. Instruction Style

Use imperative verbs.

Correct:

- Stand
- Lift
- Lower
- Breathe
- Return

Avoid passive voice.

---

# 10. Coaching Cue Rules

Maximum:

5 coaching cues.

Examples:

- Keep your core engaged.
- Drive through your heels.
- Keep your elbows close.
- Breathe naturally.

Short.

Actionable.

---

# 11. Common Mistakes

Every exercise should document:

3–7 common mistakes.

Example:

Push-up

- Sagging hips
- Flaring elbows excessively
- Incomplete range of motion
- Holding the breath

Avoid vague advice such as:

> "Doing it incorrectly."
>

---

# 12. Safety Language

Safety wording must be factual.

Preferred:

> "Stop if you experience sharp pain."
>

Avoid:

> "This exercise is dangerous."
>

The catalogue informs rather than alarms.

---

# 13. Contraindications

Use consistent categories.

Examples:

- Shoulder injury
- Wrist pain
- Recent surgery
- Balance impairment
- Pregnancy considerations

Never provide diagnosis.

---

# 14. Inclusive Language

Avoid gender-specific wording.

Preferred:

> "The participant"
>

> "The user"
>

Avoid:

> "He should..."
>

> "She should..."
>

---

# 15. Difficulty Descriptions

Difficulty refers to technical learning.

Not fitness level.

Example:

Push-up

Difficulty:

Intermediate

Reason:

Requires upper-body strength and trunk stability.

---

# 16. Metric Language

Always use canonical units.

Examples:

Correct:

- repetitions
- seconds
- minutes
- kilometres

Never:

- reps
- secs
- mins
- kms

---

# 17. Image Standards

Every exercise should eventually include:

- primary demonstration image
- start position
- finish position

Optional:

- side view
- incorrect technique
- muscle overlay

No branded clothing or equipment should be required in illustrations.

---

# 18. Video Standards

If videos are included:

Preferred duration:

30–90 seconds.

Must demonstrate:

- setup
- execution
- common mistake
- correct pacing

No motivational narration required.

---

# 19. Translation Standards

Translations preserve meaning.

Not literal wording.

Canonical IDs never change.

Technical terms should use approved glossary entries.

---

# 20. AI Authoring Rules

AI may assist in drafting content but must not introduce new canonical concepts, taxonomy values or safety classifications.

Any AI-generated content requires editorial review before publication.

---

# 21. Evidence Standards

Educational claims should be based on accepted fitness practice.

Avoid unsupported marketing claims such as:

- "Best exercise for everyone."
- "Guaranteed fat loss."

Where evidence is uncertain, use balanced wording.

---

# 22. Version Control

Every exercise records:

- author
- reviewer
- medical reviewer (if applicable)
- editorial reviewer
- version number
- review date

This provides accountability and supports future updates.

---

# 23. Quality Checklist

Before approval, every exercise must satisfy:

- Canonical name approved
- Classification complete
- Execution complete
- Safety guidance present
- Metrics validated
- Relationships defined
- Challenge compatibility reviewed
- Editorial review passed
- Translation review completed (where available)
- Governance metadata recorded

No exercise enters the production catalogue until all mandatory checks pass.

---

# 24. Review Workflow

Every new or revised exercise follows the same lifecycle:

```
Draft
    ↓
Technical Review
    ↓
Safety Review
    ↓
Editorial Review
    ↓
Translation Review
    ↓
Governance Approval
    ↓
Published
```

Each stage has a clearly identified reviewer and version checkpoint.

---

# 25. Governance Freeze

Version 2 freezes the following editorial principles:

- Exercises are educational knowledge assets, not marketing copy.
- Canonical terminology takes precedence over local or colloquial variants.
- Instructions are concise, structured and action-oriented.
- Safety guidance is factual, proportionate and consistent.
- Measurements use controlled units and terminology.
- AI-generated content must follow the same standards as human-authored content.
- Every published exercise has a documented review history.
