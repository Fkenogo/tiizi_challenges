## Cross-boundary signals

- **Exercise versus interest:** Running, Walking, Yoga and similar labels exist both as exercise-like catalogue records and as broad preference categories. A preference selection does not prove an executable/loggable activity definition.
- **Exercise versus wellness activity:** eight `movement` wellness records have their own IDs, metrics, defaults, rich content, log collection, and fallback behavior. Similar names do not make them equivalent to `CatalogExercise` records.
- **Exercise versus workout/preset:** Home Workouts, Group Fitness, HIIT/Circuit, seeded challenge presets, and challenge template entities describe sessions or programmes rather than atomic movements.
- **Variant versus duplicate:** left/right, hold/dynamic, equipment, regression/progression and difficulty wording can indicate real execution differences even when the root movement is shared.
- **Prescription versus identity:** template activity rows such as `Squat` target 100 reps and `Yoga Session` target 30 minutes combine an activity label with a prescription. They are not catalogue definitions with stable IDs.
- **Taxonomy versus content:** `Strength`, `Cardio`, `Mobility`, difficulty values, metric types and units constrain records but are not activities themselves. They remain separate inventory rows so taxonomy drift is visible.

## Review limitations

- Live Firestore catalogue/template/challenge documents were not read, so additional production-only duplicates may exist.
- Candidate groups are name-led signals. No biomechanical equivalence, equipment equivalence, or content-safety judgment was made.
- The 31-entry isometric content document uses combined left/right concepts while the JSON often splits sides. This is a provenance/variant signal, not evidence that either representation is wrong.
