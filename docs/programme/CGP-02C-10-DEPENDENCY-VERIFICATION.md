## 3. Dependency Conclusions

The dependencies establish that:

- Historical Preservation is the next Blueprint subject;
- historical intelligibility is already a recurring constitutional boundary but not yet a complete dedicated treatment;
- amendment traceability is narrower than general Historical Preservation;
- supersession, retirement, withdrawal and rejection require preserved historical context but remain distinct status treatments;
- adoption, effect and current status cannot be inferred from historical representation;
- preservation must remain technology-neutral;
- storage, archival, retention, repository, Governance Index and Validation Rules remain separate; and
- no existing package or accepted assessment authorizes drafting.

These conclusions bound planning only and establish no constitutional answer.
